A heterogeneous geological rare-earth phosphate feed represented by the high-grade monazite ore supplied by Lynas Rare Earths, Australia, to van Alin et al. (2023). The reviewed study does not establish its precise mine or deposit of origin. XRD, TIMA and elemental analyses characterize a mixed feed rather than a pure monazite species: the supplement reports monazite-group and other phosphate phases with additional gangue. This resource is distinct from monazite-(Ce), monazite-(Nd), elemental rare earths, a rare-earth commodity and secondary processing waste. The source's high-grade description is not an independently certified commercial grade. No constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Biofilm formation and qualitative surface alteration under bioleaching conditions

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Klebsiella aerogenes (NCBITaxon:548) [TaxonMech]

Klebsiella aerogenes ATCC 13048 attached to the mixed monazite ore and formed surface biofilms in laboratory phosphate-free medium. Microscopy showed preferential localization at physical surface imperfections and no detected preference for particular grain chemistry in the examined fields. Representative day-14 images show more eroded surfaces in the biotic arm than in abiotic controls, supporting a qualitative association with microbial surface alteration. This study does not provide a new quantitative rare-earth dissolution yield, a demonstrated molecular dissolution pathway, an isolated product or industrial deployment.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The ore was reported as sized to 50-200 micrometres and gamma irradiated at 50 kGy for 11 h. The subsequent biofilm Methods collectively describe the ore and two separate crystal substrates as 0.5-2 mm; this size-description discrepancy is unresolved. Cells were pregrown aerobically for 3-5 days at 30 degrees C and 120 rpm in NBRIP with 30 g/L glucose and 1 g/L each KH2PO4 and K2HPO4. Ore and separate crystal specimens were mounted on glass using E6000 adhesive, treated with 70% ethanol for 10 min and UV for 1 h per side, then incubated in 100 mL phosphate-free NBRIP at initial pH 6.2 +/- 0.4, 30 degrees C and 140 rpm. Initial inoculum was approximately 1e7 cells/mL by flow cytometry, not CFU. Glutaraldehyde fixation applied to enumeration aliquots, not the whole bioleaching inoculum. The ore was described as the sole phosphate source, but inoculum washing and an ore-free phosphorus-limited growth control are not documented; growth dependence on ore phosphorus is therefore not established. Mounted specimens were collected at 4, 8, 16 and 24 h and days 2, 3, 5, 8, 11 and 14, repeatedly washed, and stained for CLSM or prepared for SEM/FIB. Separate live-imaging assays used a reported 1% monazite slurry and continued observations through days 20-70; their images are not pooled with mounted-slide measurements. Sterile NBRIP and Milli-Q-water controls assessed abiotic surface appearance and autofluorescence. One Methods passage specifies control pH 6, 5 and 4, whereas the live-imaging passage and Figure S6 specify 7, 5 and 4. A matched pH trajectory, replicate counts and quantitative erosion statistics are not supplied.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: vanalin2023
Figures 1-3 and the Results document attached cells, biofilm development and a thin biofilm layer by complementary CLSM, SEM and a FIB cross-section; Appendix Figure S12 provides a CLSM cross-section of mature biofilm. Main Figure 1A/D and Appendix Figures S7 and S10 distinguish early attachment, greater coverage during maturation and reduced coverage during dispersion on the ore. Main Figure 3E/F localizes cells around roughness and surface imperfections. Reported attachment within 10 min and growth to approximately 1e9 cells/mL are explicitly data not shown and are not adopted here as verified quantitative observations. Images from the separate monazite-muscovite and xenotime crystal arms are contextual comparisons, not measurements of this ore.
Partial Source: vanalin2023
Figure 5 compares representative high-grade ore surfaces after 14 days of abiotic and biotic treatment and supports the authors' qualitative erosion interpretation. It is not a registered before-and-after image series of the same grain and supplies no erosion volume, elemental release time course, closed mass balance or measured recovery fraction. Similar-pH abiotic comparisons are described in the discussion, but the control pH lists disagree elsewhere and no matched pH trajectory is shown. Contact-mediated chemistry, organic-acid action and EPS contributions remain interpretations without a causal intervention in this experiment.
Partial Source: vanalin2023_appendix
Figures S1-S2 and Tables S1-S4 support a heterogeneous ore, not a pure Ce- or Nd-dominant monazite specimen. Table S3 reports 65 wt% monazite group among crystalline phases only; its listed 65, 3, 15 and 18 sum to 101 despite a printed total of 100. Table S1 describes monazite-(Ce) and cheralite refinement references, while Table S2 lists CePO4 and LaPO4 reference patterns. Overlapping crandallite/florencite peaks and a tentative kihlmanite assignment are acknowledged by the source. These model and reference-pattern differences preclude promoting the tabulation to whole-feed grade or a unique mineral-species identifier. No new extraction denominator is calculated from these phase estimates.
Partial Source: vanalin2023_appendix
Figures S5-S7 document cytometry and fluorescence controls. Hoechst and DiTO-1 stain DNA with different permeability properties; their combined signal is not a live-cell count, and Figure S7 explicitly identifies autofluorescent epoxy around grains. SEM observations independently support cell attachment. Main Figure 4C-E and Appendix Figures S15-S16 show cells on grains with different elemental composition, supporting only the reported absence of detected selective colonization in the examined fields. Figure S11 provides later live-imaging snapshots without a quantified recolonization rate.
Context_only Source: vanalin2023_video
The three source-labelled day-11/day-14 clips show depth-coded reconstructions of the mineral-associated fluorescent structures. Six sampled frames per clip span their approximately 16, 48 and 32 s display durations; these are viewing rotations, not elapsed culture time or independent replicates. They corroborate spatial imaging context but do not establish live-cell viability, metabolite activity, dissolution kinetics or rare-earth recovery.
Context_only Source: vanalin2023
The Introduction and Discussion quote rare-earth solution concentrations from Corbett and Fathollahzadeh studies published in 2017-2018. Those values are prior-study context rather than new measurements from the 2023 microscopy experiment, and are not imported as this feed's yield or as independent replication. The separate crystal arms and previously studied ore batches are not assumed to be the identical physical feed.
