## Billietite: original conflict and later panel attribution

The 2007 methods on printed949–950/PDF5–6 describe naturally sourced platy billietite crystals, one crystal per 10 mL solution in polyethylene cups. Set1 surfaces were not freshly cleaved; set2 were freshly cleaved. Acid exposures were 1.5 versus 2 h; water was 24 h, and 0.1 M carbonate at pH10.5 was examined for 1.5/24 h in set1 only. AFM was ex situ in air after washing/drying, within at most one hour, with repeat orthogonal scans. This does not establish an in situ bulk dissolution curve, an independent kinetic constant, or a recovered uranium product.

Table1, printed950/PDF6, explicitly lists 0.5 M **BaCl2·nH2O**, while the abstract, methods/results and Fig5 describe **CaCl2**. The printed table pH/ionic-strength values are retained in the numeric ledger, including calculated HCl ionic strength0.001. They are not silently normalized to an idealized solution. The original source does not supply run identifiers or an erratum that maps that Ba row to a particular Ca specimen.

Fig7, printed956/PDF12, labels the pictured Mg/Na surface as becquerelite in its caption, whereas the adjoining results describe billietite and connect Mg panels7a/b and Na panels7c/d to billietite set1. Fig5 is explicitly billietite/Ca; K treatment shows non-fresh [310] versus fresh [100] behavior. Fig6's optical before/after photographs and Fig10's area/depth plot remain surface evidence. Printed958/PDF14 states that pit depth expresses relative pit growth versus surrounding-surface lowering. The tabulated coordination-dependent ionic radii and suggested adsorption-site model do not measure adsorbate positions or absolute surface dissolution rates.

The 2011 follow-up is valuable but narrower than a formal correction. Table1, printed80/PDF2, attributes billietite AFM/SEM/FTIR/LA-ICP-MS to reference13 (the 2007 paper) and distinguishes new XPS work. On printed85/PDF7, Fig4b labels a **Ca–HCl billietite** panel and Fig4d a **Mg–HCl billietite** panel. Direct visual comparison shows the distinctive pit/vein pattern from 2007 Fig5a's right panel reproduced in4b and the clustered dark-pit pattern from 2007 Fig7b reproduced in4d. The caption explicitly calls the images modified from references12/13; this is visual reproduction lineage, not exact pixel equality or an independent assay. It corroborates the later authors' Ca/Mg attribution without supplying original specimen metadata, a correction to 2007 Table1, or a corresponding Na-panel reconciliation.

The 2011 methods cannot be transferred wholesale to 2007: its general pH2 experiments run10–30min, and AFM examination is usually12h/sometimes3d later. Its Table1 also prints billietite with seven waters, unlike the eight-water2006 original refinement and2007 source. Its new XPS Table3 gives surface atomic ratios and below-background entries; those are neither phase-pure compositions nor bulk leaching fractions. No owner import is proposed from this broader experiment without separate specimen mapping. The existing owner's cautious exclusion of the disputed original arms remains defensible.

## Compreignacite: experiment, accounting and model audit

The 2017 work uses synthetic K- and Na-compreignacite mixed with cleaned quartz, not the natural seven-water species alone. Main §2.1/§3.2.1 and Table3 describe nominal eight-water materials and nonideal measured cation ratios. The 2006 billietite first-page formula and the 1998 compreignacite first-page seven-water formula are distinct identity evidence, not instructions to repair the synthetic model.

Main §§2.1–2.4, Tables1–3 and SI1–3 establish duplicate upflow columns, approximately1cm3 total volume, 20µm frits and0.2µm inline filters, room-temperature operation and ambient CO2. Quartz-only controls include no-U and dissolved-U arms; their input chemistry differs from the mineral columns and must not be treated as an otherwise identical comparison. Mineral-column TC rises from0.21 to2.78mmol/L and pH from6.65 to8.40; Na, Ca, Mg, Cl, sulfate and mineral loadings also differ. The measured TC ratio is13.24, while the paper summarizes it as approximately tenfold. No carbonate-only causal coefficient is inferred.
