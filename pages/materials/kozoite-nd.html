Kozoite-(Nd) mineral

Kozoite-(Nd) is the neodymium-dominant orthorhombic rare-earth hydroxyl carbonate mineral with ideal formula Nd(CO3)(OH), approved as IMA 1998-063. It is distinct from elemental neodymium, kozoite-(La), hydroxylbastnäsite-(Nd) and commercial rare-earth feeds. The original Niikoba, Hizen-cho, Japan material consists of crystals smaller than 10 µm in an alkali olivine basalt cavity. Six-grain microprobe analyses show a mixed rare-earth composition, including Nd0.85 and La0.61 on a two-cation basis, rather than a pure Nd end member. Fluorine was not detected; no detection limit is supplied. Carbon and hydrogen were measured separately by triplicate elemental analysis using approximately 5 mg portions. Synchrotron powder diffraction and Rietveld refinement give Pmcn, a = 4.9829(1), b = 8.5188(2), c = 7.2570(2) Å and Z = 4. Hydrogen positions were not located, and the original study could not exclude a nearly orthorhombic monoclinic structure without suitable single crystals. The thermal experiment below concerns this natural mixed-REE material, not an isolated synthetic Nd end member. These sources do not establish microbial formation, industrial recovery or a jurisdiction-specific criticality designation.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Abiotic thermal dehydration and decarbonation of natural kozoite-(Nd)

other

Heating natural mixed-REE kozoite-(Nd) produced two endothermic mass-loss events. At the first peak, 485 °C, thermogravimetry measured 16.07 wt% loss and simultaneous gas chromatography-mass spectrometry detected CO2 and H2O. The authors attribute 11.98 wt% to CO2 and 4.09 wt% to H2O from the gas-analysis results. At the second peak, 716 °C, the mass loss was 9.89 wt% and only CO2 was detected. The observations support dehydration and decarbonation under the stated heating conditions. Heated-residue phases were not identified, so no particular Nd oxide product, isolated-metal recovery or final-product purity is established.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Miyawaki et al. (2000) used a Rigaku TG-DTA/GC-MS analyzer in helium, heating from room temperature to 900 °C at 20 °C/min with simultaneous analysis of the exhaust gas. The thermal aliquot mass, helium flow, replication and measurement uncertainty are not specified; the approximately 5 mg portions described elsewhere belong to elemental analysis and are not assigned to this thermal run. The two events are within one heating experiment, not independent replicated treatments. Estimated total evolved CO2 and H2O were 21.87 and 4.09 wt%, respectively, whereas the separate elemental analysis in Table 1 gives CO2-equivalent 21.10 wt% and H2O-equivalent 5.44 wt%. Although the source calls these results comparable, their differences and unspecified uncertainties are retained here. No microorganisms, solution extraction assay, separated metal product or industrial-scale operation was involved.
