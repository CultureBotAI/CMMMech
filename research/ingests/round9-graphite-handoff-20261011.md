## Identity and designation boundary

The [original EU Regulation 2024/1252](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1252/oj)
has an unqualified graphite entry in Annex II(v) and a battery-grade entry in
Annex I(k). Article 3(3) explicitly discusses whether synthetic graphite should
remain in the strategic list at its first update. A natural-only interpretation
would therefore lose part of this edition's scope. This inspection concerns the
original act, not a claim to have reviewed every later consolidated amendment.

The [BGS UK 2024 Criticality Assessment](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/538471/1/OR24047_report.pdf),
OR/24/047, names natural graphite in Table 1 (PDF page 11) and Table 11 (printed
page 44, PDF page 48). Both tables were read; Table 11 was also inspected as a
rendered page. This is the named assessment edition, not a new claim that it is
current government policy.

The [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf),
printed pages 92–93 (PDF pages 96–97), separates natural graphite's commercial
forms from synthetic substitutes. The first page was visually checked. It
supports a commodity distinction; it does not identify every commercial grade
with a separate mineral species, establish a battery-purity threshold, or make
carbon's element identifier a commodity identifier. Its market statistics are
not imported as concentration observations.

## Microbial lead and contrary evidence

[Langwaldt and Kalapudas (2007)](https://www.journalssystem.com/ppmp/pdf-79310-15375?filename=Bio-beneficiation-of-mult.pdf),
*Physicochemical Problems of Mineral Processing* 41:291–299, studied a
graphite-bearing black-shale feed to concentrate sulfides. Methods, results,
tables and conclusions were read; Table 6 and Figure 2 were visually checked.
Freeze-dried bacterial preparations were used in a 4-litre flotation cell.
Graphite was an unwanted component, rather than the intended purified product.
The authors did not find a substantial recovery/grade improvement from
bioflotation. Table 6 labels the relevant mixture “Graphite+Minerals”; its
percentage must not become a pure-graphite assay or recovery yield. Cultivating
biomass in larger fermenters does not establish industrial flotation deployment.
This paper is contextual counterevidence to an indiscriminate microbial-benefit
claim, not proof of useful microbial graphite recovery. The PDF spells the
first author's name Langwaldt; the journal landing page spells it Landwaldt and
gives a January date despite the PDF's June acceptance date. Use the year and
PDF authorship until the metadata discrepancy is resolved.
