## Feed, cultures and experimental design

Manuscript §2.1, pages 6–7, and Table 1 report jaw crushing, pulverization and homogenization; Figure S1 shows d80 47.5 µm. The seven feed values are Na 18.35, Al 10.95, Ca 2.02, K 2.01, Li 1.13, Mg 0.46 and F 40.23 wt.%. Their sum is 75.15%, not a complete mass closure. The paper describes digestion/ICP-OES but does not supply a complete digestion recovery, fluorine-specific validation or mineral mass-fraction refinement. Source-assigned feed phases are fluorite, cryolite, simmonsite and elpasolite.

Sections 2.2–2.3, pages 7–9, give an *A. niger* culture from the Soil and Water Research Institute collection, Karaj, without an accession, and a *G. nicotianae* isolate from Choqart iron-mine soil/wastewater/tailings samples. The latter is described by serial dilution, LB isolation, Gram stain and morphology, with a citation to Abdollahi et al. (2024), DOI 10.1016/j.jece.2024.112141. The cited paper's currently accessible preview concerns a separate nickel-bearing feed. It does not establish accession identity or transplant its controls into this study. Current BacDive type-strain metadata and its automatically text-mined citation are taxonomic context, not evidence that the experimental isolate is that type strain. No organism CURIE is asserted in the owner.

Table 2 defines six culture arms. Tests 1–3 use bacterial LB at 10, 30 and 50 g/L; tests 4–6 use fungal modified Sperber medium at the same nominal loadings. Each 250 mL flask receives 90 mL medium and 10 mL inoculum, 30 °C, 150 rpm, up to 30 days. LB contains 10 g/L NaCl, 5 g/L yeast extract and 10 g/L tryptone; its initial pH is 7 and initial reported Eh −230 mV. Modified Sperber contains 11 g/L glucose, 0.5 g/L yeast extract and 0.25 g/L MgSO4·7H2O, initial pH 3.2. These media and pH differences preclude attributing the cross-system contrast to species identity alone.

No matched sterile/pH-controlled abiotic arms, independent replicate count, error-bar definition, inoculum cell density, sampling-volume replacement correction, evaporation correction or complete Li balance were located in the complete AAM/SI scope. Isolation plates being autoclaved is not evidence that the leaching feed or experimental controls were sterilized. Figures contain error bars, whose statistical definition is unresolved. Residues are washed and air-dried for 24 h in §2.3; §2.4 separately describes 70 °C/24 h drying and gold coating for FE-SEM. These may be different preparation steps and are not automatically an incompatible protocol.

## Dissolution values and denominator limits

Equation 1, page 9, prints `Li dissolution (%) = V*C1/(m*C0)*100`, defining C0 as the WAE concentration in mg/L, C1 as leachate mg/L, m in g and V in mL. With those literal definitions, a conversion or clarified solid-feed basis is needed for a dimensionless percentage. No mg/g or mg/kg repair is selected. A conditional inventory at 10 g/L, exactly 100 mL and the reported 1.13 wt.% head is 1 g feed/11.3 mg Li, or 113 mg/L if fully dissolved. This is an arithmetic diagnostic, not a measured concentration, a correction to Equation 1, or verification of “100% recovery.”

All 36 Table S2 rows were rekeyed/checked, preserving the sequence 0, 10, 15, 17, 22, 30 days for each arm. The reported day-22 → day-30 pairs are:

| Test | Culture/loading | Reported dissolution % at 22 → 30 days | Decline in percentage points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | G. nicotianae, 10 g/L | 43.75 → 37.50 | 6.25 |
| 2 | G. nicotianae, 30 g/L | 35.32 → 27.00 | 8.32 |
| 3 | G. nicotianae, 50 g/L | 21.25 → 12.50 | 8.75 |
| 4 | A. niger, 10 g/L | 100.00 → 86.10 | 13.90 |
| 5 | A. niger, 30 g/L | 66.25 → 55.30 | 10.95 |
| 6 | A. niger, 50 g/L | 37.50 → 27.08 | 10.42 |

All six start at a printed 0.00 and strictly decline after day 22. Test 2 has an earlier 20.83 → 20.83 tie; the pre-22 sequence is nondecreasing, not strictly increasing. Figures 1c and 2b instead place the first two nonzero points near days 5 and 10. Their dates must not be silently mapped onto SI days 10 and 15. The abstract/conclusion assigns the bacterial 43.75% maximum to day 30, conflicting with the results narrative and SI. Source-reported percentages remain qualified prose, not schema `observations` (which currently require source mg/kg concentration cells and analysis dates).

Section 2.4 specifies Vista-Pro ICP-OES for lithium. Pulp filtration is described, but a Li-assay filter pore size and full aliquot ledger are not provided. The stated 0.45 µm membrane applies to HPLC mobile phases, not automatically to the Li assay. A decline in dissolved Li does not identify its destination. The authors propose precipitation with metabolites, including lithium oxalate; no Li-specific phase assignment or closed solid/liquid balance establishes that explanation here.

## HPLC, original phases and kinetics

Table S1 has 36 numeric entries and 36 dash cells. All four before/after columns are printed mg/g, with the mass denominator unresolved. Gluconic acid is 9670 → 4230, citric 1221 → 183, oxalic 10124 → 3123 and malic 180 → 124. All decrease; several values exceed 1000 mg/g. The 14 amino acids have mixed changes. Dashes remain missing, not zero. Without a clarified unit/basis and appropriate medium/culture blank, these columns do not establish net production or which ligand caused Li dissolution. The HPLC procedure and chromatographic conditions are described, but raw chromatograms, calibration/blank data and normalization masses were not acquired.

Original Figure 1b also qualifies the prose: the red and green ORP traces initially rise from −230 mV before declining, so “all values decreased until day 22” is not a faithful full-curve description. ORP is not a Li-specific redox assay. Figures 3 and S2 show the source-assigned parent phases; Figure 3b adds square-marked prosopite to fungal residues. The paper prints prosopite as CaAl2F4O4; no independent mineral-identity correction is made. The methods state a 4–80° XRD scan, while Figure S2 extends to 90°. No quantitative phase refinement or detection limit permits inference of complete dissolution/non-dissolution from relative peak heights.

Figure 4 has untreated, bacterial 10 g/L/30 d, fungal 10 g/L/22 d and fungal 10 g/L/30 d fields. All 26 printed EDX percentages were inspected. Their sums are approximately 100%; they are normalized local composition, not a bulk assay or Li mass balance. No Li column is printed. The source suggests medium Mg as an explanation for Mg in fungal residues, while the head already contains Mg; the source does not apportion those origins. The microscope date 12/23/24 is not a reactor-start or sampling date. Surface roughness and a layer-like appearance do not independently identify a lithium precipitate or rate-controlling reaction.

Figure 5's original 11 peak labels expose sample-assignment conflicts. The red fungal-30-day trace labels 1643.05 cm⁻¹, while the green fungal-22-day trace labels 1644.98 and the blue bacterial-30-day trace labels 1648.83. The text assigns 1643.05 to the optimized fungal arm and 1644.98 to bacteria. Those text/figure differences remain unresolved. Offset transmittance traces are not accepted as absolute percent-transmittance comparisons.

Equation 2 and Figure 6a both use `(1-(1-x)^(1/3))^2`; Equation 3 and Figure 6b both use `1-(1-0.45*x)^(1/3)`. There is no equation-versus-axis mismatch for the 0.45 factor, although its basis is unexplained. Figure 6 uses the rising portion through about day 22 and omits the late decline. The six printed R² values are 0.9606, 0.9796, 0.9723, 0.9923, 0.9994 and 0.9816. Conditional calculations test both printed SI times and visual main-figure times, with ordinary intercept fits and separately defined through-origin uncentered R². Using main-figure times and the latter convention reproduces tests 1, 3, 5 and 6 to four decimals; test 2 gives 0.979662 versus printed 0.9796, and test 4 gives 0.992029 versus 0.9923. This explains much of the plotting convention without correcting source dates, proving exact fit reproducibility or identifying a causal rate-limiting step.
