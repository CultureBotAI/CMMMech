Lutetium element Critical Material

Lutetium is the element Lu, represented here by its neutral-atom identity, distinct from dissolved Lu(III), chloride feed, host minerals and mixed rare-earth commodities. The United States designation refers to the named element in the Final 2025 List of Critical Minerals. A laboratory mixed-chloride experiment supports lutetium biosorption associated with washed Bacillus subtilis biomass; it does not establish a purified lutetium product or industrial recovery.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Lutetium biosorption by washed Bacillus subtilis biomass in an acidic mixed-REE solution

biosorption Microbial

Associated Organisms: Bacillus subtilis (NCBITaxon:1423) [TaxonMech]

Washed, non-autoclaved B. subtilis ATCC 7003 biomass lowered the lutetium concentration of a synthetic eight-REE solution relative to simultaneous no-biomass controls. The eight-element arm in supplementary Table S1 reports 74% relative lutetium biosorption at both two and four hours. This percentage uses the contemporaneous control concentration as its denominator, not an isolated-product recovery or a fraction proven to be intracellular.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Table 1 identifies ATCC 7003, grown in FP medium at 30 degrees C and 120 rpm for two to four days. Section 2.2 reports harvested biomass washed twice in acidified water, then exposed at room temperature and pH 2.5 (HCl) to chloride salts of La, Ce, Nd, Gd, Dy, Er, Yb and Lu, each initially 15 micromol/L, with 5 g dry biomass/L. Samples were centrifuged and filtered through 0.2-micrometre nylon before ICP-MS. Assays and no-biomass controls were duplicated; independent culture derivation is unspecified. Control-relative depletion corrects for losses present without biomass, but cannot distinguish cell-surface adsorption, intracellular uptake and cell-associated precipitation. The authors call the untreated biomass living; viability during the acidic assay was not established here. The separate 4 g/L live-versus-autoclaved comparison is not the Table S1 condition. No Lu-specific desorption, purified product or industrial operation is demonstrated by this accepted arm.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: breuker_2020_supp
Table S1, experiment 2, Lu column, reports means of two parallel assays of 74% at two and four hours. Experiment 1 and Figure S1 contain La, Nd, Dy and Yb, without Lu; their biomass-loading and time patterns are not lutetium measurements. The percentage is calculated by the main paper's Section 2.2 formula (control minus sample)/control.
Support Source: breuker_2020
Methods 2.1–2.2, Table 1 and Figure 2a locate the ATCC 7003 preparation and the Lu-specific bar for the eight-REE, 5 g/L, two-hour assay. The pattern shows greater fractional depletion for Lu than the lighter members of this mixture, without establishing universal selectivity across different feeds or proving a particular binding ligand.
Partial Source: breuker_2020
Figure 1 shows lower relative Lu biosorption at higher REE loading, while Figure 7 shows lower Lu depletion with autoclaved than untreated biomass under its separate 4 g/L conditions. Autoclaving changes cell integrity and does not by itself prove metabolically driven uptake. Section 3.1 explicitly leaves the dominant acidic-pH binding chemistry unresolved. Figure 4's separate Fe/Zr competition assay has different Lu percentages from Figure 2; its similar paired outcomes do not establish that all competing ions are harmless. The wastewater application concerns gadolinium, not lutetium.
