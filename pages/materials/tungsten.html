Tungsten element Critical Material

The element W, distinct from tungstate ions, scheelite and wolframite, tungsten carbides, experimental concentrates and mining wastes. The dated US designation concerns the named tungsten mineral commodity rather than every W-bearing material. A laboratory study supports enhanced tungsten release from an impure scheelite concentrate in Metallosphaera sedula cultures; it does not establish an isolated tungsten product, a specific tungsten redox pathway or industrial recovery.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-enhanced tungsten release from scheelite concentrate

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Metallosphaera sedula (NCBITaxon:43687) [TaxonMech]

Aerobic M. sedula cultures released more tungsten into clarified, filtered medium than corresponding uninoculated controls during incubation with an impure scheelite concentrate. This supports a microbial contribution to W mobilization in this system. The actual growth-supporting energy source and the relative contributions of direct mineral contact, microbially altered chemistry and abiotic acid dissolution remain unresolved. No enzyme-specific or tungsten-oxidation mechanism is established.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The study used M. sedula DSM5348, represented here by a species-level identifier, in DSMZ88 Sulfolobus medium initially adjusted to pH 2.0 with sulfuric acid and not further adjusted. Aerobic cultures at 73 degrees C received CO2 and 10 g/L scheelite concentrate of average particle size 1 mm from the Serido Scheelite Province, Brazil. The feed was heated at 180 degrees C for at least 24 h and autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 20 min. It was not pure CaWO4: methods report Fe- and Mn-oxide impurities, while Table 1 reports multiple elements. The methods' 19.0% Fe-oxide statement and Table 1's 22.6 wt% Fe value are not reconciled; no pure-mineral or feed-normalized yield is inferred. Uninoculated medium with identically sterilized concentrate supplied the abiotic comparison. During the 21-day experiment, centrifuged supernatants were filtered at 0.44 micrometres and analyzed by ICP-OES. Figure 3 reports three biological replicates with standard-deviation error bars. This operational filtrate measurement does not identify aqueous W species or count W retained in cells and solids. No isolated product yield or complete tungsten mass balance is reported.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: blazevic2019
Materials and Methods (Strain, Media Composition and Cultivation Setup; Metal Analysis), the first Results subsection and Figure 3A support a higher W signal in inoculated cultures than corresponding abiotic controls, with appreciable abiotic release also present. Figure 1 supports growth and mineral attachment in the tested impure concentrate. Figure 3's plotted x-axis is in days although its caption says hours; the y-axis describes log10 soluble metals with mg/L units. No exact concentration, fold change or recovery percentage is extracted from this ambiguously labeled graph.
Partial Source: blazevic2019
The intracellular Mn/Fe discussion explicitly states that the actual energy source and an oxidizable-substrate balance sufficient to explain growth could not be confirmed. The proposed Fe/Mn oxidation-driven mineral disruption is therefore a hypothesis; the Conclusion also allows indirect chemistry and acid dissolution. These limits prevent treating growth on the concentrate as proof of energy conservation from tungsten or pure scheelite.
Partial Source: blazevic2019
Figures 4-7 and the tungsten-deposit discussion support W-bearing surface encrustation in processed cells, so filtrate W alone is not total mobilized W. The microscopy preparation included 30 days of oxic dehydration after 21 days of culture and focused-ion-beam preparation. The authors describe the deposits as carbide-like and call for further W-environment spectroscopy; no pure WC phase, resolved W redox route, in-vivo product stoichiometry or useful material yield is accepted here. This associated observation does not convert the bioleaching result into demonstrated recovery.
