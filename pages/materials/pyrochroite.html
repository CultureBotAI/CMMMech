Pyrochroite mineral

Pyrochroite is the manganese(II) hydroxide mineral Mn2+(OH)2, listed as grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list. The historical year 1864 is not an approval accession. This mineral is distinct from manganese metal, manganese ore and manganese(III) oxide hydroxide species. The experiment below concerns source-assigned pyrochroite in a mixed laboratory product; it does not certify a phase-pure natural mineral or establish criticality by inheritance from manganese.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Pyrochroite-assigned product during Geobacter-associated manganese oxide reduction

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Geobacter sulfurreducens (NCBITaxon:35554) [TaxonMech]

A Manchester laboratory culture identified as Geobacter sulfurreducens transformed synthetic cobalt/nickel-bearing manganese oxide into a mixed assemblage assigned by powder XRD to pyrochroite and rhodochrosite, with transient feitknechtite. Pyrochroite-labelled reflections occur at 1, 17 and 72 hours. The evidence supports culture-associated formation under the reported anaerobic assay, not direct enzymatic precipitation of this phase, a uniquely resolved pathway, purified manganese hydroxide recovery or industrial deployment.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Buchanan's laboratory culture collection supplied Geobacter sulfurreducens without a strain accession. Living cells were grown anaerobically at 30 degrees C with acetate/fumarate, harvested in late logarithmic phase and washed three times in 30 mM bicarbonate buffer at pH 7.1 under N2:CO2 80:20. The experiment used a 5 mL aliquot of synthetic Co/Ni-asbolane suspension (stock nominally 2 mmol/L Mn in 10 mM bicarbonate at circumneutral pH), added sodium acetate at reported 10 mM and cells to final OD600 0.6, incubated anaerobically in the dark at 30 degrees C. The added cell volume and precise final mineral concentration are not reported. The synthetic feed was precipitated from Co/Ni/Mn chlorides with KMnO4/KOH, washed in alkaline NaCl and stored at 4 degrees C. Its asbolane designation is source-assigned; the thesis acknowledges structural uncertainty and mixed Mn oxidation states. No-cell controls contained the same mineral suspension and acetate. Sampling used aseptic anaerobic handling; residual slurry was dried anaerobically before powder XRD. No-cell diffraction patterns, killed-cell mineral controls or a demonstrated drying-independent phase assignment are supplied.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: buchanan_thesis
Chapter 4 Figure 4.2b-d and Results on printed pages 127-130 assign pyrochroite to post-inoculation solids at 1, 17 and 72 hours; Figure 4.2a is the starting material. Rhodochrosite coexists and dominates the displayed 72-hour pattern. The broad chemical reduction interpretation is consistent with changing Mn spectral valence, but the mineral remains a source-assigned component of dried mixed solids.
Partial Source: buchanan_thesis
Chapter 4 methods and Figure 4.1 (printed pages 123-128) show cell-associated dissolved-metal release above no-cell controls, followed by declining aqueous Mn between 17 and 72 hours. These are centrifuged-supernatant ICP-MS measurements, not direct aqueous valence measurements or isolated product recovery. Biological ICP-MS samples at 1 and 17 hours are described as triplicate, 72 hours as one sacrificial bottle, and controls as duplicate; Figure 4.1 includes 24 hours but its replication is unspecified. Neither aqueous loss nor retained metal establishes allocation to this particular mineral.
Partial Source: buchanan_thesis
Figure 4.2 (printed page 131) presents labelled mixed-phase patterns without quantitative phase refinement, phase-specific mass balance or a matched no-cell mineral time series. Pyrochroite and feitknechtite have overlapping low-angle peaks, so the accepted claim is the author's assignment, not independently re-refined purity. Figure 4.3 and pages 129-130 show cell-associated particles and heterogeneous elemental spectra; the 17-hour laths are only tentatively linked to the two phases. Elemental co-occurrence does not identify a unique cobalt/nickel host or prove lattice incorporation.
Partial Source: buchanan_thesis
Figures 4.4-4.8, the tables on printed pages 146-147 and Appendix 4.1-4.2 support a heterogeneous shift toward lower manganese valence. Fitted oxidation-state fractions carry the author's stated 10% error and differ substantially between surface-sensitive bulk measurements and spatially restricted transmission SXM. Only one imaged region per time point was possible, and coherent SXM data are absent at 72 hours. These are spectral fractions, not mineral abundances or conversion yields. A sequential one-electron pathway and direct cell-surface cytochrome participation remain interpretations; no enzyme perturbation or phase-specific electron-transfer test was performed.
Partial Source: buchanan_thesis
Source inconsistencies remain unresolved: the tables discussed as 4.1/4.2 are printed as 6.1/6.2; the 17-hour co-located Mn average differs between the main table and appendix/text; regional counts differ across captions; Appendix Table 4.2 says Mn in its caption but tabulates Co. Chapter 3 enrichment acetate wording differs from its medium table. The conclusion prints Mn(VI) where the chapter investigates Mn(IV). No corrected speciation series, growth-medium recipe or unique kinetic pathway is inferred from these discrepancies.
Context_only Source: ncbi_geobacter
NCBI 35554 resolves Geobacter sulfurreducens at species rank. It neither identifies the thesis's stock as strain PCA nor establishes mineral transformation by every member of this species.
