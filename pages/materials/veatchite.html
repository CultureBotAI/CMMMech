Veatchite mineral

Veatchite is the strontium borate mineral species with ideal formula Sr2B11O16(OH)5·H2O in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list, with grandfathered status G and original-description year 1938. That year is not a sequential approval identifier. Its three named polytypes are veatchite-1A, veatchite-1M and veatchite-2M, historically called veatchite-A, p-veatchite and veatchite, respectively. These are not three separate species owners. Veatchite is distinct from elemental strontium or boron, the borates commodity, and the Ca-Sr-ordered species calcioveatchite, SrCaB11O16(OH)5·H2O. Grice and Pring (2012) determined the structures of separate natural specimens using single-crystal X-ray diffraction: NMNH 145911 from Emet, Turkey, for triclinic 1A with space group P-1; NMNH 113264 from Reyershausen, Germany, for monoclinic 1M with P21; and RG701/1 from Sussex, New Brunswick, for monoclinic 2M with Cc. The last specimen replaced the preserved California type crystal and must not be attributed to that type locality. Borate layers and their stacking distinguish the polytypes; the authors caution that their powder patterns alone do not satisfactorily distinguish them. Hydrogen positions were located by difference Fourier maps and refined, but no new chemical analyses were performed on the two type specimens. The paper's 2M Sr/Ca refinement has a slightly Ca-dominant smaller cation site and is explicitly discussed as potentially a new species; it is not a pure Sr end-member measurement. Pekov et al. (2024) subsequently establish the distinct calcioveatchite species and emphasize site occupancy in the boundary with veatchite. They do not directly reanalyse the 2012 RG701/1 crystal, so its exact modern species assignment remains a specimen-level qualification here. The three original AM-12-015 deposited CIF models have now been inspected. Their correspondence to the paper's polytypes is inferred from the publisher association, cell metrics and structural data; the files do not explicitly identify the museum specimens. The deposited 2M model pairs Ca1 with Sr1 and Ca2 with Sr2, but its occupancies and refinement details differ from the printed Table 2c. Deposited and printed cell values also differ. The files contain neither observed reflection tables nor original refinement instructions, so they do not establish an authoritative correction of the printed values or a modern reassignment of RG701/1. The 2012 temperature/pressure associations are geological interpretations with unknown precise parameters, not controlled polytype-transition experiments. Natural mineral characterization and geological occurrence do not establish microbial activity, boron recovery, industrial deployment or a jurisdiction-specific criticality designation.
