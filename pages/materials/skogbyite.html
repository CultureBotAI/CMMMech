Skogbyite mineral IMA: Skb

IMA-approved zirconium-magnesium-manganese oxy-orthosilicate mineral of the braunite group (IMA 2023-085; symbol Skb in the primary description and Skg in the IMA Master List; status A, Approved 2024; full description published April 2025) with ideal end-member formula Zr(Mg2Mn3+4)SiO12 (written Zr(Mg2Mn3+4)O8(SiO4) in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 201; requiring Mn2O3 54.46 wt%, ZrO2 21.26 wt%, MgO 13.91 wt%, SiO2 10.37 wt%, total 100.00 wt%; Jonsson et al., 2025, European Journal of Mineralogy 37:269-277). Represents the Mg-dominant analogue of gatedalite, Zr(Mn2+2Mn3+4)SiO12, via the homovalent substitution Mn2+ <-> Mg2+, and is named after Swedish mineralogist Henrik Skogby (b. 1956) of the Swedish Museum of Natural History, Stockholm. Discovered in a complex regionally metamorphosed (Svecokarelian, ca. 1.87-1.80 Ga, 3-4 kbar, 550-600 degC) carbonate-silicate assemblage collected from the mine dumps of the Langban Fe-Mn oxide deposit, Filipstad Municipality, Varmland County, Bergslagen ore province, Sweden (59.86 deg N, 14.27 deg E), where it occurs as isolated microblasts in a Mn-bearing phlogopite, calcite, and dolomite groundmass and as inclusions within poikiloblastic pinakiolite, associated with hausmannite, jacobsite, macedonite, sparse baddeleyite, and late fracture-filling native lead, litharge, and hedyphane; holotype material (rock sample, polished thick and thin sections, and the mounted single crystal) is deposited at the Swedish Museum of Natural History (Naturhistoriska riksmuseet), Stockholm, Sweden, under catalogue number GEO-NRM20230033. Forms sparse, variably zoned, rounded to subhedral bluish-grey crystals mostly <=60 um (up to ~120 um) with a brownish-grey streak, sub-metallic lustre, opaque diaphaneity, brittle tenacity, no observed cleavage, parting, or twinning, estimated Mohs hardness 6-6.5 (by analogy with braunite and relative polishing hardness), calculated density Dcalc = 4.821 g/cm3 from the empirical formula and single-crystal unit cell, and axial ratio reported on p. 271 as "c : a = 0.4999" (which is the inverted ratio a : c = 9.4914 / 18.9875 = 0.49988, whereas true c : a = 2.0005). In plane-polarized reflected light it is bluish-grey with moderate reflectance (estimated ~17-21% in air by analogy with gatedalite), weak bireflectance, weak anisotropy, no pleochroism, and no internal reflections. Attenuated total reflectance Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR-ATR) spectroscopy (650-4000 cm-1; Figure 2) displays a strong Si-O stretching band at 904 cm-1 (shifted to lower energy compared with ~945 cm-1 in braunite due to Zr4+ replacing Mn2+ at M(1)). Wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analysis (19 point analyses, JEOL JXA-8530F Hyperprobe, 15 kV, 20 nA, 1 um beam; Table 1) yielded Mn2O3 56.02(212) wt% (range 54.80-62.52), ZrO2 14.24(91) wt% (11.43-15.88), SiO2 10.51(59) wt% (10.27-11.15), MgO 9.86(146) wt% (7.79-11.02), Fe2O3 3.78(26) wt% (3.52-5.57), Ce2O3 2.80(113) wt% (0.51-4.39), Al2O3 0.79(19) wt% (0.65-1.08), ZnO 0.09(3) wt% (0.05-0.14), CaO 0.06(7) wt% (with printed range 0.02-0.05 wt% below the printed mean 0.06 wt%), PbO 0.04(5) wt% (0-0.17), La2O3 0.01(1) wt% (0-0.08), and Na2O 0.01(1) wt% (0.00-0.02), with Table 1 printing Total = 98.42 wt% whereas the arithmetic sum of the 12 printed mean oxides is 98.21 wt% (a 0.21 wt% tabulation discrepancy). Combined with single-crystal site-scattering optimization on the basis of 12 O atoms per formula unit, the empirical crystal-chemical formula is M(1)(Zr4+0.69Ce3+0.10Mg0.06Ca0.01Zn0.01Pb0.01)Sigma0.87M(2)(Mn3+1.56Mg0.35Al0.09)Sigma2.00M(3)(Mn3+1.17Mg0.55Fe3+0.28)Sigma2.00M(4)(Mn3+1.50Mg0.50)Sigma2.00Si1.04O12 (or merged as (Zr4+0.69Ce3+0.10Mg0.06Ca0.01Zn0.01Pb0.01)Sigma0.87(Mn3+4.23Mg1.40Fe3+0.28Al0.09)Sigma6.00Si1.04O12, where the rounded M(1) cation subscripts sum to 0.88 apfu vs printed Sigma0.87). Crystallizes in the tetragonal system, space group I41/acd (#142), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 9.4914(4) A, c = 18.9875(10) A, V = 1710.52(17) A3, and Z = 8 (refined to R1 = 0.0460, R1(all) = 0.0675, wR2 = 0.1154, and GoF = 1.110 out of 903 unique reflections from 10,422 measured on a 0.08 x 0.08 x 0.09 mm crystal with MoKalpha radiation, with the Abstract on p. 269 stating 648 unique reflections with I > 2sigma(I) whereas Section 6.2 on p. 273 and Table 3 on p. 274 report 658 unique reflections with I > 2sigma(I)). The braunite-type structure (Tables 4-6) consists of eight-coordinated M(1) distorted cubes (<M(1)-O> = 2.292 A, refined s.o. Zr0.944(7), BVS = 2.97 v.u.), three Jahn-Teller-distorted octahedra M(2) (<M(2)-O> = 2.056 A, BVS = 2.58 v.u.), M(3) (<M(3)-O> = 2.079 A, BVS printed as 2.34 v.u. vs unrounded sum 2.32 v.u.), and M(4) (<M(4)-O> = 2.068 A, BVS printed as 2.43 v.u. vs unrounded sum 2.44 v.u.), and regular SiO4 tetrahedra (Si-O(3) = 1.636(3) A x 4, BVS = 3.88 v.u.). The seven strongest simulated powder X-ray diffraction lines (dcalc in A (Icalc; hkl), CuKalpha, Table 2) are 2.740 (100; 2 2 4), 1.678 (31; 0 4 8), 1.431 (16; 2 6 4), 1.678 (13; 4 4 0), 5.480 (12; 1 1 2), 2.373 (11; 0 4 0), and 1.431 (10; 2 2 12). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
