Keutschite mineral

IMA-approved copper-silver-arsenic sulfosalt mineral of the stannite group (IMA 2014-038; status A, Approved 2014; full description published January 2026; no mineral symbol is printed in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 112, or in the primary type description) with ideal end-member formula Cu2AgAsS4 (requiring Cu 29.01 wt%, Ag 24.62 wt%, As 17.10 wt%, S 29.28 wt%, sum 100.01 wt%, printed as "29.01 Cu, 24,62 Ag, 17.10 As, and 29.28 S, for a total of 100.00" with a comma decimal separator in "24,62 Ag" in Section 3, p. 3; Topa, Takagi Fredrickson, Stanley, and Dinca, 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:1-7). Represents the Ag-As analogue of stannite (Cu2FeSnS4) and is named in honour of Frank Keutsch (born 1971) for his significant contributions to the mineralogy of the Uchucchacua deposit, who first brought attention to this mineral during his study of the alabandite zone and was instrumental in the discovery and description of manganoquadratite, menchettiite, agmantinite, spryite, hyrslite, ramosite, and oyonite (and first identified argyrodite, billingsleyite, canfieldite, diaphorite, ferdowsiite, metastibnite, pirquitasite, smithite, stannite, stephanite, and trechmannite at this site; Abstract, p. 1, and Section 1, p. 2). Discovered in a sample extracted from the alabandite zone (Socorro section of the mine, characterised by high concentrations of Ag and Mn) of the Ag-Pb-Zn polymetallic deposit at Uchucchacua, Oyon District, Catajambo, Lima Department, Peru (type locality for seven Mn-bearing sulfides/sulfosalts -- benavidesite, uchucchacuaite, manganoquadratite, menchettiite, oyonite, agmantinite, and ramosite -- and seven Ag minerals including spryite); the sample utilised for description consists predominantly of alabandite and Mn-bearing calcite with associated crystals of manganoquadratite, proustite, and billingsleyite, as well as massive diaphorite and seligmannite-bournonite, with the alabandite zone also hosting chemically or structurally related agmantinite, pirquitasite, stannite, and kesterite, and keutschite having since been described at the Clara mine in Germany (Kolitsch et al., 2019) and the Sacaramb ore deposit in Romania (Dinca et al., 2025) (Abstract, p. 1; Section 2, p. 2; and Section 5, p. 5). The holotype material is deposited in the reference collection of the Naturhistorisches Museum Wien, Vienna, Austria (specimen no. N 9736), part of the specimen used for the study is deposited in the collection of the Mineralogical & Geological Museum Harvard (no. 2017.13.10), and another part (Figure 1, p. 3) is deposited in the Frank Keutsch collection (Section 1, p. 2). Occurs as rare metallic, highly lustrous, blocky, free-standing crystals measuring up to 3 mm (Figure 1 caption on p. 3 notes a twinned crystal 2.5 mm in size on proustite and manganoan calcite; Table 3 on p. 4 lists crystal colour Grey and crystal habit Irregular) that exhibit a grey colour with a slight green-brassy tint on manganoquadratite and proustite crystals (Abstract, p. 1, and Section 2, p. 2); crystals are opaque with a grey-black (Abstract, p. 1) / black (Section 2, p. 2) streak, non-fluorescent, brittle tenacity, neither fracture nor cleavage observed, Mohs hardness 4-5, Vickers micro-indentation hardness VHN25 = 352-380 with a mean of 368 kg mm-2, and calculated density Dcalc = 4.663 g cm-3 in Section 2 (p. 2, from the empirical formula and single-crystal X-ray data) or 4.66 g cm-3 in Table 3 (p. 4; density could not be measured directly owing to limited size and material available). In plane-polarised incident light, keutschite is grey-white in colour, with very weak bireflectance and very weak pleochroism in greenish-grey to grey tints, and under crossed polars is weakly anisotropic with rotation tints in shades of greenish grey to grey and no internal reflections (Abstract, p. 1, and Section 2, p. 2); reflectance values (Rmin, Rmax in %) measured in air using a Zeiss Axiotron microscope, a Crystal Structures (Lanham) superstage, a J&M Tidas diode array spectrometer, a Zeiss WTiC standard, and Cavendish Instruments Onyx software from 400 to 700 nm at 0.823 nm intervals (Abstract, p. 1, Section 2, pp. 2-3, and Table 1, p. 3) at the four COM standard wavelengths are 25.2, 26.1 (470 nm), 29.6, 29.4 (546 nm), 29.4, 29.2 (589 nm), and 28.5, 28.6 (650 nm) (reported from 400 to 700 nm in 20 nm steps in Table 1, p. 3). Wavelength-dispersive field-emission electron-microprobe analyses (JEOL "Hyperprobe" JXA 8530F FE-EPMA at the Central Research Laboratories of the Natural History Museum, Vienna; 25 kV, 20 nA, 2 um beam diameter, 10 s peak / 5 s background; standards synthetic Ag metal for AgLalpha, natural chalcopyrite for CuKalpha, synthetic hauerite for MnKalpha, synthetic InAs for AsLalpha, natural stibnite for SbLalpha, natural galena for PbMalpha, and natural sphalerite for SKalpha; n = 5 analyses on one grain; Section 3, p. 3, and Table 2, p. 4) yielded mean Ag 23.46 wt% (range 23.14-23.94, SD 0.34), Cu 29.53 wt% (range 29.32-29.74, SD 0.18), Mn 0.031 wt% (range 0.02-0.04, SD 0.01), As 16.09 wt% (range 15.29-16.71, SD 0.67), Sb 1.20 wt% (range 0.48-2.14, SD 0.84), Pb 0.11 wt% (range 0.04-0.14, SD 0.07), S 29.06 wt% (range 28.80-29.11, SD 0.15), and mean of spot totals 99.46 wt% (range 99.12-99.97, SD 0.50; sum of rounded element means = 99.481 wt%). Both the Abstract (p. 1) and Section 3 (p. 3) print the empirical formula as Cu2.05Ag0.96(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma0.99S4.00 ("based on eight atoms"), whereas Table 2 (p. 4) gives three empirical formula normalizations: Cu2.00Ag0.97(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma1.99S4.03 (SigmaMe = 3.97) on the basis of 8 apfu (4Me + 4S), Cu1.99Ag0.96(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma1.99S4.00 (SigmaMe = 3.95) on the basis of 4 S apfu, and Cu2.02Ag0.97(As0.96Sb0.04)Sigma2.00S4.05 (SigmaMe = 4) on the basis of 4 Me apfu (where Table 2 prints Sigma1.99 instead of Sigma0.99 for 0.95 + 0.04 = 0.99 and Sigma2.00 instead of Sigma1.00 for 0.96 + 0.04 = 1.00). Crystallizes in the tetragonal system, space group I-42m (#121), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters (Oxford Diffraction Xcalibur E diffractometer, graphite-monochromatised MoKalpha radiation, lambda = 0.71073 A in Table 3, p. 4, misprinted as "lambda = 0.7173 A" in Section 4, p. 3, at T = 293(2) K on an irregular 0.118 x 0.088 x 0.050 mm crystal fragment) a = 5.5834(15) A, c = 10.021(3) A, V = 312.40(14) A3, and Z = 2 (note that the Abstract on p. 1 prints the axial ratio as "a:b:c = 1:1:0.897", which equals c/(2a) = 0.8974 rather than c/a = 1.7948). The crystal structure was solved by charge flipping (SUPERFLIP) and refined on F2 (JANA2006) to R = 1.99% and Rw = 4.61% for 286 observed reflections with I > 3sigma(I) (R = 2.17%, Rw = 4.78% for all 308 unique reflections out of 1490 measured reflections, Rint [I > 3sigma(I), all] = 2.62%, 2.63%, S = 1.05 for 15 parameters; Table 3, p. 4). In the stannite-derivative structure (Table 4 and Section 5, p. 5), tetrahedrally S-coordinated cations occupy Wyckoff positions Cu at 4d (0, 0.5, 0.75; Ueq = 0.024 A2), Ag at 2b (0, 0, 0.5; Ueq = 0.032 A2), and As at 2a (0, 0, 0; Ueq = 0.009 A2) with S at 8i (0.77733(13), 0.77733(13), 0.86852(10); Ueq = 0.014 A2), forming alternating (001) cation layers of Cu atoms and ordered (Ag + As) atoms. The five strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines measured on a Rigaku Rapid II 2D image-plate diffractometer with MoKalpha radiation [dmeas in A (Im, Irel, dcalc in A) (hkl); Table 5, p. 5] are 3.09 (vs, 100, 3.1011) (112), 2.79 (ms, 11.4, 2.7917) (200), 1.966 (s, 20.4, 1.974) (220), 1.864 (s, 34.2, 1.8646) (204), and 1.659 (s, 27.2, 1.6653) (312) (note that the Abstract on p. 1 prints the five strongest lines as "[d in A (intensity) hkl]: 3.101 (100) 110; 2.792 (11) 200; 1.974 (20) 220; 1.665 (34) 204; and 2.846 (27) 312", misprinting 110 for 112, 1.665 for 1.865 on 204, and 2.846 for 1.665 on 312). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
