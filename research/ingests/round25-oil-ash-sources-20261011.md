# Heavy-fuel-oil combustion ash: source assessment

The new record uses **heavy-fuel-oil combustion ash**, a secondary resource. The inspected primary study does not identify a collection fraction that would justify labeling its actual sample specifically as fly ash. Coal ash, petroleum coke and spent catalysts remain different resources; elemental criticality is not inherited.

The [machine-readable dossier](round25-oil-ash-sources-20261011.json) records exact searches, access failures, current checks and retained-file hashes. The selected full primary source is [Rasoulnia and Mousavi (2016), IJCEA 7(6):365–368](https://doi.org/10.18178/ijcea.2016.7.6.606), acquired directly from its [publisher PDF](https://www.ijcea.org/vol7/606-G0007.pdf). All four pages and Figures 1–4 were read and visually inspected. This supports a deliberately bounded record, with incomplete controls and analytical reporting made explicit.

## Identity and overlap

Hidden and ignored files and relevant symlinks were included in records, research, reviews, history and the named round24/round25 raw directories. The round24 frontier already named the three RSC/Waste Management leads, so this is not new-literature discovery. No oil-combustion-ash root existed in the searched pre-curation corpus. The selected paper describes an unnamed thermal power plant in Tehran. The incompletely inspected RSC accepted manuscript instead names Neka; these provenance descriptions are not silently merged.

[NCBI Taxonomy 5061](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=5061&mode=Info) resolves *Aspergillus niger* at species rank. PTCC 5210 remains the paper's reported culture accession; the current collection entry was not independently resolved. No strain CURIE is invented.

## Accepted evidence and limits

Section II describes actual ash ground and screened below 75 micrometres and culture production in sucrose medium. Flask cultivation used fourteen days at 30 degrees C and 130 rpm. A 1-L bubble column produced culture liquid over seven days at 30 degrees C and 760 mL/min aeration. The authors state biomass removal without its procedure or a residual-cell test. Both spent media then contacted ash at 3% w/v, 60 degrees C for seven days. The reactor's 1-L value describes cultivation, not a verified 1-L leaching operation.

Page 367 and Figure 2 report 97% V and 50% Ni extraction with flask-derived medium, compared with 100% V and 33% Ni with reactor-derived medium. These remain explicitly source-reported values. The complete paper lacks the liquid-metal assay, sampling fraction, yield equation, replicate count and uncertainty needed to reconstruct them. Figure 1's undefined error bars concern agar colony diameter, not leach replication. No matched sterile-medium, pure-acid or pH-controlled ash-leaching comparison is reported. The record therefore supports a reported culture-liquid association rather than a quantified microbial enhancement or a purified product.

HPLC identifies different acid profiles in the two cultivation conditions. Their durations and aeration differ, so the experiment does not isolate vessel scale or one metabolite. Proposed nickel-oxalate precipitation is not independently established by Figure 3, whose treated-solid labels include NiO. The diffraction text emphasizes sodium iron sulfate, while the plotted assignments include additional phases. Peak disappearance does not prove zero residual vanadium, and selected FE-SEM surfaces do not establish quantitative porosity or safe residue reuse. Longer reactor cultivation encountered aeration difficulties from biomass accumulation. Industrial deployment and complete process balance are not demonstrated.

## Deferred source extensions

1. [RSC 2016, DOI 10.1039/C5RA24870A](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/getauthorversionpdf/C5RA24870A): the first web opening identified a 35-page accepted manuscript and exposed introductory/early-methods text. Continuation and figure requests subsequently failed, as did direct downloads. This is **not** recorded as a complete primary audit. Resolve Neka versus Tehran provenance, source overlap, chemical controls, fitted versus measured optima and TCLP interpretation before adding claims. The same authors and matching 97%/50% result cannot be counted as independent replication.
2. [RSC 2015, DOI 10.1039/C5RA00128E](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2015/ra/c5ra00128e): primary metadata and abstract only. Complete acidophile methods, controls, figures and downstream product accounting remain unavailable. No abstract recovery or material-synthesis claim is imported.
3. [Waste Management 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.wasman.2016.04.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wasman.2016.04.004): the DOI returned a small Elsevier linking page, not full text; PubMed showed a browser check and the author page offered a request. Thermal-pretreatment and fungal-method comparisons require the complete study.
4. [Bioresource Technology 2016, PII S0960852416307738](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960852416307738): indexed title/limited description identifies a related reactor-optimization lead; DOI 10.1016/j.biortech.2016.05.114 was traced in a review bibliography. The direct primary route returned 403. Full methods and overlap need assessment before any claim import.

These four leads are extensions of the same resource identity, not four new material records. EAF steel slag remains the separate prior frontier; this focused pass did not acquire or reassess that study.
