Hafnium element Critical Material

Hafnium (Hf), distinct from its Hf(IV) complexes, hafnium oxide phases, zirconium-bearing minerals and commercial alloy or waste streams. The US 2025 critical-mineral list names hafnium individually; the designation is mapped to this element without extending it to every Hf-bearing material. Curated evidence concerns cell-free binding of Hf(IV) clusters by a purified bacterial protein, not microbial ore extraction or industrial metal recovery.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Cell-free Hf(IV) cluster binding by bacterial ferric ion-binding protein

other Microbial

Associated Organisms: Neisseria gonorrhoeae (NCBITaxon:485)

Purified recombinant Neisseria gonorrhoeae ferric ion-binding protein (nFbp) retained multiple Hf(IV) equivalents after removal of unbound small molecules. Crystallography resolved oxo-Hf clusters attached to Tyr195 and Tyr196 in an open protein cleft. This supports a laboratory protein-cluster interaction; physiological Hf uptake, ore dissolution, Hf(IV) reduction and recovery of elemental hafnium were not demonstrated.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The protein was expressed in Escherichia coli TOP10 carrying pTrc99A/Fbp/Ng and purified by cation exchange chromatography. Holo protein was stripped of iron with citrate to prepare apo protein; this was a purified-protein assay, not live-cell exposure. Table 1's representative Hf preparation used a 50-fold molar excess of [Hf(NTA)2]2-, 100 mM NaCl, 4 mM phosphate and 25 mM bicarbonate at pH 7.4 and 310 K for 3 h. ICP-AES after gel filtration and/or ultrafiltration reported 4.03 +/- 0.10 mol Hf and 1.39 +/- 0.04 mol phosphorus per mol protein. Methods describes averages of three determinations on each sample, not three independent cultures. The crystallization preparation separately states four molar equivalents of Na2[Hf(NTA)2].2H2O under similar buffer, temperature and time conditions, followed by ultrafiltration in 0.1 M KCl; 50 or 100 equivalents reportedly gave similar results. Hanging-drop crystals were grown at 290 K with PEG 4000, KCl and imidazole/malate buffer pH 7.7. The paper does not identify the N. gonorrhoeae donor strain; E. coli is the expression host and does not demonstrate whole-cell hafnium handling in this experiment.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: alexeev_2003
Table 1 and Methods establish retention of Hf with purified apo nFbp. Figures 1-3 and Table 2 resolve differently ordered three- and five-Hf clusters across nine protein molecules in the crystal, with phosphate present in some clusters. Anomalous diffraction supports assignment of the heavy atoms to Hf. These are molecular clusters, not proof of a bulk HfO2 mineral product.
Partial Source: alexeev_2003
The structural comparison and single-metal holo/apo preparations support binding-site interpretation, but no Hf-specific Tyr-mutant result or matched protein-free cluster-formation outcome is reported. References to loss of iron binding in Tyr mutants do not establish Hf-specific necessity. Some Hf positions are disordered, twinning complicated refinement, and HfO2 geometry supplied refinement restraints. The paper's crystal preparation reports 4.8 Hf per protein whereas its initial feed is stated as four equivalents; these distinct reported quantities are retained without deriving a recovery fraction or resolving the discrepancy.
Context_only Source: pdb_1o7t
The deposited entry links the same DOI, bacterial protein and Hf cluster components; its current revision history records 2026 metalloprotein remediation involving nonpolymer descriptions and derived coordination information. It is associated structural evidence from the same experiment, not an independent replication or a new microbial extraction study. No coordinate re-refinement or validation of all atomic assignments was performed in curation.
