## Selected owners and primary evidence

[Newberyite](../../../data/records/newberyite.yaml) and [schertelite](../../../data/records/schertelite.yaml) are distinct grandfathered mineral species in the official September 2026 IMA list, pages 154 and 194. Their formulae are respectively Mg(PO3OH)(H2O)3 and (NH4)2Mg(PO3OH)2(H2O)4; 1879 and 1902 are original-description years, not approval identifiers. Their shared Mg/P content does not make them elements, commodities or the same phase as struvite. No criticality designation is inferred.

The prior newberyite query led to the 1994 journal publication [10.1080/01490459409377995](https://doi.org/10.1080/01490459409377995). Its metadata was verified, but the publisher PDF returned 403. The concrete primary source adopted here is the complete 198-page [Entrena doctoral thesis](https://hdl.handle.net/10481/14393), deposited by Universidad de Granada. Its cover and university defense catalogue date it to 1994; DIGIBUG publication metadata gives 1995. The records preserve this distinction. The thesis and associated article are not treated as independently replicated experiments. A 2008 stone-conservation paper cites the 1994 title with *M. xanthus*, whereas the DOI metadata and thesis identify *M. coralloides* D; no organism transfer is made from that secondary citation.

## Culture experiment and uncertainty

Complete Methods printed pages 62–75 (PDF 71–84), target Results 107–111 (PDF 118–122) and Discussion 137–139 (PDF 148–150) were visually inspected, including Table 8 and all four original diffraction figures 19–22. Supporting general Results 77–79 and Discussion 124–133 were also checked. Not every introductory or bibliography page was read. The original scan and rendered pages remain outside Git for independent review.

The live laboratory strain was historically named *Myxococcus coralloides* D, also described as *Corallococcus coralloides*, a dispersed-growth derivative obtained through liquid passage. No stock or sequence accession for D was supplied. Static aerobic cultures at 28°C used CT Casitone/phosphate/Mg media. Table 8 ties minor phases to CT-2, CT-13 and CT-15: CT-2 starts at pH 6.8 with nominal Mg 4.06 mM; the other variants have 10 mM phosphate with 10 or 20 mM Mg and derive from the pH 6.5 base. Methods permits 3 mL tubes or 100 mL in 250 mL flasks, but phase figures are not mapped to vessel sizes. Static products were collected after 20–22 days by decanting, repeated water washing and room-temperature drying, then ground for diffraction. Boiling agar extraction belongs to other experiments and is not imported into these liquid observations.

The figures show minor-phase peaks or shoulders in struvite-dominated patterns. The authors explain that SEM-EDX cannot distinguish these phases independently. Their roughly 5%-or-less estimate is not a quantitative refinement or recovery yield; the 43% detection statement lacks a usable replicate denominator. No phase-specific mass balance, purified product or industrial process is accepted. The target-phase account supplies no sterile/killed-cell result or replicate-resolved abundance. Cellophane experiments concern struvite and do not establish a minor-phase-specific mechanism.

The thesis explicitly proposes struvite aging/decomposition as an alternative origin of newberyite. Static and shaken cultures were harvested at different times (20–22 versus 10–12 days), so that comparison does not isolate agitation. For schertelite, metabolic ammonium supply is proposed; the reagent appendix lists trace ammonium in magnesium sulfate despite the absence of deliberate inorganic-ammonium addition. No assertion that all ammonium was generated biologically is made. Wet-phase identity before processing, and phase-specific final pH, remain unresolved.

## Independent chemical evidence

The complete [Frazier, Lehr and Smith 1963 original](https://msaweb.org/AmMin/AM48/AM48_635.pdf), American Mineralogist 48:635–641, was acquired from the society and all seven pages inspected visually and textually. It gives abiotic schertelite preparation at 25°C from magnesium acetate, monoammonium phosphate and ammonium acetate, with chemical, optical and X-ray evidence. Magnesium-acetate hydration is unspecified. This concentrated reagent route is not a matched control for the bacterial medium.

Pages 639–641 describe rapid incongruent schertelite dissolution in water to form struvite. Water washing therefore matters for preservation, although its actual effect on the thesis samples was not measured. Months-long air alteration products were inferred chemically and were not identified by diffraction. The abstract names a different hydrolytic mixture from the detailed body; the records retain the conflict and accept the specifically scoped body observations. The paper also supplies alternative newberyite chemistry through lower-concentration phosphate solutions and hannayite hydrolysis, without complete newberyite preparation recipes for those statements. This is context against obligatory microbial causation, not a refutation of recovered-phase assignments.
