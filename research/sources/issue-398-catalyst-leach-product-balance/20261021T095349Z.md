## Feed, process and causal limits

The selected spent Polish-market catalyst sample had 35.1 wt% Cu, 29.9 wt% Zn and 9.2 wt% Al by AAS (§2). Pellets were 4.5 mm in diameter and 3.5 mm high. Plant, service history, batch identity, sampling variation and assay uncertainty are not provided. This composition does not define every CuO-ZnO-Al2O3 catalyst. Figure 1 assigns feed phases qualitatively; Figure 2 shows fragmented material with a 500 µm scale, and Figure 3 is qualitative area EDS. Neither SEM nor EDS supplies a bulk product purity assay or quantitative mineralogy.

Four smelting arms used 100 g feed, 10 g coal and a graphite crucible, with different slag additions, temperatures and times (§§3.1–3.2). Graphite is an additional reductant. These are four conditions, not statistical replicates. No microbial actor or gene mechanism is involved. The experimental record does not establish industrial emissions performance or a waste-free process.

## Leaching recipe and version comparison

Published §4.1 specifies feed below 90 µm, then NaOH 200 g/dm3 at 75°C for 120 min, followed by H2SO4 180 g/dm3 at 60°C for 120 min. Both stages print l/s=10 with no basis or units. The peroxide solution is described only qualitatively; dose, concentration, rate and addition schedule are absent. Type 392 filtration is identified, and fine material makes filtration difficult. Wash volumes, transfer losses, dry mass of each intermediate and final liquor volume definitions remain unavailable.

The first preprint's method list lacks l/s 10; v2 and the published article add it without a unit definition. V2 also supplies the filter type and 600°C air-heating detail absent from the corresponding v1 passages. Neither inspected method text specifies a peroxide dose. V1 attributes residue carbon to the analysis method; v2 and the published article attribute it to insoluble coal remaining in residue. These version differences do not supply an independent purity assay. The published version governs current curation.

Reference 19 §2.3 explicitly defines a mass ratio for a different 20 g BOF sludge experiment, with mechanical stirring, washing and AAS. Its pp.47–48 compare mass ratios 5 and 10 and discuss solution-density effects. The catalyst article says its conditions were adjusted from prior experience and pilot tests; it does not identify an exact copied protocol or catalyst-run mass ratio. Importing the sludge ratio basis, stirrer speed, wash procedure or solution density would add unverified catalyst details. Neither conversion from l/s 10 nor the reported final 0.8 dm3 volumes establishes reagent charge or consumption.

The published text says the acid liquid contains only copper, then states that zinc is present at 100–150 times less. This does not establish zero Zn or a quantitative selectivity coefficient without the precise concentration basis and analytics. Its earlier insoluble-Cu/Zn-oxides sentence conflicts with the reported liquid fractions; the current owner already retains the narrower observations.

## Liquid products, residue and yield bases

Section 4.2 reports, from 100 g catalyst, 0.8 dm3 Zn liquid at 23 g/dm3, 0.8 dm3 Cu liquid at 43 g/dm3, and 33 g residue. The products 0.8×23=18.4 g Zn and0.8×43=34.4 g Cu differ by 0.1 g from the separately stated balance 18.5 and34.5 g. This is compatible with rounded inputs; there is no raw worksheet or precision information to choose a replacement.

The balance-derived liquid yields are approximately 61.873% Zn and 98.291% Cu, consistent with the source's 61.9% and 98%. They represent dissolved fractions, not isolated metal or precipitation yields. Alternative ratios calculated from the printed volumes/concentrations are diagnostics only.

Heating the residue in air at 600°C reduces its mass by about 10%; Figure 4 still identifies zinc aluminate and carbon. Treating 10% as exact would leave 29.7 g and remove 3.3 g, but the source says about 10% and separately assigns 30 g spinel plus 3 g carbon. These rounded statements do not establish a bulk stoichiometric phase assay, oxidation-state balance or carbon purity.

An explicit conditional check uses rounded formula masses Zn 65.38, Al 26.98, O 16 g/mol. If 30 g were pure stoichiometric ZnAl2O4, it would contain about 10.70 g Zn and 8.83 g Al. Combining that assumption with the stated liquid mass gives about 97.65% total Zn and 95.97% Al. These are illustrative accounting sensitivities, not observed recovery values or corrections to the printed 97.5%/96%. The source's overall yields include retained residue. No independently assayed spinel purity, feed-to-product complete inventory, quantitative XRD, replicate error, or measured downstream electrolysis/precipitation/reuse outcome is supplied.
