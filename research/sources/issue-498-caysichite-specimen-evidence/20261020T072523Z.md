## Specimen and model evidence

The 1974 paper distinguishes hand-picked colourless Canadian material from a separately analysed yellow grain containing Y-rich interstitial material and a probable synchysite-(Y) rim. Table 3 averages and the yellow-grain measurements must not be pooled. Its type collection number 10402 identifies deposited material, not a modern reanalysis. The 1978 crystal came from Hogarth, and its cation distribution and hydrogen/water scheme are model interpretations; hydrogen was not located. Neither a new portal upload date nor a transcription of that old model supplies new measurements.

The 2025 material is from Ploskaya Mountain, Russia. Table 1 gives chemical analyses normalized to Si+Al=4, with calculated water; its five columns include earlier literature analyses, rather than five newly measured equivalent specimens. The two new analyses each summarize five to ten spots. The methods and Table 1 identify analysis 2 as the one used for the structural study. Table 5 lists alternative proposed occupancies using the same experimental site-scattering factors, M1 33.54 and M2 31.98 electrons. Those alternatives are not independent diffraction runs. The authors explicitly qualify the elemental site assignments as plausible hypotheses. Hydrogen was not located; carbonate protonation and water allocation remain interpretations. A modern matched Canadian chemistry/structure study is still required before accepting equivalence or proposing a species split. [Primary article, pp.3–8 and 11–13](https://pureportal.spbu.ru/files/141550754/2025_caysichite_Y.pdf).

The printed 2025 Table 2 contains 26,875 measured, 4,472 independent and 3,906 observed reflections, with 4,472 used in refinement. The abstract instead describes 4,472 unique observed reflections. The actual CIF and underlying reflection data are needed to test metadata and model provenance; reading Table 2 does not authorize silently repairing the abstract.
