## Primary evidence and accepted scope

The selected primary is [Sklute et al. (2023), Frontiers in Microbiology
14:1272245](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2023.1272245), PMID 37928658,
PMC10622975. The publisher and Europe PMC XML agree on identity. Full text,
methods, ferrihydrite results, alternative-substrate results, discussion,
figure captions, and Figures 1, 3, and 4 were inspected. The article declares
CC BY 4.0. Raw article, figures and supplements remain outside Git.
Supplement PDF pages 6-9 (Figures S2-S5) were rendered and visually inspected:
room-temperature/4 K fits, the temperature series, and XRD comparisons preserve
the mixed-product and heated-control interpretation. Table S1 on page 2 is an
equilibrium prediction with suppressed phases, not a measured product yield;
its values are not imported. Poppler was unavailable, so the existing isolated
`/private/tmp/aimech-pdf-tools/bin/python` with PyMuPDF 1.28.2 rendered the pages,
without changing repository dependencies.

The experiment uses **Desulfovulcanus ferrireducens Ax17**, obtained as DSM
111878. The live [DSMZ strain record](https://www.dsmz.de/collection/catalogue/details/culture/DSM-111878)
confirms Ax17 and type-strain status. [NCBI Taxonomy 2831190](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=2831190)
resolves the species and lists Ax17/DSM111878/ATCC TSD-233 as its type strain;
this is a species-rank identifier, not a newly invented strain taxon.

- **Feed and conditions:** Methods, Microorganism and incubation conditions,
  reports separately synthesized two-line nanophase ferrihydrite at 100 mmol/L,
  50 mL modified DSM 981 medium in 160 mL bottles, pH 6.80 +/- 0.05, H2:CO2
  80:20 at 200 kPa, 0.5 mM cysteine-HCl, and 1.3 mM FeCl2 supplementation.
  A 1% logarithmic-phase inoculum had been transferred at least three times
  on the acceptor. Growth experiments were triplicate, at 55 C for about five
  days. The record preserves the reported loading rather than converting it
  into mass-based recovery or inventing a specific surface area.
- **Controls:** otherwise identical uninoculated medium at 55 C and room
  temperature, over the same duration, tested thermal and medium-driven
  transformations. Cysteine and Fe(II) additions were shared by the controls.
  No killed-cell or genetic control is claimed.
- **Outcome:** ferrihydrite-grown cells reached approximately 10^7 cells/mL;
  ferrozine after oxalate dissolution reported 24 mM Fe(II), versus 2-3 mM in
  controls. Figure 1A shows the visibly magnetic black biotic solid. This is
  oxalate-extractable Fe(II), not an exclusively dissolved fraction. The
  reported 84% filter retention is not an isolated recovery yield and is not
  imported as an observation.
- **Phase evidence:** Figures 2-4 compare reference, biotic and abiotic spectra
  and microscopy. FTIR-ATR, Mossbauer, XRD and TEM/SAED together support a
  magnetite-rich, cation-deficient mixed product. Figure 3 at 4 K excludes a
  pure magnetite product; XRD alone leaves magnetite/maghemite/cation-deficient
  magnetite ambiguity. Figure 4 shows crystalline nanograins and diffraction
  consistent with magnetite plus salts, while the heated control contains
  ferrihydrite, goethite and lepidocrocite. No numerical phase purity is inferred.
- **Counterevidence:** heated abiotic ferrihydrite transformed toward
  lepidocrocite, with a possible minor magnetite contribution in XRD. Thus
  magnetite presence alone is not uniquely biological. Akaganeite and
  lepidocrocite treatments yielded nonmagnetic, poorly resolved products and
  oxalate-extractable Fe(II) not significantly different from controls; the
  selected outcome cannot be generalized across all Fe(III) minerals.
- **Limits:** synthetic-medium laboratory biomineralization, not ore
  extraction, elemental Fe production, purified magnetite isolation, or
  pilot/industrial performance. Dissolution-reprecipitation and cellular
  phosphate contributions remain mechanistic interpretations. The paper's
  discussion gives 0.07 g KH2PO4 per 50 mL, whereas its Methods gives 0.14 g/L
  (0.007 g per 50 mL). No phosphate mass or phosphate-source mechanism is
  curated from this internal inconsistency.

The schema's quantitative observations contract covers exact dated mg/kg
source cells. The study's mM, cell counts, percentages and spectra do not fit
that contract; relevant values remain qualified prose and no observations are
created.

Live searches used the web search provider without date or domain restrictions;
only first returned result sets and selected primary/authority pages were
inspected, without pagination or an exhaustive literature review. Exact queries
and access failures are retained in the companion JSON.

[Byrne et al. (2015), DOI 10.1098/rsif.2015.0240](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2015.0240)
was an accessible alternative examined through its full primary XML. It grows
G. sulfurreducens in a 50 L vessel then transforms synthetic ferrihydrite in
separate 10 mL-10 L bottles. Its reported 120 g is a calculated capacity based
on biomass sufficient to inoculate 28 L, not a measured pure-magnetite batch
from one 50 L mineral reactor. Hematite and siderite contaminate products;
10 L error bars are repeated samples of one bottle. The main text does not
identify a strain or explicit matched uninoculated controls. It was not selected
because Sklute2023 more directly resolves strain, abiotic alternatives, and
mineralogical caveats. Byrne's supplementary archive was acquired but is not
used to support the accepted record.

[Secondary Mineralization of Ferrihydrite Affects Microbial Methanogenesis
in Geobacter-Methanosarcina Cocultures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5038019/)
remains a deferred phosphate-dependent phase-selection lead. Two Europe PMC
full-text requests returned HTTP 500; PMC web opening returned a browser check.
Search text does not substitute for an acquired methods/supplement review.

Exact-title/DOI searches for corrections and retractions returned the original
studies and related records, with no correction/retraction identified in those
bounded result sets. Publisher and PubMed pages did not display a linked notice
for Sklute2023 in this inspection. This is an access-limited check, not proof
that no notice could exist. Strong counterevidence is retained from the primary
study's controls and alternative minerals, rather than inventing a refutation.
