Strontium element Critical Material

Strontium (Sr), represented as an element distinct from dissolved Sr(II), strontianite or other mineral species, and strontium-bearing commodities or wastes. Laboratory cultures of Bacillus sp. TK2d lowered soluble Sr from a defined saline SrCl2 medium while Sr-bearing carbonate-rich material formed. The EU 2024 critical-raw-material designation names strontium; it is not inherited by every strontium compound or waste.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated strontium removal into carbonate-rich precipitates

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Bacillus sp. TK2d (NCBITaxon:1630002) [TaxonMech]

Bacillus sp. TK2d cultures removed Sr from the filtered liquid fraction and produced extracellular Sr- and Ca-bearing precipitates. Electron diffraction supported aragonite-like carbonate material, while Sr XANES fits supported a changing mixture of SrCl2-like and SrCO3-like local environments. Ureolysis and cell-surface association are consistent contributors, but an obligatory molecular pathway and a pure strontianite product are not established. The result concerns a defined laboratory salt medium, not treatment of radioactive wastewater or an isolated strontium product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Horiike et al., Medium and cultivation conditions and Sr removal test: marine broth 2216 at 3.74 g/L (described as 10% broth), final NaCl 30 g/L, autoclaved then filtered through 0.45 micrometre pores, supplemented with urea at 20 g/L and 1.0 mM Sr as SrCl2. A 0.5 mL, OD600 0.1 preculture inoculated 50 mL in a 100 mL flask, incubated at 30 degrees Celsius and 120 rpm in triplicate. Parallel salinity tests used source-labelled 0.3%, 3%, 5% and 10% NaCl. The isolation plate was set to pH 7.6; the paper does not provide a time-resolved broth pH series or a pH-matched abiotic arm. Daily aliquots were centrifuged at 21,000 x g for 5 min; the ICP-AES procedure included 0.2 micrometre filtration and triplicate analytical readings per sample. The two centrifugation descriptions specify 4 and 20 degrees Celsius, respectively; their relationship is not resolved. Uninoculated Sr-containing medium was the negative control. Microscopy, diffraction and XANES examined collected solids at different times; these are not measurements of one identical final sample. Reagent SrCl2 was used with no radionuclide activity stated; the Discussion explicitly leaves actual contaminated seawater and 90Sr tests for future work.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: horiike_2017
Results, Isolation of a urease-producing microorganism, and Figures 1-2: at 3% NaCl the text reports soluble Sr declining from 1.0 mM to 0.13 mM at day 2 and below 1.0e-7 mol/L after 4 days. The stated bound is retained rather than converted to zero or an exact 100% recovery. The Methods describe uninoculated controls; the screening Results report little decline in the negative control and four other isolates. Figures 1-2 show culture time courses rather than overlaid full negative-control curves. Ammonium accumulation accompanies Sr decline but is not a urease knockout or inhibition test.
Support Source: horiike_2017
Figures 3-5 and the solid-characterization Results report Sr/Ca in precipitates, P-containing dense regions associated with cells at day 2, and extracellular mineral material at later times. The reported electron-diffraction spacings are consistent with aragonite. Table 1 gives two-standard XANES fitted SrCO3-like fractions of 52% after 5 days and 87% after 10 days for the 1 mM starting-Sr cultures; the remaining fitted fractions are SrCl2-like. These are model-dependent spectral fractions, not crystalline-phase purities, fractions of total initial Sr recovered, or proof that all solid Sr is strontianite.
Partial Source: horiike_2017
Figure 2 and its Results show much weaker Sr removal at 5-10% NaCl than at 0.3-3%, despite growth at some higher salinities. Urease-positive isolates did not all remove Sr effectively. The chemical comparison also formed Sr-bearing carbonate without bacteria, using a different Na2CO3/CaCl2/SrCl2 recipe; it is not a chemistry-matched control proving microbial necessity for precipitation. The data do not establish a specific Mg ion-channel mechanism or universal Sr selectivity.
Partial Source: horiike_2017
Figure 1b shows a modest Mg concentration change although the Results describe no decrease; the defensible comparison is much stronger depletion of Sr and Ca, not exact Mg exclusion from all phases. EDS nondetection in selected carbonate material has an approximately 0.1% weight/weight detection limit and does not establish zero Mg throughout the precipitate. Long-term leaching stability, isolated-metal yield and deployment are untested in this study. The linked supplement was not acquired after bounded PMC, publisher and Europe PMC attempts; claims here rely on the inspected main text, Table 1 and main Figures 1-6, not an independent reassessment of supplementary micrographs or chemical-control curves.

Causal Graphs & Molecular Pathways (1)

Bacillus sp. TK2d ureolytic carbonate alkalinization and soluble strontium incorporation into mixed carbonate precipitates

REPRESENTATIVE EXAMPLE Mechanism: tk2d_sr_biomineralization

Causal pathway linking representative Bacillus UreABC urease subunits (UniProtKB:P77837, UniProtKB:P71035, UniProtKB:P75030) and reviewed Bacillus UreG nickel-incorporation accessory GTPase (UniProtKB:Q07403) to urea hydrolysis and ammonium/carbonate generation in Bacillus sp. TK2d cultures (3.74 g/L marine broth 2216, 30 g/L NaCl, 20 g/L urea, 1.0 mM SrCl2, 30 C, 120 rpm) and soluble Sr depletion (to 0.13 mM at day 2 and <1.0e-7 mol/L after day 4 at 0.3-3% NaCl) into extracellular aragonite-like Ca/Sr carbonate precipitates with two-standard Sr K-edge XANES fits of 52% SrCO3-like (day 5) and 87% SrCO3-like (day 10) coordination alongside residual SrCl2-like fractions (Horiike et al., 2017).
