Paramontroseite mineral

Paramontroseite is the orthorhombic vanadium dioxide mineral, ideal VO2, listed as grandfathered (G), original-description year 1955, in the September 2026 IMA list. The year is not a numbered IMA approval. It is structurally distinct from generic VO2 and rutile-type or monoclinic VO2 phases, and chemically distinct from montroseite VO(OH). Evans and Mrose established the natural diffuse-B phase in altered Bitter Creek montroseite crystals using a Pbnm model. The inferred natural weathering relation is distinct from the later laboratory heating of Fe-rich Prachovice paramontroseite. The 2023 specimen is zoned, Al-bearing and nonideal; its refinement favors Fe in a separate tetrahedral site and cannot be substituted for a pure-VO2 endmember or a measurement on the 1950s Colorado specimen. Criticality is not inherited from vanadium, and the curated studies establish no microbial mechanism or industrial recovery.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Inferred oxidation and hydrogen loss during natural weathering

redox transformation

Evans and Mrose interpreted natural montroseite-paramontroseite intergrowths as oxidation of VO(OH) to orthorhombic VO2 with hydrogen loss while retaining a related oxygen framework. Their proposed reaction is 2VO(OH) + 0.5O2 -> 2VO2 + H2O. This is a structural and geological inference from altered natural crystals, not a controlled conversion experiment or measured hydrogen-transport mechanism.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The Bitter Creek mine, Paradox Valley, Colorado, supplied two altered crystals. One contained sharp montroseite plus diffuse paramontroseite and a weaker unresolved diffuse-A phase; the other gave nearly pure paramontroseite diffraction with slight residual montroseite reflections. The 1955 study reused the 1953 montroseite intensity data, so the papers and the two mineral records do not represent independent replications. The proposed below-50-degrees-C atmospheric or groundwater weathering setting has no measured reaction duration, pH, oxygen activity, conversion fraction or matched laboratory control. Diffuse A could be an intermediate or a separated Fe-rich phase; its identity was unresolved.

Context_only Source: weeks1953
Pages 1239-1241 and Tables 2-3 show that bulk powder patterns and chemical analyses include altered and impure material. The Matchless composition was calculated as about 35 percent substituted oxyhydroxide and 65 percent VO2, not measured conversion in a timed assay. Bitter Creek, Matchless and Whitney separates have different impurities and must not be pooled as pure montroseite or paramontroseite.

Heating-associated oxidative breakdown of Fe-bearing natural paramontroseite

redox transformation

Curetti and Pavese observed Fe-bearing natural paramontroseite progressively break down into diffraction-assigned V2O5 and Fe2V4O13 during laboratory heating. Under their stepped powder protocol, new phases appeared in the 350-400 degrees C interval, and Figure 7 retains parent paramontroseite at 400 and 450 degrees C but not in its plotted 500 degrees C assemblage. This supports specimen-specific oxidative phase transformation, not vanadium extraction, product recovery or a universal transition temperature for pure VO2.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The Prachovice, Czech Republic, specimen contains compositionally zoned Fe/Al-bearing paramontroseite on a calcite matrix with associated U-rich material. Crystals selected from U-free mapped areas for powder diffraction still contained about 8 weight percent calcite. The powder was placed in a flat Pt holder, heated at 20 degrees C per minute, and measured at 50-degree intervals up to a stated 550 degrees C after 2-hour holds; each diffraction scan took about 150 minutes. Figure 7 ends at 500 degrees C and the cell-parameter table at 450 degrees C. The gas composition/flow and oxygen fugacity are not specified. Separate crystals heated for 6 hours at 400 degrees C were used for EDS and Raman work; those results are not an identical powder time series. There is no pure-VO2 matched comparison, measured dissolved vanadium, isolated-product mass balance or demonstrated industrial process.

Section 3.2 identifies V2O5 and Fe2V4O13 and infers oxidation of V4+ to V5+ from the assigned phases. Parent diffraction persists through 450 degrees C in Table 3 and Figure 7. Partial Source: curetti2023
Section 3.2 reports final Fe2V4O13 and V2O5 proportions of about 65 and 35 weight percent respectively, but the labeled stacked bars in Figure 7 show V2O5 as the larger final product and retain calcite. The exact product fractions and denominator therefore remain unresolved and are not treated as recovery yields. The proposed balanced reaction uses an approximate Al-free V0.8Fe0.2O2 formula, distinct from both the measured average composition and refined occupancies. Absence of an amorphous intermediate is inferred from retained calcite fraction, not a separately quantified glass assay.
Partial Source: curetti2023
Sections 2.2-2.3 and 3.1, Tables 1-2 and Figure 4 favor Fe in a separate tetrahedral site for this Fe-rich specimen, rather than simple V-site substitution. The average chemical formula V0.84Fe0.19Al0.03O2 differs from refined site occupancies, and small Al is omitted in the proposed model; EDS and refinement do not directly measure every cation valence. Section 3.3 and Figure 9 show laser-induced changes and overlapping Raman peaks; an Fe2V4O13 reference spectrum was unavailable, so individual Raman assignments are not unique and a database match is not an unaltered-paramontroseite fingerprint.
