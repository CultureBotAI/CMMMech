A natural geological feed represented by the eudialyte ore sample from the Ilimaussaq Complex, Greenland, used in Hunter's 2020 doctoral thesis. The sample was jaw-crushed and finely ground for laboratory leaching. Its mineral proportions, bulk elemental grade and particle-size distribution are not established in the reviewed experimental account. This resource is distinct from a formally identified eudialyte mineral species, the eudialyte group, an upgraded concentrate, elemental zirconium or rare earths, and a recovered product. A literature crystal structure in the thesis is not characterization of this particular feed. No criticality designation is inherited from its constituent elements.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated acidification and enhanced ore leaching

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans (NCBITaxon:920) [TaxonMech]

Inoculated iron-containing acidic medium mobilized more rare earths from the ore than the uninoculated mild-acid comparator. The thesis attributes the effect to microbial iron oxidation followed by acidification. Iron-assay trends and declining pH support this interpretation, but they do not isolate a biological advantage at matched pH. Strong nitric or sulfuric acid leached substantially more rare earths than the inoculated medium. These are laboratory aqueous-mobilization observations, not industrial deployment or recovery of a purified rare-earth or zirconium product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Methods Section 7.4.1 identifies a culture obtained from NCIMB as Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans NCIMB 10435; Chapter 4 uses the same species and abbreviates it ATs. The abstract instead says Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans. The organism identifier here maps the methods name only; no independent sequence, strain equivalence or contemporaneous culture-purity test was verified. Section 4.5.2 describes triplicate inoculated flasks with membrane screw caps, 125 mL growth medium and agitation, plus uninoculated medium. The medium's reported final concentrations are 0.1 M FeSO4 and 6.9 mM H2SO4 with ammonium, magnesium and phosphate salts. Section 4.5.3 repeats the setup with 0.50 g finely ground ore, autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 15 minutes, and includes a culture-without-ore reference. It follows leaching for about three weeks. The narrative invokes room temperature, 25 degrees C, but gives no controlled incubation set point, agitation rate or inoculum density. The acid-comparison experiment uses 1.00 g ore per 125 mL and compares inoculated medium, uninoculated medium, 2.0 M HNO3 and 2.0 M H2SO4. Sampling used filtration and ICP-OES; measured solution concentrations are not cumulative extraction fractions or a closed feed-to-product mass balance.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: hunter2020
Sections 4.5.2-4.5.4 and Figures 4.5.2.1, 4.5.3.1 and 4.5.3.4-4.5.4.1 show acidification, an iron-assay shift and enhanced dissolved Si, Zr and rare earths in the inoculated ore treatment. In the ore time course, inoculated solutions approach pH 2.2 while the uninoculated ore comparator stays roughly pH 2.6-2.8. Figure 4.5.4.1 shows greater Y, La, Ce and Nd concentrations with inoculation than without it, but substantially higher concentrations under either strong-acid treatment. The controls have different pH trajectories and therefore do not establish a pH-independent microbial effect.
Partial Source: hunter2020
Exact kinetic and mechanistic quantification is limited by internal source problems. Chapter 4 describes ferrozine measurements at 562 nm, whereas Section 7.4.9 specifies 568 nm. The printed calibration, equations and claim of complete iron oxidation are not independently recalibrated here. Section 4.5.1's FeSO4 stock molarity does not agree with its stated mass and volume. The text's Si:Zr comparison contrasts mass-concentration slopes with atomic stoichiometry; it cannot establish preferential adsorption from that ratio. Adsorption of Si onto iron precipitates remains an explanation, not a demonstrated ore-specific mechanism. No gel was observed at this low solids loading, which does not establish behavior at commercial pulp densities.
Context_only Source: hunter2020
Section 4.5.5 and Figures 4.5.5.1-4.5.5.2 describe a separate chemical solvent-extraction step. A model solution and the actual filtered bioleachate are different experiments. The actual leachate was concentrated fivefold under vacuum, adjusted to 5.0 M NaNO3 and 0.1 M HNO3 at pH 1.40, and contacted with 0.1 M IL0 or the secondary amido-ammonium extractant 2AA in toluene. This procedure contradicts the abstract and conclusions' unqualified no-pH-adjustment wording. Reported solvent-extraction percentages are not ore recovery yields; Section 7.2's printed percentage equations also leave the concentration denominator ambiguous. Low concentrations of minor rare earths limit selectivity comparisons. Neither a purified product yield nor a complete zirconium separation is demonstrated by the retained ore experiment.
Context_only Source: hunter2020_supporting
Archive member and text/workbook screening supplies provenance for the deposited computational supporting material. It does not repair the bioleaching organism discrepancy, supply an ore phase assay, or independently reproduce Chapter 4 curves.
