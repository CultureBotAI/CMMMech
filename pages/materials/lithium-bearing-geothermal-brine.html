Lithium-bearing geothermal brine primary resource

Naturally occurring geothermal saline water containing dissolved lithium, considered as a geological extraction feed. The resource is a variable aqueous mixture, distinct from elemental lithium, a mineral species, manufactured lithium carbonate and waste-derived secondary resources. The curated experiment used one unsterilized brine from an unnamed Cornish geothermal project; its composition and laboratory response do not define all geothermal brines. No criticality designation or external material identifier is inferred from the lithium constituent.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Ureolytic carbonate precipitation during lithium-brine pretreatment

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Sporosarcina pasteurii (NCBITaxon:1474) [TaxonMech]

Prepared S. pasteurii DSM 33 biomass with added urea raised pH and produced carbonate-bearing solids while measured Ca, Mg, Mn and Sr concentrations declined in one natural geothermal brine. Lithium largely remained in the measured solution. This is a laboratory impurity-removal pretreatment observation, not isolated lithium recovery or demonstrated improvement in downstream direct lithium extraction. The authors assigned calcite, vaterite and magnesite in mixed solids by XRD; phase-specific Sr/Mn incorporation and a uniquely cellular precipitation pathway were not established.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Cells grown in tryptone soy broth with 500 mM urea at 28 degrees C and 180 rpm were harvested in mid-log phase and washed with phosphate-buffered saline. The natural brine was not sterilized and was treated under reported non-growth conditions with 500 mM urea, initial OD600 values 0.05-0.4, 100 mL in 250 mL flasks, 28 degrees C, static incubation for 24 h, in triplicate. Figure 1 is the main comparison; supporting figures show higher inocula and a separate no-added-cell control. The reported Bacillus subtilis ATCC-2385 comparator and brine-plus-urea control are retained as source descriptions, without independently resolving that comparator strain accession. No matched-pH chemical or killed-biomass comparison was reported, and unsterilized brine is not an axenic system. ICP-OES measured elemental concentrations, but the main methods do not clearly specify solid/liquid separation for every metal time-point aliquot; supplementary Table S2 omits the lithium wavelength. Only qualitative treatment comparisons are curated. The paper does not demonstrate an integrated industrial process, a lithium carbonate product, or a lithium mass-balance recovery yield.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: rehmanji2025
Sections 4.1-4.2 identify the natural Cornish geothermal feed, DSM 33 preparation and flask conditions. Sections 2.1-2.2 and Figure 1 show culture-associated cation depletion with lithium retained; Figure 2 and Section 4.6 support characterization of mixed carbonate-bearing solids. The claim concerns this brine and prepared biomass under the reported conditions.
Support Source: rehmanji2025_si
Table S1 characterizes the experimental brine, including lithium 75.2 +/- 1.8 mg/L (mean +/- SEM, n = 3); this is not a universal resource specification or an mg/kg observation. Figure S5 (pp. 21-22) compares inoculated brine with brine plus urea and no added cells. Figure S7 (p. 24), rather than the main text's misreferenced Figure S6, contains the authors' XRD assignment to calcite, vaterite and magnesite.
Partial Source: rehmanji2025
Section 2.1 says initial 10 minutes whereas Figure 1 axes and Section 4.2 sampling schedule use hours. Published Mg/Mn removal percentages do not consistently match the plotted Figure 1 series; its B. subtilis Mn control also declines despite the text's broad no-precipitation statement. No percentage or 10-minute rate is accepted here. Lithium retention does not establish extraction into a separate product; the cow-urine experiment used synthetic CaCl2 solution, distinct from the natural-brine assay.
Partial Source: rehmanji2025_si
The inoculated Mn/Sr trajectories in Figure S5 differ from Figure 1 and are not pooled as one replicated series. Table S9 labels retained lithium as recovery; this record treats it as aqueous retention. Table S7 reports negative magnesite saturation indices, unlike positive values stated in Section 2.2; model output is not accepted as independent confirmation of phase formation. Mixed-solid XRD identification remains an author assignment without purified phase yields.

Causal Edges (3):
  • ureabc_urease_complex → hydrolysis of urea to ammonia and carbonate (catalyzes) → urea_hydrolysis_alkalinity : UreABC hydrolyzes urea, releasing ammonium and carbonate ions and raising solution pH.[rehmanji2025]
  • urea_hydrolysis_alkalinity → carbonate supersaturation and nucleation (drives) → precipitated_mineral_phase : Elevated pH and carbonate activity drive precipitation of Precipitated calcite/vaterite carbonate solids (leaving Li in brine).[rehmanji2025]
