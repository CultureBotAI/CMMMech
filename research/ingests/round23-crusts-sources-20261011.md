## Focal assay and accepted claim

Methods pp.1159–1160 and Table 1 describe a 2009 collection at Afanasiy–Nikitin Seamount, 2204 m depth. The crust was hammered, gently rinsed and stored cold with filtered/autoclaved bottom water. Roughly 1 g crust fragments with their unresolved indigenous community were placed in 10 mL sterile seawater in 15-mL screw-capped tubes. The factorial contrast is with/without 0.1% glucose and with/without 15 mM sodium azide; four crust-free formulations are blanks. Tubes were triplicate, dark-incubated for 30 and 50 days at 4±1°C and nominal 28°C. Replicated tubes do not establish independent field populations.

The aqueous phase after 5009 g, 10 min, 4°C centrifugation was analyzed for Co/Ni by adsorptive stripping voltammetry with ammonia buffer and dimethylglyoxime. Total cell counts after sonication are not a live/dead viability measurement. Table 2 reports interval-specific Co depletion in unpoisoned arms; poisoned glucose-amended arms instead show Co release, while poisoned unamended arms also deplete Co. The source's final sorption endpoint is inferred from the difference between days 30 and 50. It does not directly measure accumulated solid Co or an isolated product.

This supports a narrow community-associated redistribution observation. The record uses `process: other` because absorption, mineral/cell adsorption and precipitation are not separately demonstrated. The microbial flag identifies a community-bearing experiment, not proven microbial necessity. Oxidative binding of Co, reductive host-oxide dissolution and enzyme-level causes remain hypotheses. No named causal taxon, Co recovery amount, yield or deployment claim is added.

## Counterevidence and unresolved contradictions

- **Nickel reverses the broad enrichment interpretation.** Table 2 reports 1.89 µg/g dry crust apparent Ni sorption in the 4°C unamended biotic arm, versus 20.96 µg/g in its azide arm. At 28°C the unamended biotic arm releases Ni while the azide arm sorbs Ni. Both glucose-amended arms release Ni. The Results sentence saying Ni sorption occurred only in biotic incubations conflicts with Table 2 and the Discussion. These two source-table values are retained as a counterexample, not as a mass-balanced recovery yield.
- **Azide is an altered chemical control.** It can modify mineral/metal interactions; the companion primary paper explicitly recognizes this reason for adding heat-killed controls. Its extra controls cannot repair the focal experiment retroactively. Microbial and chemical contributions therefore remain inseparable here.
- **Endpoint/sign wording needs raw data.** Results p.1161 calls initial-minus-final solution concentration “metal released,” then defines final sorption using days 30 and 50. The sign and endpoint labels cannot be reconstructed confidently without underlying data. This is not a claim of dimensional inconsistency: the stated mL, mg/L and mg normalization can be reconciled dimensionally. No Co amount is adopted.
- **Temperature wording differs.** Methods gives 28±1°C while abstract/Table 1/Results give 28±2°C. Table 3 gives P = 0.736 for the glucose-amended Co temperature term, despite discussion language about temperature dependence. No universal temperature enhancement is asserted.
- **Other measurement limits remain.** Table 1 names blanks, but complete blank outcomes and the focal Co/Ni-specific correction procedure were not established. The narrative calls 7.99→7.54 an increase in pH; no exact pH series is adopted. SEM morphology/captions do not establish a new metal-bearing mineral phase, and their images were not independently inspected.
