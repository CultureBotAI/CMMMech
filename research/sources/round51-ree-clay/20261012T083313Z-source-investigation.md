## Claim-level scientific assessment

The experiment used ferruginous, poorly sorted natural clay from3.8–4.8m above volcanic breccia, Ambohimirahavavy Alkaline Complex, northwest Madagascar. Separate Aspergillus TD25 and Bacillus TD29 inocula originated from a different country-rock profile at3.4–3.6m. Nonaseptic collection and storage prevent assuming strictly indigenous provenance.

Triplicate flasks contained3g feed below0.5mm in150mL after48h inoculum cultivation,25°C/150rpm for60days. Modified phosphate-free CAB contained140g/L glucose. Comparators were uninoculated CAB and0.5M ammonium sulfate. Feed was deliberately not sterilized to preserve its surface properties. Uninoculated treatments developed acidification after approximately12days; the authors explicitly reject their use as true controls after that point. Medium-only early release was also substantial. All release cannot be attributed exclusively to the inoculated genera.

Main Figures1–3 and Tables4–5 support acidification, ligand production (predominantly gluconate), and solution release. They do not isolate proton attack from organic complexation or background-community contributions. A matched HCl-only experiment is future work. Salt leaching generally performs faster and better; cerium is the exception. Atday60 fungal Lu release70.7% versus salt73.1% refers to total feed Lu. The workbook reproduces this denominator: T7=3.76±0.04, T19=3.89±0.1 and T20=5.32mg/kg. These are mass-based source values, not solution concentrations. Si/Fe co-leaching and slow kinetics are explicit limitations. No isolated-product recovery yield or industrial deployment is established.

The workbook has no supplied analysis date and contains mean/SD text rather than directly importable single quantitative-source values with the complete native contract. Qualified prose with exact locators is used; no assay date, zero replacement, density conversion or schema extension is invented.
