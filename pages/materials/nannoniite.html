Nannoniite mineral IMA: Nnn

IMA-approved monoclinic aluminium hydroxy fluoride mineral (IMA 2024-010; symbol Nnn; status A, Approved; full description published 11 December 2024) with ideal end-member formula Al2(OH)5F (Z = 4; corresponding to Al2O3 64.53 wt%, F 12.02 wt%, H2O 28.51 wt%, -O = F -5.06 wt%, sum 100.00 wt%; simplified formula (Al,Mg)2(OH)5(F,OH); Biagioni, Mugnaioli, Lorenzon, Mauro, Musetti, Sejkora, Belmonte, Demitri, and Dolnicek, 2024, European Journal of Mineralogy 36:1011-1022). Represents the full characterization of the previously unnamed mineral UM1990-28-OHF:Al (Smith and Nickel, 2007) described by Jambor et al. (1990) from the Francon quarry, Montreal, Quebec, Canada (Section 1, p. 1012; Section 4.4.2, p. 1020). Named in honour of Roberto Nannoni (1943-2022), Italian secondary-school teacher and honorary curator of the mineralogical collection of the Museo Provinciale di Storia Naturale of Leghorn (Livorno), for his contribution to the knowledge of Tuscan mineralogy (Section 1, p. 1012). Discovered in two samples from the Cetine di Cotorniano mine (43 deg 13 min N, 11 deg 09 min E), Chiusdino, province of Siena, Tuscany, Italy, specifically from the small exploitation void known as "Stanza Santoni" (after mineral collector Valerio Santoni, 1934-2002) within the Sb ore deposit (active 1878-1948, related to Late Miocene southern Tuscany hydrothermal activity and the Tuscan Magmatic Province) consisting of jasperoids and vuggy silica masses replacing Upper Triassic "Calcare Cavernoso" silicified limestone at the contact with overlying Liguride Nappe argillic formations (Abstract and Section 1, pp. 1011-1012; Section 2, p. 1012). Type material is deposited in the mineralogical collection of the Museo di Storia Naturale, University of Pisa, Via Roma 79, Calci (Pisa), Italy, under catalogue numbers 20071 (holotype) and 20072 (co-type) (Section 1, p. 1012; Figure 1 caption, p. 1013). In type material, nannoniite is directly associated with quartz, baryte, gypsum, and F-bearing alunite (KAl3(SO4)2[(OH)5F]sum=6.00) in vugs of silicified limestone; the broader Stanza Santoni fluoride assemblage includes elpasolite (K2NaAlF6), fluorite (CaF2), gearksutite (CaAlF4(OH)*H2O), hydrokenoralstonite ([]2Al2F6(H2O)), rosenbergite (AlF[F0.5(H2O)0.5]4*H2O), opal, and dacostaite (IMA 2024-015, printed as K(Mg2Al)[Mg(H2O)6](AsO4)2F6*2H2O in Section 4.5, p. 1020, omitting the subscript 2 on [Mg(H2O)6]2 relative to its charge-balanced ideal formula K(Mg2Al)[Mg(H2O)6]2(AsO4)2F6*2H2O), formed by late-stage circulation of (Al,F)-rich hydrothermal fluids enriched in Na, K, Mg, and Ca (Abstract, p. 1011; Section 2, pp. 1012-1013; Section 4.2, p. 1017; Section 4.5, pp. 1020-1021). Occurs as (hemi-)spherical or rosette-like aggregates up to 1 mm in diameter formed by micrometre-sized spindle-like crystals (up to 0.5 mm aggregates in holotype 20071, Figure 1a) or tabular individuals (up to 1 mm aggregates in co-type 20072, Figure 1b) (Abstract, p. 1011; Section 2, p. 1012; Figure 1, p. 1013). Colourless to white, with a white streak and a vitreous to earthy lustre; brittle, with a perfect {001} cleavage; Mohs hardness was not determined owing to small crystal size (estimated close to 2-3 in analogy with gibbsite); density was not measured, with calculated density 2.492 g cm-3 based on the ideal formula and unit-cell parameters (reported rounded as 2.49 g cm-3 in Section 4.4.2, p. 1020); calculated Gladstone-Dale mean refractive index is 1.564 (Section 2, pp. 1012-1013). Under longwave UV radiation (lambda = 350 nm), nannoniite shows yellowish-white fluorescence; under shortwave UV radiation (lambda = 254 nm), the primary paper contains an internal text discrepancy, stating "bluish-yellow" in the Abstract (p. 1011) vs. "bluish-white" in Section 2 (pp. 1012-1013) and Section 4.4.2 (p. 1020, where it is compared to the bluish-white shortwave UV fluorescence of UM1990-28-OHF:Al reported by Jambor et al., 1990). Quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analyses (Cameca SX 100, 15 kV, 5 nA, 20 um beam diameter, PAP matrix correction, National Museum, Prague; average of n = 9 spot analyses; Na, P, Si, Cl, Fe, Cu, Zn, and As below detection limits; Section 3.2 and Table 1, p. 1013) yielded mean SO3 0.49 wt% (range 0.25-0.65, SD 0.14, standard Celestine), Al2O3 63.97 wt% (63.18-64.47, SD 0.46, standard Al2O3), MgO 0.51 wt% (0.46-0.59, SD 0.04, standard Diopside), CaO 0.22 wt% (0.18-0.26, SD 0.03, standard Fluorapatite), K2O 0.07 wt% (0.05-0.09, SD 0.02, standard Sanidine), F 11.72 wt% (11.03-12.29, SD 0.41, standard LiF), stoichiometric H2O(calc) 28.54 wt%, and -O = F -4.94 wt% (rounded from -4.935 wt%), with analytical total 100.58 wt% (exact sum 0.49 + 63.97 + 0.51 + 0.22 + 0.07 + 11.72 + 28.54 - 4.94 = 100.58 wt%). On the basis of (OH + F) = 6 atoms per formula unit, the empirical formula is (Al1.99Mg0.02)sum=2.01(SO4)0.01(OH)5.02F0.98 (Section 4.2, p. 1015), where minor Ca, K, and S (SO4) reflect intimate association with alunite and gypsum, and standardless TEM-EDS (JEOL JEM-F200 SDD, Cliff-Lorimer approximation) gave Al2(OH)4.72F1.28 with minor Si attributed to disseminated amorphous silica/opal (Section 4.2, pp. 1016-1017). Crystallizes in the monoclinic system, space group P21/n (#14, non-standard setting of P21/c chosen for consistency with gibbsite, Saalfeld and Wedde, 1974), Z = 4, with three unit-cell parameter sets reported in the primary study: (1) three-dimensional electron diffraction (3DED; JEOL JEM-F200 TEM at 200 kV, lambda = 0.0251 A, ASI CheeTah detector, 1 deg precession semi-angle, PETS2 refinement on a 520 x 200 x 530 nm crystal; Abstract, p. 1011; Section 3.4, p. 1014; Table 3, p. 1016) a = 8.688(3) A, b = 5.024(2) A, c = 9.734(4) A, beta = 90.77(2) deg, V = 424.9(3) A3 (summarized rounded as a = 8.69, b = 5.02, c = 9.73 A, beta = 90.8 deg, V = 424.9 A3 in Table 8, p. 1019, and Section 4.4.2, p. 1020); (2) Gandolfi-like X-ray powder diffraction (Bruker D8 Venture, Photon III CCD, microfocus CuKalpha, Celref; Section 3.3, p. 1013) a = 8.680(3) A, b = 5.018(4) A, c = 9.711(4) A, beta = 90.26(9) deg, V = 423.0(4) A3 (P21/n); and (3) weak single-crystal X-ray diffraction on a 30 x 25 x 15 um grain (Bruker D8 Venture, MoKalpha, 122 reflections above 20sigma(I); Section 3.3, p. 1013) a = 8.672(7) A, b = 5.009(4) A, c = 9.673(8) A, beta = 90.50(3) deg, V = 420.2(6) A3 (weak reflections simulated a C-centred lattice C2/c, and subsequent synchrotron XRD at Elettra Sincrotrone failed due to sample decay). The crystal structure was solved ab initio from 121 3DED frames by direct methods (SIR2019) and refined dynamically in JANA2020 across 104 zones to R(obs) = 0.1524 for 2141 unique reflections with I > 3sigma(I) (R(all) = 0.1659, wR(F2) = 0.3171, Goof = 7.9515 for 2672 unique reflections out of 7540 measured, 42 parameters; Section 3.4, pp. 1014-1015, and Table 3, p. 1016). Nannoniite is homeotypic with gibbsite (Al(OH)3): its dioctahedral sheet structure consists of ...ABBAABBA... stacked double layers of (OH/F) anions with two independent octahedrally coordinated Al sites - Al(1) (Al(1)-phi = 1.838(16)-2.063(14) A, mean <Al(1)-phi> = 1.911 A, BVS = 2.95 vu) and Al(2) (Al(2)-phi = 1.773(14)-1.940(15) A, mean <Al(2)-phi> = 1.883 A, BVS = 3.11 vu) - occupying two-thirds of the octahedral interstices, with fluorine ordered at the F(2) site (Al(1)-F(2) = 2.063(14) A, Al(2)-F(2) = 1.858(12) A) lining type 1 pseudo-hexagonal cavities together with in-plane O(1)-H(1) and O(6)-H(6) groups, whereas type 2 cavities are lined by six out-of-plane O(3)-H(3), O(4)-H(4), and O(5)-H(5) hydroxyl groups pointing alternately up and down in a UDUDUD sequence (vs. UUDD in gibbsite; Section 4.3-4.4.1, pp. 1017-1019, and Tables 4-8, pp. 1016-1019). Micro-Raman spectroscopy (HORIBA Jobin Yvon XploRA Plus, 532 nm laser, 100-4000 cm-1; Section 3.1, p. 1013; Section 4.1 and Figure 2, p. 1015) shows lattice and Al-O-Al stretching, bending, and deformation bands at 147, 187, 251 (strong), 303, 371, 446, 534 (shoulder), and 561 (strong) cm-1, an OH translation band at 697 cm-1, delta(OH) hydroxyl deformation bands at 900, 976, and 1021 cm-1, and O-H stretching bands at 3350, 3431 (shoulder), 3517, 3579, and 3615 (shoulder) cm-1. All 12 observed powder X-ray diffraction lines in Table 2 [dobs in A (Iobs; dcalc in A, Icalc, hkl); p. 1014] are 4.86 (vs; 4.867, 100, 0 0 2), 4.35 (s; 4.349, 35, 1 1 0; 4.344, 19, 2 0 0), 3.241 (m; 3.262, 4, -2 0 2; 3.254, 10, -1 1 2), 2.427 (ms; 2.436, 15, -3 1 1; 2.433, 3, 0 0 4; 2.432, 14, 0 2 1; 2.423, 14, 3 1 1), 2.223 (mw; 2.241, 7, -3 1 2; 2.232, 7, 0 2 2), 2.113 (vw; 2.130, 1, -1 1 4; 2.117, 1, 1 1 4; 2.111, 1, 2 0 4), 1.981 (m; 1.996, 13, -3 1 3; 1.990, 2, -2 2 2; 1.986, 12, 0 2 3), 1.742 (mw; 1.757, 13, -3 1 4; 1.748, 14, 0 2 4), 1.640 (w; 1.631, 2, -4 0 4; 1.627, 3, -2 2 4), 1.616 (w; 1.616, 3, 2 2 4), 1.553 (vw; 1.562, 1, -5 1 2; 1.559, 1, -1 3 2; 1.557, 1, 1 3 2; 1.552, 1, 4 2 2; 1.550, 1, 5 1 2), and 1.447 (m; 1.450, 13, 3 3 0; 1.448, 7, 6 0 0). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
