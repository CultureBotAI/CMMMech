Argentotennantite-(Fe) mineral IMA: Atnt-Fe

IMA-approved silver-copper-iron arsenic sulfosalt mineral of the arsenofreibergite series within the tetrahedrite group (IMA 2023-126; symbol Atnt-Fe; status A, Approved 2024; full description published June 2026) with ideal end-member formula Ag6(Cu4Fe2)As4S13 (reported on p. 329 as requiring Ag 37.42 wt%, S 24.10 wt%, As 17.33 wt%, Cu 14.70 wt%, and Fe 6.46 wt% for a printed total of 100.00 wt%, where the five rounded values sum to 100.01 wt%; Sejkora et al., 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:325-336). Represents the Fe2+- dominant C-site member of the arsenofreibergite series (the Fe2+ analogue of argentotennantite-(Zn), the As analogue of argentotetrahedrite-(Fe), and the S(2)-occupied counterpart of kenoargentotennantite-(Fe)). Discovered in hydrothermal Ag-sulfosalt-bearing quartz-siderite-baryte vein gangue from the central part of the San Genaro mine (13 deg 11 min 37 sec S, 75 deg 08 min 57 sec W; 4750-5000 m a.s.l.), Castrovirreyna Province, Huancavelica Department, Peru (hosted in late Miocene 11-12 Ma andesite to rhyolite rocks; also previously documented in compositional data from the Kremnica Au-Ag epithermal deposit, Slovakia, and Grube Clara, Germany), associated with argentotetrahedrite-(Zn), Ag-rich tennantite-(Fe), pyrargyrite, galena, acanthite, quartz, siderite, and baryte; the holotype polished section is deposited in the Department of Mineralogy and Petrology, National Museum in Prague, Czech Republic (catalogue number P1P 52/2023), and the single-crystal X-ray diffraction grain is kept in the Museo di Storia Naturale of the Universita di Pisa, Calci (PI), Italy (catalogue number 20070). Occurs as anhedral grains up to 100 um in size and more commonly as micrometre-sized late-stage rims and cavity/fissure replacements around argentotetrahedrite-(Zn) crystals (Figures 2-4), exhibiting a dark grey colour, black streak, metallic lustre, opaque diaphaneity, estimated Mohs hardness ~4, brittle tenacity, conchoidal fracture, indistinct cleavage, and calculated density Dcalc = 4.922 g/cm3 from the empirical formula and single-crystal unit cell. In reflected light it is isotropic and pale grey with a brownish shade and no internal reflections; Commission on Ore Mineralogy (COM) reflectance values measured in air (Table 1) are 29.9% (470 nm), 30.1% (546 nm), 29.8% (589 nm), and 28.9% (650 nm). Wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analysis of the holotype SCXRD grain (n = 5 spot analyses, Cameca SX100, 25 kV, 20 nA, 0.7 um beam; Table 2) yielded Cu 25.34(52) wt% (range 24.98-26.25), S 24.28(27) wt% (24.07-24.75), Ag 21.38(45) wt% (20.65-21.78), Sb 11.90(153) wt% (9.31-13.20), As 10.12(111) wt% (9.24-11.99), Fe 6.20(8) wt% (6.08-6.29), and Zn 0.27(3) wt% (0.23-0.29), total 99.49(16) wt% (range 99.22-99.65), corresponding on the basis of SigmaMe = 16 apfu to the empirical formula M(2)(Ag3.36Cu2.70)Sigma6.06M(1)(Cu4.05Fe1.88Zn0.07)Sigma6.00X(3)(As2.29Sb1.66)Sigma3.95S12.82 (with partial Sb-for-As substitution stabilizing the structure against steric strain). Crystallizes in the cubic system, space group I-43m (#217), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 10.4365(5) A, V = 1136.75(17) A3, and Z = 2 (refined with racemic twinning to R1 = 0.0384 for 246 reflections with F > 4sigma(F) and R1(all) = 0.0476, wR(F2) = 0.0984, GoF = 1.162, Flack parameter -0.04(13) for 284 unique reflections from 2145 measured at 293(2) K on a 0.035 x 0.030 x 0.025 mm grain using MoKalpha radiation, Table 3). In the tetrahedrite-type crystal structure (Tables 4-6 and 8), triangular planar M(2) (refined s.o. Ag0.53(4)Cu0.47(4), MAN = 38.54 e-, Ueq = 0.076(2) A2) has M(2)-S(2) = 2.242(6) A and M(2)-S(1) = 2.392(5) A x 2 (<M(2)-S> = 2.342 A, BVS = 1.36 v.u.), tetrahedral M(1) (fixed s.o. Cu0.67Fe0.33) has M(1)-S(1) = 2.328(3) A x 4 in Tables 5 and 8 (referred to as "2.329 A" in Section 4.2 on p. 332; BVS = 1.60 v.u.), trigonal pyramidal X(3) (refined s.o. As0.53(3)Sb0.47(3)) has X(3)-S(1) = 2.340(5) A x 3 (BVS = 3.18 v.u.), and S(2) at (0,0,0) is fully occupied (Ueq = 0.123(14) A2, S(2)M(2)6 octahedron volume 15.0 A3). The five strongest calculated powder X-ray diffraction lines (dcalc in A (Icalc; hkl), Table 7; note that Section 3.2 on p. 329 cites PowderCell 2.3 whereas the Table 7 caption on p. 331 cites PowderCell 2.4) are 3.013 (100; 2 2 2), 1.845 (39; 4 4 0), 1.573 (21; 6 2 2), 2.609 (20; 4 0 0), and 7.380 (9; 1 1 0). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
