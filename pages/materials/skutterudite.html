Skutterudite mineral

Skutterudite is the cobalt arsenide mineral species listed as CoAs3 with grandfathered status G and original-description year 1845 in the September 2026 IMA compilation. The year is not an approval identifier. The Handbook describes cubic Im-3 symmetry and natural nickel and iron substitution. This species is distinct from nickelskutterudite, cobaltite, elemental cobalt and engineered skutterudite-type materials. Accepted microbial evidence concerns a phase-characterized, mixed Bou Azzer concentrate dominated by skutterudite; it does not establish a pure-endmember extraction rate or transfer constituent-element criticality to this mineral.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Acidophile-associated leaching of mixed skutterudite-bearing concentrate

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans (NCBITaxon:930) [TaxonMech], Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans (NCBITaxon:28034) [TaxonMech]

A living mixed acidophile culture was used to solubilize cobalt from a skutterudite-dominated natural concentrate in an acidic laboratory reactor. The reported dissolved fraction is relative to bulk concentrate cobalt, not mineral-specific dissolution or recovered cobalt product. A separate pyrite-conditioned flask produced secondary scorodite during processing. These observations support a microbial processing context; absent matched abiotic controls and phase-specific balances, they do not establish microbial necessity, enhancement over abiotic leaching or an exclusive direct-attack mechanism.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Bou Azzer, Morocco concentrate was blended from several ore bodies, supplied in late 2017 and milled through a less-than-100-micrometre mesh. Section 2.5 experiment 4 used a 2-L reactor with 1-L working volume, 1% w/v concentrate, 0.5% w/v zero-valent sulfur, 5 mM ferrous sulfate and acidophile basal salts/trace elements. Initial pH 1.8 was allowed to fall to 1.5 and then controlled with 1 M NaOH; operation was at 35 degrees C, 180 rpm and approximately 1 L/min sterile air. Inoculum combined the original iron/sulfur-oxidizing consortium with leachate from a preceding 30-degree-C flask screen. Inoculum volume and cell density are unspecified, and the concentrate was not sterilized. The abstract says 13 initial species whereas section 2.6 lists 12 named members; this discrepancy is not silently resolved. The two organisms below were significant culturable endpoint members, not the complete initial community or individually demonstrated causal agents.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: johnson2020
Sections 2.2-2.3 and 3.1, Table 1 and supplementary Figures S1-S2 identify skutterudite as the most abundant concentrate phase by XRD and SEM-EDX, with loellingite, minor silicates, trace cobaltite, bismuth arsenide, calcite and mixed-metal arsenate. Sparse nickelskutterudite grains were also identified. Table 1 reports 91.9 g/kg cobalt in the whole concentrate. These phase and elemental measurements support skutterudite-bearing mixed-feed identity, not an absolutely pure mineral or an initial refined phase mass balance.
Support Source: johnson2020
Section 3.3, printed pages 4-5 and Figure 3, reports 49% of the concentrate cobalt solubilized after 16 days in experiment 4. This is a bulk-feed solution fraction; no isolated cobalt product or phase-specific cobalt balance is presented. Section 3.5 and section 2.6 describe culture-based enumeration with colony 16S identification: Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans and Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans were significant viable members after 16 days. This differs from the genus-level sequencing of the separate Iron Mask ore and does not identify every organism or a strain-specific contribution in the Bou Azzer system.
Partial Source: johnson2020
Section 2.5 describes one Bou Azzer bioreactor trial, while Figure 3's caption refers ambiguously to replicate bioreactors and shows one series without replicate uncertainty. Biological replication is therefore unresolved; section 2.8's three ICP measurements per sample are analytical repeats. No matched uninoculated, killed-cell or abiotic leach control is described. The nonsterile concentrate and transferred screening leachate also limit attribution to the originally named inoculum. Ferric-mediated oxidation, sulfur-derived acidity and individual microbial roles remain proposed rather than separately isolated mechanisms in this assay.
Partial Source: johnson2020_supplement
Supplementary Figure S1 retains skutterudite peaks in leached Bou Azzer material; S2 and S5 show mixed residual arsenides and new iron-arsenate precipitates. No complete skutterudite disappearance or mineral-specific conversion fraction is demonstrated. The supplementary XRD labels use Bou Azzer experiment 1/2, whereas the main methods number its reactor/flask experiments 4/5; this numbering mismatch limits exact label-level crosswalks and is not treated as proof of an additional independent experiment.
Support Source: johnson2020
Section 2.5 experiment 5 pre-established an active culture with 5% w/v sterile pyrite and 5 mM ferrous sulfate for five days before adding 1% w/v nonsterile concentrate. Sections 3.3-3.4 and Figures 4-7 identify scorodite in the final flask residue using XRD, FTIR and SEM-EDX; it was not identified in the original concentrate or non-pyrite reactor residue by those analyses. The original trace Co-Ni-Ca-rich arsenate was compositionally distinguished from the new iron arsenate, also illustrated in supplementary Figure S5. This is concurrent secondary precipitation during mixed-feed processing, not a separate replication or a purified product yield.
Partial Source: johnson2020
The pyrite flask and non-pyrite reactor differ in geometry, conditioning and operating conditions, so they do not isolate a pyrite treatment effect. The specific preconditioning recipe does not separately state a zero-valent-sulfur dose. Soluble arsenic and iron decline during the flask experiment, and iron can also derive from added pyrite; the authors therefore cannot calculate total cumulative leaching accurately. Discussion and Figures 8-9 infer arsenic oxidation states from redox chemistry and propose pyrite's catalytic, nucleation and iron-supply roles. Leachate As(III)/As(V) was not directly measured; the separate arsenite/arsenate titration is not a matched leach control. Scorodite stability, detoxification, isolated-metal recovery and industrial deployment were not tested.
