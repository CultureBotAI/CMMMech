Cadmium telluride photovoltaic module scrap

Processing-generated fragments of cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film photovoltaic modules constitute a composite feed for material recycling. The accepted experiment generated milled fragments from manufacturer-sourced modules; their service history and status before laboratory preparation were not reported. This record therefore does not establish postconsumer or industrial manufacturing-scrap provenance for those samples. The feed is distinct from crystalline-silicon and CIGS modules, a pure CdTe compound, and the cadmium or tellurium elements. Constituent criticality designations are not inherited.

End-of-life CIGS photovoltaic modules

Discarded copper-indium-gallium-selenide (CIGS) thin-film photovoltaic modules and their separated coated-glass fractions are a composite secondary resource. The curated study explicitly describes end-of-life Q-Smart panels supplied by Polyeco S.A. This scope excludes crystalline-silicon and CdTe modules, sputtering targets, production-chamber scrap, and factory-supplied cells without documented end-of-life provenance. The resource is distinct from its constituent elements and from a pure CIGS compound or mineral species. Constituent criticality designations are not inherited.

Spent vanadium-flow-battery electrolyte

Sulfuric-acid electrolyte containing dissolved vanadium in multiple oxidation states after use in all-vanadium redox flow batteries. This use-derived liquid resource is distinct from elemental vanadium, fresh electrolyte products, spent vanadium catalysts, and solid battery scrap. The two studies below concern different feeds: company-supplied failed positive/negative electrolytes and electrolyte deliberately cycled to capacity loss in a laboratory cell. Their outcomes are not interchangeable. No constituent criticality designation or microbial mechanism is inherited.
