Metatorbernite mineral

Metatorbernite is the copper uranyl phosphate mineral Cu(UO2)2(PO4)2(H2O)4·4H2O in the September 2026 IMA list, with grandfathered status G. The year 1916 is a historical description year, not a numbered IMA approval. Its ideal eight-water composition is distinct from twelve-water torbernite, arsenate metazeunerite, calcium meta-autunite, uranyl ions and elemental uranium or copper. Locock and Burns grew synthetic metatorbernite in copper-bearing silica gel at room temperature over three weeks and refined an epoxy-coated, merohedrally twinned crystal in P4/n. Its 88.3(6)% fitted copper-site occupancy and constrained hydrogen model are specimen-specific; proposed oxonium charge compensation was not directly established. The 2023 natural Cornwall crystal instead gave the preferred P4/nmm model with a smaller c repeat. These are source-specific structural models, not grounds to replace all specimens with one symmetry or transfer occupancy between them. Thermal behavior below is abiotic laboratory evidence; neither criticality nor microbial extraction, isolated-product recovery or industrial deployment is inferred.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Specimen-dependent abiotic dehydration and thermal structural change

other

Heating changes the interlayer geometry and diffraction of natural metatorbernite and its synthetic analogue. The natural powder experiment reports stepwise dehydration, whereas a separate sealed-capillary synthetic experiment becomes poorly crystalline or X-ray amorphous at its high-temperature endpoint and recrystallizes on cooling. These observations have different specimen preparation and environmental conditions and do not establish a universal dehydration threshold or uranium recovery process.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Nazarchuk et al. used natural sample 1 from Old Gunnislake Mine, Cornwall, UK; semiquantitative EDS gave Cu:U:P = 0.89:2.11:1.99 with no detected As admixture, not a quantitative water assay. Single-crystal measurements covered 100-300 K. Ground powder was prepared from heptane suspension on Pt; the metatorbernite plot is the air-heating arm. Methods specify 293-673 K, 10 K steps, 2 K/min and approximately 30 min collection per step; Figure 4 caption starts at 313 K while the plot also labels 303 K. Several runs are stated without a numerical replicate count. Separately, Kulaszewska et al. precipitated the synthetic phosphate end member from uranyl nitrate, copper chloride and phosphoric acid, settled it for 48 h, vacuum-filtered and washed it, then dried it at 40 degrees C to constant weight. High-resolution synchrotron diffraction used a 0.5-mm borosilicate capillary secured with Kapton tape, described as sealed in Discussion, initially at 293 K, cooled to 105 K and subsequently heated. Methods give nominal 50 K steps and 30 min collections; the plotted temperatures are not uniformly 50 K apart. The mixed phosphate-arsenate member and natural South Terras spoil samples are separate arms.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: nazarchuk2023
Sections 3.2 and 4 and Figure 4 report diffraction changes attributed to dehydration at approximately 353, 373 and 483 K for natural sample 1 in air, with strongest expansion normal to its autunite-type layers. Table 1 and Section 2.3 support the P4/nmm refinement of this natural crystal; the authors explicitly tested alternative models and did not obtain an equivalent satisfactory P4/n refinement. The geometric thermal-expansion fits and powder patterns are complementary measurements, not independent metal-recovery trials.
Partial Source: nazarchuk2023
The extra transitions in mixtures of pristine and partly dehydrated metatorbernite are given as 313 and 463 K in Section 3.2 but 303 and 473 K in Section 4; neither pair is adopted as a corrected threshold. The article does not provide a matched water mass balance for these diffraction arms, and instability can begin during handling or irradiation. Its fitted bond-length changes and proposed interlayer explanation do not identify every dehydration product. The cited crystallographic deposits remain unacquired, so no independent re-refinement is claimed.
Support Source: kulaszewska2019
Section 3 and Figure 4 show the synthetic phosphate end member retaining tetragonal indexing on cooling, expanding anisotropically on heating, and developing a high-temperature amorphous signature followed by an unidentified crystalline product on cooling. The source distinguishes this sealed-capillary behavior from earlier natural specimens heated in open systems. Its room-temperature synthetic solid-solution series supports phosphate-arsenate substitution as another experimental variable; it does not prove all natural samples are pure end members.
Support Source: kulaszewska2019_si
Figure S5 displays distinct synthetic metatorbernite traces through 423 K, a largely diffuse 473 K trace, and a changed 293 K trace after cooling; S6 shows departure from the preceding a/c trend above approximately 373 K. These figures support the qualitative sequence, without assigning a sharp transition temperature from coarse sampling.
Partial Source: kulaszewska2019
The abstract places amorphization at 473 K, Results says greater than 473 K, a later discussion says greater than 423 K, and Conclusion says above 373 K. Those wordings are retained as inconsistent bounds, not combined into one precise threshold; S5 distinguishes the plotted 423 and 473 K states. The study does not directly establish water stoichiometry at every temperature, and the high-temperature and cooled products are not completely identified. Its natural spoil compositions were inferred from a synthetic lattice-parameter calibration after 80-degree-C drying; they are not direct assays of untreated in-situ hydration or mineral-specific uranium retention.
