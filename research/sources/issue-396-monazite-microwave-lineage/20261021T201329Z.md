## La-specific preparation supported by the originals

Main Figure 4B (PDF page 4) and its caption distinguish the La–Nd products made from rhabdophane in 1 M H3PO4 at 210 °C for 1 h from Sm at 230 °C and Eu/Gd at 260 °C. SI Figure S2 (page 3) supplies precursor patterns; Figure S7 (page 7) supplies the separate LaCl3/LaF3 product patterns. The main methods on pages 7–8 specify the CEM Discover 2.0 microwave instrument with infrared temperature sensing and stirring, a 35 mL Pyrex tube, 8 mL of 1 M H3PO4 for the general precursor/direct-reagent method, and filtration followed by water/acetone washing. La2O3 is listed at 99.9%, LaCl3 at 99.9% and LaF3 at 99.0%. The La rhabdophane preparation belongs to the 100 °C, 30 min group. The chloride/fluoride method states 230 °C for 1 h, rather than the precursor-conversion temperature in Figure 4B.

These are phase-identification and preparation claims. The article does not provide a La-specific starting mass or molar amount, a La run identifier linking the figure to a batch ledger, an independent replicate count, quantitative La product purity, isolated-product mass yield, or a feed/liquor/wash/product elemental balance. The measured Nd loading of 100 mg cannot fill that omission. PXRD agreement and the phrase “phase-pure” do not quantify all amorphous material or trace impurities. The conventional-heating and detailed pH/time/durability comparisons are Nd experiments; the current evidence does not establish a matched La acid-free, pH-matched or conventional-heating comparison.

The standard powder method is Cu Kα at 30 kV and 10 mA over 5–65° 2θ (main page 8). More extensive Rietveld scans and the specific Nd pH-pattern settings are described separately on page 9. Instrument specifications do not by themselves supply detection limits or raw refinement files. No organism, biomass, medium or biological control applies to these abiotic La preparations.

## Separate structural, time and durability evidence

SI Table S3 (page 10) is a three-column **single-crystal** summary for NdPO4, Ca0.5Th0.5PO4 and Sr0.5Th0.5PO4. The article describes high-temperature flux growth and room-temperature diffraction, not La microwave single-crystal products. The listed P21/n, Z = 4 cells have volumes 292.235(8), 288.854(8) and 303.428(16) Å³. Central-value monoclinic volume checks agree. The three CCDC identifiers are advertised together; without their original files this report does not assign individual IDs or transfer their refinement statistics to La or the Nd pH mixtures.

Tables S5–S11 and Figures S21–S26 concern Nd powder models. Table S5 (pages 16–17) lists 41 sites: 28 O, six Nd and seven P. Multiplicity counts give Nd24P24O112; P6/P7 have multiplicity 2 and the other listed sites multiplicity 4. No H site is present. This is consistent with the ideal oxygen count for an approximately two-thirds hydrate but does not locate H or measure water content independently.

Tables S6–S10 (pages 17–19) repeat the same 18 coordinate tokens for the six Nd/P/O sites across the 1, 2, 5 and 24 h microwave models and the 1,200 °C, 0.5 h comparator. Each arm also shares one printed Biso value across all six sites: −0.226(16), 0.039(13), 0.117(11), −0.51(2) and −0.757(17), respectively. The negative parameters are source model values, not detection-limit codes or admissible replacements with zero. Their origin cannot be resolved without native refinement settings and uncertainties. Table S11's six unit cells were checked independently by a·b·c·sinβ; no re-refinement is claimed. Its 24 h GoF of 3.24 differs from Figure S25's caption value 3.25. These are retained as source-specific values.

Main Figures 6–8 supply Nd microscopy and in-situ context. The text distinguishes evidence against *complete* dissolution/recrystallization from its acknowledgement that solution-mediated or internal rearrangement alternatives are not ruled out. The abstract's 185–260 °C range, discussion of an approximately 165 °C onset, main Figure 8 caption's 220 °C endpoint and the methods' 210 °C protocol must not be collapsed into one La threshold. The time-method paragraph lists 1, 2 and 5 h, while the original figures and SI also include 24 h; the explicit 24 h arm is preserved without inventing its full run sheet.

Two internal checks concern separate Nd work and do not repair source values. Main page 8 labels one flux input Nd(OH)3, 0.2 mmol, 33.6 mg, while the separate Cs-flux recipe gives 1 mmol, 195 mg. These imply 168 and 195 mg/mmol; using the second basis for 0.2 mmol would give 39 mg. Hydration, purity and batch records are unavailable, so no amount is substituted. Main page 7 states approximately 0.008 wt% Nd loss computed at the detection limit, while pages 6 and 9 give Nd below 1.5 µg/L, approximately 10 mL leachant and 0.2–0.3 g powder. Under a conditional 10 mL/feed-powder calculation, 1.5 µg/L gives 0.015 µg and 0.000005–0.0000075 wt%, rather than 0.008 wt%. The exact calculation/run basis is missing; neither expression is adopted as a corrected measured result.

The durability experiment uses Nd powders, triplicate tests, a reference material and blanks, 90 ± 2 °C for seven days, ICP-MS, and BET areas measured after testing (main pages 6–9). Nd is censored below its detection limit; measured P may include residual reagents. No acid strip was performed, leaving wall losses incompletely balanced. The reported normalized P releases and inferred stoichiometric Nd loss are not La durability results, complete mass closure or actual transuranic-waste performance. SI Figures S27/S28 report Ca/Th and Sr/Th spot EDS atomic percentages; those data likewise do not quantify La product recovery.
