Glushinskite mineral

Glushinskite is the magnesium oxalate dihydrate mineral species, Mg(C2O4)(H2O)2, listed as redefined by the IMA in a 1987 special procedure in its September 2026 list. That date is not an approval-number identifier. It is distinct from elemental magnesium, magnesium oxalate without a specified hydration state, and the phosphate mineral feeds in the experiment. This record reuses the Suyamud 2020 experiment represented under magnesium and dittmarite; these are separate material identities, not independent experiments. No constituent-element criticality is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Glushinskite formation during fungal phosphate-mineral transformation

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Aspergillus niger ATCC 1015 (NCBITaxon:380704)

Glushinskite was identified among crystals collected after growth of Aspergillus niger ATCC 1015 on agar containing natural struvite or a synthetic feed identified as dittmarite. The organism grew during this laboratory assay; this is microbial mineral formation rather than an elemental-magnesium recovery process.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Malt extract agar contained 1% w/v natural struvite from a Veolia wastewater-treatment site in Exeter, UK, or synthetic magnesium ammonium phosphate identified by XRD as dittmarite despite the paper's synthetic-struvite terminology. A 5-mm plug from an actively growing colony inoculated each plate over cellophane. Crystals were collected after seven days at 25 degrees C in darkness, extracted from agar with water at 80 degrees C, washed at least three times and dried in a vacuum desiccator before SEM, EDXA and XRD. The selected solid-medium experiment is separate from the paper's liquid-culture assays.

Table 2 documents acidification in both mineral-fed and mineral-free fungal cultures.
Partial Source: suyamud_2020
Feed diffraction controls and mineral-free fungal growth do not provide a chemistry-matched abiotic comparison that isolates oxalate production from acidification or proves microbial necessity under all conditions. The Results also describe amorphous debris. The phase assignment is the authors' published identification, not a quantified pure-phase fraction or an isolated recovery yield. Neither magnesium-metal production nor industrial deployment is demonstrated.
