## Original 2006 experiments and table audit

The published material is a synthetic preparation represented as Na(UO2)(SiO3OH)·1.5H2O. The IMA row instead gives Na(UO2)(SiO3OH)(H2O), status Rn, special-procedure entry 2007 s.p.; the latter is a renaming entry, not an ordinary approval accession. Potassium boltwoodite, this sodium species, a synthetic preparation and elemental uranium remain distinct identities. The IMA row also cites the 1975 description and Crystal Growth and Design 22 (2022), 1202.

Ilton's methods on pp4837–4838 describe uranyl acetate/metasilicate synthesis at pH 10.5, heating, then a seven-day 150 °C hydrothermal treatment. Bulk acid analyses gave U/Si 1.011 ± 0.014 and U/Na 1.039 ± 0.020; TEM gave Si/U 0.95 ± 0.047. These approximately stoichiometric ratios and microscopy/spectroscopy support the characterized preparation, but do not establish exact bulk hydration. The powder XRD used for identification is explicitly not shown; minor impurity phases were not excluded.

Dissolution used 0.25 g per 100 mL in 250 mL HDPE vessels at 23 ± 2 °C, atmospheric CO2, zero to 50 mM added bicarbonate, with sodium maintained near 50 mM by nitrate adjustment. Seven-day, once-sampled duplicate experiments are separate from kinetic and higher-loading arms. Samples passed a 0.2 µm Teflon filter; the first 1 mL was discarded. ICP-AES detection limits were 0.4 µM U and 1.8 µM Si. Pretreatment used 50 mM bicarbonate for 24 hours. These are laboratory filtered-solution observations, with no isolated product recovery measurement.

All original pages were visually inspected. A manual audit rekeyed 153 measurement/model components from Table 1 (66 pH/Si/U values), Table 3 (80 values and errors) and Table 4 (seven reported species fractions), with condition keys retained separately. Table 2 constants and the complete figures were inspected but not independently refitted. The raw keyed values and calculations remain outside Git in `ilton-tables-audit.json`.

Table 1, p4840/PDF5, confirms every numerical endpoint used by the current owner. At zero, 1.2 and 50 mM added bicarbonate, seven-day U is 3.4, 28.7 and 774.2 µM and Si is 40.1, 11.9 and 648.1 µM. The low-range decreases remain explicit: U falls to 1.4 µM at 0.1 mM; Si falls to 8.0 µM at 0.6 mM. At 1.2 mM, pretreated versus untreated kinetic U is 8.3 versus 32.7 µM, with Si 17.2 versus 17.4 µM. The ratio 8.3/32.7 ≈ 0.254 is only a comparison of these matched endpoints, not a recovery yield or isolated pretreatment efficiency. Figures 4–8 and 10 constrain claims about monotonicity, apparent congruence, contamination and secondary precipitation.

Table 3, p4847/PDF12, contains selected steady-state summaries, not a copy of Table 1 endpoints. It combines four seven-day, four kinetic and two pretreated determinations. Its 50 mM row uses mean Si across 0.5/0.75/1 g loading arms and the final U value from the 1 g arm; the reported constants are minima. Recomputing the mean and sample standard deviation from its rounded constants gives Davies 5.846 ± 0.2598 and Pitzer 5.862 ± 0.2358. This reproduces the scale of the published summaries, but does not rerun activity/speciation fits or correct the article. Table 4 fractions at 1.2/6/50 mM sum to 0.994/0.999/0.9998 because only dominant listed species are being summed; dashes are retained as unreported cells rather than zero measurements.

Several printed source inconsistencies are retained without inventing corrections: the Davies uncertainty is printed `0.0.26`; the pretreated Table 3 U heading is `U(log)`; its 6 mM pH error is printed `4.7`; Figure 12's caption prints sodium `0.050 + 1.1 mM`, while the methods and Tables 1/3 use 50 ± 1.1 mM. These are source-level transcription cautions. They do not invalidate the separately verified Table 1 values and do not create new repository defects.

The PNNL abstract reports low-bicarbonate logK values 5.39–5.92, average 5.63, and 4.06 at 50 mM, with concern about the speciation model. Its published/revised portal dates do not establish a manuscript revision sequence. The original paper's selected steady-state calculations and the PNNL summary remain a provenance discrepancy. The related Liu paper cites 5.85 and discusses model uncertainty, but cannot identify which source version produced the PNNL values. No universal solubility constant is proposed for the natural mineral or all experimental arms.
