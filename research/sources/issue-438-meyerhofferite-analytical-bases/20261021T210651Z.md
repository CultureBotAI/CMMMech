## Specimen and analytical bases

The study examines natural, centimetric, white platy-prismatic material from Bigadiç, Turkey, in the University of Padua collection, **MM5990** (PDF pp. 2–3). It is not a synthetic feed, an elemental-boron owner or the unspecified pressure-study specimen. This investigation neither reclassifies IMA identity nor treats the geological discussion as a microbial or controlled recovery experiment.

Original pages 3–5 distinguish the following methods:

* Boron: 80–100 mg dissolved in 5 ml water and 3 ml 1 M HCl, with 1–2 h ultrasound. The solution is brought to about 100 ml; initial pH 5.5–6.5 is adjusted before adding 5–6 g mannitol. Titration uses 0.1 M NaOH to pH 8.3–8.7. The text reports **46.3(2) wt% B₂O₃**. Raw endpoint volumes, individual blank corrections and replicate-level results are not supplied in the acquired article.
* Calcium: 200–300 mg, 40 ml 0.5 N HCl, alcohol treatment, ammonium hydroxide and sodium tungstate precipitation. The washed CaWO₄ precipitate is dried at 105 °C to constant weight; the text reports **25.2(2) wt% CaO**. Actual before/after weighing records were not acquired. This is an analytical gravimetric precipitate, not an isolated boron-recovery product.
* Water estimate: a 1,000 mg sample in a lidded quartz crucible is gradually heated from ambient temperature to 800 °C. **28.4(2) wt%** is explicitly based on assuming the entire mass loss is H₂O. No evolved-gas identification, mass-versus-temperature curve or direct water-specific assay is supplied for this determination.
* Separate CHN: 200–300 mg at 950 °C for 90 s in excess oxygen, followed by an 850 °C afterburner; gases are analysed by infrared absorption for C/H and thermal conductivity for N. C and N are each below 0.02 wt%; H is **3.08(2) wt%**. This different aliquot/method is not silently substituted for the furnace estimate.
* REE/Th/U: 50 mg diluted to 50 ml, axial ICP-AES, blank and matrix-matched Ca/B calibration solutions spanning 0.001–0.050 mg/l. All 18 Table 1 rows are `<LOD`, with separate wavelength, LOD and LOQ columns. The literal footnote defines LOD as 3·S/N and LOQ as 10·S/N; that wording is retained without inventing a different noise convention.
* Other minor elements: separate acid-dissolution and carbonate-fusion protocols, 10–20 mg to 25 ml, scandium internal standard and five calibration solutions spanning 0.05–1.0 mg/l. Cs instead uses a flame-emission protocol with 50 mg sample, potassium nitrate and 1–20 mg/l calibration. Table 2 has 32 rows: 28 censored results, MgO and SiO₂ numeric results, and **discarded B₂O₃/CaO ICP data marked `d*.`**. Those two markers mean nonreproducible data were discarded, not zero or below detection. The two `n.d` cells in Table 3 are in its uncertainty column, not absent SiO₂/MgO concentrations.

These analytical calibration blanks are not sterile/killed-biomass controls. No microbial claim is under consideration. The parenthetical values and Table 3 e.s.d entries are retained as reported; no replicate number or confidence interval is invented.
