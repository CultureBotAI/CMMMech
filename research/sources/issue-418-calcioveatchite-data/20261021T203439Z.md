## Preparation, analyses and the range problem

The natural holotype is Fersman97013, from Nepskoe borehole130 at911m (pp.3–5). Other cores and localities represent different specimens. Water dissolution of host salt rock yielded a water-insoluble residue; intergrowth photographs include boracite and anhydrite. This is mineral preparation and characterization, not a recovery experiment or purity certification. No organism, microbial mechanism, yield or deployment claim is supported.

Methods(pp.5–6) include WDS electron microprobe analysis at20kV,10nA, a5×5µm raster, and Ca/diopside, Sr/SrSO4 and B/BN standards. FTIR used a KBr pellet at approximately150:1 KBr:mineral,4cm−1 resolution and16 scans. These methods are not raw per-spot results or a spot-to-crystal mapping. The FTIR discussion of water/OH environments does not add extra waters to the accepted ideal formula.

Table1(p.8) has18 numbered compositions. Column1 is the four-spot holotype mean; columns2 and18 are **calculated ideals**, explicitly identified in its footnote. Columns3–17 are selected measured compositions from five localities, with individual labels distinguished from Figure6's seven plotting-symbol categories. The figure's marker numbers are not Table1 column numbers, and the paper says the table is only a representative selection whereas the figure plots all points(pp.14–15). A measured range over all points cannot be reconstructed faithfully from a raster scatterplot or the selected table alone.

The holotype mean remains CaO7.05, SrO20.70 and B2O3 61.96wt%; its displayed SrO spot range is19.37–21.83. The Ca-rich measured column3 is CaO8.10, SrO19.56 and B2O3 63.14. Column17 has SrO35.17.
