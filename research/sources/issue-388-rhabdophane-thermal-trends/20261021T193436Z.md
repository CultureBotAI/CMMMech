## What the experiment supports

Shelyug's seven endmembers are La, Ce, Pr, Nd, Sm, Eu and Gd. Preparation on page 2 uses lanthanide chlorides, 5 M phosphoric acid, metal:P 1:1.03, 15 minutes at 60 °C then two weeks at 90 °C; water/ethanol washing and overnight air drying precede airtight storage. The series is shared with earlier Mesbah work, not independent samples generated to replicate it. A separate 200 mg, 1100 °C, six-hour air treatment makes monazite for characterization. The analytical TGA/DSC experiment uses 15–20 mg, air, 10 °C/min over 30–1000 °C, blank correction and calibration. These procedures must not be conflated.

Tables 1–2 and Figures 1–4 support synthetic phases and reported total-water coefficients. In La-to-Gd order the coefficients are 0.804, 0.732, 0.709, 0.746, 0.636, 0.555 and 0.533. Their overall direction opposes the abstract's stated decrease with increasing ionic radius, and the Nd/Pr ordering also prevents a strictly monotonic rule. Figure 2 provides the plotted element order; no new ionic-radius table or coordination-number assumption is required. Total mass loss includes adsorbed and structural water and cannot supply a universal IMA formula or refined site occupancy.

Page 4 gives a joint increasing temperature trend, whereas page 5 describes decreasing monazite-transformation temperature. Figure 4 separates T1 (squares), T2 (circles) and T3 (triangles), with open symbols for this study and filled symbols for Kijkowska's earlier study. The open dehydration series generally rise with ionic radius while the transformation series generally falls, with local variation. Its broken vertical axis and mixed study symbols preclude treating all points as one regression or replicate set. Exact temperature coordinates were not digitized. The irreversible experiment does not establish an equilibrium threshold; the discussed approximately ±10 °C inter-study differences are not supplied as per-point error bars.

The DOCX contains tables A1a, A1b and A1c (19, 17 and 17 rows), A2 elements/oxides/monazites (29 rows), and A2 continued rhabdophanes/reactions (16 rows). It contains thermodynamic cycles and compiled values, not raw thermograms or individual drop measurements. All native cells and paragraphs were inspected; their reading projection flattens sub/superscripts, and no reconstructed native visual layout is claimed. There are no Office Math or OLE objects; its single image is a publisher logo.

## Conditional numerical checks and contradictions

The external `numeric-audit.json` retains the literal extracted main tables, seven Table 2 row calculations, seven drop-solution cycles, seven reaction free-energy calculations, seven entropy-sign checks and 14 rounded cell-volume calculations. `supplement-checks.json` preserves native A2 comparisons. These are reproducible diagnostics of displayed entries, not corrected author data, new measurements, error propagation or acceptance of a thermodynamic model.

- In Table 2, Gd's displayed 49.5 kJ/mol sample divided by 0.533 gives 92.8705 kJ/mol water, whereas the table prints 86.1. Conditional intervals from half the last displayed decimal do not overlap. The other six quotient comparisons have overlapping rounding intervals. Applying printed equation 4 to Gd gives 4.848 kJ/mol versus printed 1.3; the discrepancy is within that column's stated ±5 experimental uncertainty but exceeds ordinary displayed rounding. No replacement value is selected.
- The seven printed sample and water enthalpies average 50.3143 and 74.6. If those seven different chemical endmembers were the inputs to an unweighted two-standard-error calculation, the values would be 3.5988 and 4.8791; they are not raw replicate runs. Table 2 instead gives 50.3 ±1.4 and 74.6 ±1.8, while page 5 gives 50.3 ±1.8. The underlying averaging and uncertainty basis is unavailable, so this does not substitute new experimental error estimates.
- Drop-solution cycles use 69.0 kJ/mol water at 700 °C for six elements and A1c's 73.2 at 800 °C for Pr. Pr requires its 210.67/163.31 pair in lead-borate solvent, not its separate 700 °C entries. Recalculated central values differ from printed reaction enthalpies by at most 0.0312 kJ/mol, compatible with displayed input rounding. This conditional agreement does not validate all formation data or uncertainty propagation.
- Main Table 4 and native A2 differ in the rhabdophane entropy-of-formation-from-oxides column: Sm is −109.2 versus −95.6 J/mol/K; Eu is −77.0 versus −66.8. Smaller differences occur for the other five rows and are preserved in the external comparison. Table 4 also labels its Gibbs-energy columns with entropy units; A2 supplies kJ/mol for Gibbs energy. Literal source headers are preserved, not silently relabelled as corrected observations.
- Equation 11 prints a minus sign for product water in the entropy balance. Using the displayed A2 molar entropies with the reaction's product-water plus sign reproduces all seven Table 5 entropies to the shown precision; using the printed minus sign does not. This is a stoichiometric diagnostic, not an attributable correction.
- Applying equation 12 with the printed Table 5 H/S values gives Sm −16.0424 versus −20.1 kJ/mol and Eu −6.7857 versus −9.8. These central-value mismatches exceed display rounding even though the broad reported uncertainties overlap. Nd has a smaller 0.08105 difference, slightly above the conditional 0.06991 display-rounding bound; La, Ce, Pr and Gd fall within that bound. All seven Table 5 reaction rows recur in A2, so the supplement does not resolve them. Figure 10 must not be treated as an independent experiment. The Ce reported −6.2 ±14.4 interval spans zero, preventing a blanket statistically resolved stability claim for every composition.
- Fourteen `a*b*c*sin(beta)` calculations record differences from rounded Table 1 cell volumes; no independent lattice refinement, covariance analysis or new discrepancy claim is made from them.
