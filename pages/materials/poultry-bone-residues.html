Poultry-bone residues secondary resource

Phosphorus-bearing poultry-bone residues investigated as a secondary feed for nutrient reuse, represented by cooked or boiled poultry bones ground and sieved to below 1 mm in the selected laboratory studies. The organic-mineral feed is distinct from elemental phosphorus, purified hydroxyapatite, phosphate rock, manure and bone-incineration ash. The animal species, supplier and exact cooking procedure are not reported; poultry is not silently narrowed to chicken. Material composition and treatment effects remain specimen-specific; no constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Culture-associated phosphorus elution from bone-amended soil columns

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Priestia megaterium (NCBITaxon:1404)

Bacillus megaterium PCM 1855, currently named Priestia megaterium, was associated with greater reported phosphorus elution from soil columns amended with cooked poultry-bone material. Adding concentrated growth medium increased the reported elution further. These comparisons combine inoculation with added phosphorus and nutrients, so they do not isolate a bacterial contribution or a bone-specific recovery yield.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The source describes 30 mL glass columns containing 10 g total soil and ground bone feed, at nominal 1%, 5% or 10% bone by mass. Inoculated columns received 10 mL logarithmic-phase culture; uninoculated comparators received 10 mL distilled water. Table 1 assigns an additional 6.08 mg P2O5-equivalent phosphorus to the bacterial suspension and also counts phosphorus already present in soil. The culture medium contained glucose, ammonium sulfate, NaCl, MgSO4, KCl, MnSO4, FeSO4 and yeast extract. Inoculum preparation was at 34 degrees C and 120 rpm; these are not verified column-incubation conditions. Water was applied from above, with four nominal 3 mL eluent collections per day, followed by paper filtration and colorimetric vanadophosphomolybdate assay expressed as P2O5. A second poultry-bone experiment used tenfold concentrated medium as eluent in both inoculated and control columns. Methods state 28 days, whereas Figures 2-3 and crossing-time discussions use minutes; exact elapsed time and rates are unresolved. Soil provenance, sterilization, column temperature and independent biological replication are not specified. No sterile-medium-matched or pH-matched control is described.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: wyciszkiewicz_2017_columns
Sections 2.1-2.4 and 3.1-3.2, Tables 1-2 and Figures 1-3 establish feed preparation and the treatment-associated elution pattern. Figure 2A and Table 2 report 47.3 versus 23.5 mg P2O5-equivalent phosphorus removed in the 10% inoculated and uninoculated poultry-bone arms; the figure establishes the amount unit omitted from Table 2. Figure 3 and Table 2 report greater elution in the medium-fed follow-up, including 153.5 versus 30.3 mg for the 10% arms. These are reported total-column elution amounts, not isolated product masses or phosphorus attributable solely to bone.
Partial Source: wyciszkiewicz_2017_columns
Table 1 shows unequal initial phosphorus inventories because only inoculated arms received the culture suspension. At 1% poultry bone, the reported elution difference of 15.2 versus 9.32 mg P2O5-equivalent is smaller than the 6.08 mg supplied with inoculum; a net bone-mobilization benefit is therefore not established by that difference alone. The source does not resolve phosphate from soil, inoculum and bone separately. The reported solubilization percentages and curve-crossing times are not adopted as recovery yields or rates. Soil sterility is not established by describing the comparator as without bacteria. The empirical pH model does not identify a particular organic acid or demonstrate an exclusive molecular mechanism.
Partial Source: wyciszkiewicz_2017_columns
The 28-day Methods description conflicts with the minute axes of Figures 2-3. Replicate counts, raw replicate data and a complete sampling/mass-balance calculation are unavailable in the inspected article. Table 3 gives several dose-slope p-values above 0.05 despite broad significance wording, and Table 4 labels some fitted maxima below its minima. No universal statistically demonstrated dose effect, fitted kinetic constant, agricultural efficacy, fertilizer safety or industrial deployment is inferred from this column study.

Acidophile-culture-associated phosphorus mobilization in bone-containing broth

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans (NCBITaxon:920)

In acidophile-inoculated flasks containing ground poultry-bone feed, liquid-phase phosphorus increased while pH fell. The response at the highest nominal dose was below that at 10 g/L. The proposed sulfuric-acid-mediated dissolution remains consistent with the culture conditions, but chemical acid and phosphate were already present and no matched abiotic control isolates microbial necessity.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The source-designated living Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans F7-01 was reported to have been isolated from the Iron Bridge tailings impoundment, Poland. A 10% v/v inoculum of 72-hour culture was used in 9K medium containing 44.2 g/L FeSO4.7H2O, 0.8 g/L elemental sulfur and 0.5 g/L K2HPO4, with initial pH adjusted to 2.5 using H2SO4. Methods describe a seven-day precursor cultivation at 30 degrees C, followed by addition of ground bones to 250 mL medium and 12-day shaken cultivation at 34 degrees C; shaker speed and filtration cutoff are not specified. Bone doses are given as 2, 4, 10 and 20 g/L, but Figures 1a and 2a label the highest poultry dose 40 g/L. Samples are reported as triplicate means. Permeates underwent nitric-acid digestion and ICP-OES, with elemental phosphorus expressed as P2O5 equivalent; this does not establish dissolved orthophosphate speciation. All reported feed-dose arms were inoculated; no matched sterile, no-cell or acid-only arm is presented. The precursor timeline is also internally inconsistent between Methods and Figure 3.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: wyciszkiewicz_2017_acidophile
Sections 2.1-2.3 and 3.1-3.3, Table 1 and Figures 1a/2a show culture-associated phosphorus release and lower final pH in poultry-bone-containing flasks. The lower liquid response at the highest nominal dose is retained; inhibition by bone-derived compounds is a proposed explanation rather than a demonstrated cause.
Partial Source: wyciszkiewicz_2017_acidophile
The medium supplied phosphate, ferrous sulfate, sulfur and sulfuric acid before bone addition. The paper does not establish complete blank correction or a closed phosphorus mass balance, and precursor cultivation does not prove that added chemical acid ceased contributing. Model-derived concentrations in mg/L conflict with the article's percent-concentration wording, and reported solubilization percentages are not adopted as recovery yields. Figure/model/dose inconsistencies prevent confident quantitative optimization. Table 4 and historical fertilizer-limit discussion do not establish current legal compliance, comprehensive safety, agronomic efficacy or industrial deployment. No purified phosphorus product was isolated.
Context_only Source: ncbi_920
The authority resolves the species name Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, but does not independently authenticate F7-01 or connect the experimental isolate to a strain-specific accession.
