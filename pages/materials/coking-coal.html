Coking coal commodity Critical Material

Coal suitable for making coke, treated as an end-use commodity rather than a mineral species, the carbon element, coke itself or all coal. The EIA glossary defines coking coal as suitable bituminous coal. Its separate metallurgical-coal definition includes both coking coal and pulverized coal consumed in steelmaking, whereas the USGS 2026 fact sheet uses coking and metallurgical coal interchangeably in its coke-production scope. This record follows the narrower coke-making identity and records the original EU 2024 coking-coal designation without silently equating all metallurgical-coal uses. The experiment concerns beneficiation of selected Tabas feeds described by the authors as suitable for coking; retained coke-making performance after treatment was not demonstrated.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated sulfur-content reduction in Tabas coking-coal feed

bioleaching Microbial

Prepared acidophilic mixed cultures were associated with lower residual sulfur content in selected Tabas coal feeds than thymol-containing uninoculated comparisons. The source proposes iron and sulfur oxidation associated with pyrite removal. Acid chemistry also removes sulfur, and the final hydrochloric-acid wash is part of the measured treatment sequence. This is laboratory impurity removal from coal, not microbial synthesis of coal, carbon recovery, an isolated sulfur product or demonstrated industrial coke production.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Section 2.2 describes cultures obtained from the Sarcheshme Copper Complex R&D Center, reported to contain Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans and Leptospirillum ferrooxidans. Section 2.4 specifies a 1:1:1 mixture without strain accessions or member-specific controls. Eastern Parvadeh PE primary feed was crushed to a reported +180/-350 micrometre fraction. The B4C1 and B4B2 preparations include flotation and heavy-liquid concentrates; their protocol is not silently equated to raw PE. The culture was grown for 14 days before exposure, without coal adaptation, using iron- and sulfur-supplemented medium. Sections 2.2 and 2.4 call the medium 9K and K9 and describe the iron amendment differently, so no unique iron concentration is inferred. Coal incubations used 100 mL liquid in 250 mL flasks at 34 degrees C, 150 rpm, initial pH 1.7, 5-10% w/v coal and 10-20% v/v inoculum for 10-20 days. Uninoculated comparisons contained medium, coal and 3% w/w thymol. Evaporation was compensated with water acidified to pH 1.7. Residues were filtered, washed with 10% v/v HCl and dried at 60-70 degrees C for 5-6 hours before analysis. The authors report ASTM-D3177 sulfur and ASTM-D3174 ash analyses; exact sulfur-speciation implementation is not documented. Replicate numbers and the meaning of figure error bars were not specified in the inspected full text.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: shahbazi2024
Table 1, printed p. 583, gives PE initial total sulfur of 1.85%. Table 3, p. 589, gives 0.51% after 20 days with 20% inoculum at 5% pulp density; Table 4, p. 590, gives 1.25% in the corresponding uninoculated condition. Reported reductions of 72.43% and 32.43% are consistent with those concentration changes. They are not a whole-feed sulfur mass balance, coal yield or recovered-product yield. Figure 4 shows evolving pH and ORP in the PE culture, consistent with the proposed acidophile chemistry but insufficient to identify a unique enzymatic or member-specific causal pathway.
Partial Source: shahbazi2024
Sections 2.4 and 3.3 establish chemical-treatment contributions: uninoculated acidic controls retain substantial sulfur removal, thymol makes their composition different from inoculated treatments, and final HCl washing can alter residue composition. The authors attribute organic-sulfur changes to acidic conditions rather than direct microbial attack. No chemistry-matched cell-free culture control or isolated contribution of the wash is reported.
Partial Source: shahbazi2024
Quantitative inconsistencies prevent generalizing the PE comparison. Table 3 assigns PE 68.98% ash reduction to bioleaching, whereas Section 3.4 and Figure 5 assign a similar value to bioflotation; Figure 5 also disagrees with several non-PE table values. Some non-PE initial and final sulfur entries do not reproduce the reported reduction percentages. No cross-method superiority, pooled result or exact ash-removal mechanism is accepted. Section 4 acknowledges unresolved scale and product-quality effects, and the inspected study reports no post-treatment coking-strength or plasticity test.
