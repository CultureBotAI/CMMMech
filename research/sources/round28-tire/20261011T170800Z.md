## Identity, feed and culture

The study used 400–600 µm GTR from an unnamed waste-tire recycling facility. No tire model or supplier-specific composition is extrapolated to every tire waste. Growing whole-cell **Rhodococcus rhodochrous RPK1** is reported as **DSM 103064**. The [DSMZ catalogue](https://www.dsmz.de/collection/catalogue/details/culture/DSM-103064) independently maps RPK 1 to DSM 103064 and the same species. [NCBI node 1829](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=1829) is species-level context, corroborated by [BacDive 131917](https://bacdive.dsmz.de/strain/131917); it is not a strain identifier or authentication of the experimental stock.

Methods 2.2/2.6: 1% w/v GTR in 100 mL mineral salts medium, initial pH 7.0 ± 0.05, in 250 mL flasks; 10% v/v nutrient-broth-grown inoculum, OD600 0.8–1.0; 30°C, 120 rpm, 28 days. Inoculum washing is not described, leaving broth carryover as a limitation to a sole-carbon interpretation. No killed-cell or isolated-enzyme test establishes a causal protein pathway.

Methods 2.3/2.4: three 70% ethanol washes and drying; choline chloride:urea 1:2 molar DES prepared at 70°C; rubber:DES 1:40 w/w; 24 h room-temperature swelling; 37 kHz sonication for 2 h; 180°C with stirring for 45 min; ethanol washing, filtration and drying at 50°C. This is a **chemical** stage before one bacterial arm.

Untreated GTR without bacteria and bacteria in MSM without GTR provide controls. DES-only compositional and swelling data provide a chemical comparator. No DES-pretreated abiotic 28-day comparator is shown for gravimetry or Schiff staining. Postculture removal of attached biomass and gravimetric drying are not documented as clearly as chemical preparation. These limits prevent a precise attribution of the combined treatment's net loss to bacteria.

## Quantitative boundaries

Section 2.7.3 uses 0.1 g residue, 5 mL 68.9% HNO3 for 24 h at room temperature, dilution to 250 mL in 2% HNO3, 0.2 µm filtration and externally calibrated ICP-OES at 213.857 nm. Results 3.5 gives **solid-residue** Zn fractions:

| Treatment | Zn (wt%) |
|---|---:|
| Untreated | 1.94 ± 0.06 |
| Bacteria | 2.25 ± 0.10 |
| DES alone | 1.30 ± 0.17 |
| DES then bacteria | 1.73 ± 0.11 |

The ICP replicate count and uncertainty statistic are not specified. Biological treatment increases the residue fraction in both comparisons; this does not establish microbial Zn extraction. The source attributes the chemical-stage decline to DES and calls it 33% removal. No liquid Zn inventory, purified metal product or closed feed balance supports a recovery yield. Increased fractions likewise do not prove exactly unchanged absolute Zn. Table 1 instead reports surface EDS fractions of 3.36, 2.06, 0.80 and 1.06 wt% in those groups, as means of two surface measurements. These differently sampled quantities are retained separately, including their opposite bacterial trends.

The earlier handoff's illustrative balance used control-adjusted 1.63% loss; SI now establishes gross 4.93% loss as the conditional residue basis. If the same particles conserved initial Zn, 1.94/0.9507 ≈ 2.0406 wt%, versus reported 2.25. This is only a consistency diagnostic, not a new measurement: paired inventories and raw uncertainties are unavailable. The record does not import that calculation.

Table 2 reports crosslink density in 10⁻⁵ mol/cm³, mean ± SD, n = 3: untreated 9.61 ± 0.16; bacteria 8.09 ± 0.29; DES 7.03 ± 0.25; combined 5.44 ± 0.12. The source reports a 15.81% biological decrease. Acetone extraction for 24 h and n-hexane swelling at 25°C for 72 h feed a Flory–Rehner calculation using χ = 0.45 and rubber density 1.15 g/cm³. This is model-derived network evidence, not specific enzyme causality.

SI Table S2 and Figure 9a give the following, from initial 1.00 g:

| Arm | Final mass (g) | Gross loss (%) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Untreated GTR, abiotic | 0.967 | 3.30 ± 2.36 |
| Bacteria/GTR | 0.951 | 4.93 ± 1.15 |
| Bacteria/DES-pretreated GTR | 0.841 | 15.84 ± 8.78 |

Neither table nor figure caption defines n or the ± statistic. Main-text 1.63% and 12.53% subtract the same untreated abiotic loss as percentage-point differences; rounded table values give 12.54 for the latter. These are not recovered-product yields, percentages of remaining control mass, or an isolated biological increment over DES-pretreated abiotic material. Broad uncertainties and the missing comparator limit quantitative interpretation.

## Assays and source conflicts

Figure 9b–e shows pink Schiff staining in both rubber-plus-bacteria cultures, absent from the photographed untreated-GTR abiotic and bacteria/no-GTR controls. Together with swelling, FTIR and qualitative SEM, this supports a limited transformation phenotype. It does not uniquely identify oligo-isoprenoids or quantify carbon mineralization. SI Table S1's carbon/sulfur fractions are compositional data, not elemental mass balances. Table S3/Figure S2 contain tentative LC-MS assignments without an abiotic chromatogram or standards-confirmed products; C20H32O2 plus Na would have approximately nominal m/z 327, inconsistent with listed 307. Proposed Lcp, Dsz/4S or other specific pathways are not causally demonstrated.

- Figure 1b peaks around 10¹² CFU/mL on its axis, while prose reports approximately 10⁹. Figure 1a OD continues increasing after day 14 despite the prose's joint maximum claim. Exact growth counts are unresolved and excluded.
- Figure 7 marks a 45 min isothermal hold, versus 60 min in Methods 2.5. This is separate from the microbial incubation.
- Figure 8's visible final char ranking differs from its embedded table; Figure 10's protein trajectory and day-28 control differ from the prose. Those exact ancillary outcomes are excluded.
- The printed sol-fraction equation has problematic parentheses; raw swelling data are unavailable for reconstruction.
- The disc photograph and residual OD readings do not justify general DES nontoxicity. No process sustainability, isolated Zn yield, industrial demonstration or lifecycle benefit is established.

These limitations warrant `partial` evidence. The record separates a microbial rubber-transformation mechanism from a nonmicrobial chemical-preparation mechanism; both use existing `other` process support. No schema change or quantitative-cell import is needed.
