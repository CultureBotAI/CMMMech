Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Gadolinium association with harvested cyanobacterial biomass

other Microbial

Associated Organisms: Limnospira platensis (NCBITaxon:118562) [TaxonMech]

Cultures reported as Arthrospira platensis CNMN-CB-02 yielded harvested dried fractions containing Gd after growth in synthetic Gd(III)-supplemented medium. Increasing nominal exposure was associated with increasing element concentration in this fraction. Total-element analysis does not distinguish intracellular bioaccumulation from surface association or nonbiological material retained with the biomass.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The study reports Arthrospira platensis CNMN-CB-02 from the Institute of Microbiology and Biotechnology, Technical University of Moldova. The official collection lists CB-02 as Spirulina platensis. NCBI maps both species names to Limnospira platensis; the species identifier does not verify a strain genome or a strain-specific NCBI node. Living cultures were exposed to Gd(III), supplied as Gd(NO3)3·6H2O, in separate single-element treatment series at 10, 20 and 30 mg/L elemental concentration. These are not mixed-REE selectivity assays. Supplementation occurred on day 1 with 0.4 g/L inoculum, followed by five more days of cultivation. Flasks held 500 mL of nutrient medium, including 8 g/L bicarbonate and 0.08 g/L EDTA, at pH 10.0 and 30 degrees C. Illumination was reported as 55 µM photons/m2/s; the paper uses this nonstandard printed unit, retained without silently converting it. Cultures were shaken at 100 rpm for 45 min twice daily, 12 h apart. Controls were cultures grown without added REEs. Experiments were reported in triplicate, with Student t-tests against controls. Biomass was retained on 5-8 µm filters and part was dried for instrumental neutron activation analysis (NAA); reference materials supported analytical quality control. The methods do not describe a post-filtration wash, a biomass-free exposure control or cell-surface versus intracellular fractionation. NAA measures total element in the harvested dried fraction, not free-ion speciation or aqueous depletion. Initial solution concentrations were checked by ICP-OES, but no final dissolved-element balance is reported for this experiment. No desorption, isolated product or industrial demonstration was tested.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: zinicovscaia2024
Sections 2.1-2.3 and Section 3/Figure 1 support dose-associated increases of Gd in the harvested dried biomass fraction across 10, 20 and 30 mg/L treatments. The text gives 8600-27,400 mg Gd/kg, but the highest plotted bar appears lower than that textual maximum; exact endpoint agreement is unresolved. No value is digitized or entered as a structured observation.
Partial Source: zinicovscaia2024
The harvested-fraction result supports biomass-associated Gd accumulation only. Without a reported wash, abiotic exposure control, aqueous mass balance or localization assay, surface binding, intracellular uptake, retained medium and co-retained precipitate are not separated. Alkaline bicarbonate-containing medium further makes unmeasured chemical partitioning relevant; precipitation is a possible alternative, not a demonstrated phase. The record therefore uses process other rather than asserting biosorption, biomineralization or metabolic transport as a resolved cause.
Partial Source: zinicovscaia2024
The results and Figures 2-4 retain condition-dependent responses: biomass quantity is above control at 10 and 20 mg/L and close at 30 mg/L, while carbohydrates and phycobiliproteins decline. Section 4’s broad statement that protein content was unaffected conflicts with Section 3 and Figure 3a, which show a lower protein fraction at 30 mg/L. The text’s assertion that MDA was lower than or approximately equal to control at every Gd concentration also does not match the visibly higher 10 mg/L bar in Figure 3d. Consequently this record makes no general claim of physiological safety or no stress.
