## Ruthenium: observed oxidized deposits and unresolved causality

[The ruthenium record](../../data/records/ruthenium.yaml) curates
[Omajali et al., Scientific Reports 9, 4715 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-40312-3),
published March 18, 2019. The publisher PDF, complete JATS XML, Tables 1–3,
Figures 1–4 and the full supplementary PDF were acquired. Relevant supplementary
image panels S3, S4, S6–S9 and S11 were rendered and visually inspected; the
supplement's page 12 is blank. Spectra were assessed for consistency with the
reported interpretation, not independently refitted or digitized.

The paper names **Bacillus benzeovorans NCIMB 12555**. NCBI Taxonomy 1456 uses
**Bacillus benzoevorans** and explicitly lists NCIMB 12555 among its type-strain
collection identifiers. The record preserves the source spelling in context and
uses the verified species label; a species identifier is not a new strain ID.

Methods describes washed biomass and a Ru-only feed of 2 mM Ru(III) chloride
hydrate, pH 2, followed by hydrogen saturation and 96 h at 30 °C. The sequential
Pd/Ru experiment differs: Pd was deposited first, the biomass was washed and then
exposed to a 1 mM Ru(III) solution. Nominal loadings refer to cell dry weight.
Table 1's actual loadings are inferred by solution difference using a stannous
chloride colorimetric assay. They are not directly measured solid-element yields.
The narrative's approximate 40% residual Ru cannot be treated as the exact
fraction for every Table 1 preparation.

The retained mechanism is `other`: **biomass-associated deposition of oxidized
ruthenium**. Figure 1 and S3–S5 support association with cell surfaces and some
Pd/Ru-containing intracellular deposits after seeding. A no-metal imaging
baseline does not replace a cell-free Ru control. The inspected text and supplement
do not report matched cell-free, killed-cell or hydrogen-free Ru deposition
outcomes, a Ru-removal replicate count, sustained viability, or a specific enzyme
requirement.

Several limits are material to the interpretation:

- XPS text/Table 2 assigns surface Ru(III)/Ru(VI); bulk XANES assigns Ru(III)/Ru(IV).
  Figure 3/S8 labels RuCl3 and Ru(OH)3 components, and air was not excluded.
  The record does not force one exact whole-sample valence distribution.
- The EXAFS prose says Ru–Ru peaks are absent immediately after describing a
  fitted Ru–Ru shell also present in Table 3. The record avoids the stronger
  conclusion of exclusively atomically dispersed Ru. This inconsistency is not
  affirmative evidence for Ru(0).
- Supplementary Figure S6's high-loading legend says 20% Ru/5% Pd, unlike the
  Table 1 nominal 10% Pd/10% Ru preparation. The source concludes no lattice shift
  or metallic alloy; exact high-loading sample correspondence is unresolved.
- Subsequent 5-HMF catalysis uses dried material at 260 °C and 50 bar hydrogen.
  Organic-product yields are not Ru extraction or recovery yields, and these
  conditions do not show living-cell activity.

A related primary [E. coli MC4100 study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2019.01276)
was inspected in full XML, including its methods and XANES/EXAFS results and
Table 3. It allows a small metallic Ru contribution in the bulk fit despite
predominantly oxidized Ru. This is retained as `context_only`: a different
organism and loading scheme, not a refutation or independent replication of the
Bacillus result. A separate mechanism awaits its figure and supplement review.

## Rhodium: separate single-metal and simultaneous mixed-metal assays

[The rhodium record](../../data/records/rhodium.yaml) curates
[Xie et al., Nanoscale Advances 8, 961–972](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5NA00861A).
The electronic publication date is December 16, 2025; issue metadata is 2026,
with collection date February 3, 2026 in current `PMC12713794.2` XML. These dates
are not separate experiments. Version .2 alone does not establish a formal
correction. The full JATS XML, original Figure 1–6 JPEGs, Tables 1–2 and complete
two-page supplementary Table S1 were inspected. RSC PDF routes returned 403;
Europe PMC supplied complete open figure/supplement archives instead.

The paper names **Geobacter sulfurreducens** without a culture-collection or
strain accession. NCBI species 35554 is therefore used; the type-strain accession
is not inferred. Resting cells, prepared by anaerobic cultivation and two
bicarbonate washes, were tested in 10 mL bottles at OD600 0.4, pH 6.8–7.0,
30 °C, with 10 mM acetate, 30 mM bicarbonate and an 80:20 N2/CO2 atmosphere.

For the **200 micromolar Rh-only assay**, Figure 1a and Results 3.1.1 report
rapid centrifuged-supernatant Rh depletion with cells and no comparable loss
without cells. Rh(0)-consistent XRD and SAED support a reduced solid component
after 24 h. No monometallic Rh XPS signal was resolved. Consequently the record
does not assert a total Rh(0) fraction, complete conversion or isolated-metal yield.

For the **100 micromolar Rh plus 100 micromolar Pd simultaneous assay**, Figure 1d
shows a separate no-cell Rh loss of about 20% at the later endpoint. The authors
suggest co-removal during Pd hydrolysis. Mixed-particle XRD, atomic-resolution
imaging and EDS support their alloy interpretation, but XRD/Vegard-law and EDS
composition estimates differ and Figure 5's EDS percentages are field averages.
Table 1's fitted 81% Rh(0)/19% Rh(III) concerns the mixed material's surface signal,
not all input Rh and not the monometallic preparation.

Methods mentions donor-free controls, but their Rh-specific outcomes are not
shown in the inspected article or Table S1. Figure 1 error bars and replication
are undefined, and direct cytochrome causality, cell viability and matched
killed-cell outcomes are not established. The cell-free comparisons support the
narrow observed association without resolving all biological steps. Methods and
figure time-point descriptions also differ outside the retained 1 h claim;
no full time-course table or precise kinetic fit is reconstructed here.

The subsequent 4-nitrophenol/NaBH4 catalyst assay is recorded only as context.
Its molar dose is normalized to total metal relative to initial organic substrate;
its conversion percentages do not measure Rh recovery. Supplementary Table S1
compares catalysts under differing doses/supports/additives and is not a new
metal-removal dataset. Neither curated mechanism demonstrates real-waste-feed
operation or industrial deployment.

The earlier [Shewanella algae study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hydromet.2013.06.019)
remains a concrete extension lead. Publisher abstract/section snippets describe
mixed Rh(0)/Rh(III) in recovered material, but its complete methods and figures
were not acquired, so no measurements or mechanism were imported. A recent
review also points to extracellular Pseudomonas and sulfate-reducing consortium
work; those primary references require resolution before curation.
