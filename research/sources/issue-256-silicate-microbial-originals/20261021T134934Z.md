## Phlogopite: complete manuscript and thesis crosswalk

The natural feed is Madagascar–Vohitrosy phlogopite, distinct from Pinzari’s Italian specimen, generic biotite and ideal endmember composition. Methods 2.1–2.4 describe washed 50–100 µm material, BET area 0.5±0.3 m²/g and a Mg/Fe-depleted mineral medium. The reported oxide inventory sums to **1,013.1 g/kg**, so it is not forcibly normalized or treated as a closed mass balance. The 60% relative BET uncertainty alone limits area-normalized rate precision. No post-experiment phase or isolated Mg-product assay closes the feed/solution/biomass/residue balance.

Four strain labels RA1, PA1, AR1 and PS2 refer to source-reported short-16S identifications with approximately 99% maximum matching, not independently authenticated stocks. The manuscript contains spelling variants in organism names and no verified strain-specific accession. It reports active cultures and OD600, with microscopy-based OD/cell calibration stated but not supplied; OD is planktonic biomass proxy, not a viable-cell count or accounting of attached cells. Organic acids were assayed only for R1/B1/B2. Missing acid measurements in R3/R4 are not zeros or nondetections.

The experiments used 24 °C mineral-contact assays, with culture preparation at 28 °C. Manual versus continuous agitation differs by arm, and runs labelled R1–R4 or B1–B2 are not interchangeable replicate reactors. The acid-only comparisons use nitric, citric and gluconic acids and approximate selected biotic pH/ligand conditions. They do not supply a fully crossed, glucose-, pH-, metabolite-, agitation- and biomass-matched causal design. No explicitly identified killed-cell or cell-free spent-medium control closes those alternatives. The reported analytical repeats for isotope measurements are repeat analyses of samples, not independent biological reactors.

Tables 1–2 are preserved as 41 and 23 rows. The manual ledger contains 264 numeric components and 146 blank cells in Table1, plus the four starting-mineral isotope mean/error components separately. Table2 contains 116 numeric components, 65 displayed dashes and 72 blank cells. The Table1 caption defines a below-detection dash convention; Table2 lacks its own separate limit footnote, while the earlier thesis and organic-acid discussion support nondetection interpretation. Dashes are retained literally without assigning analyte-specific limits. The explicit acetate value `0.0` is kept distinct from both blanks and dashes.

The sources support solution release and isotope fractionation observations, subject to these unresolved differences:

- **Fractions and concentrations:** thesis Tables2–3 report `F_Mg`/`F_Si` in per mille of initial mineral element; journal Table1 reports ppm and Mg/Si ratios. Figure6 explicitly labels `F_Mg (‰)`. It must not become percent recovery. Assuming ppm=mg/L and 0.1 L unchanged volume, the printed feed MgO and 3 g charge imply about 531.883 mg initial Mg; B2 day12, 6.60 ppm, would represent about 1.241‰. This is only a diagnostic: withdrawals, biomass and final volume are not corrected. Table1 ppm is not silently relabelled as mg/kg or mg/L.

- **Isotopes:** starting mineral δ26Mg is −1.22‰. Printed B1 −1.09 implies +0.13‰, versus reported Δ+0.11; B2 −0.88 implies +0.34‰, versus +0.36. Cached PDF text has the same ordering, but no original main-page pixels were obtained to settle the layout. No treatment labels were silently repaired.

Original Figure1 shows a residual dolomite peak alongside sepiolite. Figures3–5 and their captions identify B1/rich, B7/poor and B8/poor residues after fifteen days. The K-550 pattern is a separate thermal preparation, not bacterial generation of a new phase. The images support the reported absence of a major bulk-XRD structural change, not complete absence of surface alteration. Table1 before/after XRF lacks a unique residue-arm/mass join; MgO declining from 31.21 to 25.59 wt% is a compositional shift, not an 18% recovered-Mg yield. Dolomite and medium backgrounds limit attribution of all solution Mg uniquely to sepiolite.

The cached methods describe two media, three timepoints and three replicates with a non-inoculated control. No inspected evidence establishes mineral-free, killed-cell or pH-matched controls, a measured organic-acid flux, or a complete solid/liquid balance. Medium differences and endpoint solution concentrations cannot independently prove a particular acid/ligand pathway. Post-assay oxidizing cleanup also precedes solid characterization. The named organism assignments remain sequence-similarity claims rather than independently verified cultures.

Table6’s accessions are **reference matches**, not deposits of the tested isolates.
