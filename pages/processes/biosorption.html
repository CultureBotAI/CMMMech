Birnessite

Birnessite is a layered manganese oxide mineral, listed as grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list with formula (Na,Ca,K)0.6(Mn4+,Mn3+)2O4(H2O)1.5. Its composition and polytype vary. It is distinct from elemental manganese, mixed manganese ore and nodules, the buserite precursor used to prepare some synthetic analogues, and manganese oxalate products. The mechanisms below concern separately prepared synthetic sodium and cobalt-bearing forms, and a source-assigned poorly crystalline biogenic Mn-oxide/fungal composite. They do not establish behavior for natural ore or every birnessite polytype. No criticality designation is inherited from manganese.

Electroplating-bath effluents

Metal-bearing liquids originating from industrial electroplating baths, represented by five Cu, Ni, Pd and Au bath effluents supplied by Galvanica Aricci Srl, Ghisalba, Italy, in Ciani et al. (2026). These compositionally different process liquids are distinct from electroplating wastewater-treatment sludge, metallic electroforming scrap and laboratory metal-salt solutions. The study's Ni(1)+Cu mixture is an experimental combination of two supplied liquids, not a sixth independently sampled waste stream. No criticality designation is inherited from constituent metals.

Spent NdFeB permanent magnets

Discarded neodymium-iron-boron permanent-magnet material considered as a secondary resource, distinct from the neodymium element, a fixed-purity Nd2Fe14B compound, all rare-earth magnets, and the broader commodity grouping of raw materials for permanent magnets. Actual feeds can contain other rare earths, alloying elements, coatings and impurities; no universal composition is assigned. Riano and Binnemans document an end-of-life NdFeB feed. The microbial-origin biomass assay curated here instead used chemically prepared hard-disk-magnet leachate; its precise device history and waste collection provenance were not reported. This is the same Kucuker experiment represented under neodymium, not an independent replication or a criticality designation inherited from its constituent elements.

Tannery wastewater

Aqueous waste streams from tanning operations, represented here by conventionally treated effluent from the Acque del Chiampo plant in Arzignano, Italy. This heterogeneous secondary resource is distinct from wastewater-treatment sludge, chromium-tanned leather shavings, synthetic chromium solutions and elemental chromium. The accepted laboratory study concerns further removal of residual source-assigned Cr(III) using dead fungal biomass; composition and performance are specific to the tested feeds. No constituent criticality is inherited.

Titanogypsum

Heterogeneous solid residue described by the source as titanium gypsum or titanogypsum from titanium-dioxide production. The curated feed is a real Taizhou, Jiangsu plant waste, distinct from natural gypsum, titanium dioxide, phosphogypsum from phosphoric-acid manufacture and a purified calcium-sulfate product. Its unusual reported bulk composition and mixed phase assignments are retained rather than imposing pure-gypsum stoichiometry. Laboratory culture-associated redistribution of fluorine does not establish recovery of a fluorine product, a safe agricultural amendment or industrial deployment. No constituent-element criticality is inherited.
