## Catalog distinction and remaining lineage gap

The official Smithsonian search page documents `nb` catalog-number queries. Its public scripts declare a read-only query endpoint and normalization; two bounded queries returned one record each:

| Printed number | Current catalog entry | Persistent sample identifier | Current catalog label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1977 B10530 | `B 10530 00`, internal record1126431 | [10.58151/NHB0021RA](https://doi.org/10.58151/NHB0021RA) | Lanthanite |
| 1989 B10531 | `B 10531 00`, internal record1126432 | [10.58151/NHB0021RB](https://doi.org/10.58151/NHB0021RB) | Lanthanite-(Ce) |

Both entries give Skinnskatteberg, Bastnas, Sweden. SESAR landing pages and DataCite records independently resolve the two catalog identifiers as individual physical samples. They are **sample identifiers**, not mineral-species identifiers. Their 2015 publication and 2019 update dates concern registration metadata; collection dates and collectors are not provided. The landing pages list no sample family and no external parent; these empty fields do not prove that the physical material has no shared history. Nor do they verify which fragment was sent to the 1977 or 1989 investigators. The catalog labels do not newly assay B10530 or connect either entry to 1845.

The catalog records corroborate a real distinction between the printed numbers; there is no basis here to silently “correct” either publication to the other's number. A type label or a chain of custody from the original Swedish material remains unverified.
