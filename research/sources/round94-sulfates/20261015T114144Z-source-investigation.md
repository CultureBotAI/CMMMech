## Ammoniojarosite: complete Wang2007 original

**Accepted original:** Wang H,Bigham JM,Jones FS,Tuovinen OH, *Synthesis and properties of ammoniojarosites prepared with iron-oxidizing acidophilic microorganisms at22–65°C*, GCA71(2007):155–164, DOI10.1016/j.gca.2006.09.001. The complete ten-page repository PDF has actual publisher pagination and graphics. Crossref independently confirms the2007 print year; the2006 copyright/DOI year is not substituted. All text, four tables and nine figures were inspected, with full-page PNGs retained. No separate supplement is announced in the inspected complete original; this is not a universal absence claim.

Methods§2.1–2.2 pp156–157 and Tables1–2 define the cultures, medium, preparation and diffraction procedures. MAF is an enrichment dominated by *Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans*, not a deposited pure strain. The source's *Sulfolobus metallicus* is currently mapped by NCBI to *Sulfuracidifex metallicus*, taxon47303. MAF maps only at species-name scope to920. The thermophilic MT consortium was re-enriched at65°C without identifying its resulting composition; its earlier45°C community is not assigned to the65°C assays. Current batch NCBI XML verifies both species names and the archaeal synonym.

The concentration series used36°C and5.4–805mM ammonium in aerobic100mL cultures/250mL flasks,120mM ferrous sulfate, modified potassium-free nutrients and initialpH2.3–2.6. All reagents, drying/washing and missing inoculum/shaking/replication detail remain qualified in the owner. Tables1/3 and Figures1/2 distinguish schwertmannite-only identification at5.4/9.4mM, mixed phases at11.5–85.4mM and ammoniojarosite-only crystalline identification at165–805mM. The conclusion says “above165” whereas Table3 includes165; the owner adopts the explicit arm, not a universal threshold. XRD-only identification does not establish absolute purity or a recovered yield.

The separate165mM temperature series used MAF22/36°C, source-named *S.metallicus*45°C and MT65°C. §3.2 p159 describes about two months for the22°C precipitate versus seven days at36°C and higher; iron oxidation and precipitation timing are distinct. Tables4 and Figures3–8 show nonstoichiometric products and inferred hydronium substitution. Biomass nitrogen could affect bulk N. The weak NH4-series correlation in Figure3b is not converted into a reliable composition predictor.

Abiotic syntheses used ferric sulfate and different ammonium concentrations, aging times and temperatures. The authors explicitly say these are not traditional matched controls. Source sample6.6 is65°C in Methods, Tables1/4 and the plotted Figure4 label but45°C in Table3 and Figures4/9 captions. Figure7 prints35°C for the low abiotic trace versus36°C in tables. These source-label conflicts remain unresolved; exact matched-temperature microbial enhancement is not claimed. Figure9 aggregation and§3.3 cannot establish extracellular-polymer causation or a unique biosignature; inactive-cell comparisons, measured precipitation rates and monitored ammonium-consumption histories are absent. No industrial execution or quantitative recovery outcome is inferred.

## Argentojarosite: complete original2013 thesis,2016 journal context separated

**Accepted original:** Chiranjit Mukherjee, *Characterization of argentojarosite synthesized with biologically produced ferric sulfate solutions*, MScMicrobiology, Ohio State University,2013. Official OhioLink accession`osu1374139251`; title/author/year are directly verified in frontmatter, and the official catalog independently matches. The deposited PDF has71pages: experimental methods/results/conclusions printed28–53,PDF38–63; original experimental Figures5–16 and Tables7–10. Main text, all experimental figures/tables and relevant introduction/reference context were inspected; renders cover the entire PDF. There is no appendix or separate supplement announced in its table of contents or inspected original/catalog.

**Context only:** Mukherjee,Jones,Bigham,Tuovinen2016, MSE C66:164–169, DOI10.1016/j.msec.2016.04.061. Publisher/abstract material and current Crossref metadata establish a same-group research continuation; a complete journal original and all its graphics were not acquired. Its additional protocol descriptions are not silently imported into the thesis experiment, and it is not counted as independent replication. The thesis provides a complete primary basis on its own.

Thesis§2.1 p28 describes a composite *A.ferrooxidans* culture, not a verified strain. Stock culture was maintained at5%inoculum,180rpm,22±2°C,pH2.0±0.1. The modified100mL/250mL medium replaced potassium phosphate and ammonium sulfate by phosphoric and nitric acids. Silver0/5/20/40mM was added only after the dark-red color indicated iron oxidation at5–7days. Figure5 explicitly labels the upstream step biotic and the Ag-addition step abiotic. The owner sets microbial involvement for this combined route, not for demonstrated metabolism during Ag precipitation. Cell viability after Ag addition and biological replication are not established.

Figures6–9/Table10 establish a multi-reflection argentojarosite assignment, with cell parameters and chemical composition, especially the40mM/14day product. Table9 reports18.42wt%Ag,28.99wt%Fe,11.07wt%S for that collected solid. These are solid composition percentages, not feed recovery, and neither refined purity nor a mass balance was established. Table8/color discussion admits poorly crystalline residual material. The original zero-Ag phase is described as a possible schwertmannite precursor; it is not upgraded to pure schwertmannite. Table7/Figure13 improvements are reflection-dependent, not universally monotonic across20→40mM.

§3.3 and Figures14/15 retain separated-phase nulls: insufficient supernatant precipitate for analysis at14days; no identified argentojarosite in the separately Ag-treated solid. Later supernatant precipitation was not characterized. §3.5 reports no centrifuge-recoverable product in a chemical ferric-sulfate/40mM Ag14day assay; “120mM ferric sulfate” is retained as printed rather than silently converted to Fe3+ concentration. Conversely§3.4/Figure16 reports partial argentojarosite in a separate old synthetic schwertmannite reference with goethite after100mg/20mM Ag/pH2.0±0.1/14days, without an explicit suspension volume. These chemically different comparisons do not prove universal necessity of two culture phases, living cells or a specific nucleation pathway.

Several original inconsistencies are explicitly bounded. Figure6 lower curves say5mM while caption/prose say0mM; the unambiguous Figure8 supports the accepted14day comparison. Table9 and Figure11 show90.33wt%Fe for20mM/8hours and0.37wt%Fe for0mM/8hours, unexplained; no correction or yield is inferred. Figure14 prints one hour for separated-phase sampling versus the main eight-hour series, so only its explicitly discussed14day outcome is adopted. Table9's five-mM silver concentration decreases8hours→5days before increasing at14days; no universally increasing time course is claimed.
