## What the 2017 paper actually qualifies

Ochiai and Utsunomiya's *Crystal Chemistry and Stability of Hydrated Rare-Earth
Phosphates Formed at Room Temperature*, *Minerals* 7(5):84, published 19 May 2017,
[DOI 10.3390/min7050084](https://doi.org/10.3390/min7050084), is a separate abiotic
precipitation and hydroxyapatite experiment. I read its introduction, methods,
discussion, implications, identifying references and license; original PDF pages
2, 14 and 17 were also visually inspected. This is a targeted qualification
assessment, not a complete scientific review of all its plots and supplements.

| Evidence locator | Defensible interpretation |
|---|---|
| Jiang 2010 abstract, fresh NTU institutional copy | Describes yeast-associated Ce phosphate with a monazite structure. This is the authors' abstract-level assignment; the underlying original diffraction has not been examined here. |
| Ochiai 2017 introduction, page 2; references 20–21, page 18 | The cited Ce 2010 and Yb 2012 studies had diffuse electron-diffraction maxima and amorphous material that prevented full particle identification. This is an explicit limitation on prior identification. |
| Ochiai 2017 implications, page 17, last paragraph | Explicitly describes the Ce particles in reference 20 as needle-shaped rhabdophane. This is a substantive difference from the earlier monazite label, omitted from the earlier targeted dossier's summary. The passage supplies no reanalysis of the original Ce specimen. |
| Ochiai 2017 section 4.1 and Figure 12, page 14 | Reports rhabdophane among its own room-temperature light-REE products and no initial monazite in those experiments. Different reagents, phosphate supply, aging and biological context prevent substituting this outcome for the 2010 phase assignment. |
| Ochiai 2017 section 4.1, page 14 | Discusses electron-beam-induced xenotime formation in its heavy-REE samples. This motivates checking preparation/beam exposure in 2010; it does not show that 2010 Ce particles underwent that transformation. |

Neither nominal CePO4, Ce/P elemental spectra nor needle morphology alone
establishes monazite-(Ce), rhabdophane-(Ce), hydration or a pure product. The
later paper is not a formal correction or a direct reidentification experiment.
The original 2010 patterns, indexing/reference standards and specimen history
remain necessary to reconcile the labels. The preserved contradiction is more
informative than selecting either label from a later sentence.

## Assay evidence still needed

The fresh abstract gives exposure at pH 3, 4 or 5 and a 4–120-hour window, with
an intermediate 42-hour particle observation. These are discovery clues, not
curated measurements. The complete original is still needed to verify:

- Experimental strain, living/resting/inactivated state, viability, culture
  medium, washing and phosphorus carryover. A species name cannot establish a
  strain or active metabolism.
- Initial Ce/P concentrations, biomass loading, temperature, exact timepoints,
  replicate structure, detection limits and solution/solid measurement bases.
  Solution depletion is not automatically isolated-product recovery.
- Matched cell-free, killed-cell, phosphate-release and abiotic precipitation
  comparators, including pH and phosphorus availability. Outreach's mention of
  abiotic precipitates does not establish matched controls.
- Original diffraction/microscopy, hydration-sensitive characterization,
  amorphous fractions, sample drying/preparation and beam history. Whether the
  2010 article has supplementary files also remains unverified.

No gene/enzyme causality, selective Ce recovery, phosphorus mass balance or
industrial application is established by this source check. Later Yb/Sm and
separate fungal-leachate experiments cannot supply missing 2010 controls.
