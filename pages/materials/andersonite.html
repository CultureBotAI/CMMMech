Andersonite is the sodium calcium uranyl carbonate hydrate mineral with the September 2026 IMA formula Na2Ca(UO2)(CO3)3(H2O)6. Its grandfathered status G and original-description year 1951 are not an approval accession. A natural Cane Springs Canyon specimen studied in 2018 was refined in trigonal R-3m with 5.3 H2O per formula unit, distinct from the ideal six-water formula and from synthetic preparations. The authors proposed variable channel-water occupancy and a (5+x)H2O formulation; that proposal does not replace the current IMA formula here. This mineral is distinct from elemental uranium and a uranium commodity. No criticality designation, microbial process or industrial recovery is inferred.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Laboratory thermal alteration of natural andersonite

other

In-air powder X-ray diffraction showed loss of andersonite crystallinity around 100 degrees C: unit-cell trends changed after 95 degrees C and the last diffraction relicts occurred at 105 degrees C. Subsequent heating produced diffraction peaks assigned to other uranium-bearing phases. Cooling did not reverse the reported transformation. Water release and framework collapse are the authors' structural interpretation; this study did not directly measure water loss by simultaneous thermogravimetry or evolved-gas analysis. These are laboratory phase observations, not a uranium extraction yield or a demonstrated recovery process.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Natural sample 1272 in the University of Notre Dame collection came from Cane Springs Canyon, San Juan County, Utah, USA. Ground crystalline material was heated on a platinum strip in air from 25 to 800 degrees C at 2 degrees C/min, with 5-25 degree C increments and approximately 30 minutes of diffraction collection per step. PXRD and EDX indicated minor quartz inclusions; the sample was not asserted to be perfectly pure. Single-crystal refinement at 100 K gave 5.3 H2O per formula unit with disordered water sites and some restrained O-H distances. Powder loading, particle-size distribution and independent replicate counts were not specified.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: gurzhiy2018
Section 2.4 describes the heating and cooling protocol. Section 3.2 and Figure 4 show the nearly unchanged initial powder pattern, changes after 95 degrees C and the last andersonite relicts at 105 degrees C. Residual platinum-holder and quartz peaks are distinguished from andersonite. These temperatures describe this stepped experiment, not universal equilibrium or kinetic decomposition thresholds.
Partial Source: gurzhiy2018
Sections 3.1 and 4 connect refined channel-water occupancy and anisotropic thermal expansion with inferred dehydration. Direct gravimetric water-loss curves are absent from this experiment. The structural estimate of 5.3 H2O is not an independent bulk water-content measurement, and the authors' molecular explanation remains distinct from observed PXRD.
Context_only Source: gurzhiy2018
Section 3.2 assigns Na4(UO2)(CO3)3 near 340 degrees C, CaUO4 at 575 degrees C, Na2U2O7 above 600 degrees C and NaUO3 beginning at 700 degrees C. NaUO3 is described as the only distinguishable phase at 800 degrees C, with a quartz peak overlapped; this is not a quantified pure-product yield or a direct uranium-valence determination. The methods specify CoKalpha radiation, whereas Figure 4 labels its horizontal axis CuKalpha. That unresolved discrepancy precludes reconstructing exact peak positions here.
