Titanium metal commodity Critical Material

Titanium in metallic commercial forms, considered as a raw-material commodity. USGS distinguishes titanium sponge metal and downstream metal forms from titanium dioxide pigment and mineral concentrates. Sponge is one metal form, not the entire commodity. This record is distinct from the titanium element as a chemical identity, titanium-bearing minerals, titanium oxides and any particular alloy formulation or waste stream. The designations preserve the titanium-metal scope in the original EU 2024 lists. A culture-containing grinding study used titanium sponge alongside an incompletely identified titanium-industry waste feed and reported fine titanium-compound powders. Its pooled results do not establish sponge-specific recovery, microbial enhancement or production of pure titanium metal.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-containing ferric-solution grinding of titanium sponge feeds

other Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans (NCBITaxon:920) [TaxonMech]

Baranov and colleagues processed titanium sponge and a source-described titanium slag in a rotating disintegrator with ferric-sulfate solution containing Thiobacillus ferrooxidans. They obtained fine agglomerated powders reported to contain titanium oxides and hydride. This supports a culture-associated mechanical and chemical workflow; it does not isolate bacterial causality, demonstrate microbial titanium reduction, or establish a pure titanium-metal product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Figure 1 on page 132 shows sponge feeds labelled 0.1-1 mm and 0.1-3 mm, and a waste feed labelled titanium slag, 10-100 mm. The stated research tasks instead refer to titanium cut-offs. Waste provenance, grade and initial phase composition are not resolved, so the waste is not equated with a defined smelting slag or with sponge. Feed and bacterial solution were mixed 1:1 by mass. Reported product data are not assigned to separate sponge sizes or waste arms, and it is unclear whether the feeds were processed separately or combined. No strain accession, culture-purity check, inoculum amount, ferric concentration, nutrient recipe, pH, temperature, duration, rotation speed, vessel volume or replicate count is provided.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: baranov2013-sponge
Pages 132-134 document the feeds, culture-containing dispersing solution, repeated fragment collisions and recovered fine solids. The page 133 table reports average relative Ti contents of 95.84 percent in starting material and 98.14 percent in powder, with Cr, Fe and Ni also listed. These four-element compositions are not titanium extraction yields or metallic phase purity. Elvax light XRF does not account for oxygen or hydrogen here; the authors explicitly acknowledge missing gaseous impurities. No isolated masses or solution/solid mass balance is supplied.
Partial Source: baranov2013-sponge
The source reports oxide and hydride phases from DRON-4-07 diffraction but displays no diffractogram or phase fractions; it says unclear individual peaks prevented crystallite sizing. The title and some captions call the material titanium powder, while the results and conclusion identify titanium compounds and reserve pure titanium production for future work under an altered atmosphere. Neither a named oxide polymorph, hydride stoichiometry nor pure Ti(0) nanoparticle production is adopted.
Partial Source: baranov2013-sponge
Figure 2 SEM images show agglomeration; Figure 3 TEM images follow ultrasonic preparation and carry 100 nm and 500 nm scale bars. Figure 3's caption repeats the 2500-fold and 1000-fold labels used for Figure 2, so those nominal magnifications are not adopted as calibrated TEM measurements. The text estimates an average grain size not exceeding 150-200 nm and the conclusion gives 100-200 nm, without a counted size distribution, sampling error or yield of that size fraction. These estimates do not establish a uniformly nanoscale powder or a bulk crystallite size.
Partial Source: baranov2013-sponge
Pages 133-134 attribute redox-potential maintenance and acid regeneration to the bacteria, but provide no measured redox, acidity, culture-growth or iron-oxidation series. There is no matched sterile ferric-solution, acid-only or mechanical-only control. The paper's generic faster-leaching, low-cost and waste-free claims are therefore not demonstrated comparisons. Equipment throughput and deployment are not established; the proposed inert-atmosphere refinement is future work, not an experimentally demonstrated pure-metal recovery stage.
Context_only Source: ncbi-ferrooxidans
The authority maps the paper's historical species name to Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, NCBITaxon:920. This does not authenticate an unaccessioned stock or establish that a pure culture caused the observed material changes.
