Selenium element Critical Material

Selenium (Se), chemical element with atomic number 34. The experimental substrate is soluble selenite, distinct from the element identifier, a selenium mineral, or a secondary resource. Australia's dated selenium material designation is mapped to this element without extending it to salts.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Non-growing MR-1 selenite reduction with conditional FccA and CymA involvement

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 (NCBITaxon:211586) [TaxonMech]

Washed Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 cells transformed selenite to selenium-associated particles, with XPS supporting elemental Se in the analyzed cell fraction. Gene-deletion phenotypes support FccA and CymA involvement under the tested conditions; they do not prove an exclusive pathway or direct catalysis by purified FccA.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory cells grown in LB at 30 degrees C were washed twice and added at OD600 1.0 to 40 mL anaerobic mineral medium under nitrogen, with 0.5 mM selenite and 20 mM lactate at 30 degrees C. The medium contains HEPES/NaOH but the paper does not state a measured pH. Fumarate at 20 mM was added only in the separate competition assay. The authors report no growth and declining protein-based biomass. Kinetic rates refer to the initial 12 h; XPS sampled washed, lyophilized cells after 48 h. These are laboratory transformations, not demonstrated purified-product recovery or industrial deployment.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: li_2014
Figure 2 and Results report initial selenite-depletion rates of 0.54 +/- 0.13 micromol/h for delta-fccA and 1.35 +/- 0.05 for WT; the caption specifies standard errors of triplicate cultures. Figure 4 preserves substantial later residual reduction in delta-cymA. Thus reduced rates support involvement, not absolute necessity. No complementation or purified-FccA assay is reported.
Support Source: li_2014
Figure 5 shows predominantly intracellular and smaller surface particles. EDX establishes elemental composition; the 48 h Se 3d XPS peak supports the Se(0) assignment. Neither proves exclusive periplasmic localization or a phase-pure isolated product. Supernatant selenite was assayed by acid/ascorbate colorimetry; Table 1 provides a separate digestion/ICP-AES selenium balance, not an isolated recovery yield.
Support Source: li_2014_supplement
Figure S1 caption identifies autoclaved cells as the adsorption comparison; main Results report negligible adsorption. Figure S2 caption and S4 describe a BSA-equivalent Micro BCA protein assay for biomass, rather than a viable-cell count. Supplement captions and methods were read; embedded graph pixels were not rendered, so no supplementary numeric points are transcribed.
Partial Source: klonowska_2005
In a different LB growth context, the paper describes extracellular or cell-attached selenium deposits (Figure 1, 2 mM selenite) and reports that a cymA-deletion mutant still reduces selenite (data not shown). These observations limit universal localization and pathway claims. They are not a matched quantitative refutation of Li's rate defect. The older EDX assignment alone does not resolve selenium oxidation state.
