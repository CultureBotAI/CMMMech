Metal-rich solid burrs generated by machining, represented here by an industrial sample obtained in Mexico City and mechanically reduced before laboratory testing. This resource denotes machining offcuts rather than a particular alloy, elemental metal, smelting slag or coolant-trap grinding sludge. The sampled batch was iron-rich but its alloy and phase identities, facility, collection date and machining operation were not specified. Industrial origin does not establish industrial bioleaching deployment, and no criticality designation is inferred from its constituents.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Sulfur-oxidizing culture-associated leaching of machining burrs

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans (NCBITaxon:930) [TaxonMech]

A source-assigned Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans DSM 26636 culture incubated with industrial burrs and added elemental sulfur showed increased sulfate, titratable acidity and acidification relative to uninoculated controls. The study also reports limited metal mobilization after subtracting control losses. These observations support a culture-associated acid-generating treatment, with proton-mediated dissolution proposed by the authors. They do not establish a specific enzyme, direct microbial oxidation of the burr alloy, complete metal extraction or isolated-product recovery.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The 2020 original Sections 4.1-4.4, pages 7-9, describe burrs from an unnamed Mexico City industry, cut and crushed to 10-15 mm long and 1-2 mm wide. A four-day inoculum grown at pH 3, 30 degrees C and 150 rpm in modified Starkey medium plus 1% elemental sulfur was added as 3 mL to 30 mL medium in 125 mL flasks, with 0.33 g each of burrs and sublimed sulfur: nominal 1% w/v of each in 33 mL. Controls held 33 mL medium and the same solid additions without inoculum. Eighteen inoculated and nine control flasks were distributed across three destructive sampling times: six and three respectively every seven days over 21 days, at 30 degrees C and 150 rpm. Supernatants were centrifuged and filtered through 0.22 micrometre membranes; sulfate, NaOH-titrated acidity and pH were measured. End-point liquid aliquots were acid-digested and analyzed by ICP-OES. The method calls the chamber-counted inoculum 2 x 10^8 CFU/mL; chamber counting alone does not establish colony-forming viability, and no direct growth series in the burr assays is shown. The medium was autoclaved, but this does not establish sterilization of the burr feed or independent stock authentication. Sampling dates for native concentration observations were not supplied; quantitative observations are not encoded here.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: gomez_ramirez2020_burrs
Section 4.2 and Table 1 on page 8 establish the actual industrial burr feed and its measured elemental composition. Table 1 reports 410159.5 +/- 21163.2 mg/kg Fe and additional Cr, Si, Al, Mo, V, Mn, Mg, Ni, Cu, Sb, Li and Zn. It does not establish a named alloy or mineral phase; the unusually large Al dispersion is retained as source uncertainty rather than corrected. The introduction's generic machining examples do not identify the sampled factory's exact process. No coolant-sludge or slag result is transferred.
Support Source: gomez_ramirez2020_burrs
Figures 1-3 on pages 3-4 compare inoculated and uninoculated arms. Sulfate and titratable acidity rise with the culture while controls remain low; inoculated pH decreases while control pH increases. The plotted significance groups do not distinguish days 14 and 21 for these endpoints. Figure 3 labels its time axis in hours, whereas Sections 2.1 and 4.3 and Figures 1-2 specify days; the experimental duration follows the explicit 21-day methods and the conflicting axis is preserved as a source error. The abstract and Figure 2 indicate approximately 0.16 M acidity, while Results and Discussion quote 0.15 M. Neither titration nor sulfate alone identifies a particular enzyme or proves direct attack on metal.
Partial Source: gomez_ramirez2020_burrs
Figure 3 shows different starting pH values, approximately 2.5 in the inoculated arm and 3 in controls, consistent with unequal inoculum chemistry rather than a pH-matched causal comparison. Modified Starkey medium contains MgSO4, FeSO4, ammonium sulfate and added molybdate and was initially acidified with sulfuric acid. Control subtraction is described, but separate medium-only, washed-inoculum, pH-matched acid, killed-cell and sulfur-free arms are not reported. The experiment supports culture association and acidification, not unique attribution of all measured metal to burr dissolution or separation of acidity from other culture effects. The sulfate method designation in Section 4.3 also differs from the standard named by reference 36.
Partial Source: gomez_ramirez2020_burrs
Section 2.2 and Figure 4 on page 4 report small metal removal values, including 4658.5 mg/kg Fe and 63.2 mg/kg Mo, alongside source-labeled percentage values. Section 4.4 on page 9 describes a day-zero/day-21 concentration difference with abiotic-loss subtraction, but prints the percentage equation with control metal content in the numerator and metal removal in the denominator. This does not supply a coherent independently reproducible recovery calculation. The liquid-to-mg/kg normalization, individual control metal data, full residue and precipitate mass balance, analytical recovery and uncertainty propagation are not supplied. The reported concentrations are retained as source-defined assay outcomes, not validated extraction fractions or recovered-product yields; no replacement formula is silently imposed.
Partial Source: gomez_ramirez2020_burrs
Discussion page 6 proposes acid-mediated dissolution and mentions brown precipitates, including precipitation during NaOH titration. Their composition and metal content were not characterized; the suggested ferrous-sulfate assignment is not phase evidence. The oxidation equations are mechanistic proposals rather than direct speciation or molecular measurements. No selective metal recovery, product purity, economics, detoxification endpoint or industrial treatment was demonstrated. Suggested downstream biosorption or precipitation remains prospective.
Context_only Source: gomez_ramirez2021_context
The same group's 2021 original Sections 2-3, Figures 1-2 and Tables 1-2 describe two separately named and compositionally different Mexico City industrial feeds, crushed below 1000 micrometres: IW-steel and IW-scrap. The Al-rich IW-scrap arm did not acidify and its sulfate response was lower than IW-steel; this cautions against assuming uniform response across machining wastes. These feed identities and results are not assigned to the 2020 burr batch, and overlapping authors, strain and assay approach do not constitute independent replication. The 2021 paper has its own concentration, volume and textual inconsistencies; its percentages and proposed metal-toxicity mechanism are not imported as burr performance or demonstrated causal effects.
Context_only Source: ncbi_thiooxidans
The current NCBI species entry resolves Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans to NCBITaxon:930. This validates the species-level label only. DSM 26636 remains the original study's strain designation; the queried DSMZ catalogue route redirected to a generic search page and did not independently confirm the experimental stock or alias.
