Aluminium salt slag secondary resource

Salt-flux-bearing mixed slag from aluminium melting or remelting, containing process salts and oxide-rich solids. Separate studies examine a Brazilian scrap-remelting rotary-furnace batch and a salt-rich foundry slag attributed to NaCl/KCl flux. The latter contributes salts to a combined laboratory flux-regeneration and alloy-remelting trial. This resource is distinct from the existing post-metal-recovery secondary aluminium dross specimen, generic smelting slag, bauxite residue, elemental aluminium or a single mineral. Laboratory salt separation and a separate qualified reuse trial are documented; microbial action, inherited criticality and industrial deployment of these recipes are not.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Abiotic chloride-salt dissolution and evaporative crystallization

separation

Water dissolved chloride salts from crushed salt slag. Filtration and evaporation yielded NaCl/KCl-bearing crystals and a residue with lower salt content. The reported chloride-removal fraction is a head-assay normalized leachate measure, not aluminium recovery or the mass yield of saleable salt. Product characterization supports recovery of mixed salts with impurities. The paper reports a preliminary scaled solids and elemental balance; it does not measure an industrial throughput or a complete gas and water balance.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Section 2.1 sampled 481 kg comprising a rotary-furnace slag batch from a 14-tonne charge of manufacturing scrap, end-of-life scrap and primary aluminium dross, with reported NaCl:KCl flux ratio 3:1. After four days cooling, jaw crushing below 19 mm and staged homogenization/riffling, separate aliquots were roller crushed below 2.8 mm or ball milled below 0.5 mm. Section 2.2/Figures 2-3 used 70-160 g laboratory portions, distilled or tap water initially at pH 6.9-7.0, 25 degrees C and 55 rpm, with 20, 30 or 40 wt.% solids and sampling over two hours. The authors state duplicate tests; the listed 72 assays describe combinations, not 72 independent replicates of one condition. The selected arm used tap water, below-2.8-mm feed, 30 wt.% solids and 90 min. Filtered solids were dried at 50 degrees C for eight hours. The evaporation temperature is inconsistent: Section 2.2 specifies 85 degrees C for 24 h, while Figure 3 gives 50 degrees C for 24 h. No biological treatment was used.

Section 3.2/Figure 5b and page 9 report 91.2% chloride removal for the selected tap-water arm at 90 min; the same curve reaches 92.2% at 120 min. The abstract/conclusion greater-than-92% summary is therefore not accepted for the 90-minute selected condition. Equation 1 defines removal using normalized leachate/head-slag chloride concentrations. Figure 4 preserves a less favorable coarse-feed result: below-19-mm feed at 40 wt.% solids could not be agitated. Finer grinding offered limited improvement over roller crushing for more dilute pulps; those observations concern this feed only.
Support Source: teixeira2022
Section 3.3/Figures 8-11 identify NaCl and KCl in evaporated crystals and diminished salt peaks in the residual slag. Table 5 separately reports K, Na and Cl mass percentages of 8.78, 28.84 and 51.92 in recovered salts, and 0.24, 0.78 and 1.41 in residual slag. Thus salt-forming elements total about 90% in the crystals, with appreciable impurities; these percentages are composition, not recovery yield. The ED-XRF estimate of about 95.6% differs from titration/FAAS. The source proposes filtration/evaporation improvements and does not demonstrate remelting with this product.
Support Source: teixeira2022
Section 4/Figure 12 reports a preliminary balance scaled to the 481 kg furnace batch: 116.4 kg recovered salts and 364.6 kg leached residue, with 24.2% of feed mass dissolved. The authors estimate about 90% salt purity and 92% overall salt recovery using titration/FAAS analyses. Their elemental closure error is at most +0.15% for K using those assays, versus approximately -8% for K using ED-XRF. This reported solids/element balance is distinct from the Figure 5 chloride-removal measurement; it is not evidence that the laboratory recipe was operated on the entire 481 kg batch. The recovered NaCl:KCl ratio of 4.3:1 motivates proposed KCl addition before reuse, whose performance was not tested here.
Partial Source: teixeira2022
Tables 1 and 3 list high Al percentages in ED-XRF elemental compositions without oxygen; they do not quantify metallic-Al content, bulk alumina recovery or the aluminium mass balance. XRD phase non-detection does not demonstrate absence of hydrolyzable constituents or negligible gas emission: no direct gas balance is reported. Table 3's zero K entry is a non-detection, whereas Table 5 gives 0.24% K by another method, not evidence for zero potassium. Impurities below the stated approximately 5% XRD detection limit can remain despite only NaCl/KCl peaks in the recovered crystals. Figure 12 is a proposed flowsheet. Subsequent aluminium recovery, complete detoxification and industrial closed-loop flux reuse are outside the demonstrated experiment.

Abiotic salt regeneration and laboratory alloy remelting

recycling

Mechanical separation and water treatment of salt-rich foundry Slag No.1 yielded metallic pieces and a NaCl/KCl-bearing salt fraction. The salts were combined with products from a separate acid-treated, oxide-rich Slag No.2 and additional fresh ingredients to formulate a flux used in a laboratory remelting trial. A cast aluminium alloy was obtained. This demonstrates a qualified reuse step, not wholly waste-derived flux, pure aluminium production, a verified overall slag-to-metal yield or repeated industrial closed-loop operation.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Sections 2.1-2.3 and Table 1 distinguish Slag No.1, attributed to NaCl/KCl flux, from oxide-rich Slag No.2, attributed to MgCl2 flux. The foundry supplier and original metal-charge composition are not specified. Three consecutive samples from a batch were examined for compositional consistency; this does not establish independent process replicates. Crushing, screening and planetary milling separate metal from the oxide/salt fraction. Section 2.3.1 gives the No.1 water arm as 80 +/- 5 degrees C, 200 rpm and one hour, followed by filtration, evaporation at 90 degrees C and residue drying at 200 degrees C for two hours; its water-to-solid loading is not specified. The No.2 context arm instead gives 35 g in 100 mL of 35% HCl, whereas Section 3 states 1 M HCl. Section 3 also specifies 250 rpm and 30 min for dissolution, unlike the No.1 subsection. These accounts do not define one reconciled recipe. No organism or biological treatment was used.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: kulikov2026
Table 1 assigns Slag No.1 35.90 wt.% NaCl, 16.70 wt.% KCl and 14.40 wt.% metallic Al. Section 2.3.1, Figure 8 and Table 3 support separation of a NaCl/KCl-bearing evaporated product from an oxide-rich residue. The reported product entries, 64.50 wt.% NaCl and 35.50 wt.% KCl, are quantitative phase-analysis estimates, not recovery fractions or proof of absolute purity. Residue entries still include 3.29 wt.% NaCl and 1.70 wt.% KCl. Gas evolution was observed during treatment without a measured gas balance. Table 3 prints MgAl3O4 and SiO3 headings inconsistent with Figure 8 phase labels; those headings are not adopted as verified mineral identities.
Support Source: kulikov2026
Section 2.4/Figure 10 combines salt products from both slag arms with unquantified fresh raw-material additions. The designed flux contains 45.1% NaCl, 33.7% KCl and 21.2% MgCl2. Its approximately 650 degrees C melting point, 0.00195 Pa s viscosity and 1.592 g/cm3 density are calculated properties, not independent measurements of the regenerated product. The oxide-rich No.2 arm is retained as an input to this combined trial, not equated with the salt-rich No.1 feed or the Brazilian 2022 specimen.
Support Source: kulikov2026
Sections 2.5 and 3, Figures 11-12 and Table 6 describe briquetting separated metal and remelting a 500 g charge in a laboratory induction furnace. At 740 degrees C, 22 g regenerated flux with 5 wt.% NaF was added; the melt was held for ten minutes and stirred every three minutes. The reported cast product weighed 470 g and contained 95.500 wt.% Al and 3.430 wt.% Mg by optical emission analysis. Product composition and the remelting charge/product masses do not establish recovery of aluminium from the original slag. No matched no-flux or virgin-flux control, independent run count or serial-reuse experiment is reported. Figure 13 is a process scheme; the conclusion identifies laboratory testing and proposes continuous operation as future work.
Partial Source: kulikov2026
Section 2.5 reports 370 g metallic Al per kg initial slag, which cannot be reconciled with Table 1's metallic-Al entries of 14.40 and 19.33 wt.% without an unreported feed or denominator change. The headline 85% recovery, 80 +/- 5% mechanical separation and 95% target-component separation statements lack a closed, sample-resolved material balance here and are not accepted as measured overall yields. Source recipe conflicts further limit reproducibility. Underlying data are not publicly posted according to page 18. Proposed residue uses do not demonstrate detoxification, and neither industrial feed origin nor one remelting trial proves industrial deployment or repeated closed-loop recovery.
