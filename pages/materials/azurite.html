Azurite mineral

Azurite is the monoclinic copper hydroxycarbonate mineral Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2, distinct from malachite, calcite, generic copper carbonate and elemental copper. The September 2026 IMA list gives grandfathered status G and original-description year 1824. A laboratory bacterial study assigns a minor diffraction feature in a mixed precipitate to azurite; this does not establish a pure azurite product or a phase-specific copper recovery yield. No criticality designation is inherited from copper.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Azurite assignment in a bacterial-culture-associated mixed carbonate precipitate

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Sporosarcina soli (NCBITaxon:334736) [TaxonMech]

Kang and colleagues assigned azurite, malachite, calcite and copper carbonate in an XRD pattern of precipitate associated with the ureolytic isolate designated Sporosarcina soli B-22. The species mapping follows the paper and its cited sequence, while the B-22 versus B7-22 strain-label discrepancy is unresolved. Ureolysis, increasing pH and carbonate precipitation provide a proposed explanation; the experiment does not demonstrate a unique enzyme pathway or exclude matched chemical precipitation. The accepted azurite observation is qualitative and belongs to a mixed solid.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Bacteria were isolated from abandoned-mine soil at Imgi, Busan, Korea. Isolation BPU agar contained 3 g/L beef extract, 5 g/L peptone, 20 g/L urea, 10 g/L microagar and 100 micrograms/mL cycloheximide at pH 7, incubated at 30°C for three days. YA broth was 20 g/L yeast extract and 10 g/L ammonium sulfate, pH 7. The calcite-production protocol describes 24 h in YA followed by 72 h in BPU at 30°C and 200 rpm, then mixing 500 microlitres of bacterial suspension with 500 microlitres of 350 mM calcium chloride dihydrate solution. Figure 7 identifies its XRD material as B-22 grown in BPU, but the exact copper dose and correspondence to this generic calcite protocol are not fully specified. The microscopy/diffraction preparation placed suspension on glass and dried it at 50°C; SEM samples were water-resuspended, redried and platinum-coated. The separate YA copper-removal assay used synthetic copper salts, not a real mine-waste feed. This is a laboratory culture and processed-solid observation, not industrial mineral recovery.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: kang2016
Page 210 Figure 7 labels a small feature A as azurite alongside M (malachite), C (calcite) and U (copper carbonate). The SEM and XRD Results explicitly assign these phases. Figure 6 supplies morphology of calcium- and copper-carbonate-associated solids but cannot identify azurite by shape alone. No raw diffraction data, phase-fit uncertainty, quantitative azurite fraction or separately isolated azurite product is provided. The source’s abbreviated formula in prose omits a hydroxyl subscript; this record uses the authority formula and does not interpret that printing inconsistency as evidence for a different species.
Partial Source: kang2016
Methods p. 208 describe a separate YA assay with 5 microlitres of 12-hour culture added to 45 mL broth in a 100 mL flask, nominally 5 mM urea, 25 mM CaCl2 and 2 mM copper, at 30°C and 200 rpm for 48 h. Controls without added cells are stated for all experiments. Solution removal is calculated from initial and final supernatant ICP-OES concentrations after centrifugation, not from recovered mineral mass. Figure 8 and p. 211 instead describe 72 h and report 61.8% copper removal; the p. 211 dose is printed as 2 M, while the figure’s starting Cu concentration is about 270 mg/L, inconsistent with nominal 2 mM elemental Cu. These values are retained as source inconsistencies, not reconciled or assigned to azurite. Figure 7 uses BPU whereas Figure 8 and the removal protocol use YA.
Partial Source: kang2016
Figures 1–3 support the authors’ isolate assignment, urease activity and copper tolerance; Figure 8 shows increasing pH in the B-22 culture. However, an azurite-specific uninoculated diffraction comparison, matched-pH chemical control, urease-inhibited treatment and phase-resolved copper mass balance are not shown. The generic statement that controls were included is not equivalent to a reported azurite-specific control result. The distinct sand-column and EPS experiments in Figures 4–5 do not establish an azurite-specific contribution to immobilization.
Partial Source: genbank_kj485701
The accession cited for B-22 resolves to Sporosarcina soli B7-22 and NCBI taxon 334736, with soil as isolation source. This supports the species label but does not independently authenticate the differently labelled experimental isolate. A submission address is not treated as specimen geography. The paper reports 99% 16S similarity; species-level mapping does not remove the unresolved strain-label mismatch.

Causal Edges (3):
  • sporosarcina_soli_ureabc_complex → urea hydrolysis to ammonium and carbonate with culture pH increase (catalyzes) → bpu_ya_ureolytic_carbonate_alkalinity : Urease complex (UreABC activated by nickel-incorporation accessory GTPase UreG) in Sporosarcina soli B-22 hydrolyzes supplied urea in BPU/YA media, generating carbonate alkalinity and elevating culture pH.[kang2016]
  • bpu_ya_ureolytic_carbonate_alkalinity → alkaline carbonate/hydroxycarbonate co-precipitation in BPU culture (drives) → mixed_carbonate_precipitate_with_minor_azurite : Elevated pH and carbonate alkalinity in BPU-grown Sporosarcina soli B-22 culture promote precipitation of mixed calcium- and copper-carbonate solids containing a minor XRD feature assigned to azurite.[kang2016]
  • dissolved_copper_and_calcium_ions → co-precipitation of supplied copper and calcium into mixed carbonate solids (incorporates_into) → mixed_carbonate_precipitate_with_minor_azurite : Supplied dissolved Cu(II) and Ca(II) ions co-precipitate into the mixed B-22 carbonate solid containing XRD-assigned calcite, copper carbonate, malachite, and minor azurite.[kang2016]
