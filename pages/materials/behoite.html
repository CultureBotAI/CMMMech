Behoite mineral

Behoite (IMA 1969-031) is an IMA-accepted beryllium hydroxide mineral, the natural orthorhombic beta-Be(OH)2 polymorph, with IMA September 2026 Master List formula Be(OH)2. Described by Ehlmann and Mitchell (1970) from the Rode Ranch rare-earth pegmatite, Llano County, Texas, USA (and also recognized in hydrated Vitro-tuff at Honeycomb Hills, Juab County, Utah, USA), where it occurs as colorless, pseudo-octahedral crystals ({011} prism with {110} prism faces) in white to tan powdery alteration rinds replacing brown gadolinite in association with tengerite, bastnaesite, albite, and quartz. Orthorhombic (space group P212121), with a = 4.64(2) Å, b = 7.05(2) Å, c = 4.55(3) Å, Z = 4, SGmeas = 1.92, Dcalc = 1.94 g/cm3, Mohs hardness ~4, and biaxial (–) optics with alpha = 1.533(3), beta = 1.544(3), gamma = 1.548(3), and 2V = 82°.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Abiotic two-step endothermic DTA dehydroxylation of behoite

other

Two-step endothermic thermal dehydroxylation of natural orthorhombic beta-Be(OH)2 (behoite) to BeO and H2O, exhibiting initial endothermic inflections near 265 °C and 290 °C and a principal endothermic peak at 310 °C.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Abiotic laboratory differential thermal analysis (DTA) on Rode Ranch behoite using a DuPont 900 thermal analyzer in air at 20 °C/min; no microbial, enzymatic, or bioleaching mechanism was tested or reported in Ehlmann and Mitchell (1970).
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: ehlmann_1970_behoite
Differential thermal analysis (DTA) of Rode Ranch behoite on a DuPont 900 analyzer in air at 20 °C/min shows a two-step endothermic dehydroxylation reaction of beta-Be(OH)2 with inflections beginning at approximately 265 °C and 290 °C and reaching an endothermic peak at 310 °C (p. 7, Fig. 4).
