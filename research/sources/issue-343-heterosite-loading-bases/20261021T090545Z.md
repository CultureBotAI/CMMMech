## Assay and specimen crosswalk

Paper PDF3 specifies about 0.1 g solid digested with 2.5 mL 65% HNO3, 0.6 mL 30% HCl and 2.5 mL water, microwave heating to 230°C, then dilution to 50 mL. Fluid and digestion solutions use ICP-OES; selected digestion trace elements use ICP-MS. Thiosulfate-containing fluids are analysed in water because acid destabilizes the additive, with acknowledged possible underestimation of some metals. Analytical duplicates, standards and blank-derived 3σ LOD/10σ LOQ do not establish independent experimental replicates. Each experimental run included blanks.

The equation on PDF3 defines Q = (C0 − Cequ)V/m, allowing m to mean experimental or digested mass. It does not provide a complete per-arm calculation dictionary. Row 2 `mg/L` headings coexist with later `load [mg/g]` and `load [mmol/g]` blocks; importing all numbers under one unit would be incorrect. Sample IDs recur in converted and Kd blocks. Stable reuse keys therefore need workbook hash, worksheet, block, sample ID, analyte and assay role, not sample ID alone. Trailing `a`/`c` labels and the NDB kinetic starting-material row must be handled explicitly in joins.

## What can and cannot be recalculated

`workbook-audit.json` records 1,419 successful checks: 809 numeric mg/g-to-mmol/g conversions using the workbook's molar masses, 550 available Q/Cequ Kd ratios, 47 duplicated lithium loadings across paired figure sheets, and 13 synthetic-isotherm mass/volume ratios. These validate published downstream arithmetic within the specified blocks, not original instrument assays or the entire workbook's scientific correctness. Forty separately labelled diagnostics retain assumptions rather than replacing reported values.

For example, the synthetic isotherm's 0.5002 g in 250 mL and 8.05295 mg/L residual would imply aqueous Q of about 1.47 or 0.97 mg/g if one substitutes rounded initial concentrations of 11 or 10 mg/L. The reported digestion value is 1.31724 mg/g. The difference cannot select a corrected number without actual initial concentration, dilution, solid baseline and mass conventions. Similarly, Bruchsal row9 gives approximately 30.34 mg/g from nominal 158 mg/L and 200 mL, versus 26.7575 mg/g labelled digestion. Rounded feed values are not vial-specific measurements.

Combining paired kinetic residuals with published Q and experimental masses gives conditional implied C0 ranges of approximately 154.78–163.26 mg/L (Bruchsal) and 11.06–11.65 mg/L (synthetic, assuming 200 mL). These are inverse calculations, not recovered original initial assays or proof of a source error. The thesis's general instruction to sample each initial solution is compatible with variable actual concentrations but does not supply their missing values.

The decisive censoring joins are:

- Bruchsal `Fig. 10, Fig. 11a+b` J14:J18 are numeric 0, while matched `Fig. 12b` B14:B18 are `<LOQ`; associated Li Kd cells B53:B57 are absent.
- Synthetic `Fig. 10, Fig. 11c+d` L15:L20 are `<LOQ`, while matched `Fig. 12d` B15:B20 are numeric 0; associated Li Kd cells B60:B65 are absent.
- Bruchsal kinetic zero-sorbent blanks E20:E22/J20:J22 carry nonzero K20:K22 values in a load-labelled block. Synthetic kinetic E20:E22 are zero, F20:F22 absent, and K20:K22 nonzero. Their normalization cannot be deduced by dividing by zero or assuming an unstated reference mass. Synthetic-isotherm blank Li cells are absent, not measured zero uptake.

The paper calls the high-loading result >99% Li recovery (PDF16); Figure 11 and the workbook concern solution residuals and sorbent loadings. To verify >99% solution depletion, an original-fluid residual bound must be below 0.01C0: 1.58–1.60 mg/L for the rounded Bruchsal inputs, or 0.10–0.11 mg/L for the rounded synthetic inputs. Workbook LOD/LOQ headings alone do not settle original-fluid versus analytical dilution bases. No isolated-product mass, product purity or closed material balance follows from these thresholds, and exact-zero/100% recovery is not established.
