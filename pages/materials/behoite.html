Behoite mineral

Behoite (IMA 1969-031) is an IMA-accepted beryllium hydroxide mineral, the natural orthorhombic beta-Be(OH)2 polymorph, with IMA September 2026 Master List formula Be(OH)2. Described by Ehlmann and Mitchell (1970) from the Rode Ranch rare-earth pegmatite, Llano County, Texas, USA (and also recognized in hydrated Vitro-tuff at Honeycomb Hills, Juab County, Utah, USA), where it occurs as colorless, pseudo-octahedral crystals ({011} dome with {110} prism faces) in white to tan powdery alteration rinds replacing brown gadolinite in association with tengerite, bastnaesite, albite, and quartz. Semiquantitative spectrographic analysis by A. S. McCreath and Son (Table 1, p. 4) shows Be as the major cation with Si (1–10%), Fe (0.3–3%), and minor/trace elements. The authors observed minor clay impurities and did not establish silicon or iron as essential behoite constituents. Orthorhombic (space group P212121), with a = 4.64(2) Å, b = 7.05(2) Å, c = 4.55(3) Å, Z = 4, measured natural specific gravity SGmeas = 1.92 ± 0.01 (cited synthetic measured density 1.924), Mohs hardness ~4, and biaxial (–) optics with alpha = 1.533(3), beta = 1.544(3), gamma = 1.548(3), and 2V = 82°.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Abiotic two-step DTA thermal decomposition of behoite

other

Two-step thermal decomposition of natural orthorhombic beta-Be(OH)2 (behoite) recorded by unplotted DuPont 900 differential thermal analysis (DTA, 20 °C/min in air), described in the p. 7 thermal-data section as a two-step endothermic reaction with inflections beginning near 265 °C and 290 °C and peaking at 310 °C (whereas the p. 1 abstract refers to the 310 °C DTA feature as an exothermic peak; reaction products were not separately characterized in the DTA paragraph).

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Abiotic laboratory differential thermal analysis (DTA) on Rode Ranch behoite using a DuPont 900 thermal analyzer in air at 20 °C/min; no microbial, enzymatic, or bioleaching mechanism was tested or reported in Ehlmann and Mitchell (1970).
