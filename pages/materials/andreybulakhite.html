Andreybulakhite mineral IMA: Adb

Andreybulakhite (IMA 2023-037, symbol Adb) is an IMA-accepted hydrated nickel oxalate mineral of the humboldtine group, the first natural nickel oxalate, with September 2026 IMA Master List ideal formula Ni(C2O4)(H2O)2 (status A, PDF p. 11; written Ni(C2O4)·2H2O in Vereshchagin et al. 2025, p. 63) and holotype empirical formula (Ni0.63Cu0.27Mg0.08Co0.02)Sigma1.00(C2O4)·2H2O on the basis of (Ni + Cu + Mg + Co) = 1 apfu (Table 1, p. 65). Energy-dispersive X-ray microprobe analysis (5 spots at 20 kV, 0.5 nA, defocused 5 um spot; Table 1, p. 65) gave NiO 26.21 (range 24.87–27.09), CuO 11.99 (9.69–13.74), MgO 1.86 (0.96–2.42), CoO 0.72 (0.46–1.00), stoichiometric C2O3 40.15 (39.67–40.48), stoichiometric H2O 20.09 (19.85–20.25), total 101.03 wt% (100.37–102.07 wt%), with Fe below the detection limit in all analyses (ideal Ni(C2O4)·2H2O: NiO 40.88, C2O3 39.42, H2O 19.70, total 100.00 wt%). Described by Vereshchagin, Britvin, Pankin, Zelenskaya, Krzhizhanovskaya, Kuz'mina, Vlasenko, and Frank-Kamenetskaya (2025) from the Nyud-II (Nud-II) magmatic Cu–Ni sulfide deposit, Monchegorsk layered mafic–ultramafic pluton (Monchepluton), Kola Peninsula, Russia (67°53'14" N, 32°54'4" E; holotype deposited at the Mineralogical Museum of the Department of Mineralogy, Saint Petersburg State University, catalogue number ML OF-978), where it occurs in the upper part of the fruiting bodies (apothecia) of the saxicolous crustose lichen Lecanora cf. polytropa overgrowing heavily weathered, millimeter-scale sulfide veinlets (~85 vol% pyrrhotite, 10 vol% pentlandite, 5 vol% chalcopyrite) hosted in orthopyroxenite/norite (~70 vol% plagioclase/anorthite and 30 vol% enstatite–ferrosilite pyroxene) together with secondary nickel sulfates morenosite (NiSO4·7H2O) and nickelhexahydrite (NiSO4·6H2O) (pp. 63–65, Fig. 1). Named in honor of Russian mineralogist Prof. Andrey Glebovich Bulakh (1933–2020). Forms tiny colorless to pale green, transparent platy to prismatic (including box-shaped / hopper-like) crystals up to 2 x 1 x 1 um in aggregates up to 40 x 30 x 30 um intergrown with fungal hyphae inside the lichen apothecia (Fig. 2), with vitreous lustre, no UV fluorescence, estimated Mohs hardness ~1.5–2 by analogy with humboldtine-group oxalates, Dcalc = 2.420 g/cm3 (for the empirical formula), and calculated Gladstone–Dale mean refractive index 1.594 (p. 65). Monoclinic, space group C2/c (Z = 4, alpha-Ni(C2O4)·2H2O structure type with chain layers along [010] of [NiO4(H2O)2] octahedra cross-linked by planar [C2O4]2- ions and hydrogen bonds; Fig. 3, p. 69); powder X-ray diffraction on the natural holotype (CoKalpha, lambda = 1.79021 Å; Table 2, p. 65, and Table 5, p. 69) gives a = 11.8392(5) Å in the abstract and text (printed as 11.8393 in Table 5, p. 69), b = 5.3312(2) Å, c = 9.8357(7) Å, beta = 126.723(5)°, V = 497.59(3) Å3, with three strongest diffraction lines [d in Å (I%) (hkl)] at 4.743 (100)(-200, -202), 3.946 (24)(002), and 2.954 (29)(-402). Characteristic Raman bands (Table 3, pp. 63–64, 69–70; 532 nm laser) occur at 3391 (O–H stretching), 1701 (nu_s(C=O)), 1621 (nu_as(C=O)), 1454 (nu_s(C–O) + nu(C–C)), 924 (nu_s(C–O) + delta(O–C=O)), 597 (H2O librations), 550 (delta(O–C=O)), 307, and 226 cm-1 (Ni–O stretching/bending), and FTIR absorption bands (Table 4, pp. 64, 70–71) occur at 3389, 1640, 1357, 1315, and 818 cm-1. Distinct from humboldtine (Fe(C2O4)·2H2O), moolooite (Cu(C2O4)·nH2O), glushinskite (Mg(C2O4)·2H2O), lindbergite (Mn(C2O4)·2H2O), katsarosite (Zn(C2O4)·2H2O), and elemental nickel; no criticality designation or industrial recovery yield is asserted.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Natural biomineralization of andreybulakhite in Lecanora cf. polytropa apothecia coupled with oxidative Ni2+/Fe3+ weathering fractionation

biomineralization Microbial

Vereshchagin et al. (2025, pp. 63–65, 71–72, Figs. 1–2, Table 1) document that natural andreybulakhite crystallizes in the upper part of the fruiting bodies (apothecia) of the crustose saxicolous lichen Lecanora cf. polytropa overgrowing weathered pyrrhotite–pentlandite–chalcopyrite veinlets at the Nyud-II deposit. Oxalic acid excreted by the lichen mycobiont reacts with divalent cations (Ni2+, Cu2+, Mg2+, Co2+) released during oxidative weathering of primary sulfides and transient secondary sulfates (morenosite and nickelhexahydrite), precipitating platy to prismatic and hopper-like (Ni0.63Cu0.27Mg0.08Co0.02)(C2O4)·2H2O crystals intergrown with fungal hyphae. Despite growing directly on Fe-rich pyrrhotite (~85 vol% of the sulfide veinlets), andreybulakhite contains no detectable Fe (0.00 wt% across all 5 EDX analyses in Table 1, p. 65), which the authors attribute to natural geochemical fractionation during acidic sulfide oxidation (pH < 4): Fe2+ oxidizes to Fe3+ and either hydrolyzes as insoluble ferric oxyhydroxides or forms stable soluble ferric tris-oxalate complexes [Fe(C2O4)3]3-, whereas Ni2+ remains mobile as aquo/sulfate species until precipitating as nickel oxalate at higher pH within the lichen apothecia.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Field and mineralogical observation of holotype specimen ML OF-978 from the Nyud-II Cu–Ni sulfide deposit, Monchegorsk pluton, Kola Peninsula, Russia (Vereshchagin et al. 2025, pp. 63–65, 71–72). The host lichen was identified morphologically as Lecanora cf. polytropa (open nomenclature cf., without molecular barcoding or isolate accession, so no species-level NCBITaxon CURIE is assigned), and no bulk nickel recovery yield or engineered bioleaching rate is reported.

Laboratory aqueous precipitation of the synthetic alpha-Ni(C2O4)·2H2O anthropotype of andreybulakhite

other

To obtain a single-phase structural and spectroscopic reference standard ("anthropotype") for andreybulakhite, Vereshchagin et al. (2025, pp. 66–71, Tables 3–5, Figs. 4–5) synthesized monoclinic alpha-Ni(C2O4)·2H2O by aqueous precipitation at room temperature (23–25 °C) and pH 6.0–7.2 (adjusted with NaOH) from 500 mL distilled water containing dissolved NiCl2·6H2O and Na2C2O4 (atomic ratio Ni/C2O4 varied from 1:1 to 5:1, oxalate concentration 0.01 M). After aging the solution with precipitate for 5 days, filtering, washing with distilled water, and drying at room temperature, powder X-ray diffraction (Table 5, p. 69) confirmed pure monoclinic C2/c alpha-Ni(C2O4)·2H2O with a = 11.837(6) Å, b = 5.343(1) Å, c = 9.914(3) Å, beta = 127.14(4)°, and V = 499.9(3) Å3, matching the PXRD, Raman (Table 3, p. 70), and FTIR (Table 4, p. 70) spectra of natural andreybulakhite.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Abiotic laboratory synthesis in 500 mL aqueous NiCl2·6H2O + Na2C2O4 solutions at 23–25 °C and pH 6.0–7.2 over 5 days reported by Vereshchagin et al. (2025, pp. 66–71, Tables 3–5).
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: vereshchagin_2025_andreybulakhite
Pages 66–71 and Tables 3–5 report the room-temperature aqueous synthesis protocol (NiCl2·6H2O + Na2C2O4 in 500 mL distilled water at 23–25 °C, pH 6.0–7.2, 5 days aging) and the resulting monoclinic C2/c unit cell (a = 11.837(6) Å, b = 5.343(1) Å, c = 9.914(3) Å, beta = 127.14(4)°, V = 499.9(3) Å3), Raman bands, and FTIR bands of the synthetic anthropotype.
