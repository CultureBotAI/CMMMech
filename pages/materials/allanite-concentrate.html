Allanite concentrate primary resource

A geological rare-earth-bearing feed represented by the REE-concentrated crushed allanite ore supplied as Zappall from WRE in Schmitz et al. (2025). The paper also calls this material allanite mineral sand. Its generic allanite designation does not establish a particular allanite species, phase purity, mine of origin or commercial grade. This concentrated primary resource is distinct from an ideal mineral species, elemental rare earths, their commodities and secondary waste. No constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-generated acidic biolixiviant mobilizes rare earths

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Gluconobacter oxydans (NCBITaxon:442) [TaxonMech]

Gluconobacter oxydans B58 and engineered derivatives generated acidic biolixiviants that mobilized rare earths from this concentrate in laboratory assays. Clean deletion of pstS combined with the P112 promoter upstream of mgdh increased solution extraction compared with the wild-type culture under the reported conditions. The evidence establishes a genotype-associated leaching difference, not a uniquely demonstrated acid composition, phosphate signaling pathway, pure-mineral dissolution fraction or isolated REE product. Cells are not documented as removed before ore addition; this is not described here as a cell-free supernatant assay. Industrial deployment was not tested.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
B58 cultures were grown in yeast-peptone-mannitol medium at 30 degrees C. After saturation and back-dilution, 3 mL culture and 3 mL 40% filter-sterilized glucose were combined in 100 mL flasks, giving a reported final 20% glucose. Methods describe 24 h biolixiviant production at 30 degrees C and 250 rpm, while figure labels report pH at 36 h; this timing discrepancy is unresolved. The usual leaching assay added 500 mg concentrate to 5 mL biolixiviant (10% mass/volume pulp density), followed by 24 h at room temperature and 200 rpm; a separate comparison used reported 1% pulp density. Exact room temperature and ore size distribution are not supplied. Leachates were settled, centrifuged, filtered at 0.45 micrometres, diluted into nitric acid and measured by ICP-MS. The printed 500 mL filtration aliquot conflicts with the preceding 1 mL sample and is retained as an unresolved source unit error, not silently corrected. Main leaching comparisons used triplicates; their preparation includes splitting an upstream culture, so independence of biological culture replicates is not established. Wild-type and other mutants were comparators; Data S1 includes no-bacteria controls. A separate Figure S2 comparison used 0.1 g ore in 1 mL lixiviant at 1500 rpm for 24 h and included medium-only and neat nitric acid arms. It must not be pooled with the 200 rpm flask assay.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: schmitz2025
Figures 1-3 and Results support increased solution extraction for selected engineered cultures relative to wild-type, with the combined pstS deletion and P112:mgdh strain performing best among those tested. The reported 53.1% improvement at 10% pulp density and 73.1% at 1% are relative increases over wild-type, not absolute ore recovery percentages. Figure 3 displays much smaller extraction fractions. No isolated product, closed process mass balance or industrial performance was established.
Partial Source: schmitz2025_data1
All Data rows 23-25 contain no-bacteria controls with much higher pH and lower dissolved REE totals than the cultures. They support a culture-associated effect but do not isolate acidity, secreted composition and cell contact using matched-pH or matched-metabolite controls. Figure 1 does not plot these rows.
Support Source: schmitz2025_data2
Figures 2A and 2B rows 2-4 and 20-22 record the wild-type and combined-mutant triplicates. Cell S22 calculates fractional improvement relative to the wild-type mean; this confirms the relative denominator. Other promoter and deletion combinations have smaller or absent additional effects. Leachate composition similarities in Figure 2C do not demonstrate selective separation of rare earths from all co-leached elements.
Partial Source: schmitz2025_data3
Figure 3 sheet compares source-labelled 1% and 10% arms and calculates extraction using its mineral-sand row 21 as denominator. Its total omits Er and Sc, whereas other datasets include different element sets; it is not a universal total-REE definition. Calculation sheets also contain other pulp-density and volume combinations whose mapping to the main plotted arm is not fully explained. Absolute yields and new normalized measurements are therefore not promoted to structured observations.
Partial Source: schmitz2025_supplement
Figure S1 records longer lag phases of pst deletion mutants in a separate plate growth assay. Figure S2 shows substantially greater REE extraction by neat nitric acid than by the engineered biolixiviant under its own vortexer conditions. This is counterevidence to general superiority over chemical leaching, not refutation of the narrower genotype comparison. Figures S3-S4 are qualitative sausage exposure demonstrations, not toxicological validation or a process life-cycle assessment.
Partial Source: schmitz2025_data6
Figure S2 source sheets contain four rows per treatment, distinct from the main-text triplicates. They include medium-only, wild-type, combined-mutant and nitric-acid arms. The Panel B genotype label omits the pstS deletion while the corresponding Panel A label includes it; the main figure identifies the combined mutant. The discrepancy is retained, and no pooled quantitative selectivity or product-purity claim is made.
Context_only Source: schmitz2025
The Discussion explicitly leaves unresolved whether engineering changed biolixiviant concentration or composition and presents pho regulation and oxygen-transfer explanations as hypotheses. Prior-study phosphor/catalyst economics and author commercialization interests are not deployment evidence for this allanite feed. The related 2023 preprint represents the same study, not independent replication.
