Cobalt-rich ferromanganese crusts

Naturally occurring layered iron-manganese oxide and oxyhydroxide deposits on exposed marine rock surfaces, commonly seamounts. This resource identity covers the cobalt-bearing, predominantly hydrogenetic crusts formed by accumulation from seawater; it does not denote every hydrothermal encrustation. The heterogeneous crust material is distinct from sediment-hosted polymetallic nodules, constituent cobalt or manganese elements, a pure mineral species, and a processing residue. Maciąg et al. characterize crusts and their altered basalt interfaces from Dirck Hartog Ridge; their sample-specific mineral mixtures are not a universal formula or grade. Collection and physical preparation do not make this geological resource secondary. The laboratory mechanism below records conditional redistribution of cobalt and nickel in whole-crust microcosms; it does not establish a specific receiving phase, a purified metal product or industrial deployment. The resource inherits no constituent criticality.

Rutile

Rutile is the mineral species with IMA-approved formula TiO2. The September 2026 IMA list gives grandfathered status G and original description year 1803; the year is not an IMA approval identifier. This mineral is distinct from elemental titanium, traded titanium products, rutile-bearing concentrates and other TiO2 polymorphs or manufactured TiO2 nanoparticles. The curated primary experiment calls its electrode coating natural rutile and reports an average mineral diameter of 40 micrometres, but does not supply a specimen locality, bulk chemical purity or diffraction-based phase-purity determination for that coating. Its observed role is light-assisted electron transfer supporting microbial iron cycling in a laboratory reactor, not demonstrated transformation of rutile or recovery of titanium. No criticality designation is inherited from a constituent element.

Titanium metal

Titanium in metallic commercial forms, considered as a raw-material commodity. USGS distinguishes titanium sponge metal and downstream metal forms from titanium dioxide pigment and mineral concentrates. Sponge is one metal form, not the entire commodity. This record is distinct from the titanium element as a chemical identity, titanium-bearing minerals, titanium oxides and any particular alloy formulation or waste stream. The designations preserve the titanium-metal scope in the original EU 2024 lists. A culture-containing grinding study used titanium sponge alongside an incompletely identified titanium-industry waste feed and reported fine titanium-compound powders. Its pooled results do not establish sponge-specific recovery, microbial enhancement or production of pure titanium metal.
