## Four protocol boundaries and source wording

PDF 3 §1.3.2 describes staged charging at 1:1 positive/negative volumes, removal of the first positive V5 fraction, replacement with aged feed, a second charge, removal of the second positive fraction and return of the saved first fraction. Removed fractions lack a complete material ledger. Direct electrolysis starts 100 mL aged per compartment, removes positive fluid after charging and transfers 50 mL of negative fluid into the positive side for testing. Table 2 explicitly limits restoration to negative fluid while positive valence rises.

The acid arm instead uses 100 mL aged negative and 100 mL 3 M acid positive fluid. It removes positive fluid, replaces the corroded positive collector and graphite felt, reassembles, then transfers50mLnegative fluid for testing. Hardware is therefore changed before its capacity comparison. Table 2's acid advantage wording calls positive fluid electrolyte, while methods specify acid; the source phrase is preserved, not silently corrected. Claims that all fluid can be restored are not a measured whole-feed balance. The asymmetric beaker arrangement uses large felt negative/small collector positive, comparison to new electrolyte through a salt bridge, 5 A until <0.01 V difference and approximately 5 h for 100 mL; no actual integrated current/time, energy or metal balance is supplied.

A further printed §1.1.3 phrase repeats V3 for both standard curves despite V3/V4 channels elsewhere. These are original-source wording conflicts, not confirmed defects in the current qualified owner. Figures carry plotted observations, but no accessible supplementary table gives raw cycle IDs, arm assignments, current/time integrals, replicates or error definitions. Reuse of a control comparison across panels cannot be treated as independent experimental replication.
