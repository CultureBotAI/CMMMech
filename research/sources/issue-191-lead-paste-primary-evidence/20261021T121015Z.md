## What the accessible evidence does and does not establish

The [main abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12153311/) distinguishes a laboratory gas-lift anglesite arm from a mixed spent-paste arm, with hydrogen and added sulfate or sulfur. Its rates use different feed numerators:19kgPbSO4m−3day−1 versus up to17kgpastem−3day−1. The quoted phase outcomes describe crystalline lead phases in sludge:>98%PbS with1–2%Pb for anglesite; estimated>96%PbS with1–2%Pb and1–2%PbO2 for paste. The seven source quantities, including ten numeric components, are preserved as abstract-only tokens. They are not independently verified table cells, whole-feed conversions or isolated-metal yields.

The main methods are still required to determine paste supplier/batch, measured total lead and sulfur, phase-quantification basis, moisture/inert fractions, reactor/working volume, slurry concentration, residence/retention times, temperature, pH and medium composition. Sulfate-reducing and sulfur-reducing functional labels do not supply strain identities or an authenticated community profile. Inoculum origin, adaptation, biomass dose and growth state remain unresolved. No live-versus-sterile/killed or matched sulfur/acid control is assumed present or absent from the abstract. Replicate definitions and assay uncertainty remain unknown.

A product phase percentage needs a quantified phase basis, representative product sampling and harvested mass before it can enter a lead balance. The feed's lead inventory must be reconciled with dissolved lead, residual feed, retained/wasted reactor solids, fines, sampling losses and any downstream product. Sulfur inputs from feed, sulfate and elemental sulfur, together with sulfide, sulfate, residual sulfur and off-gas, require their own accounting. Rates, selected crystalline-phase proportions and a proposed electrowinning step cannot by themselves close either balance. No conditional recovery calculation or source-number correction is invented here.
