## Accepted experimental scope

The assay uses whole, EPS-producing one-week cultures rather than a purified polymer or a demonstrated active-growth treatment. Cultures are pretreated inside 12–14kDa dialysis tubing with 0.1 M acid or base. Medium-only controls undergo the same pretreatment. Smaller bags contact the liquids at 1:10 biosorbent:effluent volume ratio, 25 °C, 200 rpm and 24 h in 100 mL glass cylinders; the cylinder capacity does not specify working volume. ICP-OES residual solution concentrations support control-corrected specific uptake per gram dry biomass. Drying at 105 °C for 4 h is analytical dry-weight determination, not preparation of dried sorbent. Post-treatment viability and axenicity are unreported.

Table 1 identifies differing pH and dissolved metal concentrations. The original acidic Cu bath failed preliminary biosorption/neutralization attempts. Its later 4:1 mixture withNi(1) gave pH 2.41, 598 mg/L Ni and 6 mg/L Cu. Ni(2) is separately supplied, pH 4.11 and 386.94 mg/L Ni. Table S2 reports 16Som2 Ni(2) uptake 168.81, 162.87 and 178.24 mg/g DW (2.88, 2.77 and 3.04 mmol/g). This conditional biosorption observation is supported; it is not a percentage feed recovery, isolated product or industrial treatment yield. The source's no-significant-Cu-removal interpretation and small nonzero SI entries are both retained. Nd in the feed table and missing na entries in the SI are not converted to zero.

## Contradictions and causal limits

Section 3.1 assigns HCl to Ni(1)+Cu/Ni(2), NaOH to Pd/Au; section 3.2 later calls Ni/Cu pretreatment alkaline. The 2024 source describes acid duration/washes only. An analogous NaOH protocol cannot be invented. Final pH varies among arms; a matched pH time course is unavailable.

PDF Figures 3–4 axes/captions use micromol/g DW, whereas XML/HTML captions use mmol/g DW. Table S3 lists 16Som2 Pd 8.14, 8.09 and 0 mg/g; all three do not reproduce the main 76.2±0.3 micromol/g summary. The zero is not silently discarded. S1 CE4 and S3 Vi22M each have a missing row despite nominal triplicates. The broad section 3.2 Cu/Zn <20 micromol/g statement conflicts with higher SI cells. These are retained source conflicts, not resolved by recalculating a preferred headline estimate.

EPS binding, competition and anionic functional-group explanations remain proposed. No EPS-specific intervention, direct real-feed metal localization/oxidation-state measurement or metabolism test separates the molecular pathways. Medium-only comparators account for some abiotic losses without proving every removed metal is intracellular or bound to a particular ligand. No real-feed desorption, product purity, complete metal balance, reuse-cycle result or plant deployment is demonstrated.
