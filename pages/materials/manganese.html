Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Mn(II) and complexed Mn(III) oxidation by Pseudomonas putida GB-1

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Pseudomonas putida GB-1 (NCBITaxon:76869)

Growing GB-1 cultures converted defined soluble Mn substrates to filter-retained oxidized Mn. Mn(III)-citrate and Mn(III)-desferrioxamine B (DFOB) supported oxide accumulation, with lower and slower accumulation from the strongly complexed DFOB substrate.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory triplicates used 5 mL Minimal Media A with HEPES at pH 7.8, 30 degrees C, darkness, and 100 micromolar Mn supplied as MnCl2, Mn(III)-citrate, or Mn(III)-DFOB. Sterile medium and a GB-1 triple Mn-oxidase knockout were negative controls. Table 4 reports final GB-1 particulate oxide concentrations of 79.8 +/- 2.0 micromolar after 96 h from Mn(II), and 54.3 +/- 2.9 micromolar after 168 h from Mn(III)-DFOB. The table does not define the reported error statistic. These are LBB oxidizing equivalents calculated assuming particulate MnO2, not identification of a crystalline mineral or recovery of a purified product. No industrial extraction or recovery operation was tested.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: wright2018
Culture conditions, Mn(III)-L oxidation methods, Fig. 1 and Table 4 support these assays. Mutants retaining MnxG or McoA oxidized Mn(III) complexes; this is genetic support, not purified-enzyme evidence.
Partial Source: wright2018
Fig. 3 and Discussion show abiotic Mn(III)-DFOB formation, so its accumulation alone is not proof of microbial oxidation. MopA activity was limited in darkness; the discussion reports stronger activity in light in separate KG_mopA cell-free-extract experiments. These findings limit universal enzyme and environmental extrapolation.
