Spent sulfuric-acid-production catalyst secondary resource

Used catalyst from sulfur-dioxide oxidation in sulfuric-acid production, represented here by vanadium-bearing material from a copper-smelter acid unit. This process-defined secondary resource is distinct from petroleum hydrodesulfurization catalysts, automotive converters, fresh catalyst and its constituent elements or mineral phases. The selected silica-rich feed does not define every spent acid-plant catalyst. No constituent-element criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Loading-dependent culture-associated vanadium mobilization

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans (NCBITaxon:930) [TaxonMech]

Living Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans DSM 9463 culture with elemental sulfur was associated with increased filtered-liquid vanadium at low catalyst loading, together with acidification and increasing sulfate. Higher-loading experiments lacked the same advantage over uninoculated controls. The evidence supports a conditional laboratory solubilization observation, without establishing direct enzymatic vanadium reduction, isolated vanadium recovery or industrial deployment.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Mikoda et al. (2020) studied actual spent catalyst from a copper-smelter sulfuric-acid unit: approximately 10-mm granules were comminuted and sieved. Main Methods name 100- and 200-micrometre sieves, whereas supplementary Figure S1 names a 250-micrometre sieve; the fraction used for each bioleaching arm is not explicitly assigned. Table 3 reports 65.5 wt% SiO2 and 5.50 wt% V2O5 on an oxide-composition basis, not pure vanadium oxide or a universal feed composition. Culture DSM 9463 was supplied by DSMZ, pregrown in mineral medium with 1% w/v sulfur to reported pH 1.7, and added at 2% v/v. Assays used fresh mineral medium initially adjusted to pH 2.5 with sulfuric acid, 2% w/v added elemental sulfur, 28 degrees C and 100 rpm. Materials were autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 15 minutes. The paper names 50-cm3 flasks but does not clearly specify working liquid volume. Pulp densities were 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20%, with at least duplicate biotic and uninoculated batches sampled on days 1, 7, 14, 21 and 28. Pulp-density percentages are retained as source labels rather than silently assigned an unstated basis. Liquid aliquots were filtered at 0.22 micrometres, diluted and acidified for ICP-MS total-element V analysis; the stated quantification limit was 0.001 mg/L. No pH-trajectory-matched sterile acid control was reported.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: mikoda_2020
Results 3.2, Figure 7 and Perspectives 3.3 assign day-21 liquid V release of 93% to 1% pulp density and 85.5% to 2%, versus at most 17% in the corresponding uninoculated series. These source percentages normalize filtered-liquid V to the initial feed V inventory; they are not isolated product yields. Lower culture pH and increasing sulfate in Figure 9 support culture-associated acidification, without separating the effects of cells from their evolving solution chemistry.
Partial Source: mikoda_2020
The abstract instead attributes 93% to 2% pulp density. Results 3.2 also labels both day-28 values, 80% and 74%, as 1%, while Figure 7 distinguishes the two arms. These inconsistencies remain unresolved. The low-loading liquid V inventory declined after day 21. At 5-20% pulp density, Figure 8 shows decreasing liquid V with controls often exceeding inoculated arms; no universal microbial advantage or monotonic extraction is established. Oxygen-transfer limitation and precipitation are proposed explanations, not independently isolated causes.
Partial Source: mikoda_2020_supplement
Figures S2-S3 show a qualitative sulfur-settling transfer assay and changing solution color. These are activity/speciation clues, not cell counts, quantified survival, or direct measurement of V oxidation state. Figure S1's sieve description conflicts with main Methods. The observed colors cannot independently establish a microbial V(V)-reduction pathway.
Partial Source: mikoda_2020
Figures 1-2 and Table 3 describe mixed feed and residual phases; disappearance of XRD peaks and lower residual-solid V concentration do not supply residue mass or a complete V balance. Color and the pH/Eh comparison in Figure 6 do not directly identify dissolved V species. Kinetic fits use selected incomplete time series to improve R-squared values (Methods 2.5), so they do not demonstrate a unique rate-controlling mechanism. Separate reagent-grade citric-acid leaching is abiotic, under different temperature, duration and vessel conditions; it is not evidence that DSM 9463 produced citrate.
Context_only Source: mikoda_residues
This related same-group study explicitly follows the earlier work and repeats its raw/residue oxide compositions. Its 2%-pulp-density 93% claim does not resolve the earlier arm conflict or establish independent replication. The approximately 96% statement compares solid V concentrations, without residue-mass accounting for a recovered yield. The separate SPLP assay (1 g residue, 20 mL pH-4.2 solution, 18 hours) reports bioleached-residue V and Al of 6.4 and 4.8 mg/L in Table 3; residual leachability remains. These observations do not demonstrate a metal-free or universally safe residue, a regulatory determination, validated concrete use, or a recovered vanadium product.
