## Later structure context and unresolved contradictions

The Handbook cell is 15.870, 8.010, 22.256 Å, beta 116°26′, P21/m, Z 1, while its formula and calculated density remain Na6Mg[B6O7(OH)6]4·10H2O and 1.91 g/cm3. Grice 1999 Table 2 independently prints the same three lengths and 116.43°, but P21/c. Its p. 744 text calls the 1973 report preliminary and says complete structural details were unavailable; the topology discussion is not a new atom-site determination. Its bibliography p. 760 lists all three authors through continuation marks and volume 60; the discussion cites only Dal Negro and Ungaretti. The Handbook lists only two authors and volume 69. The IMA row also prints 69, while fresh Crossref says 60. The inaccessible 1973 original is still required to settle its author/bibliographic details at primary-source level.

Conditional cell-volume calculations yield 1266.61555 Å3 for the 1967 abstract cell and 2533.37405 Å3 for the Handbook cell, a ratio 2.00011. Keeping the modern formula and printed Z 1 with the larger cell gives about 0.95565 g/cm3; hypothetical Z 2 gives 1.91130 g/cm3. This flags a compilation inconsistency, but is no authority to choose Z 2, change symmetry, identify a setting transformation or claim a measured revised density. The modern and historical formulas both total Na6MgB24O62H44; elemental equality cannot locate H or distinguish OH from water experimentally.

Herwig's 2009 thesis Table 7.1 continuation (PDF 124/printed 108) groups rivadavite among finite hexaborate clusters, and PDF 157/printed 141 discusses this family. Figure 7.27 on PDF 159/printed 143 depicts rivadavite with b and c/2 directions, but its caption explicitly omits H and H2O. The acquired copy is monochrome despite a colour-key caption.
