## Experimental joins and denominators

The bulk static-aging, UV–visible, synchrotron and dry-heating/rehydration arms are distinct. Main Methods pp. 946–948 describe 40 mL of 50 mM REE nitrate mixed with 40 mL of 50 mM sodium carbonate for bulk aging, followed by 5 mL suspension sampling, 0.2 µm filtration, isopropanol rinsing and air drying. These imply 2 mmol nominal REE input and, conditionally on additive volumes, 25 mM nominal postmix REE; neither calculation is a measured dissolved concentration or solid-metal balance. Only ambient-temperature replicas are described as two experiments; the 35 °C La and Ce rows each identify reactor 1. Rows marked reactors `1,2` do not supply separate observations, error bars or a variance estimate.

Powder XRD, database phase identification and TOPAS Rietveld analysis supply the reported crystalline phase fractions. The preferred-orientation treatment concerns lanthanite (002). No inspected raw refinement output supplies a resolved denominator including amorphous solids, dissolved REE and losses, or uncertainty for each percentage. Slashes remain source missing/nonreported cells, not measured zeros. Table 1's 80 °C / 72 h hydroxylbastnäsite slot is blank in both PDF and XML. The author ledger distinguishes this from a slash. Numeric row sums are consistency diagnostics only, with causes unresolved.

The UV–visible arm uses 1 mL of 10 mM REE solution and 1 mL of 10 mM carbonate in a 5 mL cuvette, gentle stirring, 450 nm and 1 s acquisition. The conditional nominal mixed concentration is 5 mM. Normalized turbidity is a proxy; the assumptions linking its rise and maximum to crystallization are not an independently measured mass yield. Table 5's Pr/Nd products and footnotes are retained rather than treated as four identical lanthanite-only experiments.

The synchrotron Ce arm uses equal volumes of 50 mM solutions in a 250 mL three-neck reactor, with 200 mL/min injection, 300 rpm stirring and 50 mL/min circulation through a 1.5 mm capillary. The reactor capacity is not a reported actual fill volume. Initial temperature is 18 °C; 16 keV observations have 30 s intervals. Those conditions and its time origin cannot simply be transferred to the UV–visible arm.

For the separate La thermal experiment, Methods p. 948 gives a 2 g portion in a lidded 50 mL crucible at 80 °C and 0.2 g samples. The preceding La synthesis scale and number of batches are unspecified. Rehydration uses 0.3 g product in a water-filled reactor of 15 mL capacity at 21 °C, without an independently stated water dose. Figure 10 (p. 955) labels phases and times 0, 5.5 and 7 h; it does **not** print quantitative percentages. Figure 11a,b explicitly concerns aqueous 5.5 h specimens, whereas c–f concerns the solid-state treatment. This panel distinction prevents pooling all microscopy into the same thermal arm. No new organism, biological control, industrial scale or isolated metal product is established.
