Erzwiesite mineral IMA: Erw

IMA-approved silver-lead-bismuth sulfosalt mineral (IMA 2012-082; symbol Erw; status A, Approved 2013; full description published April 2026) with ideal end-member formula Ag8Pb12Bi16S40 (requiring Bi 41.92 wt%, Pb 31.76 wt%, S 16.08 wt%, Ag 10.82 wt%, printed total 100.00 wt% in Table 2 column IF on p. 242 and Section 4 on p. 241, whereas the arithmetic sum of the four rounded weight percentages is 100.58 wt%), representing the natural orthorhombic N = 8 (8,8L) member of the lillianite homologous series with ideal andersenite/gustavite substitution percentage Subst. = 66.67% and x = 8 (Topa et al., 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:237-247). Discovered at an unnamed prospect at 2390 m above sea level west of "Blei Beul" ("Beim Bleibau") in the Erzwies mining area, Gastein Valley, Hohe Tauern, Salzburg Province, Austria (47 deg 05 min 40 sec N, 13 deg 02 min 15 sec E; sampled by Herbert Putz in summer 2002), where it occurs in quartz nests hosted by Mesozoic dolomitic marble and Palaeozoic chlorite-mica schist within a Cu-poor, Ag-Bi-rich sulfosalt assemblage associated with galena, heyrovskyite, treasurite, bismuthinite, pyrite, aikinite, krupkaite, gustavite, vikingite, eskimoite, ourayite, gold (electrum), tetradymite, and quartz; the holotype polished block containing the crystal fragment used for structure determination is deposited at the Natural History Museum Vienna (NHMW), Austria, under catalogue number O 2407 (and the species has also been reported from Kutna Hora, Czech Republic, with 1.95-2.96 wt% Sb, and from Altenberg, Salzburg Province, Austria). Forms black needle-like or anhedral crystals up to ~1-3 mm in length with a black streak, metallic lustre, opaque diaphaneity, brittle tenacity, irregular fracture, no observable cleavage or parting, Vickers microhardness VHN50 = 195-224 kg/mm2 (mean 210 kg/mm2, corresponding to Mohs hardness 3-3.5), and calculated density reported as Dcalc = 7.075 g/cm3 in the Abstract (p. 237) vs 7.045 g/cm3 (empirical formula) and 7.074 g/cm3 (structural formula) in Section 3 (p. 240). In reflected light it is greyish-white with distinct pleochroism and distinct anisotropy showing weak to moderate bluish-grey to brownish-grey rotation tints; Commission on Ore Mineralogy (COM) reflectance values in air (Rmin, Rmax in %; Table 1) are 44.5, 47.6% (470 nm), 41.9, 45.0% (546 nm), 41.5, 44.5% (589 nm), and 40.4, 43.6% (650 nm). Field-emission electron microprobe analysis (JEOL JXA-8530F, 25 kV, 20 nA, 1.5 um beam; Table 2) of the grain used for single-crystal X-ray diffraction (Grain 4, n = 6 points) yielded Bi 42.90(63) wt%, Pb 29.50(77) wt%, S 16.08(11) wt%, Ag 11.10(27) wt%, Te 0.21(14) wt%, Cd 0.17(1) wt%, Se 0.08(5) wt%, Cu 0.04(3) wt%, and Sb 0.00(0) wt%, total 100.10(63) wt% (arithmetic sum 100.08 wt%), giving on the basis of 76 atoms per formula unit (36 Me + 40 S, Z = 1) the empirical formula Ag8.18Cu0.05Pb11.31Cd0.12Bi16.30Sb0.01S39.81Te0.13Se0.06, simplified as (Ag,Cu)8.23(Pb,Cd)11.43(Bi,Sb)16.31(S,Te,Se)40.03 with Nchem = 7.91(26), Subst. = 69.01(11)%, and x = 8.16(40) (while all four analysed grains span 8.01L55.6 to 7.91L69.0). Crystallizes in the orthorhombic system, space group Cmcm (#63), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 4.085(5) A, b = 13.462(15) A, c = 33.92(4) A, V = 1866(4) A3, and Z = 1 for Ag8Pb12Bi16S40 (or Z = 4 in Table 3 for the quarter-formula Ag2Pb3Bi4S10; refined to R1 = 0.0524, R(all) = 0.0632, wR(on Fo^2) = 0.1232, and GoF = 1.138 for 308 reflections with Fo > 4sigma(Fo) out of 352 unique reflections from 1423 measured on a 0.035 x 0.045 x 0.09 mm fragment using MoKalpha radiation, where Section 5 on p. 242 contains a copy-paste typo referring to "the available crystal from Madoc" from the companion lopatkaite paper and the Fig. 8 caption on p. 244 twice spells the mineral "erwiesite"). The symmetric 8,8L lillianite-type structure contains five independent cation sites (Table 4): bicapped trigonal-prismatic Pb2 (Pb1.00, CN = 8, BVS = 1.968 v.u.) on the mirror planes separating alternating 8-octahedra-wide (311)PbS-like slabs, and four octahedral slab sites (CN = 6), comprising marginal Pb1 (Pb0.63(4)Ag0.37(4), BVS = 1.672 v.u.), purely occupied Bi1 (Bi1.00, BVS = 2.915 v.u.), mixed Bi2 (Bi0.45(4)Pb0.25Ag0.30(4), BVS = 2.107 v.u.), and central Bi3 (Bi0.59(4)Ag0.41(4), BVS = 2.168 v.u.), giving the structural formula Ag8.64Pb11.04Bi16.32S40 (Z = 1). The seven strongest calculated powder X-ray diffraction lines highlighted in bold in Table 6 (dcalc in A (Irel; hkl), CuKalpha) are 2.919 (100; 1 3 3), 2.846 (99; 1 3 4), 3.387 (98; 1 1 5), 3.288 (85; 0 2 9), 3.588 (64; 0 2 8), 2.039 (43; 1 5 7), and 3.349 (37; 0 4 1) (alongside unbolded reflections at 2.043 (39; 2 0 0) and 2.059 (38; 1 1 14)). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
