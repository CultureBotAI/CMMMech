Whewellite mineral

Whewellite is the monoclinic calcium oxalate monohydrate mineral species Ca(C2O4)(H2O), distinct from weddellite, caoxite, calcium or phosphorus elements, and apatite-nepheline ore as a mixed resource. The September 2026 IMA list marks it approved with 1967 s.p.; that special-procedure notation is not a new-mineral accession. This record covers culture-associated whewellite and separate field-associated calcium oxalates from one 2019 study, without a recovery yield, industrial deployment or inherited constituent criticality.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Culture-associated whewellite on fluorapatite-rich rock

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Aspergillus niger (NCBITaxon:5061) [TaxonMech]

Growing A. niger cultures were associated with whewellite crystallization on mixed apatite-nepheline rock. Diffraction and microscopy support a calcium-oxalate product; fungal acid secretion and host dissolution are proposed explanations rather than independently isolated causal steps.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Section 2.2 used source-named Aspergillus niger Ch4/07, isolated from damaged Proconesos marble at Chersonesos and identified using ITS sequence KF768341. The official record is KF768341.1, strain Ch 4/07; this correspondence is not independent authentication of the experimental stock. Conidia from a 10-day solid culture inoculated approximately 5 by 5 mm biofilm-free fragments of fluorapatite-rich apatite-nepheline rock from the sample-1 dump block, covered by 15 mL liquid Capek-Dox medium in Petri dishes. Experiments ran in triplicate at 20-25 degrees C for 2-15 days. The reported medium contains, in g/L, NaNO3 2.0, KH2PO4 1.0, MgSO4·7H2O 0.5, KCl 0.5, FeSO4·7H2O 0.01 and glucose 30.0. Initial pH 5.5 fell to 4.0 at day 5 and rose to 4.5 by the endpoint. Inoculum density, mineral mass, sterilization protocol and handling-dependent water content of harvested solids are not specified. No experiment used the sample-2 rock.

Whewellite associated with natural lichen biofilms

biomineralization Microbial

Whewellite was assigned with weddellite in natural lichen-associated material on fluorapatite-rich ore surfaces. The accepted observation is mineral occurrence and spatial association; the study does not identify which organism supplied oxalate or establish microbial necessity.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The 2018 field collection sampled biofilms on apatite-nepheline ore blocks from Khibiny massif dumps (sample 1) and the Kola Science Centre Rock Garden (sample 2). Thin sections and XRD identified mainly fluorapatite and nepheline with minor aegirine, titanite and titanomagnetite, rather than a pure mineral substrate. The authors associated calcium oxalates with fluorapatite-rich areas in thalli identified morphologically as Farnoldia jurana in sample 1 and Lecanora polytropa and Polysporina simplex in sample 2. These are mixed natural biofilms, not isolated-strain formation experiments; co-occurring micromycetes were also identified. Local crystallization pH, temperature, duration and metabolic state during formation were not measured. The separately cultivated A. niger strain is not assigned to those field specimens.
Curated Evidence Points:
Partial Source: frank_kamenetskaya_2019
Sections 2.1, 2.3-2.5 and 3.1-3.3, Table 2 and Figures 1-6 support source-assigned mixed whewellite/weddellite associated with lichen thalli. Figure 3 trace 2 explicitly represents Farnoldia jurana material; separate diffraction patterns for each of the other named lichens are not displayed. Figure 6 shows crystal-hypha association and source-assigned morphologies. The stated close proportions are not a quantitative phase refinement or a recovered-mass fraction.
Partial Source: frank_kamenetskaya_2019
The field association does not isolate a causal organism, demonstrate oxalate secretion by each lichen mycobiont, or trace calcium from a particular host phase. Discussion hypotheses about organic acids, Sr/Al impurities, citrate and community-dependent stabilization were not tested by matched community manipulations or dissolved-metabolite measurements in this study. Carbon-coated EDX spectra establish elemental context, not independently a crystal hydrate, valence or lattice-site substitution. No sterile field comparator, phase-specific extraction yield or industrial process is demonstrated.
Context_only Source: weddellite_handbook
Potential atmospheric dehydration of weddellite is a handling limitation. The field samples were not followed in situ from nucleation through collection, so no original phase ratio or compulsory transformation sequence is inferred.
