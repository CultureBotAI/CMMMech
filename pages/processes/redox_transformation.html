Chromite ore processing residue

Chromium-bearing waste from chemical processing of chromite ore, distinct from the chromite mineral species and physical-beneficiation tailings. The curated case is a cementitious, high-lime-process residue from a Glasgow waste-fill site that received material from the Rutherglen Chromite Processing Works. High-lime processing oxidized ore Cr(III) to soluble Cr(VI), followed by aqueous extraction. The 2015 study sampled field-moist material from an exploratory borehole during the M74 motorway project and initially identified it tentatively as COPR from its pale granular appearance and high-pH yellow leachate. Subsequent XRF and XRD characterized a mixed residue rather than proving a pure feed. XRD identified cementitious and hydrated phases, including calcite, portlandite, brownmillerite, pyroaurite, hydrogarnet and brucite; remnant chromite was not detected by that assay. Table 4 reports 3.8 wt% Cr in the XRF composition, without loss-on-ignition correction; this is neither a mineral fraction nor a field-wet feed concentration. This record does not confer a constituent element's criticality designation on the residue or assert commercial microbial processing.

Lithiophilite

Lithiophilite is the lithium manganese(II) phosphate mineral LiMn2+(PO4), listed by IMA-CNMNC in September 2026 as grandfathered with original-description year 1878, not an approval identifier. It is distinct from Fe2+-dominant triphylite, synthetic battery materials of unspecified phase and oxidized lithium-deficient group members. Losey2004 measured natural Mn-rich crystals from the Bennett Quarry, Maine, and Fillow Quarry, Branchville, Connecticut, using microprobe and single-crystal diffraction in orthorhombic space group 62 (Pbnm setting; Handbook Pmnb is another setting). Natural samples contain substitutional Fe and are not pure endmembers. Lithium in that study was constrained or assumed rather than independently measured. The separate Schmid-Beurmann2013 oxidation assay used a laboratory-synthesized, diffraction-assigned LiMnPO4 powder and found no detected phase or lattice change under one specified treatment. That conditional null does not establish universal resistance to lithium dissolution. Neither source demonstrates microbial transformation, isolated lithium recovery or inherited criticality.

Ludlamite

Ludlamite is the ferrous phosphate mineral with ideal formula Fe2+3(PO4)2(H2O)4, recorded as grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA list. It is a distinct mineral species from the three-water phosphoferrite and eight-water vivianite. Moore (1971) investigated natural ludlamite crystals from the Palermo number 1 mine in an abiotic heating comparison. The lower-temperature arms retained the starting powder pattern, whereas the 240 degrees C arm gave a ferric product with diffuse diffraction unrelated to the initial mineral. This observation does not identify a pure crystalline product or establish a general transformation temperature for all ludlamite specimens. The ludlamite and phosphoferrite observations curated in this corpus are different mineral arms of the same study, not independent replications. No microbial process, isolated-product recovery or constituent-derived criticality designation is asserted.

Montroseite

Montroseite is the vanadium oxyhydroxide mineral with ideal formula V3+O(OH), listed as grandfathered (G), original-description year 1953, in the September 2026 IMA list. That year is not a numbered IMA approval. The original Colorado Plateau specimens were iron-bearing, commonly altered and incompletely separated from associated minerals. Single-crystal diffraction assigned the sharp orthorhombic Pbnm lattice to montroseite; the diffuse lattices and much of the original bulk powder pattern represent altered material, subsequently distinguished as paramontroseite or unresolved diffuse A. The historical Fe2+/V4+ compensation model is a chemical interpretation, not a modern site-specific valence measurement. Montroseite is distinct from paramontroseite, elemental vanadium and mixed uranium-vanadium ore. The cited natural evidence does not establish microbial formation, extraction, isolated-product recovery or industrial deployment; criticality is not inherited from vanadium.

Phosphoferrite

Phosphoferrite is the ferrous phosphate mineral with ideal formula Fe2+3(PO4)2(H2O)3. The September 2026 IMA list records redefined status under the 1980 special procedure. It is distinct from the more hydrated species vivianite and ludlamite, the Mn-dominant related mineral reddingite, and ferric kryzhanovskite. The historical Hagendorf Sud specimen studied by Moore (1971) contained Mn and Ca and was not a pure synthetic Fe endmember. Laboratory heating produced a source-reported ferric crystalline product with diffraction similarities to the starting material. Later structural work by Moore, Araki and Kampf (1980) corrected substantial indexing and unit-cell errors in the earlier natural kryzhanovskite comparison; this record therefore retains the limited heating observation without treating the earlier cell dimensions, exact structural model or pure-product identity as established. These are abiotic laboratory observations, not microbial extraction or demonstrated industrial recovery. No criticality designation is inherited from iron or phosphorus.

Triphylite

Triphylite is the lithium iron(II) phosphate mineral LiFe2+(PO4), listed by IMA-CNMNC in September 2026 as grandfathered, with original-description year 1834 rather than an approval identifier. It is distinct from Mn2+-dominant lithiophilite and oxidized, lithium-deficient members of the group. Losey2004 characterized natural pegmatite crystals by electron microprobe and single-crystal diffraction in the Pbnm setting of orthorhombic space group 62; Pmnb in the Handbook is another setting. The natural samples are solid solutions, including Mg substitution, not all pure LiFePO4. Lithium was not independently measured in that study: Li2O was assumed for matrix correction and Li-site occupancy fixed during refinement. The accepted transformation study uses a separately characterized natural triphylite specimen with minor sarcopsides visible microscopically, and demonstrates abiotic oxidation with spatially resolved lithium depletion, not microbial weathering or a recovered lithium product. The historical ferrisicklerite product name in the 2013 original is discredited under the 2022-approved nomenclature published in 2023; that publication classifies the studied Tri-77 and Tri-87 products as heterosite. No constituent-element criticality designation is inherited.
