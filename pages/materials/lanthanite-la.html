Lanthanite-(La) mineral

Lanthanite-(La) is the lanthanum-dominant lanthanite mineral, with ideal formula La2(CO3)3(H2O)6·2H2O, equivalently La2(CO3)3·8H2O. The September 2026 IMA list treats it as a renamed species, with year 1987 s.p.; this nomenclatural action is not a unique mineral approval number. It is distinct from elemental lanthanum, the lanthanite group, other carbonate hydrates and commercial rare-earth feeds. Historical locality labels require caution: the IMA row retains Sweden, whereas Atencio et al. (1989) found their Bastnäs specimen B10531 to be Ce-dominant. Their reassessment of published Curitiba analyses includes La-dominant and Nd-dominant specimens, so neither all Bastnäs material nor all Curitiba material can be assigned to this species solely from its locality. The experiment below concerns a separately synthesized La carbonate analogue of lanthanite, not dissolution of a natural mixed-REE specimen. No jurisdiction-specific criticality designation, microbial process or industrial deployment is established by these sources.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Abiotic dehydration and partial rehydration of synthetic La lanthanite

other

Synthetic La lanthanite heated at 80 °C changed to a phase identified by powder X-ray diffraction as La tengerite-type carbonate after 7 h. When this product was placed in water at 21 °C, lanthanite reappeared. Figure 12 reports 89 wt% lanthanite and 11 wt% tengerite-type phase at 120.5 h, establishing partial return at that sampled endpoint. These are fitted crystalline phase fractions, not lanthanum extraction yields or recovery efficiencies. The experimental tengerite-type product is not assigned a natural La-dominant mineral-species identifier.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory abiotic experiment in Terribili et al. (2025). Initial lanthanite was synthesized by homogeneous mixing at 21 °C; synthesis scale and number of batches for this thermal sample are not specified. A 2 g portion of the resulting solid was heated in a lidded 50 mL porcelain crucible at 80 °C; 0.2 g samples were collected for XRD. A 0.3 g portion of the dehydration product was placed in a 15 mL glass reactor filled with Milli-Q water and left static at 21 °C; 15 mL is the stated reactor capacity, not an independently specified water volume. The initial phase and successive time points provide phase controls. Replicate numbers and uncertainty for this separate dehydration/rehydration arm are not specified. The text's broad statement about eventual transformation of all tengerite is not demonstrated by the final plotted sample, which still contains tengerite; Table SI-1 also lists both phases at 120.5 h. SEM changes and diffraction support a hydration-dependent structural change, while comparable forward/reverse activation energies and the proposed surface-energy explanation remain hypotheses. No microorganisms, natural ore feed, isolated-metal product or industrial-scale test was involved.

Figure 10 shows lanthanite at 0 h, mixed phases at 5.5 h and tengerite-type material at 7 h; Figure 12 shows increasing lanthanite from 2 wt% at 5 h to 89 wt% at 120.5 h, with 11 wt% tengerite remaining.
