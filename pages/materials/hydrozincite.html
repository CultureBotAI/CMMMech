Hydrozincite mineral

Hydrozincite is the monoclinic zinc hydroxycarbonate mineral Zn5(CO3)2(OH)6, listed by IMA-CNMNC in September 2026 with grandfathered status G and original-description year 1853, not a numbered approval. It is distinct from smithsonite, zincite, elemental zinc and mixed zinc-bearing sediments. The curated evidence concerns hydrozincite-bearing field mats associated with photosynthetic microorganisms and a separate sterile precipitation experiment. It supports a qualified biological association rather than a pure-culture demonstration of microbial necessity, a phase-specific metal recovery yield, or industrial deployment. No criticality designation is inherited from zinc.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Hydrozincite precipitation associated with a photosynthetic field mat

biomineralization Microbial

Hydrozincite-bearing precipitates encrusted filamentous microbial structures in the Rio Naracauli mine-drainage stream in Sardinia. The source interprets photosynthetic alkalinization and binding by acidic extracellular polysaccharides as promoting precipitation. Diffraction, microscopy and recovery of viable phototrophs support this field association, but do not independently prove the necessity or separate causal contribution of either community member.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Podda et al. sampled white mats at stations 3 and 4 in April 1997 during a photosynthetic bloom and surveyed 14 water stations along 3.4 km. Table 2 gives station 3 at 17 degrees C and pH 7.1 and station 4 at 20 degrees C and pH 7.7; these are field observations, not controlled incubation settings. About 2 g of year-old dried precipitate was incubated at room temperature in dim light with tenfold-diluted BG-11 medium to recover associated organisms. Morphological identification assigned Scytonema sp. ING-1 and Chlorella sp. SA-1; no sequence- or collection-authenticated species mapping is asserted. This resuscitation assay does not measure new hydrozincite production. No matched sterile field system or controlled isolate-by-isolate precipitation assay is reported.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: podda2000
Hydrozincite determination and distribution, Figure 2, assigns hydrozincite in the station 4 solid with quartz and calcite; hydrozincite decreases downstream and is not detected at station 8. Microbiological evidence of hydrozincite formation, Figures 3-4, documents mineral-encrusted tubing, Zn-rich EDX signals and phototroph growth from stored precipitate. These observations support mineral identity and organism association, not phase purity or proof that all mineralization was biological.
Partial Source: podda2000
Table 1 is bulk mixed-sediment chemistry, not a hydrozincite lattice substitution analysis. Table 2 and Water analysis show concurrent downstream changes in pH, dissolved metals and bicarbonate, but also acknowledge dilution and bicarbonate-rich inflows. Reported percentage decreases between stations therefore are not adopted as microbial removal efficiencies or phase-specific recovery. Conclusions propose surface alkalinization and polysaccharide binding; their individual causal contributions were not isolated.
Partial Source: ngwenya2014
Sections 2 and 3.1, Figures 1-2, examine May 2006 samples from the same stream system, not a replication of the 1997 sampling event. Mineral spheroids are associated with organic strands interpreted as EPS. Sections 3.2-5 and Figures 3-6 also show similar structures in sterile organic-amended solutions and acknowledge comparable morphologies in abiotic systems. Morphology alone is therefore not a unique biosignature; proposed protection against entombment and metabolite exchange were not measured survival outcomes.

Sterile hydrozincite precipitation with organic morphology modifiers

other

In a separate laboratory experiment, hydrozincite was assigned to precipitates from sterile zinc sulfate-bicarbonate solutions with ammonium-carbonate gas diffusion. Organic additives altered aggregate morphology relative to no-additive controls. This demonstrates an abiotic organic-associated route and does not establish active microbial secretion or a necessary living-cell mechanism.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Ngwenya et al. used 100 mL of autoclaved equimolar 20 mM ZnSO4 and NaHCO3 in 250 mL triple-neck flasks connected to a flask containing about 10 g ammonium carbonate. Initial pH was adjusted to about 6 with nitric acid. L-aspartic acid, L-glutamic acid or L-cysteine was added at 1 mg/mL, or xanthan at 0.25 mg/mL; no-organic controls were included. Incubation lasted just over a week. The paper does not specify incubation temperature or independent replicate counts. These artificial solutions are not the natural stream samples.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: ngwenya2014
Sections 2 and 3.2, Figure 3a-j, distinguish mostly flat control discs from additive-associated aggregates, with botryoidal structures particularly evident for aspartic and glutamic acids. Sections 3.3-3.4 and Figures 4-5 provide vibrational spectra and pH trajectories. XRD confirmation is stated as data not shown; spectroscopy supports the assignment without a supplied diffraction pattern or quantitative phase fraction. No isolated mineral yield or metal recovery percentage is established.
Partial Source: ngwenya2014
Sections 4-5 acknowledge that natural specimens lacked diagnostic organic vibrational bands despite visible organic strands, and cysteine-associated bands could reflect separate solid cysteine. Figure 6 is a conceptual and thermodynamic model, not a measured survival or transport experiment. These limitations prevent a unique molecular nucleation or self-preservation claim.
