## Kaliborite structure and the bounded correction

Burns and Hawthorne, *The Canadian Mineralogist* 32 (1994), 885–894, describe natural kaliborite from specimen CMN 53372. Original PDF page 2 / printed page 886 is decisive: the methods prose, its later repeated total and Table 1 all read **3642**. The table's 2403 observed reflections, final R of 4.4% and weighted R of 4.7% are separate quantities. A fresh high-resolution original rendering is retained as `R/kaliborite-table1-detail.png`. Some indexed OCR renders the table number as 3842; this is not evidence for a different experimental version. The maintained owner and historical Round 114 text inherited the false comparison. Finding **482-R1**, stable key `kaliborite-1994-false-total-reflection-discrepancy`, records the repository defect, its preserved exact predecessor and the local correction. The old native bundle had no corresponding finding occurrence; no earlier finding is invented for it.

The original abstract uses an inclusive observed-amplitude criterion, while Table 1 prints a strict criterion. That smaller source notation difference is retained separately from the corrected total count. No original list of observed intensities was acquired to retest either selection. The paper says structure factors were deposited with CISTI, without a presently retrieved observation file in this investigation.

Original Table 2 / PDF page 3 gives ten H sites, with one shared H isotropic displacement value. H1 lies on a special position; counting labels as equally populated independent sites would miscount the formula. Table 4 gives two equal 1.212(7) Å H1–O distances, an O–O distance of 2.421(4) Å and an angle of 173(7)°. These are the reported average model. The later discussion on printed page 892 describes a trial split model and a separation of 0.7(2) Å, but says splitting did not improve the R index. Figure 5's difference-density maxima and bond-valence interpretation do not independently distinguish static disorder from dynamic motion. Original Figure 6 / page 893 supplies the IR context, not a time-resolved hydrogen experiment.

One derivative-source difference is now explicit: original Table 3 / page 887 gives OH(2) U11 = **80(8) × 10⁻⁴ Å²**, whereas COD's O-h2 U11 is **0.00900 Å²**. `R/kaliborite-table2-3-detail.png` preserves the high-resolution original check. Neither number is silently repaired. This selected ADP comparison is not a complete rekey of every printed heavy-atom ADP. No maintained owner claim quotes that parameter, so it is a source limitation rather than a second record correction.

## Thermal evidence and unresolved formulas

Stoch and Waclawska (1994) is a comparative experimental paper, not a second independent replication of the 1993 kaliborite study that it cites as reference 5. Printed page 183 describes coarse-crystalline natural borates considered pure from the Wroclaw University mineral museum, without a kaliborite accession, locality or quantitative specimen assay. Q-1500D and C-derivatograph thermal analyses are described. The stated **2.5 °C/min** heating rate belongs to the structural-examination treatments, which were cooled and examined by X-ray diffraction and IR. It is not evidence that every DTA/TG trace used that ramp. Kaliborite-specific mass, gas, holds, replication and raw run linkage remain unavailable.

For kaliborite, printed page 185 discusses events at 258 and 272 °C and progressive water/hydroxyl release through 400 °C. Table 1 / page 184 assigns an amorphous state at 400 °C, magnesium-borate crystallization at 679 °C and the later potassium-borate step at 729 °C; melting is placed at 954 °C. Figure 2a(C) / page 186 distinguishes original, amorphous and two recrystallized patterns. The crystal assignments MgB4O7 and Mg2B2O5, later KB5O8, are not quantitative phase fractions or isolated boron recovery. Table 1 also labels an amorphous KB7O11 matrix at the first crystallization step and B2O3 at the later step. These are reported assignments, not a measured whole-product balance.

The original discrepancy is reproducible: Table 1 prints amorphous **KMg2B12O31**, whereas Table 4 / page 196 prints **KMg2B12O21**. High-resolution images of both tables are retained. Neither is substituted for an assay or balanced reaction. Conditional formal-charge arithmetic is recorded only to expose the limits of adopting the literal formulas; it cannot select an intended correction. The 640 °C endotherm is interpreted as a glass transition, with structure-inheritance and mobility arguments. It is not a validated useful glass product or an equilibrium boundary. Celsius and Kelvin Tg/Tm ratios are separately calculated as diagnostics, without revising the source interpretation.

The complete 1993 *Thermochimica Acta* 215:265–271 original remains missing. Its available publisher abstract enumerates dehydroxylation and dehydration in one order and then says dehydration precedes dehydroxylation. That abstract cannot reconcile thermal arms, apparatus, specimen identity or phase balance. No universal sequence is adopted.

## Preobrazhenskite reflection and hydrogen limits

Burns and Hawthorne, *The Canadian Mineralogist* 32 (1994), 387–396, reports an Inder-deposit crystal and 2645 total reflections. The abstract and Table 1 use **I ≥ 2.5σ(I)** for 2094 observed reflections; the prose on original PDF page 2 / printed page 388 instead prints **I ≤ 2.5σ(I)**. The count alone does not decide which observations were selected. The derivative coordinates have no observations from which to reconstruct the subset or residuals. No preferred correction is imported.

Table 2 / page 388 and the site figures identify H3 and H6 separately. In the printed-page-394 discussion, the OH3 argument switches to H6 labels in two subsequent bond expressions. Table 4's symmetric average H6 distances and the maps in Figures 4–6 do not remove that textual ambiguity. The paper itself retains uncertainty about the local H configuration and discusses a possible higher-temperature transition conditionally; this is not a measured transition. The H3/H6 labeling inconsistency is preserved rather than propagated into a new causal claim.

IR preparation differs from kaliborite: the original preobrazhenskite method uses approximately 4 mg sample with 150 mg KBr, versus 2 mg with 150 mg for kaliborite. Both describe grinding about 20 mg to generally below 2 μm and pressing a 13 mm pellet. The 0.01 cm⁻¹ statement is the manufacturer's frequency accuracy, not a measured instrumental resolution. These quantities do not establish purity or replicate uncertainty.

Original Table 6 / page 395 reports five bands at 3204, 3485, 3544, 3590 and 3631 cm⁻¹, assigned to H6, H7, H3, H11 and H12. The discussion says 3203 cm⁻¹ for the first band. Figure 9 corrects intensities for H multiplicity; its H6 point must not be equated directly to Table 6's uncorrected 163.1 intensity. Conditional doubling gives 326.2 and is recorded as arithmetic, not a second measured intensity. The authors explicitly say a skew function would fit the band shape better than their current solution. Raw spectra, a fit covariance/error model and residual data are not supplied in the acquired materials.
