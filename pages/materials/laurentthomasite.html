Laurentthomasite mineral

Laurentthomasite (IMA 2018-157, approved April 2019) is an IMA-accepted anhydrous magnesium-potassium-beryllium-aluminium milarite-group (osumilite-type) double-ring cyclosilicate mineral (Strunz 9.CM.05) with September 2026 IMA-CNMNC Master List ideal formula Mg2K(Be2Al)Si12O30 (PDF p. 122, status A; printed with a missing trailing zero as Mg2K(Be2Al)Si12O3 in Table 1 on p. 357 of Ferraris et al. 2020), representing the anhydrous Mg-dominant A-site analogue of milarite Ca2K(Be2Al)Si12O30 and related to oftedalite ScCaKBe3Si12O30 and friedrichbeckeite Mg2NaKBe3Si12O30. Described by Ferraris, Pignatelli, Cámara, Parodi, Pont, Schreyer, and Wei (2020) from pockets in a coarse-grained quartz-syenite pegmatite (~550 Myr Pan-African granitoid hosted in the 2550–2500 Myr Antananarivo block reworked at ~750–500 Myr) in the Ihorombe region, Fianarantsoa Province, ~80 km northeast of Betroka (23°16'03" S, 46°05'49" E), southern Madagascar (discovered in late 2017 by Laurent Thomas; holotype MNHN_MIN_218.1_a and cotypes MNHN_MIN_218.1_b and MNHN_MIN_218.1_c deposited at the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle, Paris, France, pp. 355–357). Occurs as euhedral tabular hexagonal crystals up to 15 mm wide and 5 mm thick flattened on {0001} and bounded by {10-10} prism and {0001} pinacoid forms (sometimes forming clusters up to 2 cm on quartz or floating in cavity-filling clay), associated with orthoclase (1–5 cm), smoky to grey quartz (1–3 cm), pale green prismatic hexagonal apatite (0.2–2 cm), colourless to yellow hexagonal phenakite (0.2–1.5 cm), pale blue prismatic beryl (0.2–2 cm), albite, magnetite, and sub-millimetric SEM-EDS-identified thortveitite (Sc2Si2O7), cheralite (CaTh(PO4)2), and microscopic Nb–Ta–W phases, while hosting aligned micrometric inclusions of an Fe2+-rich phase (pp. 356–358). Physical and optical characterization (pp. 355–359, Figs. 1–4) shows transparent crystals with vitreous lustre, light blue streak, non-fluorescence, Mohs hardness ~6, brittle tenacity with irregular to conchoidal fracture, poor {0001} cleavage, Dmeas = 2.67(8) g/cm3 (Archimedes balance across 5 fragments), Dcalc = 2.66(4) g/cm3, uniaxial (+) optics at 589 nm with omega = 1.540(2) and epsilon = 1.545(3), Gladstone–Dale compatibility index 1 - (KP/KC) = 0.028 (excellent), and strong cobalt-blue (along [0001]) to green-yellow (along [1000]) dichroism attributed by 295 K 57Fe Mössbauer spectroscopy (backscattering MIMOS II, Section 3, p. 356, and Section 4.1, pp. 357–358, Fig. 3) to intervalence charge transfer between octahedral Fe2+ at the A site (Doublet 1: IS = 1.15 mm/s, QS = 2.51 mm/s, FWHM = 0.25 mm/s, 92.0% area) and tetrahedral Fe3+ at the T(2) site (Doublet 2: IS = 0.14 mm/s, QS = 1.66 mm/s, FWHM = 0.34 mm/s, 5.8% area, plus 2.2% area from inclusion Fe2+ at IS = 1.07 mm/s, QS = 1.00 mm/s, FWHM = 0.26 mm/s; normalized laurentthomasite Fe2+/(Fe2+ + Fe3+) = 94 ± 2%), while micro-Raman spectroscopy (514.5 nm, Section 4.2, pp. 358–359, Fig. 4) confirms the absence of H2O/OH bands above 1300 cm-1 and exhibits [T(1)12O30] double-ring modes at 1145, ~550, and ~480 cm-1 with a split band at 263 and 288 cm-1. Combined 40-point WDS electron-microprobe analysis (Cameca SX Five, 15 kV, 10 nA, 5 um beam), ICP-OES/ICP-MS (on 120 mg of pure crystals for Be, Y, and trace elements), and Mössbauer Fe partitioning (Section 5.1, pp. 357, 359, Tables 2–3) yielded SiO2 73.10 wt% (range 71.29–74.90, SD 0.41), BeO 6.02 (ICP-MS, <5% uncertainty), K2O 4.30 (3.92–4.67, SD 0.03), MgO 4.01 (3.80–4.09, SD 0.07), Sc2O3 3.78 (3.70–3.86, SD 0.05), Al2O3 3.11 (3.03–3.20, SD 0.05), FeO 2.69 (printed range 2.50–2.67, where the printed mean 2.69 wt% slightly exceeds the printed upper range bound 2.67 wt%, SD 0.07), MnO 1.91 (1.77–2.04, SD 0.07), Y2O3 0.22 (ICP-MS, <5% uncertainty), Fe2O3 0.19 (calculated from FeO and Mössbauer ratio), BaO 0.16 (0.09–0.23, SD 0.04), Na2O 0.15 (0.10–0.18, SD 0.02), CaO 0.07 (0.06–0.08, SD 0.01), ZnO 0.04 (0.01–0.07, SD 0.03), TiO2 0.02 (printed range 0.02–0.019, SD 0.01), total 99.77 wt% (plus Table 3 ICP-MS trace elements in ug/g including Y 1754, Rb 1405, Yb 922, Lu 321, Zn 176, Er 91.2, Cs 82.9, Tm 43.4, Ga 37.3, As 36.6, Dy 29.4, Sr 21.2, Ni 15.3, Ho 12.8, Zr 7.81, In 7.72, Hf 6.59, Gd 5.56, Cr 5.5, Nd 3.88, Ce 3.62, Co 3.49, V 3.4, Sm 3.07, Cu 3.0, Nb 2.99, Sn 2.36, Tb 2.32, Ge 2.15, Ta 2.02, La 1.50, Th 0.87, Pb 0.85, Pr 0.749, U 0.70, Sb 0.57, Eu 0.239, Bi 0.07, Cd 0.07), giving on 30 anions pfu the empirical formula (Mg0.86Sc0.54Fe2+0.35Mn0.26)Sigma2.01(K0.89Na0.05Y0.02Ca0.01Ba0.01)Sigma0.98[(Be2.35Al0.50Mg0.11Fe3+0.03)Sigma2.99(Si11.90Al0.10)O30] and simplified formula (Mg,Sc)2(K,Na)[(Be,Al,Mg)3(Si,Al)12O30] (ideal Mg2K(Be2Al)Si12O30 requires SiO2 75.92, MgO 8.49, Al2O3 5.37, BeO 5.27, K2O 4.96, total 100 wt%). Hexagonal, space group P6/mcc (no. 192), Z = 2 (Sections 5.2–5.3, pp. 359–363, Tables 4–9): Rietveld powder XRD refinement (CuKalpha1, Rp = 4.9%, Rwp = 6.5%, p. 359) gives a = 9.95343(6) Å, c = 14.15583(8) Å, V = 1214.54(1) Å3 (ten strongest observed PXRD lines with I >= 2 in Table 5, p. 361: 3.171 (10)(211), 4.064 (8)(112), 2.881 (8)(114), 4.965 (6)(110), 2.732 (4)(204), 3.533 (3)(004), 7.055 (2)(002), 4.302 (2)(200), 3.675 (2)(202), and 1.825 (2)(315) Å, whereas the Abstract on p. 355 prints 2.732 – (8) – 204 in place of 2.881 – (8) – 114 before 2.732 – (4) – 204 and omits 1.825 (2)(315)); single-crystal CuKalpha XRD refinement (430 reflections with Io > 2sigma(I) out of 431 independent, R1 = 1.89%, wR2 = 4.76%) is reported in Table 6 (p. 362) as a = 9.95800(10) Å, c = 14.14920(10) Å, V = 1215.085(19) Å3 for crystal size 0.40 x 0.29 x 0.17 mm, whereas Section 5.2 prose (pp. 359–360) prints a = 9.95800(7) Å, c = 14.114916(11) Å (a digit-duplication typo for 14.14916(11) Å, as confirmed by the reported volume), V = 1215.081(15) Å3 for crystal size 39.6 x 28.7 x 16.9 um. Distinct from milarite, oftedalite, friedrichbeckeite, almarudite, and elemental beryllium or scandium; no criticality designation for laurentthomasite itself, no microbial or biological mechanism, and no industrial recovery yield are asserted.
