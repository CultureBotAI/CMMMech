Material Kinds

Records are classified into six distinct material kinds to preserve the boundary between chemical elements, crystalline minerals, commercial commodities, and secondary waste feeds.

  • Elements 64 records

    Chemical elements distinct from ores, alloys, and waste streams.
  • Minerals 84 records

    Naturally occurring crystalline inorganic substances with defined IMA mineral status.
  • Secondary Resources 52 records

    Post-consumer and industrial byproducts including spent batteries, slags, red mud, and scrap.
  • Commodities 32 records

    Aggregated commercial products, traded raw materials, and manufactured chemical compounds.
  • Primary Resources 20 records

    Raw unrefined geological deposits, mined ores, and run-of-mine materials.
  • Mineral Groups 4 records

    Mineral supergroups and solid-solution series sharing related crystal structures.

Microbial Transformation Processes

Each biological mechanism captures experimental feedstocks, microbial agents, reaction conditions, and formal evidence evaluations.

  • Bioleaching 121 mechanisms

    Microbial solubilization and mobilization of target metals from ores, concentrates, and wastes.
  • Biomineralization 38 mechanisms

    Biological precipitation, crystallization, and synthesis of mineral phases or nanoparticles.
  • Redox Transformation 25 mechanisms

    Enzymatic or cell-surface oxidation-reduction reactions altering metal valence states.
  • Biosorption 13 mechanisms

    Passive binding and retention of metal ions by microbial cell walls and biomass.
  • Bioseparation 9 mechanisms

    Selective partition or affinity purification using microbial biomolecules (e.g. lanmodulin).
  • Recovery 6 mechanisms

    Integrated microbial processing schemes yielding concentrated or isolated materials.
  • Other Transformations 25 mechanisms

    Complex community transformations, multi-stage processes, or unclassified interactions.

Evidence & Curation Contract

CMMMech records enforce rigorous scientific limits:

  • Chemical identity separation: An element (e.g. Lithium) is strictly distinct from its mineral hosts (e.g. Spodumene, Jadarite) and its commercial form.
  • Mechanism evidence boundaries: Laboratory solubilization or binding is explicitly distinguished from full industrial recovery, elemental yield, or isolated pure metal.
  • Negative & control evidence: Abiotic controls, matched acid baselines, and null results are recorded alongside positive extraction claims to prevent inflated efficacy.
  • Structured provenance: Every material links to primary research citations, ChEBI, and NCBI Taxonomy identifiers.
