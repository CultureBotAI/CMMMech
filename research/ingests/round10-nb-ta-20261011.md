## Niobium: ash release is conditional and the mechanism remains unresolved

[Andrzejewska-Górecka et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.24425/aep.2019.128643)
was acquired as the primary seven-page journal PDF. All text was read; printed
pp.73–76 were rendered and visually inspected, including Tables 1–4 and
Figures 3 and 7. The adapted activated-sludge community has no resolved taxon
or strain. The 150 mL culture/10 g ash, five-day assay must be kept separate
from its initial 1:1 sludge/water plus sulfur adaptation.

The key observation is the paper's reported 100% Nb release from ash A at
24 °C/120 rpm versus 2.3% averaged control. This is a release calculation using
initial ash inventory. It is not purified-metal yield. The same ash under
compressed-air mixing reports 0.0%, and sewage-sludge ash stays near 15% across
the variants. No detection threshold establishes a true zero. Initial culture
pH below 3 rises to 10 in the highest-Nb condition, while enhanced sulfate
production accompanies the condition with no reported Nb release. Sulfur
oxidation cannot therefore be assigned as a demonstrated Nb mechanism.

The paper does not adequately specify control composition/sterilization,
leachate filtration or digestion, culture blank correction, or independent
biological replication. Instrument exposures are not flask replicates. Table
4 labels its 37 °C column 120 rpm, contrary to Table 2, figures and running
text's 50 rpm; the record preserves this discrepancy. No microbial species,
Nb host mineral, oxidation state or isolated product is invented. The two ash
types and all control limitations remain explicit.

## Tantalum: a documented unsuccessful direct bioleaching attempt

[Sikander et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph191610006),
PMC9408593.1, was acquired as full JATS XML, institutional primary PDF and
complete supplementary ZIP. The full text was read. Table 1 and Figures
12–14 were visually checked; all four data-bearing supplement pages were
rendered and read (S1–S7). The consortium's three named species are not
strain-resolved or causally separable, so no taxon CURIE is assigned to the
experimental mixture.

The accepted mechanism is specifically the ten-day acidophile assay with
2% w/v capacitor scrap, two particle fractions, 30 °C/150 rpm and sulfuric-acid
maintenance of pH 1.8. Section 4.3 reports Ta non-detection, Figure 12 has no
Ta bar, and S6's two Ta.Cap rows have `u` concentration cells and `0`
percentage cells. The supplement does not define `u` or supply a numerical
Ta detection threshold. Accordingly, no zero concentration or quantified
Ta recovery is curated. Base-metal removal is not a measured Ta enrichment
factor or recovered product.

S1 is a composition table for digestion without HF, despite the Methods
referring to it as analytical validation. It has no calibration/detection-limit
study. Table 1's HF-containing digest gives the bulk-Ta inventory; S1 cannot
replace that denominator. The Methods sentence about sterilizing the samples
is grammatically ambiguous and is not used to guarantee sterile feed. The
final iron concentration for the capacitor assay is also unresolved.

The fungal spore/filtrate results in S7 likewise show Ta `u`/`0`, but they
remain a separate inspected assay, not an extra accepted mechanism. Table 3
labels fungal experiments ten days whereas Methods/figures specify twenty;
this discrepancy does not change the scoped ten-day bacterial record.

## Contradictory leads and acquisition limits

The follow-on [Sikander et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11356-023-26592-3)
abstract reports an unidentified nonsterile Ta-mobilizing system and a hybrid
route. It is a material counter-lead to any universal impossibility claim.
That claim is therefore explicitly excluded. The publisher PDF URL returned
HTML, and three listed institutional routes returned 503/403. The web tool
briefly recognized a 31-page accepted manuscript and exposed an indexed
Methods excerpt, but subsequent full-text opens timed out. The full results,
figures, controls and distinction between executed and proposed hybrid stages
were not verified. No abstract measurement or claimed purity was imported.
Next action: obtain and assess the complete manuscript and supplements.

[Tonietti et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1741305),
PMC13106364.1, was assessed as a possible common rock-leaching source. Full
XML, the 12-page supplemental PDF and two-sheet XLSX were acquired. Inspection
of the complete workbook analyte list and supplemental text found no Nb or Ta.
This source supplies **no Nb/Ta assay**, rather than evidence of no Nb/Ta
leaching. Its other analytes require their own review.

Two useful Nb mechanisms remain research handoffs rather than automatic
imports. [Shi et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1155/2010/307578), PMC2860717.1,
has acquired full XML/PDF and text-inspected methods/results for recombinant
bacterial Fbp binding of Nb(V). Figures and EXAFS require a separate review;
protein binding, Fe displacement and uncertain multinuclear assignments must
remain distinct from whole-cell uptake or recovery. The NCBI species-485 XML
was acquired for that lead, but is not an accepted record identifier.
[Dead-biomass biosorption in 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biteb.2026.102748)
was accessible only as publisher abstract/introduction with direct retrieval
403. Full methods and precipitation controls are required before import.
A [LAB-supernatant niobium-oxide study](https://www.beilstein-journals.org/bjnano/articles/17/77)
is another unassessed primary lead.
