Silicon element Critical Material

The element Si, distinct from dissolved silicic acid, biogenic silica, crystalline silica minerals and the silicon-metal commodity. The United States 2025 list's Silicon material designation is mapped to this element; it does not designate every silicate or imply that diatom silica formation recovers semiconductor-grade or metallic silicon.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Conditional SIT contribution to diatom silicic-acid uptake and silica incorporation

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Thalassiosira pseudonana CCMP1335 (NCBITaxon:296543) [TaxonMech]

Living Thalassiosira pseudonana CCMP1335 cultures depleted silicic acid from medium and incorporated a fluorescent silica tracer into their frustules. Joint SIT1/SIT2 knockdown delayed solution depletion and reduced the silica-associated fluorescence at low silicic-acid supply. This supports a conditional contribution to silicon utilization and cell-wall biosilicification, without establishing an exclusive transporter, a directly demonstrated sensing pathway, or metal recovery.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory stock cultures grew in NEPC medium containing 100 micromolar Si(OH)4, on an orbital shaker at 18 degrees C under continuous light of 100 microeinsteins/m2/s. Exponentially growing cells were harvested and deprived of silicic acid before replenishment. Silica-incorporation Methods specify 24 h deprivation; Figures 7-8 describe overnight starvation. Sodium silicate supplied a nominal 20 micromolar Si(OH)4 concentration for the selected assays. Silicomolybdate colorimetry of centrifuged supernatant measured disappearance, normalized per cell; this is a net-depletion proxy for uptake. PDMPO at 100 ng/mL, added five minutes before silicate, provided a flow-cytometric proxy for newly incorporated silica. A subset of clones was compared at 200 micromolar supply in a separate incorporation assay. Measured assay pH and an abiotic medium-depletion blank are not specified in the inspected main methods. No isolated silica mass, crystallographic phase, Si(0) product or industrial deployment is established here.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: shrestha_2015
Figure 7 compares wild type with RNAi clones R-13, R-14 and R-22 and antisense clones A-2, A-7 and A-8. Results report that wild type depleted the supplied silicic acid by 2 h, whereas the RNAi clones required an additional hour; antisense clones showed a similar delay. Figure 8A-B shows lower PDMPO fluorescence in the knockdowns at 20 micromolar supply. These are different assays and do not equate net solution depletion with the amount of solid silica.
Partial Source: shrestha_2015
Figure 8C tests wild type, R-13, A-2 and A-7 at 200 micromolar supply. The authors report no incorporation difference in this comparison; the plotted trajectories are not numerically identical. Figures 7-8 state standard deviations with n=3 but do not identify independent culture versus technical replication. The captions provide no inferential statistical test for the high-supply null statement. This limits claims that SIT knockdown universally suppresses silicification or that passive diffusion was directly demonstrated by this assay.
Partial Source: shrestha_2015
Vector design and Figure 6 establish knockdown rather than gene deletion. Sequence similarity prevented separate SIT1 and SIT2 perturbations; the immunoblot antiserum recognized all three SITs. No rescue experiment is described in the inspected main article. A sensing role is the authors' hypothesis, with an upstream sensor retained as an alternative. Supplementary construct/alignment files could not be acquired through the checked legitimate routes, so their sequences and supplementary predictions were not verified.
