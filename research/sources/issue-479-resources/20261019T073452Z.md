## 2026 salt-slag assessment

**Identity and independence of the experiments.** The publisher PDF and
Crossref agree on 4 March 2026 publication, Recycling 11(3):52, and the DOI.
This is distinct from the Brazilian 2022 rotary-furnace experiment; their
observations must not be pooled. Section 2.1 describes industrial foundry slags
and three consecutive samples from one batch for composition checks. Table 1
assigns Slag No.1 35.90 wt.% NaCl, 16.70 wt.% KCl and 14.40 wt.% metallic Al.
That salt-rich, flux-derived feed fits the salt-slag owner. Its foundry and
original metal-charge details are not supplied. Slag No.2 is separately
described as oxide-rich and attributed to MgCl2 flux. It is context for another
salt input to the combined flux, not interchangeable with Slag No.1 or the
existing post-metal-recovery secondary-dross specimen. No mineral or elemental
identifier identifies either mixed waste.

**Laboratory separation.** Sections 2.2-2.3.1 describe jaw crushing, screening,
planetary milling and separation of metallic pieces from the oxide/salt
fraction. Slag No.1's water route specifies 80 +/- 5 degrees C, 200 rpm and
one hour, filtration, 90 degrees C evaporation, and 200 degrees C residue
drying for two hours. Figure 8/Table 3 assign the evaporated product 64.50 wt.%
NaCl and 35.50 wt.% KCl by phase analysis. These are normalized phase
composition estimates, not a measured salt recovery fraction or proof of
absolute purity. The residual product retains NaCl/KCl entries of 3.29/1.70
wt.%. Gas evolution is observed, without a measured gas balance or proven
detoxification. Water/solid loading for this arm is not sufficiently specified.

**Flux formulation and actual reuse trial.** Section 2.4/Figure 10 combine salts
from the water-treated No.1 and acid-treated No.2 arms, with fresh raw-material
additions whose amounts are not quantified. The designed flux is 45.1% NaCl,
33.7% KCl and 21.2% MgCl2. Its approximately 650 degrees C melting point,
0.00195 Pa s viscosity and 1.592 g/cm3 density are calculated properties, not
reported independent product measurements. Sections 2.5 and 3 describe
briquetting separated metal, remelting 500 g in a laboratory induction furnace,
adding 22 g regenerated flux with 5 wt.% NaF at 740 degrees C, holding ten
minutes with stirring every three minutes, and casting a 470 g product.
Table 6 reports 95.500 wt.% Al and 3.430 wt.% Mg in that alloy. The 470/500 mass
comparison concerns the remelting charge; it is not recovery from the original
slag. The trial demonstrates use of a mixed regenerated/fresh flux in one
reported laboratory cycle. No matched virgin-flux or no-flux control,
independent replicate count, serial-reuse trial or industrial throughput is
provided. The conclusion expressly calls the testing laboratory scale and
leaves continuous operation for future work.

**Contradictions and limits that must accompany curation.**

- Section 2.5's 370 g metallic Al per kg initial slag cannot be reconciled with
  the Table 1 metallic-Al entries of 14.40% and 19.33% without an unreported
  change of feed/denominator. The 85% headline recovery and 80 +/- 5%
  separation efficiency lack a closed, sample-resolved material balance. The
  paper's 95% target-component separation statement is also not independently
  established by the product-composition table. Preserve these as unresolved
  source claims rather than accepted yield observations.
- The No.2 acid arm gives 35 g in 100 mL of 35% HCl in Section 2.3.2, whereas
  Section 3 states 1 M HCl. Section 3 gives 250 rpm/30 min for dissolution,
  unlike the No.1 subsection's 200 rpm/one hour. Do not silently synthesize a
  single reproducible recipe from these conflicting accounts.
- Table 3's printed MgAl3O4 and SiO3 headings conflict with Figure 8's phase
  labels; no new mineral identity or corrected formula is inferred from them.
  Table 2 also prints 660 degrees C under Al2O3. These entries are not reliable
  independent chemical-property evidence and are not proposed for import.
- Figure 13's return arrow is a process scheme, not proof of repeated closed
  cycles. Refractory/landfill uses in Section 2.6 are possibilities and cited
  external context, not measured safety or reuse endpoints for these residues.

The data-availability statement on page 18 says underlying data are available

PDF is CC BY 4.0 according to page 1 and Crossref; full text/rendered pages

events for the same study, not separate experiments. The abstract names
Aspergillus niger and a sequence of biological leaching and chemical separation,

missing methods text. The older 2007 abiotic HFC owner cannot validate this
fungal experiment. No microbial mechanism or abstract-only percentage should

the text-mining policy link in Crossref is not an established redistribution

manuscript and its relevant figures/supplements. The present bounded searches
