Muscovite mineral

Muscovite is a mica mineral species with ideal formula KAl2(Si3Al)O10(OH)2, distinct from generic mica, biotite, phlogopite, traded potash and potassium-bearing feldspars. The September 2026 IMA list records approval by a 1998 special procedure, not a numbered mineral accession. The curated laboratory specimen is a chemically characterized natural museum muscovite flake; its composition is not an ideal pure endmember. No criticality designation is inferred from its potassium or aluminium content.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Fungal-associated muscovite surface alteration under potassium limitation

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Paxillus involutus (NCBITaxon:71150) [TaxonMech]

After 21 days with Paxillus involutus, muscovite surfaces showed uneven erosion and localized removal of surface layers relative to the pre-exposure images. These observations support a bounded fungal-weathering interpretation. They do not demonstrate an efficient potassium supply: potassium concentration in harvested mycelium was not significantly greater than in the potassium-depleted, mineral-free fungal control. Neither surface alteration nor gene-expression associations establish a quantified potassium extraction yield or isolated recovery product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Pinzari2024 used the source-reported ATCC 200175 strain, with ITS Sanger identity checking described in SI S2. SI S3 identifies muscovite NHM specimen BM 1937,454 from a granite pegmatite at Jegdalek, Siah Koh, Afghanistan. SEM-EDX and microprobe provided its structural composition; XRD detected no associated minerals. Approximately 2 cm2 flakes were ultrasonically cleaned in deionized water and autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 15 minutes. Flakes rested on sterile cellophane over 30 mL modified Melin–Norkrans agar in 100-by-20 mm Petri dishes. Three 5 mm plugs from the edge of seven-day cultures inoculated each dish; incubation was 21 days at 25 degrees C in darkness. The muscovite arm and mineral-free negative control omitted KH2PO4, with ammonium phosphate adjusted to balance phosphorus against the complete-medium positive control. These recipe changes are not a matched abiotic mineral control or proof that every nitrogen-related variable was identical. Residual potassium in agar and cellophane means the depleted arm was not potassium-free. Five biological replicates per condition were used for elemental analysis; five per condition were sequenced, although ten were initially allocated to RNA extraction. The assay lacked plants and is a laboratory fungal system, not demonstrated industrial bioleaching or plant nutrient transfer.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: pinzari_2024
Methods 2.1–2.3 and Results 3.3, especially Figure 1a1–a3, compare initial muscovite surfaces with those exposed to the fungus and document localized alteration. The comparison is before versus after exposure, not a matched, aged, fungus-free muscovite treatment. Figures 1b/c concern other minerals and do not establish their effects for muscovite.
Support Source: pinzari_2024_si
Methods S1–S3 and Figure S1 establish the medium, organism verification procedure, separate mineral/control arms, specimen provenance and preparation. Muscovite was assigned structural formula (Si3.13Al0.87)(Al1.72Mg0.11Fe3+0.19)(Na0.10K0.83)O10(OH)2. Reported lack of XRD-detected accessory phases is not proof that all trace contamination is absent. The KCl exchange treatment applies only to vermiculite, not muscovite.
Partial Source: pinzari_2024
Table 3 and Results 3.2/3.4 explicitly limit potassium acquisition: mycelial K was 6.80 ± 0.96 micrograms per gram dry mycelium with muscovite versus 5.52 ± 1.69 in the depleted control (means ± SD, n=5); both share statistical group c under the reported ANOVA/Tukey comparison. These are concentrations in digested harvested material, not percent release from the mineral or total recovered K. Mineral flakes were removed before digestion; carryover and abiotic-aged-mineral contributions were not separately mass-balanced. Trace K in agar/cellophane and possible inoculum carryover prevent interpreting detected K alone as mineral-derived.
Partial Source: pinzari_2024
Table 1 reports initial agar pH 5.6 ± 0.1 and final muscovite-arm pH 2.12 ± 0.19, but the depleted control shares a statistical group with the mineral arms. Figures 2–5 and Table 5 show transcriptomic responses largely overlapping potassium depletion, with some differential expression. Proposed redox or metal-binding weathering pathways are interpretations of expression and morphology, not knockout-tested causal mechanisms or direct enzymatic-rate measurements. No significant upregulation of the identified potassium-transporter genes versus the depleted control was reported. The earlier Pinzari2022 study is cited as related work, not counted here as an independently verified replication.
Context_only Source: ncbi_paxillus
Taxon 71150 resolves the species name only. ATCC 200175 is retained as reported by the primary study; the checked ATCC product URL did not resolve, and no independent authentication of the culture stock or strain-specific taxonomy mapping is claimed.
Context_only Source: geo_158973
The live series metadata links the same five-condition, twenty-five-sample RNA-seq study to the publication and taxon 71150. The sequence dataset is a provenance lead, not a new mineral-weathering experiment; raw read reanalysis and gene-level supplemental-table validation are outside the accepted surface and concentration comparisons.
