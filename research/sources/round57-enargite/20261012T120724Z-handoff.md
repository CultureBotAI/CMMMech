## Source conflicts affecting admission

- **Two-stage claim:** batch methods describe inoculation at the start. Section 3.3 proposes ten-day chemical pretreatment followed by inoculation, but no separate experimental curve demonstrates that sequence's headline yield.
- **Ferric doses:** prose assigns 74.5% Cu and 10.5% As to 7.5 g/L Fe(III). Figure 6 places that dose near 60% Cu and roughly 7–8% As. The curve near 74.5% Cu is labelled 19 g/L; the curve near 10.5% As is labelled 26 g/L. These approximate readings diagnose disagreement; they are not replacement measurements.
- **High ferric arsenic:** 35.5% As belongs to the 49 g/L curve, not every dose at or above 26 g/L as generalized in prose.
- **Ferrous doses:** prose assigns 62.4% Cu to 17.5 g/L Fe(II), while Figure 5 places that dose near 74%; the 5 g/L curve is near 62–63%.
- **Duration:** chemical methods and Figure 3 say 96 days; results repeatedly say 96 hours.
- **Pretreatment endpoint:** results assign 33.1% Cu at ten days to the 2.5 g/L Fe(II) abiotic control; the conclusion assigns it to 10 g/L pretreatment.
- **Lowest ferrous dose:** methods say 0 g/L; all Figure 5 legends say 0.5 g/L.

The three arms cannot exchange compositions, mineral fractions or residue evidence. Table 2's Cu/As composition precludes assigning all copper to enargite. Proposed enzymes, conductive structures, organic acids, microbial toxicity and secondary minerals are not directly resolved by the displayed measurements. Figure 4's surface spectral contributions are not a closed bulk sulfur balance or proof of complete sulfate conversion. Its arsenic spectrum does not establish biological arsenic immobilization.

The 1,000 t/day and 9:1 liquid-to-solid calculation is an economic scenario; the actual biological loading is 25 L/kg. Salt prices are combined with elemental-iron doses without an explicit salt-mass conversion. The sludge arsenic leach-test claim lacks a preparation protocol and underlying dataset here. Industrial deployment, verified costs, legal waste classification and safe long-term disposal are not accepted.
