Lazerckerite mineral IMA: Lzc

Lazerckerite (IMA 2022-113) is an IMA-accepted silver–lead–antimony–bismuth sulfosalt mineral belonging to the andorite branch of the lillianite homologous series (N = 4; 4,4L homologue, Strunz class 02.JB.40a, Dana class 3.04.15) with September 2026 IMA-CNMNC Master List ideal formula Ag3.75Pb4.50(Sb7.75Bi4)S24 (PDF p. 122, status A; written Ag3.75Pb4.5(Sb7.75Bi4)S24 in the article title and Abstract, corresponding to Nchem = 4.00, Ag+ + (Sb3+,Bi3+) <-> 2Pb2+ substitution degree L% = 93.75%, and Bi/(Bi+Sb) = 0.34; official IMA symbol Lzc, whereas Figs. 2 and 3 on p. 643 use label "Lze"; ideal composition Sb 25.21 wt%, Pb 24.13 wt%, Bi 20.28 wt%, S 19.92 wt%, Ag 10.47 wt%, printed total 100.00 wt%, arithmetic sum 100.01 wt%). Described by Pažout, Dušek, Sejkora, Plášil, Ilinca, and Dolníček (2024) from medieval mine dumps of the Staročeské pásmo Lode, Kutná Hora Ag–Pb–Zn ore district, Central Bohemia, Czech Republic (named after mining and assaying scholar Lazarus Ercker, 1528–1594; holotype polished section ST 48 deposited in the Department of Mineralogy and Petrology, National Museum in Prague, under catalogue no. P1P 11/2022, pp. 641–643). In the holotype sample (ST 48) it occurs as anhedral grains up to 0.6 x 0.3 mm in quartz gangue containing grains and lenses of pyrrhotite several mm in size (with no other base sulfides present), in association with terrywallaceite, Bi-rich jamesonite, and remnants of Ag,Bi-bearing galena (Fig. 2, p. 642); in other samples it forms replacement rims up to 100 um wide replacing earlier Ag–Pb–Bi–Sb phases along the sequence Ag,Bi-bearing galena -> terrywallaceite -> holubite -> lazerckerite followed by Bi-rich jamesonite needles along grain edges (Fig. 3, pp. 642–643), within the broader Kutná Hora Bi-sulfosalt crystallization sequence native Bi -> galena solid solution -> matildite -> izoklakeite/cosalite -> eskimoite/treasurite/vikingite -> gustavite -> Sb-rich gustavite -> staročeskéite/terrywallaceite -> holubite -> lazerckerite -> Bi-rich jamesonite (Section 2, pp. 642–643). Macroscopically steel-grey / lead-grey with a grey streak and metallic lustre, opaque, no observed cleavage, fluorescence, or internal reflections, and calculated density Dcalc = 5.920 g/cm3 from the empirical formula, 5.960 g/cm3 from the ideal formula, and 6.001 g/cm3 from the refined structural formula on the single-crystal cell (Abstract, p. 641, Section 3, p. 644, and Table 4, p. 646). In reflected light it is greyish-white with weak bireflectance and pleochroism (grey tints) and weak to moderate anisotropy (grey to bluish-grey rotation tints); reflectance values in air (Rmin, Rmax, 400–700 nm, Section 3, pp. 644–645, Table 1, Fig. 4; note Table 1 prints Rmax at 500 nm as "39-2" for 39.2%) at the four COM standard wavelengths are 36.1, 39.6% (470 nm), 34.4, 38.6% (546 nm), 33.7, 38.0% (589 nm), and 33.1, 37.7% (650 nm). Mean of 5 WDS electron-microprobe point analyses on the holotype (JEOL JXA-8600 at University of Salzburg, 25 kV, 35 nA, 5 um beam, corroborated by CAMECA SX100 analyses at National Museum, Prague, 25 kV, 20 nA, 2 um beam; Section 4, pp. 645–646, Table 2, p. 644) yielded Sb 24.91 wt% (range 23.93–25.30, SD 0.26), Pb 24.48 (23.92–25.21, SD 0.42), Bi 20.38 (19.63–21.02, SD 0.57), S 20.12 (19.88–20.37, SD 0.14), Ag 10.12 (9.96–10.38, SD 0.14), Cu 0.06 (0.00–0.10, SD 0.04), Fe 0.02 (0.00–0.05, SD 0.02), Cd 0.02 (0.00–0.07, SD 0.03), Se 0.02 (0.00–0.10, SD 0.03), Total = 100.13 wt% (range 99.33–100.99, SD 0.71), giving on the basis of 44 apfu the empirical formula (Ag3.61Cu0.04)Sigma3.65(Pb4.55Fe0.01Cd0.01)Sigma4.57(Sb7.87Bi3.75)Sigma11.62(S24.15Se0.01)Sigma24.16 in the Abstract (p. 641) and Section 4 (p. 646) (whereas Table 12 on p. 654 prints (Ag3.61Cu0.04)Sigma3.65(Pb4.54Fe0.02Cd0.01)Sigma4.57(Sb7.87Bi3.75)Sigma11.62(S24.14Se0.01)Sigma24.15) and simplified formula (Ag,Cu)3.7(Pb,Fe,Cd)4.6(Sb7.9Bi3.8)Sigma11.7S24. Monoclinic, space group P21/c (no. 14), Z = 2, unit-cell type A of N = 4 lillianite homologues (Sections 5–7, pp. 646–654, Tables 3–12, Figs. 6–13): single-crystal MoKalpha XRD refinement on a twinned 0.040 x 0.040 x 0.035 mm fragment (Oxford Diffraction Gemini with SuperNova CCD; twin domains 0.548(5) and 0.452(5) on (001); 2750 observed reflections with Iobs > 3sigma(I) out of 5189 unique; Table 4 on p. 646 lists 26,345 measured reflections, Rint = 0.0839, and GOF(all) = 2.097, whereas Section 5 prose on p. 646 writes 25,883 measured reflections, Rint = 0.0852, and GOF = 2.10; R = 0.1063, wR = 0.1876, R(all) = 0.1769, wR(all) = 0.2050, GOF(obs) = 2.651) gives a = 13.2083(9) Å, b = 19.4595(8) Å, c = 8.4048(13) Å, beta = 90.032(7)°, V = 2160.3(4) Å3; the ten independent cation sites comprise bicapped trigonal-prismatic Pb1 and Pb2 (CN 8), pure octahedral Ag10 and Sb8, and six mixed octahedral sites (Table 5 on p. 647 refined SOFs: M3 = 0.640(13) Sb + 0.360(13) Pb, M4 = 0.623(15) Sb + 0.377(15) Bi, M5 = 0.577(14) Sb + 0.423(14) Bi, M6 = 0.487(15) Sb + 0.513(15) Bi, M7 = 0.382(13) Sb + 0.618(13) Bi, and M9 = 0.825(14) Ag + 0.125(14) Bi, corresponding to Table 4 structural formula Ag3.65Pb4.72Sb7.42Bi4.11S24, whereas Section 5 prose on p. 648 gives an EPMA-adjusted site-population formula summing to Ag3.61Pb4.54Sb7.87Bi3.75S24 and Section 7 prose on p. 650 prints M9 with 0.835Ag + 0.125Bi) with a split sulfur site S9 (SoF = 0.5 in Table 5 on p. 647, with unsplit/shorter/longer bond distances denoted S9a/S9b/S9c in the Table 8 footnote on p. 651); because paucity of material precluded experimental powder XRD, the calculated powder pattern (Diamond 4, Table 3, p. 645) shows eight strongest reflections [dcalc in Å (Icalc; hkl)] at 3.3530 (100; -250), 2.9023 (39; -3-22), 2.9009 (37; 3-22), 3.4071 (34; 1-32), 3.4079 (32; -1-32), 2.1012 (29; 004), 3.0027 (25; 3-12), and 3.0043 (22; -3-12). Distinct from quatrandorite, terrywallaceite, oscarkempffite, clino-oscarkempffite, holubite, and staročeskéite; no criticality designation for lazerckerite itself, no microbial or biological mechanism, and no industrial recovery yield are asserted.
