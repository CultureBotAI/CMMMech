## Thermal evidence and unresolved mass accounting

Table 1 (p. 4) gives the specimen origin as Larderello, Italy. Its formula typography omits parentheses, while the accepted owner retains its existing IMA-backed formula. Figure 1b (p. 7) reports fitted starting fractions of 94.3% larderellite, 0.4% bassanite and 5.3% polyhalite. The displayed fractions sum to 100%; the two impurities sum to 5.7%. These are diffraction-fit proportions, not an independent bulk chemical assay or heated-residue analysis. Their individual contributions to later mass loss are not measured here.

Section 2.3 (p. 5; native XML 'sec2dot3-minerals-10-00902') describes approximately 20 mg TG on a Netzsch STA 490 and separate approximately 3 mg DSC on a Mettler Toledo instrument, both at 10 °C/min under 50 mL/min air. Figure 2b (p. 8) extends TG to about 1023 K and DSC to about 773 K. These are separate instruments and aliquots. The inspected methods state no coupled evolved-gas analysis or replicate uncertainty. The nominal 20/3 mass ratio cannot be used as an automatic correction factor.

Section 3.1's p. 8 thermal paragraph and the new JATS both retain:

- a six-step description but eight enumerated temperatures: 361, 383, 463, 483, 563, 573, 613 and 673 K;
- approximately 10.5 wt% assigned to the first pair, an unquantified 463/483 K group, approximately 10 wt% at 563 K, and approximately 12% assigned to the final three temperatures;
- approximately 32.5% total mass loss.

The three reported percentages sum to 32.5 only if treated on a common initial-mass basis. That does not allocate the unquantified 463/483 K group or establish whether the group assignments overlap. It must not be converted to a new disjoint stage table with a zero-valued missing group. Water, hydroxyl and ammonia are interpreted assignments, not independently measured evolved-gas fractions.

The red TG curve in original Figure 2b ends visually near 72–73% remaining mass, corresponding only approximately to 27–28% loss. The prose total instead implies 67.5% remaining. The approximate discrepancy is 4.5–5.5 percentage points. No raw curve was obtained or digitized, and the visual range is not an uncertainty interval or corrected yield. The separately measured DSC curve cannot close this accounting gap.

As an explicitly conditional chemical check, ideal NH8B5O10 has formula mass 236.111 using N 14.007, H 1.008, B 10.81 and O 15.999. Atom-balanced conversion to 2.5 B2O3 + NH3 + 2.5 H2O gives 26.28785% total volatile mass, 7.21313% NH3, 19.07471% water and 73.71215% residue. These are ideal-formula calculations, not this specimen's yields, a demonstrated pathway or an impurity correction. Their numerical proximity to the graph does not select a corrected thermal result. Likewise, the source's 12% ammonia assignment cannot be validated from this calculation.

## Product assignment and spectral boundaries

The preceding p. 7 paragraph explicitly cites Waclawska 1997, reference 58. Its 573 K water-removal statement, 603–688 K ammonium-to-ammonia wording, 723 K completion statement and 743 K amorphization/product statement are borrowed interpretations. The current PDF and JATS literally retain 'BO3' and chemically problematic oxidation wording. No correction to B2O3 is silently chosen. A secondary indexed abstract mentions B2O3, but the unavailable original methods, heated patterns and phase-identification evidence must be inspected before accepting that product. References to a separate 2005 larderellite study and other ammonium-borate calcination papers are additional leads, not replacements for the 1997 experiment or corrections to the 2020 mass balance.

Figure 1 is a starting-sample refinement and structural illustration; the discussion of cooling-cycle diffraction does not establish a high-temperature residue identification. No isolated boron product, closed boron inventory, phase-pure product yield or industrial process follows from these data.

Ten blank assignment-column groups are retained as missing, not zero. The spectral assignments concern the low-temperature reflectance experiment and cannot identify evolved gases or heated crystalline products. Literal 293/298 K labels differ across methods/abstract and figures/results; Table 3's 1.77 µm water-region label differs from Table 4's 1.76 µm. These differences are preserved without choosing a repair. The p. 17 concentration-like 7.63 g/mol wording is also retained only as source context, not imported as a measured concentration.

## The SSHADE lead

The DataCite 'larderellite' query returned 17 objects on its one declared page: 16 museum/IGSN sample records and one relevant SSHADE experiment. The latter advertises 14 near-infrared spectra, collected 2020-11-09, created 2020-11-14 and issued/updated 2022-04-23. It is explicitly linked to the Icarus 2022 paper, DOI 10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115055. Collection postdates the 2020 thermal article's publication. Shared authors and mineral name do not establish the same specimen or thermal run.

The DOI title gives 300–90 K, the abstract 297–90 K, and three grain-size ranges 32–80, 80–125 and 125–150 µm. The public landing says display/search are public but downloads require login. This investigation stopped at metadata, without login or numerical-spectrum acquisition. Its citation/reuse statement is retained separately; no open bulk-license or downloaded-spectrum claim is made. The official mineral page's alternative hydroxyl grouping has the same elemental count but is not used to replace the owner formula.

This is a useful spectral lead and a bounded exclusion from the thermal-data search. It cannot resolve TG/DSC normalization, the number of thermal stages, gas identities or heated-product phases.
