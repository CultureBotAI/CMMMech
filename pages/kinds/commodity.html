Bauxite

Naturally occurring, heterogeneous aluminium ore used as a commercial raw material. Bauxite contains varying proportions of aluminium hydroxide minerals, principally gibbsite, boehmite and diaspore, together with silica, iron oxides, titania and other constituents. Commercial uses include metallurgical feed, abrasives and refractories. This commodity is distinct from a single mineral species, refined alumina, aluminium metal and the red-mud residue of alumina production. The EU designations explicitly name bauxite within the combined bauxite/alumina/aluminium value chain. No microbial mechanism is asserted here.

Coking coal

Coal suitable for making coke, treated as an end-use commodity rather than a mineral species, the carbon element, coke itself or all coal. The EIA glossary defines coking coal as suitable bituminous coal. Its separate metallurgical-coal definition includes both coking coal and pulverized coal consumed in steelmaking, whereas the USGS 2026 fact sheet uses coking and metallurgical coal interchangeably in its coke-production scope. This record follows the narrower coke-making identity and records the original EU 2024 coking-coal designation without silently equating all metallurgical-coal uses. The experiment concerns beneficiation of selected Tabas feeds described by the authors as suitable for coking; retained coke-making performance after treatment was not demonstrated.

Electrolytic manganese dioxide commodity

Manufactured manganese dioxide products obtained by electrolysis. Tosoh identifies uses as positive-electrode material in alkaline primary batteries and as feed for lithium-manganate cathode materials, with application-specific representative product properties. USGS separately reports chemically and electrolytically produced synthetic manganese dioxide, supporting the process distinction. This commodity is distinct from manganese metal, the manganese element, natural manganese-dioxide ore or a particular mineral species, spent battery material, and a finished electrode. The manufacturer reports active oxygen expressed as manganese dioxide in one product table; this does not establish a universal crystalline-phase purity or grade threshold. The product family does not itself resolve the broader battery-grade manganese designation. No inherited criticality designation, unique crystal phase, or microbial production of qualified electrolytic manganese dioxide is asserted.

Electrolytic manganese metal commodity

Manufactured manganese metal obtained by electrolytic deposition, supplied in forms including flake and powder. MMC describes purified manganese solution deposited on cathodes, followed by stripping and product preparation; its separate low-hydrogen and low-oxygen products illustrate variation within this family. Tosoh identifies alloy-addition applications. This process-defined commodity is distinct from the manganese element, manganese ores, dissolved manganese, manganese dioxide, sulfate salts and mixed manganese-aluminium briquettes. Producer specifications are examples, not universal purity or battery-qualification rules. MMC distinguishes total manganese from a metallic-manganese calculation excluding nonmetallic components; its product page and linked 2017 low-oxygen sheet disagree on the oxygen maximum, so no numerical grade boundary is assigned. No criticality designation or microbial production of this qualified metal product is asserted.

Feldspar commodity

Feldspar traded as an industrial raw material, including mined and beneficiated material ground for glassmaking, ceramics and fillers. The constituent feldspars are aluminosilicate minerals containing potassium, sodium or calcium; this commodity is distinct from the feldspar mineral group and its individual species. The shared USGS chapter does not make nepheline syenite part of this record: it reports feldspar and nepheline syenite separately. The original EU 2024 Annex II(k) designation is mapped to industrial feldspar. Potassium release from an experimental feldspar powder does not establish manufacture of a commercial feldspar product or potash. No microbial mechanism is asserted.

Ferroboron commodity

Graphite anode material for lithium-ion batteries

Graphite raw-material products manufactured and supplied as active material for lithium-ion battery negative electrodes. This end-use commodity family is a subset of the broader graphite commodity family, not a second disjoint graphite identity; no single origin or numerical qualification threshold is assigned to all members. SGL Carbon describes synthetic graphite anode materials with properties tailored to customer applications and distinguishes them from carbon-silicon composites and conductive additives. The record excludes complete electrodes, electric-arc-furnace electrodes and spent battery waste. Manufacturer use and testing descriptions establish product context, not a universal battery-grade purity rule or a microbial recovery result. No criticality designation is generalized from the broader graphite record.

Heavy rare-earth commodities, EU assessment convention

This commodity subgroup uses the EU 2020 critical-raw-material assessment's ten-member heavy-rare-earth convention: europium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, holmium, erbium, thulium, ytterbium, lutetium and yttrium. Scandium is assessed separately and promethium is excluded in that source. The assessment covers oxide mixtures and low-purity single oxides in its initial-processing stage, and high-purity metals or interalloys in advanced refining. These are related commodity forms, not one mineral or alloy. The EU 2024 Annex II designation names heavy rare earth elements without enumerating members; its mapping here uses the explicitly stated EU 2020 assessment convention. Light/heavy boundaries in other conventions are not asserted equivalent. This group overlaps individual element records and the broader rare-earth commodity family; no group-wide microbial mechanism or strategic designation is inferred from individual members.

Lithium carbonate commodity

Lithium carbonate (Li2CO3) supplied as a manufactured chemical commodity. This product family is distinct from elemental lithium, lithium-bearing brines and ores, and other lithium salts. Albemarle describes separate technical, battery and pharmaceutical product grades; grade names do not make every lithium carbonate lot interchangeable. Its dated product 401125 technical sheet specifies at least 99.0% Li2CO3 and lists cathode manufacture among its applications, illustrating why a battery-related use alone does not establish a universal battery-grade threshold. That supplier-specific assay is not the definition of this whole family. SAMR separately registers YS/T 582-2023 for battery-grade lithium carbonate, but its complete technical tables were not inspected. No criticality designation, microbial production or qualified recovered-product yield is asserted here.

Metallurgical coal commodity family

Coal products used in steelmaking, scoped here to the broader commodity family recognized by the EIA glossary and the IEA Coal 2020 report. EIA includes coking coal and pulverized coal consumed in steelmaking; IEA includes coking, semi-soft blending coal and pulverised coal injection (PCI) coal. This family explicitly overlaps cmmmech:coking-coal as a narrower coke-making subset; it is not a second disjoint coking-coal identity. IEA excludes anthracite from its metallurgical category even when used in metallurgy. In contrast, USGS Fact Sheet 2026-3061 uses metallurgical coal and coking coal interchangeably within its coke-production scope. These source-dependent boundaries are retained rather than asserted to be a universal coal classification. The record denotes an end-use commodity, not elemental carbon, a mineral species, manufactured coke or every coal burned industrially. No numerical grade threshold, criticality designation or microbial mechanism is generalized across this family.

Potash

The commodity category of mined and manufactured salts containing potassium in water-soluble form. USGS distinguishes potassium chloride (muriate of potash), potassium sulfate (sulfate of potash), and potassium magnesium sulfate products in agricultural use, while also describing industrial uses. Rare-earth-element commodities, Australian list scope

This collective commodity family follows Australia's 20 February 2024 rare-earth-elements entry: yttrium and all fifteen lanthanides, including promethium. Scandium has a separate entry in that list. This sixteen-member economic grouping differs from the broader seventeen-element chemical definition, which also includes scandium, and from the EU 2020 assessment's fifteen-member light/heavy split, which excludes both scandium and promethium.
