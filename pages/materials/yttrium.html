Yttrium element Critical Material

The element Y, distinct from its trivalent ion, experimental chloride salt, host minerals and rare-earth commodity mixtures. The dated US designation concerns the named yttrium mineral commodity; it does not establish criticality of every Y-bearing substance. Laboratory evidence concerns yttrium associated with washed algal biomass, without establishing intracellular transport, isolated-product recovery or industrial deployment.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Yttrium association with washed Galdieria ACUF 427 biomass

other Microbial

Associated Organisms: Galdieria (NCBITaxon:83373) [TaxonMech]

Living cultures reported as Galdieria sulphuraria ACUF 427 yielded washed biomass containing yttrium after exposure to synthetic Y(III) solutions. The measured biomass-associated amount was lower in a quaternary REE mixture than in the corresponding single-element treatments. The experiment does not separate surface binding from intracellular accumulation or other material retained with the biomass.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
ACUF 427 originated from acidic soil in Gunnuhver, Iceland, and was obtained from the University of Naples algal collection. The 2022 source calls it G. sulphuraria; a 2025 primary study calls ACUF 427 G. daedala. The record therefore uses the verified genus identifier and retains the exact strain designation, without assigning the former species identifier to a newly named taxon. Cultures were prepared in Allen medium, pelleted and washed twice before exposure. Living biomass at an initial optical density of 5 at 550 nm was incubated in 2 mL water-plus-metal solutions in 24-well plates, with tilting agitation, at 37 degrees C for 24 h. Chloride salts supplied nominally 178 micromol/L of Y alone or each of Y, Ce, Eu and Tb in a mixture. The four initial pH values were 2.5, 3.5, 4.5 and 5.5, adjusted using sulfuric acid. The methods specify acid adjustment, although the abstract calls the solutions buffered. Metal-only and biomass-only controls were included; initial metal concentrations were checked by ICP-MS. Experiments were performed in triplicate, with mean and standard deviation reported. After exposure, supernatant and biomass were separated; the supernatant was filtered at 0.2 micrometres, and biomass was washed twice with water at the exposure pH before acid digestion and ICP-MS. Reported micromol/g values derive from the analyzed biomass fraction and its dry-mass denominator. They are not pure-metal yields. No resolved intracellular localization, desorption product or industrial operation is demonstrated.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: iovinella2022
Sections 2.1-2.2 and Figures 1b and 3b support the individual-Y comparison: at initial pH 2.5 the text reports 22.43 +/- 2.05 micromol Y/g dry biomass in the single-element arm and 5.08 micromol/g in the mixed arm. These are source-reported biomass-associated amounts from distinct assay arms, not an isolated recovery percentage or a general selectivity coefficient.
Partial Source: iovinella2022
For ACUF 427, Sections 2.1-2.2 and Figures 2b and 4b report no significant Y improvement across the tested pH range, unlike the stronger pH response of SAG 107.79. The abstract's broad pH conclusion is not applied uniformly to ACUF 427. Matched biomass-free controls and washing improve attribution, but the main article does not supply numerical Y control recoveries, complete fraction balances or a localization assay for these quoted results; its supplementary tables were unavailable in this curation. Binding, active uptake and biomass-associated precipitation remain unresolved; process other preserves that limitation.
Context_only Source: ciniglia2025_taxonomy
Section 2 and its strain comparisons use Galdieria daedala ACUF 427. This supports the taxonomy caveat, not a new yttrium experiment or an inference that predicted transporters cause the 2022 biomass association.
