## Szomolnokite evidence and limits

[Lacalamita et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/min11040392), Minerals 11(4):392, was re-inspected in full from the previously acquired 15-page original `/private/tmp/cmm-round97-sulfates-research/melanterite-thermal.pdf`. Its acquisition is round97's, not a fresh round98 download. All text, Table 1, figure captions and references were read; Figures 2, 7 and 9 and the reaction-scheme page were rendered and visually checked. The complete original announces no separate supplement. The live MDPI landing route remained inaccessible in this pass; a complete current supplement inventory and raw diffraction data were not available.

- Methods, PDF page 3: natural Fornovolasco melanterite was ground in agate, mounted on corundum, and analyzed by air-XRPD from 25 to 775 degrees C with 25-degree steps and 5 min equilibration. The stated scan range is 10-82 degrees 2theta, step 0.0263 degrees and counting time 257 s/step. That unusual reported counting time is not silently reinterpreted.
- Section 3.2/PDF pages 5-6/Figure 3 gives 78 wt% melanterite plus 22 wt% rozenite initially. Grinding is proposed, not experimentally isolated, as the cause of initial rozenite. Figure 2 and discussion on page 10 support weak, broadened szomolnokite diffraction peaks at 100 degrees C. Figure 7 shows subsequent decline; section 3.2 identifies only FeOHSO4 at 200 degrees C. Refined fractions are not isolation yields.
- Onset is reported differently: section 3.2 introduces additional szomolnokite/FeOHSO4 from 100 degrees C, while discussion on page 10 locates a szomolnokite peak at 75 degrees C. The infrared discussion on page 12 assigns abrupt loss to 100-150 degrees C, whereas Figure 7 and reaction scheme extend the loss toward 200 degrees C. Both differences remain visible; a single universal threshold is not accepted.
- Separate TG/DTA used about 13 mg under nitrogen at 10 degrees C/min. The reported approximately 260-degree DTA event is distinct from the 200-degree air-XRPD endpoint. The approximately 50% RH statement concerns the early room-temperature-to-50-degree interval, not a full controlled high-temperature humidity record. No independent heating-replicate count is supplied.
- Pages 11-12 propose oxidation using atmospheric O2, but an additional 300-degree nitrogen-treated aliquot also yielded szomolnokite plus FeOHSO4. This is qualitative phase evidence, not direct oxygen accounting. Earlier FeSO4 pathways cited by the authors were not observed in this experiment; their complete originals were not re-reviewed here. Structural diagrams are not direct observations of each atomic reaction step. Figure 9b's caption cross-reference to Figure 4 is erroneous (Figure 4 is FTIR); it does not support a different product assignment.
- KBr-pellet versus pure-powder/crystal infrared experiments have workup and saturation limits (pages 3 and 12). The shared experiment cannot establish microbial causality, a pure-szomolnokite starting substrate, environmental universality, extraction efficiency or industrial deployment.

## Paracoquimbite evidence and limits

[Ackermann et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.2138/am.2009.3179), American Mineralogist 94:1620-1628, was acquired and read in full. Tables 1-7, Figures 1-6, methods, discussion and references were inspected; the optical/TG page, Tables 4-6 and Figure 6 were rendered and visually checked. The actual first author is Ackermann, although the publisher's historical PDF filename contains Majzlan.

- Methods, printed page 1621: 2.93 g analytical-grade X-ray-amorphous ferric sulfate was dissolved after combining and cooling 3.1 mL water plus 0.526 mL 96% sulfuric acid. The open vial aged for one year at room temperature and 30-40% RH. White powder was filtered from remaining abundant mother liquor and dried at room temperature. Exact room temperature, pH, replicate synthesis count and product yield are unreported. The warning about excess acid producing persistent likely rhomboclase is part of the procedure, not evidence that all synthesized material was rhomboclase.
- Figure 1b/page 1622 shows the small ditrigonal product crystals. Methods and Table 4/page 1625 give a powder-based paracoquimbite assignment/refinement in R-3. Crucially, the detailed electron-microprobe measurements (35 spots) and single-crystal refinement refer to kornelite, not paracoquimbite. The broad abstract wording is not promoted to a mineral-specific microprobe result.
- Pages 1622-1624 distinguish TG water estimates of 9.02 versus 9.38 per Fe2(SO4)3, depending on plateau endpoint, from the adopted nine-water diffraction model. Paracoquimbite H positions were not experimentally located in that work. Supplement access remains unavailable; the printed footnote labels the deposit AM-09-052, while the current indexed MSA landing result labels AM-09-062. This identifier discrepancy is unresolved, not a license to assume a particular CIF. Detailed coordinates and re-refinement are excluded.
- Tables 5-6 describe measured dissolution enthalpy versus Hess-cycle-derived formation enthalpy. These are distinct quantities and neither is a recovery yield; no quantitative thermochemistry is imported into this owner. Table 6's kornelite cycle uses a 6.53 coefficient where the surrounding 7.75-water discussion would suggest 6.75; this kornelite-specific inconsistency is not transferred to paracoquimbite.
- Pages 1626-1627/Figure 6 explicitly treat entropy estimates and illustrative phase boundaries as uncertain. The initially impossible diagram and later adjusted diagram are not independently measured stability limits. The record scopes this limitation to the 2009 source, without claiming that later experimental phase-boundary work does not exist. The old polytype/dimorph terminology is reconciled with IMA 19-F's current compositional distinction.
