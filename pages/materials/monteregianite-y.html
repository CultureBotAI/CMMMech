Monteregianite-(Y) mineral

Hydrated potassium-sodium-yttrium double-sheet silicate mineral with current September 2026 IMA-CNMNC approved formula KNa2YSi8O19·5H2O, status Rn (renamed), and special-procedure tag 1987 s.p. (originally approved as monteregianite by the IMA Commission on New Minerals and Mineral Names prior to publication by Chao 1978, and suffixed Monteregianite-(Y) in 1987; listed under 1987 s.p. in the IMA Master List rather than a numbered CURIE). Chao (1978) described the holotype specimen (National Museum of Natural Sciences, Ottawa, no. 37130; additional study specimens at Carleton University) from miarolitic cavities, metamorphosed inclusions, and rheomorphic breccias in nepheline syenite at Mont St-Hilaire, St-Hilaire Parish, Rouville County, Quebec, Canada, associated with calcite, quartz, aegirine, microcline, albite, pyrite, sphalerite, rhodochrosite, fluorite, epididymite, ekanite, narsarsukite, harmotome, ashcroftine, and lorenzenite. Combined chemical analysis of colourless monteregianite (Table 3, Column 1, p. 563: a single wet-chemical analysis on 180 mg by C. Murray for SiO2, Al2O3, CaO, MgO, BaO, Na2O, and K2O; electron-microprobe mean of 6 analyses using a synthetic YFeO3 standard for Y2O3; and TGA weight loss up to 1000 °C for H2O) gave SiO2 60.30, Al2O3 0.50, Y2O3 11.97, CaO 0.65, MgO 0.15, BaO 0.35, MnO n.d., FeO n.d., Na2O 9.14, K2O 5.36, H2O 11.40, total 99.82 wt% (rare-earth elements other than Y were not detected by energy-dispersive microprobe analysis, and Ce, La, Nd, Gd, Dy, and Er were <0.05 wt% by wavelength-dispersive analysis; F and B were not detected by wet methods; a mauve variety gave partial electron-microprobe values Y2O3 10.67, MnO 1.38, K2O 5.89, Na2O 9.37 wt% in Table 3 footnote **** with trace Ti and Fe in prose p. 562), corresponding to the empirical formula (Na4.54K1.75Ca0.18Ba0.04Mg0.06)Sigma6.57Y1.63(Si15.45Al0.15)Sigma15.60O38·9.74H2O (printed on p. 563 as calculated on the basis of 28 anhydrous oxygens for an O38 formula unit, simplified to Na4K2Y2Si16O38·10H2O or (Na,K)3YSi8O19·5H2O; theoretical Na4K2Y2Si16O38·10H2O in Table 3, Column 2 is printed as SiO2 59.96, Y2O3 14.08, Na2O 7.73, K2O 5.88, H2O 11.23, total 98.88 wt%). Physical and optical determinations in Chao (1978, Table 1, p. 562) give Mohs hardness ~3½, very brittle tenacity, {010} perfect, {100} good, and {001} very weak cleavage, Dmeas = 2.42(2) g/cm3 (heavy liquids on 4 specimens), Dcalc = 2.393 g/cm3, and biaxial (+) optics with alpha = 1.510(1), beta = 1.513(1), gamma = 1.517(1) (Na light, 23 °C), 2Vmeas = 83(1)°, and 2Vcalc = 82°10', initially indexed as orthorhombic Bmab or B2ab with a = 14.014(4), b = 23.910(5), c = 13.096(2) Å, V = 4388(1.5) Å3, Z = 4 for (Na,K)3YSi8O19·5H2O (strongest 114.6 mm Gandolfi CuKalpha powder lines in Table 2, p. 563: 12.0(100)(020), 7.03(100)(200), 4.42(100)(240), 3.409(90)(203,143), 2.873(85)(441,044), 6.54(60)(002), 6.00(50)(040), and 3.028(50)(440) Å; readily decomposes in cold 1:1 HCl and HNO3 and slowly in 1:1 H2SO4, leaving gelatinous silica, p. 565). Ghose, Sen Gupta, and Campana (1987; spelled montregianite throughout their paper) solved the crystal structure by single-crystal MoKalpha X-ray diffraction on a Mont St-Hilaire crystal donated by G.Y. Chao (4877 observed reflections, R = 0.059, Rw = 0.061, Tables 1–4, pp. 366–373), establishing true monoclinic (pseudo-orthorhombic) symmetry in standard space group P21/n with a = 9.512(2), b = 23.956(4), c = 9.617(2) Å, beta = 93.85(1)°, V = 2186.5(6) Å3, Z = 2 for Na4K2Y2Si16O38·10H2O (Z = 4 for KNa2YSi8O19·5H2O), and Dcalc = 2.41 g/cm3 (non-standard pseudo-orthorhombic setting C1121/a: a = 13.973(2), b = 13.064(2), c = 23.956(4) Å, gamma = 90.62(1)°, V = 4372.8(10) Å3, Z = 4), built of alternating (010) layers of (1) an open mixed octahedral sheet of isolated [YO6] octahedra (mean Y–O = 2.261(30) Å; octahedral Y site refined with 0.60 Y + 0.40 Ce in the structure-refinement crystal, Table 2, p. 368) and three distinct [NaO4(H2O)2] octahedra (Na(1) mean 2.493 Å for 6 bonds or 2.551 Å including a weak 7th O(5B) bond at 2.900(6) Å; Na(2A) mean 2.416 Å; Na(2B) mean 2.411 Å, Table 3, p. 369) and (2) an apophyllite-type double silicate sheet ([Si8O19]6-, Si(1)–O(1)–Si(4) = 176.5(3)°, Table 4, p. 373) housing split 10-coordinate potassium sites (K(1) occupancy 0.62, mean K–O = 2.978 Å; K(2) occupancy 0.38, mean K–O = 2.991 Å, separated by 0.34 Å) and six independent water positions (W(1)–W(2) fully occupied in the octahedral sheet; W(3) occupancy 0.95 and W(4)–W(6) occupancies 0.35–0.46 in the channels). The second reference listed in the IMA Master List (Journal of Physical Chemistry B 102 (1998), 4379) was not inspected in full text and no claims from that 1998 study are asserted here. Distinct from Rhodesite-group silicates, apophyllite, ashcroftine-(Y), and elemental yttrium; no criticality designation, microbial mechanism, or industrial recovery yield is asserted.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Laboratory reversible zeolitic dehydration-rehydration and high-temperature vitrification of natural monteregianite-(Y)

other

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA on 4.1984 mg heated at 10 °C/min in air from 25 to 1000 °C, Figure 3a) and differential thermal analysis (DTA on 15.4 mg heated at 20 °C/min in N2 at 1 atm, Figure 3b) reported by Chao (1978, pp. 564–565) show continuous zeolitic dehydration between 25 and ~400 °C in three distinct stages (25–80 °C, 80–200 °C, and 200–400 °C) with weight losses in an approximate 1:2:2 ratio corresponding to DTA endothermic peaks at 69, 167, and 288 °C, followed by an additional ~0.25 wt% water loss between 400 and 750 °C (11.40 wt% total H2O loss up to 1000 °C). When heated at 10 °C/min to 650 °C and cooled in air, dehydrated monteregianite-(Y) reabsorbs ~99% of its lost water within 2 hours and yields an X-ray powder diffraction pattern identical to that of the unheated mineral. Incipient melting begins at 750 °C (corresponding to a sharp DTA endothermic peak at 738 °C), and material heated to 1000 °C converts to an X-ray amorphous glass.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory TGA, DTA, air-cooling rehydration, X-ray powder diffraction, and structural channel-water experiments on natural Mont St-Hilaire monteregianite-(Y) reported by Chao (1978, pp. 564–565, Figure 3) and structurally interpreted via partially occupied channel and octahedral H2O sites W(1)–W(6) by Ghose, Sen Gupta, and Campana (1987, pp. 365, 368–369, 372, Table 2).
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: chao_1978_monteregianite
Pages 564–565 and Figure 3a–3b document three-stage TGA dehydration from 25 to ~400 °C (weight-loss ratio ~1:2:2; DTA endotherms at 69, 167, and 288 °C), ~0.25 wt% loss between 400 and 750 °C (11.40 wt% total H2O loss to 1000 °C), ~99% water reabsorption within 2 hours in air after heating to 650 °C with full restoration of the unheated XRD powder pattern, and incipient melting at 750 °C (738 °C DTA endotherm) to an X-ray amorphous product at 1000 °C.
Support Source: ghose_1987_monteregianite
Pages 365, 368–369, and 372 and Table 2 resolve six independent water positions in the monoclinic P21/n double-sheet silicate structure: two fully occupied octahedral-sheet water molecules W(1) and W(2) bonded to Na, plus four partially occupied channel water sites W(3) (occupancy 0.95) and W(4)–W(6) (occupancies 0.35–0.46) hosted within the apophyllite-type [Si8O19]6- channels that account for the zeolitic dehydration behavior.
Partial Source: chao_1978_monteregianite
Page 565 notes that no single-crystal X-ray diffraction pattern of the dehydrated intermediate at 650 °C was recorded in situ prior to air rehydration, and the 1000 °C product was X-ray amorphous.
