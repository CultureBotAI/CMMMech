## Recovery and conditioning evidence

Journal sections 2.1–2.5 describe supplier materials assigned by QXRD to about 97.4% pyrite and 38.8% arsenopyrite/60.6% loellingite. A 1 g, 1:1 mixture was conditioned in a 100 mL flask at 30 degrees C and 155 rpm for 6, 8 or 10 hours. A flask's capacity is not its liquid charge. Living-culture arms used 1.5 x 10^7 cells/mL; the filtered supernatant was not a purified or composition-normalized EPS reagent. HH medium at pH 1.8 and Milli-Q water at pH 9 differ in both chemistry and pH. The 5-minute flotation used 0.4 L/min air with or without PIPX, and the article reports triplicate means and standard deviations.

Thesis section 4.2.4 (PDF 98, printed 81) explicitly refers all microflotation tests to section 3.3.2.2 (PDF 82–83, printed 65–66). Those original pages specify 20 mL preconditioning and top-up to 50 mL in the Hallimond tube, then collection, vacuum filtration, air-drying, weighing and QXRD analysis of both fractions. These details support the attributed record addition. They do not supply actual fraction weights or a recovery equation. The thesis describes 1 g for a single mineral source and 0.5 g for a differential-mineral fraction; this is not a run-specific weighed feed ledger.

The thesis distinguishes single-source Figure 4.13 (PDF 113) from mixed-feed Figure 4.15 (PDF 117), followed by growth Figure 4.16 and supernatant Figure 4.17. The nominal arsenopyrite source itself is a loellingite-bearing mixture. The mixed plots and quoted endpoints correspond to journal Figures 6–8. Thus the article's single-source wording in section 3.3 cannot justify pooling all baseline results. Pyrite bars above and arsenic-phase bars below the axis are a display convention; the latter are not negative recoveries. They are not product-composition fractions required to sum to 100. For example, reported 8-hour HH pyrite 70.6% and arsenopyrite 50.8% already sum to 121.4%; that diagnostic does not determine the missing analytical equation.

The 92.9% supernatant/PIPX endpoint is pyrite recovery at 10 hours. The 94.7% pyrite maximum with arsenic-feed-grown cells likewise cannot be relabeled loellingite recovery or assumed to maximize selectivity. Weak alkaline baselines and depressed all-phase flotation with pyrite-grown cells remain relevant. Conditional nominal feed calculations of 0.303 g loellingite, 0.194 g arsenopyrite and 0.487 g pyrite assume a mass-based 1:1 mixture and representative source assays. They are research diagnostics only, not measured run inventories, recoveries or accepted quantitative observations.

Protocol differences remain visible: thesis general methods give 20 minutes UV, whereas Chapter 4 gives 30; general EPS collection is late exponential, whereas Chapter 4 says beginning stationary phase. The 1 mL PIPX addition marked 10^-4 M does not unambiguously establish stock versus final concentration or reconcile the addition volume with the total. A 1 L upstream culture harvest is not a measured EPS composition dose in each flotation flask. The general XPS text prints adventitious-carbon 248.8 eV, differing from the journal's 284.8 eV; that source token is not silently repaired or imported. The broader thesis SEM timing includes 4 hours, so an article-only missing-panel observation cannot establish that the study never imaged that time.

## XPS comparisons and denominator checks

Journal Tables 2–6 compare arsenic-feed-grown culture at 8 hours with pyrite-grown supernatant/PIPX at 10 hours. These arms differ in culture product, substrate, collector and duration. The thesis adds HH 10-hour and HH-culture/PIPX 10-hour arms; these are fitted summaries, not raw acquisition or fitting files or composition-matched EPS-depleted controls. The matching tables and figures support shared lineage, but the thesis publication list does not explicitly declare the later article, so that lineage remains an inference.

Survey Table 2 uses atomic percent; Tables 3–5 give within-element fitted-component percentages plus a separate survey-total column. Table 6 alternates FWHM and region peak percentage, then appends survey sulfur atomic percent. Its region includes a Bi 4f contribution and an energy-loss component. In the supernatant arm, those are 21.8% and 4.3%, respectively. The region is not a sulfur-only composition, and multiplying its fractions by survey sulfur or renormalizing away those components would invent a different denominator.

The retained HO-C=O values are 6.9% versus 4.4% (a −2.5 percentage-point change), whereas C-OH and O-C-O increase. Elemental sulfur is 6.1% versus 3.1% (−3.0 points), whereas polysulfide rises from 5.0% to 9.6%. The O 1s metal-oxide fraction rises from 16.6% to 19.9%, while the total oxygen survey signal changes from 21.9% to 8.7%; a relative component fraction alone does not establish the absolute surface amount. The 23 saved arithmetic diagnostics preserve denominators and missingness. Dashes are unreported components, never zero-filled. No table is silently corrected and no spectrum is assigned uniquely to loellingite grains. SEM preparation and the absence of phase-assigned individual grains also preclude accepting viability or a loellingite-specific enzymatic process from morphology alone.

The thesis exposes one PDF and no embedded files. Its appendix headings/captions and representative original pages identify Py/Cp Raman/cold-stage XPS and Pn/Po room-temperature XPS, not a loellingite run ledger. Other mineral experiments are not transferred into this record.
