## Original 2002 model and the derivative coordinate checks

The original concerns natural Sultancayir material G3769 in the South Australian Museum. It reports synchrotron powder measurements and a constrained ab initio model. Boron positions were geometrically determined rather than freely refined, H atoms were not located, and shared isotropic displacement values and soft geometric restraints limit interpretation of local detail. The powder model is not single-crystal hydrogen evidence.

The original abstract on printed p. 1199 states [100], the body on p. 1202 states [001], and Figure 3b on p. 1203 draws the repeating chain along the labelled a direction. All five figures were inspected; no setting transformation or formal correction is supplied by the currently acquired sources that reconciles all three. The current owner deliberately adopts neither disputed direction, so this remains a source question rather than a maintained-owner defect.

Table 1 on p. 1200 reports 1,931 reflections while nearby procedural prose reports extraction of 1,019; their distinct procedural bases are retained. Its density is literally 2.427 g/cm³. With the ideal formula, Z = 4, V = 936.72 Å³ and the explicitly recorded standard atomic weights, the conditional density is 2.476478 g/cm³; COD 9004696 reports 2.477. This diagnoses source consistency, without selecting a replacement for the original table. The owner does not adopt that disputed 2002 density. Table 1 residual labels are Rwp/Rp and background-subtracted Rwpb/Rpb; the latter are not Rexp/RBragg.

Every scalar and loop in both CIFs was parsed independently for this author audit. COD 9004696 has 20 sites, four symmetry operations, no explicit H coordinates and no anisotropic loop; its attached-hydrogen annotations account for the ideal H inventory. COD 9014971 has 27 sites including seven H sites, 19 anisotropic rows and four operations. Its 173 K description is in the publication-title note; no ambient-temperature scalar was inferred from that note. The two models have literal P 1 21/c 1 symmetry. Symmetry expansion yields Ca8B20O52 for the first model before annotated H, and Ca8B20O52H28 for the second. The 2011 ADP matrices are positive under the recorded leading-minor check; seven nearest H–O distances were computed. This is not a full hydrogen-bond or refinement validation.

All 80 central coordinate/U values in original 2002 Table 2 (p. 1201/PDF 3) match the corresponding COD values after the explicit atom-label crosswalk. Printed parenthetical uncertainties remain in the rekeyed ledger; the CIF's central values do not recover those uncertainties. Neither CIF contains observed-reflection loops, a complete refinement instruction set or deposited original intensity data.

For each model, periodic B–O connectivity was calculated at 1.55, 1.65 and 1.80 Å cutoffs. Each cutoff yields 76 links per model, four tetrahedral and one triangular B in the asymmetric formula and two periodic components; their nonzero cycle translations are only ±(0,0,1). Thus both derivative coordinate models support c-periodicity in their literal settings. The repeated cutoff checks are not additional observations. These calculations neither rerun a refinement nor prove that the abstract/figure should be corrected, and they do not equate the natural and synthetic specimens.
