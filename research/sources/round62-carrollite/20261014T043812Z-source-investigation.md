## Accepted handoff: Yang et al.2015

The natural crystal was supplied by Luanshya Mine,Zambia. Table1 gives Cu18.42%,Co38.15%,S40.20%,other3.23%; Figure1 shows an XRD pattern assigned to CuCo2S4. This supports source-assigned natural carrollite, with the minor unspecified fraction retained rather than a claim of analytically perfect purity.

Culture ZY101 is reported as a mixture of *Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans*, *A. thiooxidans* and *Leptospirillum ferrooxidans*. No strain accession or authenticated membership is supplied, and individual causal roles are not resolved. Section2.2 mixes300mL culture and2700mL9K medium in a4L316L tank at45°C. The reported pre-exposure redox potential is about650mV against a calomel reference; the initial pH is1.5. Two10×10×3mm crystals are introduced. One is in a plexiglass container covered with a1.0µm-pore membrane intended to limit cell contact. Evaporative loss is replaced with sterile water.

Figure2 compares the untreated surface and the two exposure arrangements at96h: the directly exposed surface shows more pronounced pits/alteration than the membrane-separated surface, which also corrodes. Figure4 shows increasing pits, deposits and cracks across0,24,48,72,96 and144h. This supports laboratory surface alteration associated with the reported bioleaching system. It does not establish a dissolved-metal yield, statistical rate enhancement, purified product or deployment.

Evidence limits that must survive curation:

1. The membrane-separated specimen is in the same cultured system, not a wholly sterile control. The paper does not validate bacterial exclusion, match membrane mass transfer, report a membrane-only sterile control, quantify cells at the surface or isolate a particular consortium member's effect. Thus differences are compatible with contact-associated promotion but do not prove its unique cause.
2. Replicate counts, error estimates, dissolved-Co/Cu balances, direct measurements of EPS iron concentration, and a specimen-specific iron-reoxidation assay are not provided. The cited EPS concentration is prior literature, not this experiment's measurement. Bulk solution-redox conditioning and corrosion morphology are insufficient to establish a molecular pathway.
3. Figure3/Table2 contain a consequential sulfur-assignment uncertainty. Peaks near161.5–161.8eV are labelled sulfide at0–48h, while161.641eV at72h and161.606eV at96h are labelled elemental sulfur. Oxidized-sulfur assignments already occur at24h. Therefore these data do not establish an obligatory temporal S−2→S0→S+4→S+6 sequence. The polysulfide pathway, EPS-mediated iron concentration and passivation explanation remain author interpretations; no accepted pathway or product-speciation claim is required for the narrower corrosion observation.
4. Figure5 labels a deposited layer as passivation, but there is no paired dissolution-rate experiment demonstrating inhibition by that layer. Likewise, selected SEM fields do not establish population-wide mineral loss.

## Context retained separately

**Chen2016 mixed tailings.** Table2's96.51%Co and26.32%Cu at10%pulp/fourdays describe the mixed feed, not isolated carrollite. Figure2 is a chalcopyrite/carrollite pair at0,48,120h with a brown surface change attributed by the authors to oxidation. It does not identify the reaction product or measure a mineral-specific yield. HQ0211 is described as a mixed culture, and must not be equated with ZY101. The flask procedure is45°C; the separate5L experiment is40°C. Subsequent Cu solvent extraction, iron precipitation and cobalt-oxalate precipitation are chemical stages, not microbial product synthesis. Keywords and Section2.1 print CoCu2S4, whereas Equation4 and adjacent prose give CuCo2S4: retain the former as a source error and use IMA's ideal identity. Downstream reporting also disagrees: Figure6/text place large cobalt loss differently on the pH axis, while Section3.3.3 asserts only0.5%loss under its chosen combined conditions; unlike temperature/pH settings and no closed balance prevent accepting the claimed overall>95%recovery here.
