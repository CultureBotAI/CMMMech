Lead blast-furnace slag secondary resource

Partly vitrified residue generated during lead blast-furnace smelting. The curated example is granulated slag from the former lead-zinc smelter at Noyelles-Godault, northern France, containing a silicate glass matrix, crystalline phases and metallic lead droplets. It is a process-defined secondary resource, distinct from elemental lead or zinc, a pure mineral, copper-smelter slag, steelmaking slag, furnace dust or zinc-leach residue. Its composition is sample-specific. No criticality designation is inherited from constituent elements.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Heterotrophic microbial alteration of lead blast-furnace slag

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Pseudomonas aeruginosa (NCBITaxon:287) [TaxonMech]

Living Pseudomonas aeruginosa cultures altered a real lead-smelting residue in laboratory reactors with weekly medium renewal. Compared with sterile growth medium, the inoculated treatment increased cumulative elemental release into the operational digested-liquid fraction. Filtered and digested fractions differed, consistent with particulate association as well as dissolved mobilization. These measurements do not uniquely quantify cell-surface sorption, isolate a metal product, or establish a commercial recovery process.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Chapter 8 Section 8.2 describes duplicate experiments with 2.5 g of slag grains, reported as 2 mm diameter, in 75 mL of medium in 150 mL polycarbonate flasks at 23 +/- 2 degrees C and 100 rpm for 140 days. The three arms were ultrapure water, sterile growth medium, and growth medium with P. aeruginosa. Fresh medium was supplied every seven days. Per litre the medium contained 4 g succinic acid, 1 g ammonium sulfate, 1 g disodium hydrogen phosphate and 6 g TRIS. Methods specify NaOH adjustment to pH 7.8; results instead describe initial medium pH 8.2, increasing to 8.5 in the biotic arm and remaining 8.2 in the sterile arm. These reported settings are retained separately. General thesis methods, Section 4.1.2, identify the research strain as CIP 105094 from Institut Pasteur; Chapter 8 itself names the species without repeating its accession. An inoculum dose and a Chapter-8-specific slag sterilization protocol were not specified in the inspected methods. The digested sample used hydrogen peroxide and heating; the filtered sample excluded suspended cells and neoformed precipitates. The PDF prints the filter pore-size unit as micrograms, which is dimensionally invalid; a corrected pore diameter is not inferred. Separate polished-section observations at 60 and 150 days are not the same endpoint as the 140-day liquid series or the 128-day cumulative table. Industrial feedstock origin does not establish industrial operation of this laboratory weathering experiment.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: yin2014_thesis
Sections 8.2.1-8.2.3 establish feed origin, medium, duplicate control arms and ICP-AES measurement. Figures 8.1 and 8.7 and Table 8.3 support greater cumulative release in digested biotic liquid than in sterile medium under the reported conditions. This is a treatment-level comparison, not a demonstrated molecular mechanism. Figure 8.6 shows bacterial colonization of the slag surface; the neighboring abiotic images also show glass and lead-droplet alteration.
Partial Source: yin2014_thesis
Table 8.3 reports 128-day cumulative Zn release of 260.4 micrograms per gram of slag in digested biotic liquid and 257.0 micrograms per gram in ultrapure water, versus 42.6 in sterile medium. The water arm therefore precludes describing Zn release as universally or exclusively microbially driven. The filtered biotic Zn value is 136.8 micrograms per gram of slag; it represents a different sampled fraction, not a lower total extraction experiment. The source does not establish statistical superiority over water or a recovered-metal yield.
Partial Source: yin2014_thesis
Sections 8.3.1 and 8.4.1 and Figure 8.7 attribute differences between digested and filtered liquid to metal association with cells or biofilm. Filtration also removes neoformed precipitates, so that difference alone cannot distinguish biosorption, intracellular accumulation and noncellular particulate metal. The narrative's 28% dissolved and 72% associated Fe are not arithmetically consistent with Table 8.3 values 91.1 and 270.1 micrograms per gram. No corrected fraction or exclusive biosorption yield is adopted.
Context_only Source: yin2014_thesis
Section 8.4.3 and schematic Figure 8.9 propose siderophore complexation and cell-surface binding to explain enhanced mobilization. They do not directly identify or quantify those ligands in this experiment. Table 8.4 extrapolates complete leaching times while neglecting protective secondary layers; these are model extrapolations, not observed field lifetimes or industrial treatment times. Neither criticality nor a separated product recovery is established.
