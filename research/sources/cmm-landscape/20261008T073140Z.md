## Source assessments

### A1–A2: dated designation, not universal criticality

**A1.** The Interior/USGS final notice published 7 November 2025 enumerates
60 commodities, including Co, Nd and Pd (printed pages 50495–50496). It is
already cited by Pd. Add a separate 2025 Nd designation rather than replacing
its historical 2022 entry; Co could gain an additional US jurisdiction entry.
The bounded search and USGS FAQ point to 2025, but do not establish universal
absence of later notices. GovInfo's [policy](https://www.govinfo.gov/about/policies#public-domain)
distinguishes government works from third-party content. No local notice
download or XML export was inspected. Map to `criticality` with jurisdiction,
list name, dated edition and source reference; mineral/waste scope is separate.

**A2.** Original `32024R1252` and consolidated `02024R1252-20240503` are
different citation objects. This pass did not acquire complete annexes or
establish amendment history: challenged/empty responses remain failures.
The prior [cobalt review](../../../reviews/records/cobalt/20261006T042218Z.md)
verified the original Annex II §1(h), page 57; that is inherited evidence,
not this run's verification. Keep strategic Annex I and critical Annex II,
group scope and grade qualifiers distinct. New Nd/Pd mappings and current
legal-status claims require accessible authentic annexes. Reuse terms were
not freshly verified. This authority contributes no microbial measurements.

### D1: reusable commodity statistics with real parser and identity constraints

USGS's [report](https://doi.org/10.3133/mcs2026) is version 1.3, May 2026.
The dataset's [root metadata](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/696a75d5d4be0228872d3bf8?format=json)
records publication 6 February and update 27 May; its landing page displays
18 September. No separate dataset semantic version was established.

The CC0 CSV contains **8,886 rows, 12 columns and 127 commodity labels**;
those labels are not 127 elements. Its dictionary defines 11 columns, omitting
`Is critical mineral 2025`; that flag cannot replace A1. UTF-8 failed and cp1252
decoded the file. Observed: 15 blank units, 720 interval-year entries
(`2021–24`), and 20 duplicate groups under the proposed eight-field key.
Preserve withheld/unavailable/small-value codes, estimates and notes.
Use file hash plus row ordinal until observation identity is resolved.

There are 102 cobalt rows; two inspected 2025 production rows distinguish
mine production from secondary purchased-scrap production. These are economic
statistics, not microbial yields. Current schema mapping is a source and
qualified descriptive context. Structured statistical ingestion needs a separate
unit/time/identity design. Bulk raw CSV is retained temporarily, not imported.

### I1–I3 and D2: species, specimens, ions and occurrences are distinct

**I1.** IMA-CNMNC's dated 244-page PDF advertises 6,239 valid species; that total
was not independently row-counted. Page 1 defines fields/statuses; page 2 gives
CC BY-SA 3.0. Inspected cobaltite (CoAsS, grandfathered, page 48) and
monazite-(Nd) (Nd(PO4), approved 1986-052, page 146). Cobaltite's 1832 is an
original-description year, not an approval accession. These are mineral species,
not Co/Nd elements or a generic monazite group. Use `material_kind: mineral`,
sources and descriptions; establish any identifier namespace before minting a
CURIE. First-discovery country is not global occurrence. Preserve the exact
September edition and its license if redistributing source content.

**I2.** The sampled RRUFF ZIP has **25 spectral files, five specimen IDs and
four mineral labels**, with 13 processed and 12 raw files. These are not 25
independent samples. The R060020 processed corundum file has 1,238 numerical
pairs; its header separates ideal/measured chemistry, locality and specimen
status. Numerical-axis units and a general reuse license were not verified in
the inspected documentation/archive. The [status guide](https://www.rruff.net/help/mineral-status/)
also distinguishes identification confidence. R060020 is a specimen, not a
mineral-species identifier. The chosen archive demonstrates format, not a
Co/Nd/Pd recovery assay. Acquire relevant specimens and clarify units/rights
before reuse; do not equate portal totals with sampled coverage.

**D2.** Burger et al.'s USGS cobalt release (CC0) advertises over 60 selected
regions/mines/deposits, with a 1,000-tonne contained-Co threshold. Its landing,
mirror citation and metadata dates differ (2020 versus 2018); original version
notes remain necessary. The [INL mirror schema](https://maps.inl.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Critical_Minerals/USGS_Critical_Mineral_Data/MapServer/1)
exposes site/feature/reference keys, but its extent is narrower than the release's
stated scope. Original and mirror row requests failed: **zero rows inspected**.
Do not silently substitute a mirror, infer absent deposits or recoverable waste
tonnage, or use site IDs as chemical identifiers. MRDS was also deferred after
HTTP 403. Resource context requires original files, dictionary and reference joins.

**I3.** Issuing ChEBI pages confirm `CHEBI:30145` (lithium atom, charge 0,
`[Li]`) and `CHEBI:49713` (lithium(1+), charge +1, `[Li+]`). Their displayed
modification dates are 2011 and 2015, not this run's access date. Neither identifies
spodumene, jadarite or a lithium-bearing feed. Use the atom for an element record;
keep dissolved ion and substrate identity in mechanism context. No ontology dump,
taxonomy bulk dataset or general licensing assessment was performed for this
identity check. Lookup supports identity, not extraction efficacy.

### E1: actual experimental observations with explicit censoring and controls

Kane, Selvage, Ciechanowicz and Campbell-Hay's USGS release is CC0. The
[landing](https://www.usgs.gov/data/data-acquired-experiments-bioelectrochemical-systems-critical-mineral-recovery)
describes laboratory *Shewanella oneidensis* MR-1 bioelectrochemical experiments
covering As, Sb, U, V and Cr. ScienceBase/FGDC publication is 15 September 2026;
the landing is dated 17 September. These dates are retained separately.

Of 11 advertised attachments, this run read FGDC methods and three CSVs:
**T1: 61 definition rows; T3: 87 ICP-OES assay rows; T6: 22 digestion rows**.
T3 includes solution and digest-derived samples. Concentrations are
dictionary-defined mg/kg (ppm); negative numbers encode
below-detection limits. The general null convention is −9999, but T3 also has an
empty `K_ppm` at CSV line 74. T6 reports recovered millimoles from electrode,
holder/filter and reference-frit fractions, with different digestion procedures.
It distinguishes abiotic, biotic and initial-abiotic conditions, but has no Sb
row and only an initial-abiotic As row. SampleIDs are unique within each sampled
assay table; the independent forward test matched 22/22 digest rows to T3 by
SampleID and normalized analysis date. This does not certify experiment-level
aggregation or recovery denominators.

FGDC steps describe controls, electrode potential and extraction procedures;
abiotic Cr electrode digestion is unavailable. No recovery percentage, oxidation
state or microbial effect size was inferred from these tables. Next: reconcile
experiment/sample IDs, missing controls, censoring and electrode fractions;
inspect solid-phase files before claiming biomineral identity or isolated product.

### M1–M4: primary experiments and their associated data

**M1 — Marecos et al. (2025), Gluconobacter bioleaching.** Methods
“Direct REE-bioleaching measurements,” Figures 3–5 and Supplementary Data 4
support B58-derived biolixiviants tested on synthetic mixed monazite-Nd/
rhabdophane-Nd (Mon2), not industrial waste. Laboratory conditions include 1%
pulp density, 24 h, 30°C and 200 rpm, with WT/proxy-WT/no-bacteria controls.
Transposon improvements relative to proxy-WT are not absolute recovery yields;
a clean deletion did not reproduce one disruption phenotype. Retain that limit.

The actual XLSX has **42 observations/14 labels in 4A and 9/3 in 4B**, despite
formatting through row 270. Headers specify µg Nd/g NdPO4, pH and OD; no
replicate-ID column. Preserve row coordinates and δ/Δ strain distinctions.
[Zenodo 15132509](https://zenodo.org/records/15132509), version 1.0, contains
137 files; README, MIT license and two plate-reader samples were inspected,
without executing code. Article CC BY 4.0 and code MIT are separate rights.
Add a distinct Nd `bioleaching` mechanism only after identifier and evidence
review; preserve the existing algal biosorption mechanism.

**M2 — Schmitz et al. (2021), a related waste experiment.** Methods “Direct
measurement of REE bioleaching” and Figure 4 test clarified B58-derived
biolixiviant on retorted fluorescent-lamp phosphor: 20 mg/500 µL, 36 h, room
temperature. The reported 5.5% versus 4.7% total-REE extraction is 18% relative
improvement, not 18 percentage points and not Nd-specific yield. Controls and
replication are described; full text is CC BY 4.0. Six XLSX supplements and
genome accession JAIPVW010000000 were identified but not independently
sampled/resolved. This is a separate experiment related to M1, not a replicated
synthetic-feed study. Obtain element-resolved supplements before Nd yield claims.

**M3 — Kirk et al. (2024), conditional lithium evidence.** Methods §§2.1–2.3,
Results §§3.2–3.4 and Figures 3–6 compare mineral feeds with a live ore-derived
culture reported as *At. ferrooxidans* (genus spelling unresolved), uninoculated
medium and sulfuric acid controls (23°C, static, pH 1.8,
triplicates). Jadarite-bearing material showed a biological enhancement in the
tested comparison; sulfuric acid alone performed better. Spodumene and lepidolite
showed essentially no microbial enhancement. No isolated product is demonstrated.

Section 2.3 calls 2 g in 190 mL medium plus 10 mL inoculum “2% w/v”; arithmetic
gives 1%. Preserve the discrepancy. Resolve strain/taxon spelling and mineral
group identity. [Mendeley V1](https://doi.org/10.17632/djkf7tkm8d.1) advertises
raw data under CC BY 4.0, but file access failed; no rows/dictionary were inspected.
Retain qualitative supporting and limiting evidence; defer normalized numerical
comparisons until loading, denominator and raw data are resolved.

**M4 — Wright et al. (2018), Mn oxidation/speciation.** Culture methods,
Table 1, Table 4 and Figures 1/3 compare *Pseudomonas putida* GB-1 with defined
oxidase mutants and abiotic controls. Dark laboratory assays use pH 7.8, 30°C
and 100 µM Mn substrates. LBB quantification of particulate Mn(III,IV) oxide
equivalents assumes MnO2; it does not identify a crystalline mineral species.
Mn(III)-DFOB can also form abiotically, and light regime changes the interpretation
of MopA activity. Full text is CC BY 4.0; the light/dark supplement was not
acquired. No separate deposited observation dataset was verified. Candidate:
new Mn element record with `redox_transformation`, qualified biomass/ligand/
control context, resolved identifiers, and no industrial-recovery assertion.

## Priorities and curation handoff

1. **Extend Nd evidence with M1:** best combination of an existing record,
   distinct mechanism, accessible methods and sampled data. Resolve B58/strain
   identity and preserve synthetic-feed boundaries; cite sheet/row coordinates.
2. **Investigate E1 observations:** strongest new reusable multi-element assay
   dataset. Verify sample joins, censoring, controls and solids characterization
   before selecting one narrowly supported mechanism. Do not infer that every
   assayed element is critical in every jurisdiction.
3. **Add Mn transformation from M4:** useful material/mechanism breadth with
   mutant and abiotic comparisons. Confirm IDs; avoid an invented mineral phase.
4. **Maintain authoritative context:** A1 can add explicitly dated US coverage;
   I1 grounds mineral identities; D1 supports resource context. These have
   different roles and must not become mechanistic evidence.
5. **Resolve named gaps:** M3 loading/strain/raw data; M2 element-specific
   supplements; A2 authentic current annexes; D2 original occurrence files;
   I2 numerical units and reuse license. Keep negative results rather than
   replacing them with a success-only shortlist.

For records, map citations into `sources`; designation into `criticality`;
verified entities into `identifiers`/`organisms`; and assays into separately
scoped `mechanisms.context` and `evidence.explanation`. The current schema can
hold qualitative reviewed claims. Bulk observation/statistics ingestion is a
different task, not a reason to change the schema during discovery.

were not exhaustive or Crossmark-certified. Abstract-only leads on bauxite

chronology was unresolved at this run date.

universal list membership, or accepted new record is claimed. Canada, Australia,
UK/DOE lists, systematic taxonomy resolution and full citation-network searches
were outside scope. Source access, structure profiling and limited samples do
not establish whole-dataset scientific validity. All promising records still
require the repository's separate scientific review and history process.
