## Authority and natural-specimen evidence

Fresh [IMA September 2026](https://cnmnc.units.it/files/editor/IMA_Master_List_(2026-09).pdf) pages 1-2, 71, 112 and 204 were visually inspected. Stannite is Cu2FeSnS4, G, original-description year1832, UK; kësterite is Cu2ZnSnS4, G, year1956, Russia. These years are not IMA approval identifiers. Ferrokësterite is separately listed as Cu2FeSnS4, Rn,1985-012. Formula alone cannot identify the stannite/ferrokësterite polymorph, and historical Fe/Zn formulas do not supersede the current species boundary.

[Černý, Masau, Ercit, Chapman and Chackowsky (2001)](https://www.jgeosci.org/content/JCGS2001_1-2__cerny2.pdf), *Stannite and kesterite from the Peerless pegmatite, Black Hills, South Dakota, USA*, is a complete seven-page official original, printed27-33. All seven scanned pages were visually read, including all four figures and three tables; the PDF has no usable text layer, and its empty extraction is not evidence. The observed official landing-page PDF link led to the successful download. Current publisher metadata says27-34, while the actual original ends on33; both facts are preserved, and no DOI was guessed.

The authors measured four natural nodules (PPK-1..4) by electron microprobe and powder diffraction. Table1 reports Zn-bearing stannite PPK-1/4 and Fe-bearing kesterite PPK-2/3; two instruments and different internal standards were compared on two specimens. Importantly, page29 explicitly says that names follow chemical composition because cation-distribution/space-group correlation requires structural refinement. Unit-cell fitting is not that refinement. The record therefore does not certify cation order or pure endmembers. Figure3 compares natural samples with historical synthetic data; the synthetic points are not new natural measurements. Suggested rapid cooling/metastability and a roughly350-450°C quartz-core temperature are interpretations from other literature, not microbial assay conditions. Figure1, Table3 and text retain uncertain multiphase alteration assignments; no extraction yield is inferred. Minor source inconsistencies include O4 formulas in the page27 introductory prose despite sulfide identity throughout, and a tennantite sample label in page29 prose that differs from Table2. Neither is used to change identity or create extra records. No separate SI link was shown on the inspected publisher landing page; this bounded inventory does not prove no unpublished supporting data exist.

[Haase et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.5194/ejm-34-493-2022), EJM34:493-506, was acquired as official PDF, HTML and XML. All fourteen original pages, Figures1-8 and Tables1-4 were read and visually inspected. The natural samples are grains from old uncovered mine-waste dumps at Giftkies near Jáchymov and Kaňk near Kutná Hora, Czech Republic. Electron-microprobe composition, FIB sections, TEM and selected-area diffraction characterize selected grains; Figures6b and8b explicitly identify stannite. The broad stannite-kësterite series name and microprobe chemistry do not independently prove every grain's species or ordering. No comparable natural kësterite diffraction identification is inferred.

The 2022 study observes weathering-associated Sn-rich chalcopyrite, amorphous Sn-Fe-As material, native Cu/Ag and oxide phases. Diffusion-driven reaction versus dissolution/reprecipitation remains interpretive; static textures are not measured reaction rates. No organism or controlled microbial intervention was studied, so neither microbial causation nor exclusion is inferred. Natural precipitates are not recovered products. Current ferrokësterite identity differs from Table1's historical formula. Additional source-quality limits retained outside record claims are a dimensionally odd heavy-fraction cutoff unit, a ratio labelled mol% in Section3.1, Table3 cassiterite columns whose listed components do not reconcile with their printed totals, and AgO versus Ag2O headers. No concentrations, inferred purities or repaired totals are imported.

## Complete microbial original and arm limits

[Teh GH (1979)](https://gsm.org.my/wp-content/uploads/gsm_file_2/702001-101558-PDF.pdf), *Recovery of tin by geomicrobiological techniques*, Warta Geologi5(6):85-91, November-December1979, is in a complete27-page official society issue. The cover and all seven article pages (PDF3-9) were visually inspected, including all three figures; the following page confirms article end. This is not a claim to read every unrelated issue page. No DOI is verified.

Synthetic sulfides were prepared in evacuated sealed silica tubes and reportedly verified by X-ray diffraction; the paper provides no patterns, cation-order refinement or quantitative phase-purity assay. The natural stannite came from Hock Leong Mine, Ampang, Selangor; the natural cassiterite comparator from a different Malaysian mine is not a new owner here. There was no natural kësterite arm. The named pure culture was historical Thiobacillus ferrooxidans from Kambrischer Alaunschiefer, Ranstad, Sweden, maintained in9K medium. Fresh [NCBI Taxonomy920](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=920&mode=Info) confirms the homotypic-synonym mapping to Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans; this does not identify the unaccessioned historical strain or establish genomic purity.

The assay follows35days,32°C, particles below0.16mm and0.5% pulp. Prose says initialpH1.5 while Figures1-2 say2.5; neither is preferred. The separately stated0.1% optimum loading is not the Figure1 loading. Exact iron concentration, inoculum, assay volume/aeration, replication and uncertainty are not reported. Figure1 has a synthetic-stannite sterile trace, not separate matched sterile natural-stannite or kësterite arms. No matched pH trajectory isolates biological activity from acid/ferric chemistry. Direct cell attack versus indirect iron oxidation is unresolved.

The Results report54.45% Sn from synthetic stannite,31.30% from natural stannite and72.66% from synthetic kesterite. These are author-attributed percentages, not validated mass balances, isolated metal yields or measurements of natural kësterite. AAS with colorimetric confirmation establishes soluble-tin measurement. The Figure1 axis literally says tin concentration (x1000ppm), with an unreconciled concentration/loading scale and uneven printed tick progression; no graph is digitized or repaired. Separate organic-agent assays used autoclaved substrate/reagents, different pulp and pH, and water/sulfuric-acid controls. They do not establish microbial production of the added acids or an equal-condition comparison. No metal isolation, product purity, continuous process or industrial deployment was demonstrated.
