Chlorite group mineral group

Chlorite denotes a hydrous phyllosilicate group with a hydroxide interlayer, not a single species or the dissolved chlorite anion. The nomenclature authority distinguishes constituent species by composition and structure. The microbial study used a natural Michigamme, Michigan chlorite preparation with residual magnetite and another unresolved iron-bearing impurity. Its author-assigned ferroan clinochlore identity is retained as specimen context, without converting this group owner into a phase-pure clinochlore or chamosite record. No constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

MR-1 reduction of iron associated with a natural chlorite preparation

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 (NCBITaxon:211586) [TaxonMech]

Washed Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 suspensions increased iron(II) in a weak-acid-extractable fraction relative to uninoculated controls. Bulk Mössbauer fits support reduction of chlorite-associated iron(III), alongside unresolved impurity contributions. Most reduced iron remained associated with solids; small aqueous iron increases and greater susceptibility to a later acid extraction do not establish isolated-metal recovery.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory resting-cell bottles contained chlorite powder smaller than 100 micrometres, prepared by abrasion, hand picking and sieving of a Michigamme metamorphic specimen, at a 1:40 solid-to-solution mass ratio. The anaerobic medium was 30 mM bicarbonate at pH 7 under 80% nitrogen and 20% carbon dioxide, with 10 mM lactate and, in selected bottles, 10 micromolar AQDS. MR-1 was precultured at 30 degrees C, harvested and washed twice; the inspected main and supplement do not explicitly give the resting-assay temperature or final cell density. Residues were collected after 28 days. Figure 1 reports standard errors of triplicate measurements, without clearly establishing independent culture replication. The separate eight-week growth experiments used biotite flakes and are not chlorite growth evidence.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: brookshaw_2014
Figure 1B shows elevated weak-acid-extractable Fe(II) with MR-1, with and without AQDS, relative to uninoculated controls. The denominator is total iron extracted by 0.5 N HCl for one hour, not all structural iron or a recovery yield. Figure 2C-D and Table 2 show a decrease in fitted chlorite Fe(III) area from 10.4% to 8.2%; the authors interpret this as about 21% of the starting chlorite Fe(III) reduced. These are spectral component estimates, not a mineral mass balance. AQDS accelerated early reduction without a clear increase in its final extent.
Partial Source: brookshaw_2014
Methods explicitly retain magnetite below the XRD detection limit, and Mössbauer fits include magnetite plus an unresolved impurity. The paper calls the chlorite Fe-rich clinochlore although its calculated formula is Fe-dominant; no new species assignment or quantitative purity is inferred. Figure 1D shows aqueous iron changes, but peak timing in the AQDS curve differs from the prose. ICP-AES measures elemental iron rather than independently establishing dissolved oxidation state. Figure 3 is a subsequent 0.5 M HCl extraction, not culture-only dissolution. The proposed long-distance electron-transfer pathway remains interpretive; no molecular perturbation proves it.
Partial Source: brookshaw_2014_si
Supplemental Figure S2 establishes the initial source-assigned ferroan clinochlore diffraction pattern. Figures S1 and S2 are explicitly unaltered powders and do not display a before/after XRD comparison, although the main text cites them when reporting no appreciable structural change or new phases. Main microscopy and the source interpretation support predominantly solid-associated reduction with that evidential limit. Figure S3 and the growth/counting methods concern biotite only. No industrial process, isolated iron product or complete phase-specific reduction follows.
