Fluor-rewitzerite mineral

IMA-approved monoclinic paulkerrite-group hydrated potassium-manganese-aluminium-titanium oxyfluorophosphate mineral (IMA 2023-115; status A, Approved; full description published 26 June 2024; no mineral symbol is printed in the primary paper or in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 76) with ideal end-member formula [(H2O)K]Mn2(Al2Ti)(PO4)4(OF)(H2O)10 * 4H2O (formula weight 935.7 in Table 3, p. 547; requiring K2O 5.03 wt%, MnO 15.16 wt%, Al2O3 10.90 wt%, P2O5 30.34 wt%, TiO2 8.54 wt%, F 2.03 wt%, H2O 28.85 wt%, -O = F -0.85 wt%, and total 100.00 wt% in Section 4, p. 543; Hochleitner, Grey, Kampf, Boer, MacRae, Mumme, and Wilson, 2024, European Journal of Mineralogy 36:541-554). Named as the F-dominant analogue (X = OF) of rewitzerite ([(H2O)K]Mn2(Al2Ti)(PO4)4[O(OH)](H2O)10 * 4H2O, IMA 2023-005, Grey et al., 2023a) under the IMA-approved nomenclature for the paulkerrite group ABM12(M22M3)(PO4)4X2(H2O)10 * 4H2O (Grey et al., 2023b; Section 1, pp. 541-542). Discovered in a specimen of altered zwieselite collected by Erich Keck in the mid-1970s from the 67 m level of the Hagendorf-Sud pegmatite mine quarry (49 deg 39 min 1 sec N, 12 deg 27 min 35 sec E), Oberpfalz (Upper Palatinate), northeast Bavaria, Germany (Section 1-2, pp. 541-542). The holotype specimen is housed in the mineralogical collections of the Bavarian State Mineral Collection (Mineralogische Staatssammlung Munchen), Munich, Germany, under registration number MSM 80824, and a cotype specimen used for optical properties, powder X-ray diffraction, and the Raman spectrum is located at the Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County, Los Angeles, California, USA, under catalogue number 76307 (Section 1, p. 542). The holotype specimen consists of altered light-brown zwieselite with many vugs up to 5 mm in diameter lined with tiny (< 0.1 mm) colourless crystals of fluor-rewitzerite sitting directly on corroded zwieselite, associated with fluorapatite, white needles of Al-rich strunzite, tabular yellow crystals of Na-bearing jahnsite-(CaMnMn), yellow microspheres of Zn- and Al-bearing earlshannonite, massive black Zn-bearing cryptomelane filling some vugs, tiny white balls of iangreyite sitting directly on fluor-rewitzerite as the youngest member of the association, and older massive sphalerite, tabular crystals of columbite, and small grains of pyrite embedded in the zwieselite (Section 2, p. 542). Forms clusters of colourless stubby prisms up to 0.1 mm long, flattened on {010} and elongated along [100], showing the crystal forms {100}, {010}, {001}, {111}, and {-111}, with twinning by 2-fold rotation about c; measured density Dmeas = 2.42(2) g cm-3 (by flotation in mixtures of methylene iodide and toluene), and calculated density Dcalc = 2.418 g cm-3 (for the empirical formula and single-crystal unit-cell parameters; Abstract, p. 541, Section 3, p. 542, and Figures 1-2, pp. 542-543). Optically biaxial positive (+), with refractive indices measured in white light of alpha = 1.569(3), beta = 1.582(3), and gamma = 1.602(3) in the Abstract (p. 541) vs gamma = 1.6021(3) in Section 3 (p. 542), 2V(meas) = 78(1) deg (from extinction data analysed with EXCALIBR, Gunter et al., 2004), and 2V(calc) = 78.6 deg; dispersion was not observed, optical orientation is X = b, Y = c, Z = a, and the Gladstone-Dale compatibility index (Mandarino, 1981) is 0.035 (excellent) based on the empirical formula and the measured density (Section 3, p. 542). Raman spectroscopy (Horiba XploRA PLUS spectrometer, 532 nm diode laser, 100 um slit, 1800 grooves mm-1 diffraction grating, 100x [0.9 NA] objective; Section 5, p. 543, and Figure 3, p. 544) shows a broad O-H stretching peak assigned to H-bonded water with a maximum at 3355 cm-1 and a shoulder at 3115 cm-1, a hydroxyl ion stretching shoulder at 3570 cm-1, an H-O-H water bending peak at 1650 cm-1, three strong symmetric P-O stretching peaks at 955, 970, and 1015 cm-1 with weaker antisymmetric P-O stretching peaks at 1095 and 1130 cm-1, a strong peak at 835 cm-1 with a shoulder at 780 cm-1 assigned to Ti-O stretching vibrations of short Ti-O bonds in linear M2-M3-M2 trimers of corner-connected octahedra, and (PO4)3- bending vibrations at 600 cm-1 and at 425, 445, and 480 cm-1, with lattice vibrations at lower wavenumbers. Quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron-microprobe analyses (JEOL JXA-8530F Hyperprobe equipped with a liquid-N2 cold stage; 15 kV, 2.0 nA, defocused 8-10 um beam on Ir-coated polished crystals, with calculated water content iterated in the matrix correction procedure, average of n = 13 analyses on 4 crystals; Section 4, pp. 542-543, and Table 1, p. 544) yielded mean K2O 5.78 wt% (range 5.40-6.22, SD 0.20, standard Adularia), MnO 11.35 wt% (9.64-12.44, SD 0.91, standard MnSiO3), MgO 0.42 wt% (0.23-0.52, SD 0.08, standard Spinel), Al2O3 9.27 wt% (6.20-11.14, SD 1.35, standard Berlinite), Fe2O3(total) 7.01 wt% (4.04-9.81, SD 1.99, standard Hematite; partitioned on the basis of Fe2+ at M1 and Fe3+ at M2+M3 into Fe2O3 3.54 wt% and FeO 3.11 wt%), TiO2 7.64 wt% (6.93-8.94, SD 0.60, standard Rutile), P2O5 30.28 wt% (28.42-31.61, SD 1.02, standard Berlinite), F 1.88 wt% (1.18-2.42, SD 0.40, standard Fluorite), stoichiometric H2Ocalc = 28.80 wt% (15 H2O per 4 P), -O = F = -0.79 wt%, and Total = 101.28 wt%. Normalized to 9(M1 + M2 + M3 + P) cations per formula unit, the empirical formula is A1[(H2O)0.85K0.15]sum=1.00 A2(K1.00) M1(Mn2+1.50Mg0.09Fe2+0.41)sum=2.00 M2+M3(Al1.70Ti4+0.89Fe3+0.42)sum=3.01(PO4)3.99 X(O1.09F0.92)sum=2.01(H2O)10 * 4.12H2O (Abstract, p. 541, and Section 4, p. 543). Crystallizes in the monoclinic system, space group P21/c (#14), Z = 4. Synchrotron single-crystal X-ray diffraction (Australian Synchrotron MX2 microfocus beamline, Dectris Eiger 16M detector, lambda = 0.7109 A, T = 100 K, crystal size 0.040 x 0.050 x 0.070 mm3; Section 6.2, pp. 544-546, and Table 3, p. 547) gave unit-cell parameters a = 10.407(1) A, b = 20.514(2) A, c = 12.193(1) A, beta = 90.49(2) deg, V = 2603.0(4) A3, while room-temperature Gandolfi powder X-ray diffraction (Rigaku R-Axis Rapid II curved-imaging-plate microdiffractometer, monochromatised MoKalpha radiation, JADE Pro profile fitting; Section 6.1, p. 544, and Table 2, p. 545) gave a = 10.425(18) A, b = 20.591(18) A, c = 12.285(18) A, beta = 90.47(10) deg, V = 2637(6) A3, Z = 4. Full-matrix least-squares refinement on F in JANA2006 (with 2-fold rotation twinning about c giving twin volumes 0.800(3) and 0.200(3), 89 constraints, and 477 parameters) converged to Robs = 0.058, wRobs = 0.076 for 6186 observed reflections with Io > 3sigma(I) and Robs = 0.062, wRobs = 0.077 (GooF = 1.15) for all 6931 independent reflections out of 47535 collected (theta = 1.94 to 32.05 deg), with largest diff. peak and hole 0.99 and -1.25 (printed with a typographical unit error "e A2" instead of "e A-3" in Table 3, p. 547). The heteropolyhedral framework structure (Section 6.2-7, pp. 546-552, and Tables 4-9, pp. 548-552; note that Table 5, p. 549, reports refined site scattering A1 = 8.6 e and A2 = 17.0 e whereas Table 9, p. 552, transposes them as "A1, A2 = 17.1, 8.9") consists of (001) layers of [100] krohnkite-type chains of corner-connected PO4 tetrahedra and M2O4X(H2O) octahedra linked along [010] by M1O2(H2O)4 octahedra and interconnected along [001] via corner-sharing with M3O4X2 octahedra to form linear [001] trimers M2-M3-M2 (where refined site occupancies in Table 5, p. 549, and Section 7, p. 550, show Al dominance at the central M3a [0.753(15) Al + 0.247 Ti] and M3b [0.685(15) Al + 0.315 Ti] sites and at outer site M2a [0.593(12) Al + 0.407 Ti], and Ti+Fe3+ dominance at outer site M2b [0.476(12) Al + 0.524 Ti]). The eight strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines marked in bold in Table 2 (p. 545) [dobs in A (Iobs, hkl)] are 10.256 (46, 020), 7.414 (62, 111), 6.149 (100, 002), 5.190 (41, 200), 3.704 (64, -113, -222, 222, 240), 3.119 (74, 251), 2.866 (80, 104, -322, 322, 260), and 2.603 (45, -204, 204, -342, 342, 262). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
