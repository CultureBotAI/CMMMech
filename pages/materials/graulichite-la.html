Graulichite-(La) mineral

IMA-approved lanthanum iron arsenate hydroxide mineral of the dussertite group in the alunite supergroup (IMA 2020-093; status A, Approved 2021; full description published June 2022) with ideal end-member formula LaFe3+3(AsO4)2(OH)6 (requiring As2O5 33.49 wt%, Fe2O3 34.90 wt%, La2O3 23.74 wt%, H2O 7.88 wt%, total 100.00 wt%; Biagioni et al., 2022, European Journal of Mineralogy 34:365-374). Represents the La-dominant analogue of graulichite-(Ce) and the Fe3+-dominant analogue of arsenoflorencite-(La) in accordance with Levinson REE nomenclature. Discovered on a specimen originally collected in the late 1960s by Jacques Emile Dietrich in the active underground workings of the Patte d'Oie mine, Bou Skour Cu-hydrothermal mining district, Djebel Saghro mountain range, ~50 km southeast of Ouarzazate, Anti-Atlas, Morocco, where it formed by oxidation of primary sulfide-bearing quartz veins (chalcopyrite, bornite, chalcocite, galena, and tetrahedrite-group minerals) and occurs on malachite grown on iron oxides in association with malachite, agardite-(La), conichalcite, and an undetermined synchysite-like REE carbonate; the holotype specimen is deposited in the Museo di Storia Naturale, University of Pisa, Calci (Pisa), Italy, under catalogue number 19924. Forms yellow rhombohedral crystals up to 0.1 mm in size (internally porous and composed of very thin fragile laminae) with a light-yellow streak, resinous lustre, transparent diaphaneity, very weak pleochroism from light yellow to yellow, non-fluorescence, brittle tenacity, irregular fracture, no observed cleavage or parting, estimated Mohs hardness ~3.5, Gladstone-Dale mean refractive index n = 1.889 (Domain #1) and 1.928 (Domain #2), and calculated density Dcalc = 3.907 g/cm3 (for Domain #1) and 3.962 g/cm3 (for Domain #2). Micro-Raman spectroscopy (532 nm laser, 50x objective, 6.25 mW; Figure 3) displays bands at 846 cm-1 (strongest band, nu1 symmetric stretching of (AsO4) groups), 995 and 1092 cm-1 (nu1 and nu3 stretching modes of (SO4) and (PO4) groups), 714 cm-1 (O-H deformation modes), 466 cm-1 (strongest bending band, nu4 mode of (AsO4) groups with a low-wavenumber nu2 (SO4) shoulder), 565 and 618 cm-1 (nu4 modes of (SO4) and nu2 modes of (PO4) groups), 330 cm-1 and lower wavenumbers (M-O and lattice vibrations), a broad weak O-H stretching band between 2900 and 3800 cm-1, an unattributed sharp feature at 2906 cm-1, and a band at 1441 cm-1 interpreted as (SO4) overtone modes. Wavelength- dispersive electron microprobe analysis (JEOL JXA 8200, 20 kV, 10 nA, 1 um beam on an unpolished flat crystal surface due to fragility; Table 1) identified two chemically homogeneous domains (n = 4 spot analyses each) with low raw analytical totals due to crystal porosity: Domain #1 yielded Fe2O3 22.52(22) wt% (normalized 28.75 wt%), As2O5 18.55(28) wt% (norm. 23.68 wt%), La2O3 7.16(18) wt% (norm. 9.14 wt%), Al2O3 5.63(11) wt% (norm. 7.19 wt%), Ce2O3 4.28(13) wt% (norm. 5.46 wt%), SO3 3.90(16) wt% (norm. 4.98 wt%), P2O5 3.65(36) wt% (norm. 4.66 wt%), CuO 2.06(12) wt% (norm. 2.63 wt%), PbO 1.36(20) wt% (norm. 1.74 wt%), SrO 0.97(9) wt% (norm. 1.24 wt%), CaO 0.79(2) wt% (norm. 1.01 wt%), K2O 0.25(2) wt% (norm. 0.32 wt%), and H2Ocalc 7.22 wt% (norm. 9.22 wt%), total 78.34 wt% (norm. printed as 100.00 wt%, arithmetic sum 100.02 wt%), giving on the basis of 6 cations and 14 O atoms per formula unit the empirical formula (La0.34Ce0.20Ca0.11Sr0.07Pb0.05K0.04)Sigma0.81(Fe3+2.16Al0.84Cu0.20)Sigma3.20(As1.23P0.39S0.37)Sigma1.99O14H6.13; Domain #2 yielded Fe2O3 28.60(128) wt% (norm. 34.19 wt%), As2O5 14.48(73) wt% (norm. 17.31 wt%), La2O3 8.59(35) wt% (norm. 10.27 wt%), P2O5 4.89(55) wt% (norm. 5.85 wt%), Ce2O3 4.87(31) wt% (norm. 5.82 wt%), SO3 4.40(56) wt% (norm. 5.26 wt%), Al2O3 3.41(36) wt% (norm. 4.08 wt%), CuO 2.15(8) wt% (norm. 2.57 wt%), PbO 1.62(34) wt% (norm. 1.94 wt%), SrO 1.46(30) wt% (norm. 1.75 wt%), CaO 0.69(8) wt% (norm. 0.82 wt%), K2O 0.40(8) wt% (norm. 0.48 wt%), and H2Ocalc 8.10 wt% (norm. 9.68 wt%), total 83.66 wt% (norm. printed as 100.00 wt%, arithmetic sum 100.02 wt%), giving (La0.38Ce0.22Sr0.10Ca0.09Pb0.05K0.06)Sigma0.90(Fe3+2.60Al0.49Cu0.20)Sigma3.29(As0.91P0.50S0.40)Sigma1.81O14H6.53. Crystallizes in the trigonal system, space group R-3m (#166), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 7.252(13) A, c = 16.77(3) A, V = 764(3) A3, c : a = 2.3125, and Z = 3 (refined isotropically with fixed Uiso and 7 parameters to R1 = 0.0956 for 36 reflections with Fo > 4sigma(Fo) and R(all data) = 0.1222, wR(on Fo^2) = 0.2162, Goof = 1.237 for 47 unique reflections up to 2theta = 31.75 deg from 118 measured on a 0.040 x 0.035 x 0.030 mm lamina using MoKalpha radiation, Table 3; Gandolfi CuKalpha powder data refined from 13 reflections to a = 7.2469(9) A, c = 16.767(2) A, V = 762.59(17) A3). The alunite-type hexagonal tungsten bronze (HTB) layered structure (Tables 4-6) comprises 12-fold-coordinated A sites (<A-O> = 2.78 A, s.o.f. La0.95, BVS = 2.22 v.u.), octahedral B sites (<B-O> = 1.98 A, s.o.f. Fe0.94, BVS = 3.20 v.u.), and tetrahedral X sites (<X-O> = 1.58 A, s.o.f. As0.70, BVS = 5.49 v.u., where an apparent bond-length inversion X-O(1) = 1.65(9) A vs X-O(2) = 1.56(5) A x 3 is noted by the authors as an artifact of low data resolution). The eight strongest observed Gandolfi X-ray powder diffraction lines (dobs in A (Iobs; dcalc, Icalc, hkl), CuKalpha, Table 2) are 5.86 (m; 5.88, 60, 1 0 1), 3.045 (s; 3.042, 100, 1 1 3), 2.511 (mw; 2.513, 17, 0 2 4), 2.239 (m; 2.238, 24, 1 0 7), 1.960 (mw; 1.960, 24, 0 3 3), 1.813 (mw; 1.813, 23, 2 2 0), 1.689 (mw; 1.686, 5, 2 1 7), and 1.478 (m; 1.479, 10, 0 2 10). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
