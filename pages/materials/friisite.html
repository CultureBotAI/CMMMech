Friisite mineral

IMA-approved hexagonal anhydrous lead-aluminium-chlorine phyllosilicate mineral with a polysomatic relation to jagoite (IMA 2024-047; IMA symbol Fii; status A, Approved; full description published 2 April 2026; September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 78; note that the first sentence of the Abstract on p. 169 contains the typographical spelling "Friiste") with ideal end-member formula Pb8Al3Si8O27Cl3 (requiring PbO 71.38 wt%, Al2O3 6.11 wt%, SiO2 19.22 wt%, Cl 4.25 wt%, Cl = O -0.96 wt%, sum 100.00 wt%; Holtstam, Camara, and Karlsson, 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:169-177). Named in honor of Danish mineralogist Prof. Henrik Friis (born 1977), curator of the Mineral Collection at the Natural History Museum, University of Oslo, Norway, and former Chief Editor of the European Journal of Mineralogy (Section 1, p. 170). Discovered on a museum specimen collected from the Canberra stope at a depth of 220 m in the Langban Fe-Mn-(Ba-As-Pb-Sb-W-Be-B) mine (59 deg 51 min N, 14 deg 15 min E), Filipstad district, Varmland, Sweden, and provided in 1961 by mine engineer John Gustaf Herman Weslien (Section 1-2, p. 170). The holotype specimen (including a polished mount and separated grain mounts) is deposited in the type mineral collection of the Department of Geosciences, Swedish Museum of Natural History, Stockholm, Sweden, under catalogue number GEO-NRM #19610234 (Section 1, p. 170). Occurs in a sub-millimeter-wide fissure cross-cutting fine-grained quartz-bearing hematite ore, associated with jagoite (Pb18Fe3+4Si27O84Cl6), melanotekite, aegirine-augite, albite, baryte, fluorapophyllite-K, margarosanite, alamosite, native lead, a serpentine-group mineral, and a wickenburgite-like mineral (labelled W, Pb3CaAl2Si10O27(H2O)4; Abstract, p. 169, and Section 2, p. 170). Forms subhedral flaky grains up to 150 um in size (in aggregates of 10-150 um across), white to colorless in appearance with a white streak and a sub-adamantine luster; transparent, non-fluorescent under ultraviolet light, brittle tenacity with an uneven fracture, perfect {001} cleavage, Mohs hardness 4-5 (inferred by analogy with jagoite), and calculated density Dcalc = 5.54(1) g cm-3 in the Abstract (p. 169) and Section 3 (p. 170) vs rho_calc = 5.54 g cm-3 in Table 3 (p. 173) from the empirical formula and single-crystal XRD unit-cell volume. Optically non-pleochroic and uniaxial negative (-), with a Gladstone-Dale calculated average refractive index of n = 1.88 (Mandarino, 2007; Abstract, p. 169, and Section 3, pp. 170-171). Polarized micro-Raman spectroscopy (Horiba LabRAM HR Evolution spectrometer, 532 nm solid-state YAG laser at 2.06 and 5.02 mW, 100x VIS objective, 1 um spot, 600 grooves mm-1 grating, 15 s x 10 accumulations; Section 5 and Figures 3-4, pp. 171-172) shows no Raman bands above 1100 cm-1 (confirming the absence of H2O and CO2), weak SiO4 stretching bands at 1095, 1060, 1053, 986, 976, 969, 954, 907, 905, 883, and 865 cm-1, ring Si-O-Si bridging vibrations at 658, 611, 563, 561, 543, and 541 cm-1, SiO4 bending and (Al,Fe)-O vibrations at 482, 436, 409, 408, 340, 337, 306, and 303 cm-1, and intense low-wavenumber lattice/Pb-O/Pb-Cl modes reported on p. 171 at 29, 42, 52, 63, 72, "96 123" (printed without a separating comma), 179, and ~213 cm-1 (whereas p. 172 prints "64 and 73 cm-1" for the two low-frequency bands). Quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron-microprobe analyses (FEI Quanta 650 FEG-SEM equipped with an AZtecWave WDS system; 20 kV, 14 nA, 10 mm working distance, 20 nm carbon coat, average of n = 6 spot analyses; Section 4 and Table 1, p. 171) yielded mean Na2O 0.14 wt% (range 0.10-0.17, 2sigma 0.05, standard jadeite), Al2O3 4.42 wt% (3.27-4.87, 2sigma 1.10, standard almandine), SiO2 19.80 wt% (19.10-20.38, 2sigma 0.93, standard almandine), Cl 4.19 wt% (3.99-4.42, 2sigma 0.30, standard tugtupite), CaO 0.19 wt% (0.16-0.26, 2sigma 0.07, standard bustamite), MnO 0.31 wt% (0.17-0.57, 2sigma 0.30, standard rhodonite), Fe2O3 0.63 wt% (0.45-0.91, 2sigma 0.33, standard hematite), ZnO 0.42 wt% (0.34-0.61, 2sigma 0.18, standard willemite), PbO 69.83 wt% (69.10-71.16, 2sigma 1.50, standard crocoite), O = Cl = -0.94 wt%, and printed Total = 98.98 wt% (range 98.26-99.99; note that the exact arithmetic sum of the rounded mean wt% values is 0.14 + 4.42 + 19.80 + 4.19 + 0.19 + 0.31 + 0.63 + 0.42 + 69.83 - 0.94 = 98.99 wt%, a 0.01 wt% rounding difference). Normalized on a basis of 30 anions (O + Cl), the empirical formula is (Pb7.89Na0.11Ca0.08)sum=8.08(Al2.19Si0.31Fe3+0.20Zn0.13Mn2+0.11)sum=2.94Si8O27.02Cl2.98, with Al2O3 negatively correlated with Fe2O3 + MnO + ZnO (linear R2 = 0.95; Section 4, p. 171). Crystallizes in the hexagonal system (ditrigonal dipyramidal class, point group -6m2), space group P-62c (#190), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters (Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy diffractometer, PhotonJet microfocus MoKalpha radiation lambda = 0.71073 A, HyPix-6000HE detector, T = 293 K, crystal size 0.102 x 0.063 x 0.033 mm3; Section 6.2, p. 172, and Table 3, p. 173) a = 8.5955(1) A (Abstract, p. 169) / 8.59550(10) A (Table 3, p. 173), c = 23.4092(2) A, V = 1497.82(4) A3, Z = 2, and c:a = 2.7234, while Gandolfi powder X-ray diffraction (lambda = 0.71073 A; Section 6.1 and Table 2, p. 172) gave a = 8.599(6) A, c = 23.379(8) A, V = 1497.11(4) A3, Z = 2. Full-matrix least-squares refinement on F2 in SHELXL (87 parameters, 0 restraints, merohedral twin ratio 0.735(7)/0.265(7), Flack parameter 0.000(3)) converged to R1 = 0.0144 for reflections with I > 2sigma(I) and R1 = 0.0163 (GooF = 1.062) for all 2461 independent reflections out of 60680 collected (2theta = 3.48 to 72.776 deg, Rint = 0.0500, Rsigma = 0.0160), with largest diff. peak and hole 1.16 and -1.03 e A-3 (Table 3, p. 173). The layered phyllosilicate structure (Section 6.2 and Tables 4-6, pp. 172-175; Nickel-Strunz class 9.EG.45) is a 1:1 polysome of single tetrahedral layers (SL, formed by 6-membered rings of [Si2O4] tetrahedra, <Si2-O> = 1.617 A) and double tetrahedral layers (DL, formed by [Si1O4] tetrahedra, <Si1-O> = 1.619 A, and Al-dominant mixed T tetrahedra with assigned occupancy 0.422 Al + 0.308 Si + 0.144 Zn + 0.126 Mn and <T-O> = 1.716 A) alternating along [001] with octahedral layers of [AlO6] octahedra (<Al-O> = 1.892 A, with minor Fe3+), three inequivalent Pb sites (Pb1, Pb2, Pb3), and two Cl sites (Cl1, Cl2) (compared to SL/DL = 2:1 in jagoite; note also that reaction (R2) in Section 7.1, p. 176, prints "NaAlSi3O8 (albite)" without the stoichiometric coefficient 3 required to balance 3Na+ and Si/Al). The eight strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines (Abstract, p. 169, and Table 2, p. 172) [d in A (I, hkl)] are 3.896 (38, 006), 3.428 (100, 114), 3.214 (49, 203), 2.990 (94, 204), 2.799 (88, 121), 2.651 (39, 108), 2.519 (44, 124), and 1.894 (39, 1 1 11). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
