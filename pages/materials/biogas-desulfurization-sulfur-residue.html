Biogas-desulfurization sulfur residue secondary resource

Sulfur-rich material recovered from biogas desulfurization, represented by dried powder obtained from a Danish biogas-plant desulfurization unit. Its process origin defines this secondary resource; it is not pure sulfur as an element, a natural sulfur mineral, commercial acid or the battery black mass subsequently leached with its conversion liquor. The selected study does not establish universal residue composition or inherited criticality.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated conversion of recovered sulfur to acidic liquor

redox transformation Microbial

A living mixed acidophile culture was cultivated with biogas-derived sulfur powder to produce sulfate-containing acidic liquor for subsequent leaching. This is an observed laboratory use of the sulfur resource as an oxidation substrate. One reported strain identifier conflicts with its collection assignment, so exact culture membership and strain-specific causality remain unresolved. No species identifier is assigned to the mixed mechanism.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Section 2.2 identifies dried powdered sulfur from a biogas desulfurization unit in Denmark, without a supplier-specific composition, purity or drying protocol. The paper names A. thiooxidans DSM 1927 and A. caldus DSM 9466, separately pregrown for 21 days at 30 degrees C and 150 rpm in 250 mL flasks, then combined 1:1 v/v. The DSM 1927 species conflict remains explicit rather than being repaired from assumption. Acid production used a 2 L reactor with 1.8 L working volume, initial medium pH 3.5, 1% w/v sulfur, 30 degrees C, 250 rpm magnetic stirring and 50 L/h pressurized air. The medium is source-labelled basalt salt medium with a literature reference rather than a complete recipe in this paper. After 14-21 days, 1.2 L was withdrawn and replaced with fresh medium and sulfur; the remaining 0.6 L inoculated the next cycle. Ten cycles yielded approximately 12 L collected liquor. Withdrawals were centrifuged at 9000 rpm for 20 minutes to remove solids and stored at 4 degrees C. A verified sterile filtrate is not established. Ion chromatography and NaOH titration with phenolphthalein were used for the reported acid concentration. Independent acid-production replication, uninoculated residue controls, per-cycle sulfur accounting and residue characterization are not reported here.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: lalropuia2026
Methods 2.2 and Results 3.1 directly connect the actual desulfurization powder to cultivated acidic liquor. Results report 0.63 M biogenic sulfuric acid and pH 0.44 in liquor used for downstream experiments. These reported solution measurements are not a single-cycle sulfur conversion fraction, acid purity specification, isolated-product yield or validated mass balance. Retained liquor and repeated sulfur additions prevent deriving conversion from the nominal initial loading alone.
Partial Source: dsm1927
The live collection identifies DSM 1927 as A. ferrooxidans F221, whereas the paper calls it A. thiooxidans. Neither the catalogue nor the paper resolves which stock was used. The record therefore preserves the mixed-culture observation without a verified assignment of its first member or a strain-specific mechanism.
Context_only Source: dsm9466
DSM 9466 resolves to A. caldus C-SH12, consistent with the paper's second culture label. This supports collection nomenclature only; no pure-culture comparator or independent stock authentication in this study establishes which member caused the observed acid production.
Partial Source: lalropuia2026
Faster oxidation than commercial sulfur and benefits of organic coatings are attributed to earlier publications in Section 3.1, not demonstrated by matched sulfur-source controls in this experiment. The repeated-cycle acid-production protocol does not establish a universal feed advantage, complete sulfur recovery or an identified molecular pathway. No economic or life-cycle assessment of this process is supplied.
Context_only Source: lalropuia2026
Sections 2.3-2.4 and Figures 1-4 concern a different material, NMC battery black mass, leached with the collected liquor at 22 degrees C with peroxide and surfactant comparisons. Those duplicate laboratory assays, including a nominal 1 L vessel, are downstream use evidence rather than measurements of the sulfur residue. Biosurfactants did not improve metal leaching; peroxide-free controls also gave the reported high Li/Mn endpoints. These comparisons do not establish sulfur-source superiority or live-cell necessity. Filtered-liquid metal release and local SEM-EDS non-detection do not prove isolated metal products or industrial deployment.
