Hematite mineral

Hematite is the Fe2O3 mineral species listed as approved (A), with 1971 special-procedure status, in the IMA-CNMNC September 2026 master list. It is distinct from iron element, iron-ore commodities, other Fe2O3 polymorphs and mixed iron-bearing feeds. The experimental author-designated hematite below has substantial non-Fe oxide components; its bulk Fe2O3 percentage is not a quantified hematite phase abundance. No constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

PMA2-2 culture-associated iron mobilization from a hematite feed

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Priestia megaterium (NCBITaxon:1404) [TaxonMech]

PMA2-2 cultures produced higher filtered Fe concentrations than uninoculated hematite-feed controls during a short laboratory assay. Culture acidification accompanied this mobilization; the individual chemical and cellular contributions remain unresolved.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Living Priestia megaterium PMA2-2, isolated from Dracaena cochinchinensis rhizosphere soil in Purple Mountain, Nanjing, China, was precultured in LB for 12 hours at 37 degrees C and 160 rpm, harvested, washed and resuspended in 0.85% NaCl. The other-mineral assays followed the dissolution protocol: triplicate 220 mL flasks with 60 mL sterilized glucose minimal medium, 0.60 g of 75-150 micrometre mineral feed and 0.2 mL of 10^8 cells/mL suspension, incubated at 37 degrees C and 160 rpm, with separate flasks for 12- and 24-hour endpoints. Medium preparation pH was 7.0; its stated composition was 0.015% MgSO4.7H2O, 0.05% Na2HPO4.2H2O, 0.025% (NH4)2SO4, 0.01% KNO3 and 0.5% glucose. Element measurements used ICP-OES after centrifugation and 0.45 micrometre polyethersulfone filtration of a 10 mL aliquot; these are operationally filtered total-element concentrations, without oxidation-state resolution. Feed origin, cleaning procedure and quantitative phase analysis were not specified in the inspected methods and supplement. Table S7 reports 86.58% Fe2O3 and 9.82% SiO2, with other minor oxides, for the hematite feed. The organism is linked to GenBank CM125967.1; the paper's duplicated accession in its first Methods paragraph is not used to identify the comparator strain.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: dong_2026
Supplement Table S6 (p11; mean +/- SD, n=3) reports filtered Fe of 25.95 +/- 0.90 and 71.43 +/- 1.56 micromol/L at 12 and 24 hours with PMA2-2, versus 0.04 +/- 0.00 micromol/L at both uninoculated endpoints. Culture pH was 4.69 +/- 0.05 and 4.66 +/- 0.06, versus control pH 7.16 +/- 0.04 and 7.12 +/- 0.05. These values support culture-associated mobilization from the stated impure feed; the rounded control SD is not proof of analytical exactness.
Partial Source: dong_2026
Uninoculated feed controls establish the conditional culture-associated difference, but no pH-matched or killed-cell hematite/feldspar comparator separates acidification from other effects. The acid-buffer comparison, detailed transcriptomics, metabolomics, EPS and biofilm assays concern biotite; their quantitative or causal results are not transferred to this feed. Figure 5 reports a separate 12-hour RT-qPCR response to this mineral; expression associations do not demonstrate gene necessity, and the Discussion explicitly calls for functional validation. No isolated-metal product, whole-feed recovery percentage, mineral-specific mass balance or industrial deployment is demonstrated.

Priestia megaterium PMA2-2 mineral-inducible gene response, culture acidification, and iron mobilization from hematite feed

REPRESENTATIVE EXAMPLE Mechanism: pma2_2_hematite_dissolution

Causal pathway linking the six mineral-inducible genes verified by 12-h RT-qPCR on hematite in Priestia megaterium PMA2-2 (Figure 5 and Table 2 of Dong et al., 2026: spo0A / UniProtKB:P52932 and UP000031829 UniProtKB:A0A0B6ANB3, acs / acsA UniProtKB:D5DTY8, wecC / UniProtKB:A0A0B6ASU1, lysA / UniProtKB:A0A0B6ACY5, flgF / UniProtKB:A0A0B6AS33, and ltaS / ltaS1 UniProtKB:A0A0B6APG1, induced 1.4- to 395-fold across tested minerals with ltaS peaking in hematite) to culture-associated acidification of glucose minimal medium (pH 4.69 +/- 0.05 at 12 h and 4.66 +/- 0.06 at 24 h vs 7.16 +/- 0.04 and 7.12 +/- 0.05 in uninoculated controls, Table S6) and 0.45-um-filtered Fe mobilization (25.95 +/- 0.90 and 71.43 +/- 1.56 uM at 12 and 24 h vs 0.04 +/- 0.00 uM in controls) from 0.60 g hematite feed (75-150 um; 86.58% Fe2O3, 9.82% SiO2, Table S7), while preserving that no pH-matched abiotic hematite control or gene knockout was performed.
