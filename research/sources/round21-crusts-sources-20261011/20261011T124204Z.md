## Claim-level evidence and decisions

1. **USGS Fact Sheet 2025–3017, v1.1, April 2025 — accept identity.** Re-inspected page 2 and rendered Table 1. It places cobalt-rich crusts on rock surfaces and nodules on sediment-covered abyssal plains. Its 2022-list asterisks describe individual constituents, so no resource criticality is inherited. Page 2 sample photographs support the physical distinction, not a uniform mineral formula.
2. **Maciąg et al. 2019, DOI [10.3390/min9020084](https://doi.org/10.3390/min9020084) — accept primary material identity.** Read Introduction, Methods 3, Results 4.1–4.2 and source descriptions; visually checked Table 1/Figures 2–3. Three dredged Dirck Hartog Ridge samples and subdivided basalt/crust interfaces are characterized using microscopy, diffraction and microanalysis. The study supports a heterogeneous, naturally occurring material. Low crystallinity and method-specific phase identification remain source limitations; no pure phase, universal grade, microbial assay or recovery claim is derived. The 33-page PDF states CC BY 4.0 on its final page.
3. **Nakazawa and Sato 1995, DOI [10.1016/0301-7516(95)00005-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0301-7516(95)00005-X) — defer mechanism.** Crossref confirms authors, title and June 1995 publication. Full methods/figures were not acquired. Sulfur/pyrite and bacterial-control claims in citation abstracts remain leads; they are not curated measurements. Full text is needed for strain, feed, medium and matched controls.
4. **Sujith et al. 2017, DOI [10.1080/1064119X.2017.1298169](https://doi.org/10.1080/1064119X.2017.1298169) — promising existing-root extension after assay review.** Selected author-posted full-text passages describe crust microcosms with glucose and azide contrasts. Complete visual figures, supplements and referenced analytical methods were not inspected. Temperature-tolerance wording varies between sections; solution loss does not identify its receiving solid or prove isolated product recovery. Azide chemistry and experiment overlap with the thesis/companion pressure study need review. No result from this experiment is accepted in the record yet.
5. **Kato et al. 2018, [PMC6307992](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6307992/) — context only.** Read the Europe PMC XML material/methods and discussion. Spatial cell/Mn associations and community profiles do not isolate a microbial mechanism. The authors retain Mn-depleted clay infilling as an alternative explanation. A genus association cannot identify a causal strain. No microbial-release claim is imported.
6. **Konishi 2008, DOI [10.2473/journalofmmij.124.844](https://doi.org/10.2473/journalofmmij.124.844), and JOGMEC September 2018 presentation — experimental routes, not accepted assays.** Read review section 5.3/Figure 10 caption and original-study references, and presentation cover/bioleaching slides. The review points to Japanese patent publication 2007-113116 and Konishi/Saitoh/Ooka at the 2008 Ocean Engineering Symposium. JOGMEC cites Ooki et al. 2015. The 2022 URL upload directory is not the presentation date. Original methods, replication and product characterization remain needed. No industrial operation is inferred.
7. **2026 Minerals paper, DOI 10.3390/min16010091 — defer.** Acquired 23-page PDF and extracted text; no full claim-level review. Imaging/mineralogical observations must be separated from proposed microbial causes before adoption.
