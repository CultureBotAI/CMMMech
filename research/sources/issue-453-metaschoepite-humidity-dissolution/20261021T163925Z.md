## Humidity arms: what the original pages support

Kirkegaard accepted manuscript pp.4–5 and SI p.S2 describe uranyl nitrate heated at 300 °C for 24 h, hydration of UO3 with D2O at 80 °C, ambient evaporation and rinsing. These synthetic preparations are distinct from Finch's natural crystals and from Stanley's uranyl-acetate/ammonium-hydroxide preparation. D2O handling does not establish complete retained deuteration.

Table 1, accepted p.5, describes storage in separate bell jars near 22 °C with weekly stirring. Salt-derived RH values were computed at 20 °C and are approximate. The tabulated masses are material loaded into neutron cans, not complete initial reaction inventories:

| B | 2 | 1.491 | K2CO3 in D2O, approximately 45% RH | 18 d |
| C | 1 | 1.285 | Silica desiccant; RH not numerically given | 41 d |

Table 2, accepted p.11, reports fitted metaschoepite/dehydrated-phase weight-percent pairs A 95.5(2)/4.5(3), B 70.7(1)/29.3(1), C 10.8(5)/89.2(6). Each fitted pair sums to 100%; that is not a gravimetric water balance, complete phase inventory or recovery yield. C retains hydrate despite essentially-complete wording. The later discussion says sixteen days for B, while both tables say eighteen. SI Figure S4 says 75% RH for A, versus 78% in the main tables and conclusion. Fresh identical accepted/SI bytes preserve both discrepancies without choosing corrections.

Initial batch 2 already contains a minor dehydrated component; the initial XRD scans also differ in duration (12 h versus 1 h). Different batches, initial phases and times prevent a controlled rate comparison. Figures 14–15 and SI Figure S8 retain model-dependent coordination/spectral assignments rather than identify an unequivocally pure natural product. No threshold RH, universal natural-air stability, reversibility, kinetics or industrial behavior is established. The existing owner already states these qualifications.

## Stanley preparation, analytical scope and controls

Cached §§2.1–2.3 distinguish synthetic metaschoepite made from 0.09 M uranyl acetate plus NH4OH at pH 8.2 from uranophane made with 0.09 M uranyl acetate, 0.05 M calcium acetate and 0.09 M sodium silicate at pH 8.0. Preparations were held at 60 °C for two weeks under nitrogen, then filtered, rinsed with hot deionized water and vacuum-desiccated. XRD assignments are compared with reference patterns; no entire natural-mineral identity population is established from this synthetic preparation.

The dissolution method gives 20–120 mg synthetic solid in triplicate **45-mL vials**, pH 4–10 buffers, N2-purged water, 25 °C and up to 60 d. The 45 mL describes vessel capacity, not an explicit filled liquid volume. Buffers include 5 mM KHP/MES/HEPES, 6 mM NH4Cl–NH4OH and 16 mM NH4OH; the text does not give an exact recipe/solid loading/filled volume for every plotted point. Added phases/reagents include calcite, sodium silicate, calcium nitrate and bicarbonate. No microbial arm is described. No matched independently characterized precipitated-product inventory follows from solution concentrations.

Solutions were filtered at 0.22 µm, acidified below pH 2 and measured by ICP-OES using EPA 200.7; low-U samples (<100 µg/L) used HR-ICP-MS using EPA 200.8, quantitation level 1 µg/L at R=4000. These are total-element analyses of operationally filtered liquid, not measurements of individual uranyl complexes. SI QC reports blanks below QL and acceptable duplicates/other checks, **but also failed calibration verifications and low matrix-spike recoveries**. The proposed high-U/spike-mismatch explanation is the authors' interpretation. It does not justify marking all QC as passed or assigning a correction factor. Native tables provide means and ± values; their table headings do not explicitly identify every uncertainty as a replicate standard deviation.

TGA/DSC-MS used heating to 1000 °C at 10 °C/min, argon 50 mL/min and monitored m/z 18/44. The cached text's 416–516 °C calculation gives 11.25±0.05 wt% water and 1.96 waters per UO3, whereas an explicitly conditional whole-initial-mass/UO3-residue calculation gives about 2.01256 waters (11.20–11.30% gives a small interval around that). The thermal denominator/normalization cannot be silently supplied; no correction of 1.96 is proposed. The uranophane 550–650 °C/5.2-water account is separate. Original Figure 1c and workbook J3/K3 both label DSC in µV/mg; no unit conflict is asserted.
