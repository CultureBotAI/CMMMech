## Experimental design and supported scope

Indexed Methods§2 describes an axenic **Gloeocapsa sp. f-6gl** culture from a Kamchatka spring, precultured in low-phosphate BG11. This is a source strain label, not a verified accession or species assignment. Reactors contain added soluble magnesium/bicarbonate at25±2°C. Continuous light, day/night and dark arms differ in stirring, shaking and air bubbling. Routine purity checks are author-reported; no new sequence or taxon grounding is inferred.

Abiotic arms include sterile culture supernatant containing exometabolites and medium controls, with sodium azide added. Thus “abiotic” does not mean absence of biologically derived dissolved organics. The mineral-free culture is a separate uptake/isotope comparison with different starting chemistry. These controls do not isolate a universal biological requirement, and the dark culture is not a sterile control. Reactor treatments, sampling times and analytical repeats are different levels of replication.

The new Figure1 originals show biotic and abiotic diffraction patterns compared with dypingite and nesquehonite standards. Indexed captions associate dypingite traces with Bio-A30d/Abio-A27d and nesquehonite traces with Bio-B23d/Abio-C15d. SEM Figure2 distinguishes needle and rosette morphologies. TEM Figure3 shows cell-associated deposits in enriched medium and a mineral-free comparison; Mg/C/O chemistry alone does not assign an individual deposit to a specific crystalline phase or establish a unique biochemical pathway.
