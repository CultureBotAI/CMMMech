Akaganeite mineral

Akaganeite is a distinct iron oxyhydroxide mineral species, listed with formula (Fe(III),Ni(II))8(OH,O)16Cl1.25·nH2O, renamed status (Rn), and IMA number 1962-004 in the September 2026 IMA master list. The experimental abbreviation beta-FeOOH identifies the synthetic phase assignment used by the study; it does not establish the ideal IMA composition or nickel content of that preparation. This record is separate from iron element, other iron oxyhydroxide species, iron-ore commodities, and mixed waste feeds. No constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Condition-dependent akaganeite reduction by Geobacter sulfurreducens PCA

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Geobacter sulfurreducens PCA (NCBITaxon:243231) [TaxonMech]

Living wild-type Geobacter sulfurreducens PCA cultures increased acid-extractable Fe(II) from freshly prepared XRD-assigned akaganeite. Comparisons with delta-imcH and delta-cbcL mutants associate the timing and extent of reduction with those electron-transfer systems, with strong dependence on starting pH and autoclaving. The study supports conditional microbial Fe(III) reduction, not elemental iron production, a purified mineral product, or direct measurement of akaganeite redox potential.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The synthetic substrate was prepared by slowly titrating 0.4 M FeCl3 with 25% NaOH over 90 minutes to pH 7, holding for one hour, washing with water, and using it within 24 hours. Powder XRD assigned the product to akaganeite. Mineral incubations used 1 mL mineral suspension plus 9 mL modified NB basal medium, acetate as electron/carbon donor, no added fumarate, nitrilotriacetate-free trace minerals, and N2:CO2 (80:20). The basal recipe specifies 20 mM acetate; phosphate was increased to 0.69 g/L NaH2PO4·H2O before autoclaving. Separate comparisons used pH 6.8 or 6.3, with fresh medium or 20 minutes autoclaving at 121 degrees C. Each received 100 microlitres of stationary-phase cells grown on acetate/fumarate from separate colony picks. Figure 2 reports triplicate culture means and standard deviations, representative of at least duplicate incubations, over approximately 14-15 days. Samples were extracted in 0.5 N HCl for at least 24 hours at 23-25 degrees C before the ferrozine assay; that temperature is for extraction, while the mineral-culture incubation temperature is not stated in the inspected Methods. Acid-extractable Fe(II) is not a dissolved-only fraction. Initial akaganeite Fe loading is not independently quantified here; the nearby approximately 20 mM Methods statement follows the goethite preparation and is not transferred to this assay.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: levar_2017
Methods and Results, Evidence that the electron transfer pathway changes even in the absence of XRD pattern changes, plus Figure 2 show wild-type reduction at pH 6.8 and no observable reduction in the reported 14-day cell-free controls. Delta-cbcL starts reducing but stalls below wild type; fresh-medium delta-imcH has a roughly seven-day lag before approaching the wild-type endpoint. Autoclaving at pH 6.8 shortens that lag. At pH 6.3, delta-imcH reduction is strongly suppressed and autoclaving does not restore it. Table 1 fumarate-growth comparisons help exclude a gross pH-dependent growth defect. These are condition-specific phenotypes, not universal knockout inability.
Partial Source: levar_2017
The discussion explicitly states that mineral redox potentials cannot be directly measured in these experiments; quantitative voltage measurements in Figure 1 concern soluble Fe(III)-citrate, not akaganeite. Similar powder XRD before/after autoclaving does not exclude changes in aggregation, short-range order or surface properties. Direct ImcH/CbcL electron movement from the quinone pool is not demonstrated, and complementation is cited from earlier studies rather than performed as a Figure 2 control. The record therefore retains pathway association and inferred redox interpretation without treating a particular mineral voltage, direct protein mechanism, complete feed conversion, isolated recovery or industrial deployment as demonstrated.
Context_only Source: sklute_2023
A separate 55 degrees C hydrogen-fed Ax17 experiment on synthetic nanophase akaganeite gave a dark nonmagnetic product with amorphous iron-rich nanospheres; oxalate-extractable Fe(II) was not significantly different from controls, while temperature-dependent Mossbauer analysis detected new ferric and ferrous components with nonunique phase fits. Heated abiotic controls also changed. That different organism, medium and analytical extraction is not a direct replication or refutation of PCA reduction, and cautions against assuming a universal crystalline recovery product or equating an extraction null with no transformation.
