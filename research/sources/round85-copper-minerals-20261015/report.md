# Copper mineral source assessment — round85

Azurite and tenorite are distinct mineral owners. The current IMA list and complete Handbook entries establish mineral identities; they do not authenticate experimental products or confer elemental copper criticality.

Kang et al.2016 (DOI10.2323/jgam.2016.03.001) supports a narrowly qualified azurite assignment in a mixed diffraction trace. Figure7 names BPU medium, whereas the separate copper-removal experiment uses YA broth. Strain B-22 does not exactly match the currently deposited B7-22 label. Conflicting dose, duration and inoculum statements remain unresolved, and aqueous removal is not an azurite recovery yield.

Badawy et al.2021 (DOI10.3390/biology10030233; acquisition filename fouda2021) supports a source-assigned CuO product after200°C heating of a fungal-conditioned preparation. The record scopes the abiotic thermal stage, preserving upstream biomass-free A.niger G3-1 filtrate. Preheating diffraction and matched heated controls are missing. XRD coordinates conflict internally; CuI/CuII XPS assignments and sulfur-rich EDX preclude a phase-pure product assertion. Copper(II) to CuO does not establish copper reduction.

Li and Gadd2017 (DOI10.1007/s00253-017-8451-x) offers provisional FTIR trace-azurite context with amorphous XRD. Xue2022 (DOI10.3389/fmicb.2022.1001464) models copper phases; those predictions are not experimental phase confirmation. These sources are not independent quantitative confirmation of the accepted observations.

The paired JSON retains exact source artifacts, hashes, access scope, search queries, notice-search limits and the bounded hidden/ignored-inclusive deduplication. Raw text and graphics remain outside Git. No laboratory observation establishes industrial deployment, pure mineral recovery or microbial necessity. Source clarification and orthogonal phase evidence remain useful follow-ups; discovery is not exhausted.
