Alabandite

Alabandite is the cubic manganese sulfide mineral, ideal formula MnS, grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA list. It is distinct from the MnS polymorph rambergite, elemental manganese and a whole polymetallic ore. The accepted experiment identifies alabandite as one component of natural Talvivaara black shale and measures its content in fractions from laboratory flotation with freeze-dried bacterial biomass. This is not pure-mineral testing, proof of a microbial surface mechanism, manganese product recovery or a criticality designation inherited from Mn.

Aluminium salt slag

Salt-flux-bearing mixed slag from aluminium scrap remelting, containing process salts and oxide-rich solids. The accepted study sampled a rotary furnace batch from a Brazilian recycler using NaCl/KCl flux. This resource is defined by salt-flux remelting origin, distinct from the existing post-metal-recovery secondary aluminium dross specimen, generic smelting slag, bauxite residue, elemental aluminium or a single mineral. Laboratory water leaching and recovered salt crystals are documented; microbial action, inherited criticality and industrial deployment of this recipe are not.

Cassiterite

Cassiterite is the mineral species with IMA-approved formula SnO2. The September 2026 IMA list records grandfathered status G and original description year 1832; that year is not an IMA approval identifier. The mineral is distinct from elemental tin, a tin commodity, and a cassiterite-bearing experimental feed. The curated separation study used a Brazilian mineral sample reported as 84.7% pure by X-ray fluorescence, rather than a demonstrated pure SnO2 phase. No criticality designation is inherited from tin.

Fluorite

Fluorite is the calcium fluoride mineral CaF2, listed with grandfathered status G in the IMA-CNMNC September 2026 mineral list. Its original-description year is unresolved in that list; no numbered approval accession is asserted. The Handbook of Mineralogy describes cubic fluorite and natural hydrothermal, sedimentary and igneous occurrences. This mineral owner is distinct from the traded fluorspar commodity and elemental fluorine. The experiments below use ground natural crystals reported as 99% CaF2 and concern bacterial-cell-associated flocculation and settling. They establish neither mineral dissolution nor production of a specified fluorspar grade. No criticality designation is inherited from the commodity or element, and no industrial deployment is claimed.

Löllingite

Löllingite, commonly written loellingite, is the orthorhombic iron arsenide mineral with ideal formula FeAs2. The September 2026 IMA list records it as grandfathered, with original-description year 1845. It is distinct from arsenopyrite, skutterudite, elemental arsenic and a mixed arsenic-bearing ore. Natural compositions need not be exact endmembers. The accepted microbial experiment concerns a quantified loellingite-arsenopyrite feed mixed with pyrite, not purified loellingite. Its flotation response does not establish dissolved-metal extraction, an isolated loellingite product or industrial deployment. No constituent-element criticality is inherited.

Ludwigite boron-iron ore

Ludwigite ore denotes a heterogeneous boron-bearing iron ore or its beneficiated boron-iron concentrate, represented here by the mixed geological feed used by Liang et al. (2024). It is not the mineral species ludwigite, elemental boron or iron, a borates commodity specification, or the secondary residue left after processing. The experimental substrate for water leaching was a soda-ash-roasted derivative of that concentrate; its altered composition must not be assigned to the original ore. The paper's Table 2 reports 5.12 wt% B2O3 and 58.23 wt% total iron in this reduced feed, and Figure 9 identifies metallic iron, sodium magnesium silicate, sodium silicate and magnesium-ferrous oxide. Its bulk-feed result cannot establish which individual original boron mineral supplied the dissolved boron. The exact mine/sample identifier and original batch mineral proportions are not established in the inspected 2024 article, which refers to earlier work for the ore characterization. No constituent's criticality designation is transferred to this resource.

Rare-earth-element commodities, Australian list scope

This collective commodity family follows Australia's 20 February 2024 rare-earth-elements entry: yttrium and all fifteen lanthanides, including promethium. Scandium has a separate entry in that list. This sixteen-member economic grouping differs from the broader seventeen-element chemical definition, which also includes scandium, and from the EU 2020 assessment's fifteen-member light/heavy split, which excludes both scandium and promethium. Inclusion of promethium preserves the Australian source's stated membership and does not assert an ordinary mined promethium supply. This family overlaps individual element records and narrower rare-earth commodity groups; it is not one mineral, alloy or fixed mixture. The mechanism below concerns only a synthetic neodymium/dysprosium solution and does not establish recovery of every family member or treatment of ore, magnets or industrial waste.

Heterogeneous residual aluminium dross after an earlier recovery of metallic aluminium from primary dross by screening or flux-assisted remelting. This source-defined secondary dross is narrower than the umbrella aluminium-dross category; the accepted specimen came from an aluminium remelting plant in Guangxi, China. It contains metallic aluminium, aluminium oxides and nitride, magnesium-aluminium spinel, and other oxides and process salts. It is not a mineral species, elemental aluminium, bauxite residue, spent potlining, or an automatically equivalent feed to an unspecified aluminium-smelting slag. Laboratory chemical extraction is documented without a microbial mechanism, inherited criticality designation, or demonstrated industrial deployment.

Spent selective catalytic reduction catalyst

Used vanadia-tungsta/titania catalysts from coal-fired power-plant flue-gas denitrification, represented by separately characterized laboratory feeds. Niu and colleagues studied an arsenic-contaminated honeycomb catalyst from a Xinjiang plant; Su and colleagues separately studied gray and yellow regions of three spent honeycomb catalysts, S3–S5. This manufactured, heterogeneous secondary resource is distinct from refinery hydroprocessing, fluid catalytic cracking and sulfuric-acid-production catalysts, pure constituent oxides, mineral species and elemental vanadium or tungsten. Fresh catalysts S1–S2 in Su's study are comparators, not spent-resource observations. No criticality designation is inherited from the constituents.

Tungsten skarn-processing tailings

Residual mineral solids generated by processing tungsten-bearing skarn ore. The curated case is historical tailings in the Smaltjärnen repository at the closed Yxsjöberg tungsten, copper and fluorspar mine in Sweden. The processing plant used gravity separation, magnetic separation and flotation. The characterized layered material contains scheelite among mixed silicate, carbonate, sulfide and other minerals; composition and particle liberation vary by sampled layer. This resource is distinct from tungsten as an element, pure scheelite, unprocessed ore, waste rock and a saleable concentrate. Hällström et al. document scheelite alteration, hydrous ferric oxide associations and tungsten transport in a field investigation of this repository; those geochemical observations do not establish microbial causation. No criticality designation is inherited from constituents, and the laboratory results below do not characterize all tungsten tailings.

Zinc purification hot filter cake
