## Claim boundaries and unresolved phase questions

The primary source isolated bacteria from a bench wastewater-treatment biofilm but observed phosphate crystals in artificial nutrient media. Its wastewater medium supported growth without crystals at 30 days. Reactor COD removal is not a phosphate-mineral yield or evidence of industrial biomineral production.

Selected colonies were cultured at 25 C for 30 days. SS1/SS2 agar media had different magnesium/phosphate recipes and initial pH 7.2. Table 3 records struvite plus spherulites only for isolates 1, 2, 5, 7, 9 and 12; it does not identify a separate quantitative bobierrite/baricite fraction per isolate. No spherulites are assigned to the liquid arms. Uninoculated and autoclaved dead-cell controls reportedly lacked crystals. These support a bounded association, without isolating alkalinity, soluble metabolites, surfaces or a unique enzymatic cause.

Crystals were recovered by placing agar pieces in boiling water and dried at 37 C. The paper reports unchanged optical morphology, without pre/post-processing diffraction of the wet crystals. This leaves culture-phase versus recovered-phase identity unresolved; no particular heating transformation is asserted here.

Figure 4 is a source-assigned diffraction-intensity map with SP1/SP2 labels, not raw per-isolate quantitative phase refinement. Figure 5 names bobierrite and Mg3(PO4)2·2H2O spherulites, whereas the adjacent text and Figure 4 name baricite. EDX cannot alone establish hydration state or distinguish polymorphs. These internal labels require explicit treatment before accepting a stronger phase-specific mechanism.

Methods describe approximately 650 nt 16S sequences, Figure 3 approximately 200 nt, and Table 2 identities span 107–198 positions. Table 2 comparison accessions do not identify the actual experimental stocks. Strain 12 is 100% in the table but 96% in prose. Avoid invented organism CURIEs or strain authentication. Liquid methods describe biofilm suspensions while Table 3 reports strain rows; resolve or preserve that reporting ambiguity.
