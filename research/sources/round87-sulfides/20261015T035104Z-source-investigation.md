# Alabandite source investigation

Saved at 2026-10-15T03:51:04.997761Z. This is a source assessment and curation handoff; independent scientific acceptance remains separate.

Alabandite is the selected new mineral identity. Complete primary evidence supports source-assigned alabandite partitioning during flotation of a mixed natural ore with freeze-dried bacterial biomass. It does not demonstrate improved mineral recovery, a pure-phase surface mechanism, microbial metabolism or industrial deployment.

## Identity and study lineage

The September 2026 IMA list (page 5, status G, year 1832, MnS) and complete Handbook entry establish cubic alabandite, distinct from rambergite and the Talvivaara black-shale resource. No mineral CURIE or criticality claim is inferred. NCBITaxon:1281 resolves to the species *Staphylococcus carnosus*; the experiment supplies no traceable strain identifier.

The accepted original is Langwaldt and Kalapudas (2007), *Bio-beneficiation of multimetal black shale ore by flotation*, Physicochemical Problems of Mineral Processing 41:291–299 ([official PDF](https://www.journalssystem.com/ppmp/pdf-79310-15375?filename=Bio-beneficiation-of-mult.pdf)). ## Original inspected and claim locators

All eleven PDF pages and every Figure 1–2 and Table 1–6 were read and visually inspected. Table 4 on PDF page 5 is image-only; PDF page 10 is blank and page 11 duplicates Table 4 in landscape orientation. No claim-bearing graphic is missing. The complete publisher HTML links and PDF exposed no supplement or correction. This is a bounded inspected-artifact result, not a universal absence claim.

- Printed pages 292–294 and Tables 1–3 describe freeze-dried cells, 1 kg ore subsamples, a 4 dm3 laboratory cell, nominal 25% slurry density of unspecified basis, 3 dm3/min air and 1800 rpm. BIO11 and BIO15 differ in biomass dose, collector dose and operating pH. Conditioning at pH 6.4–6.6 is separate from those operating conditions.
- Printed pages 294–295 and Table 4 describe SEM/EDAX/MLA and microprobe assignment of alabandite at 1.79% of the ore, containing 58.47% Mn, 36.43% S and 3.24% Fe. No phase-specific diffraction tests its polymorph. The experimental material remains source-assigned natural alabandite, not a demonstrated pure ideal MnS preparation.
- Table 6, page 297, reports BIO11 feed 1.3% and CC3 0.7%, versus BIO15 feed 1.1% and CC3 2.4%. These are mineral contents of fractions, not mass-recovery efficiencies. Zero-valued cells do not prove absolute absence.
- Figures 1–2 and Table 5 quantify nickel. Table 5's BIO20 cannot be substituted for BIO15. The 53% manganese recovery in the abstract and conclusion belongs to conventional chemical flotation.
- The conclusion on page 298 states that bioflotation did not substantially improve overall product recovery or grade, and xanthate remained necessary. The reference flotation series is not an otherwise matched alabandite-specific control. No independent assay replication, uncertainty estimates or pure-mineral adhesion experiment is reported.

The English abstract/conclusion calls *S. carnosus* “minor hydrophobic,” whereas the Polish summary describes it as the most hydrophobic tested strain. The record does not convert that inconsistent wording into a quantitative physiological property. General selective-adhesion theory in the Introduction is not treated as a demonstrated mechanism for alabandite.
