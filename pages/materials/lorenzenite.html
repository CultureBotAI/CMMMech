Lorenzenite mineral

Lorenzenite is the sodium titanium silicate mineral with ideal formula Na2Ti2O3(Si2O6), equivalent to Na2Ti2Si2O9. The September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list gives grandfathered status G and original description year 1901, not an IMA approval number. Ramsayite is a historical name for the same species, not a separate material owner. Sundberg, Lehtinen and Kivekas (1987) refined a natural crystal from Helsinki museum specimen 4937, labelled from Lovozero on the Kola Peninsula. Their Pbcn model contains pyroxene-type silicate chains, distorted TiO6 octahedra and seven-coordinate sodium. The single-crystal refinement used 778 selected reflections from 1167 measured independent reflections and reported R=0.026. The chemistry cited for that specimen was from earlier work, not a new bulk assay in 1987. Historical cell-setting and oxygen-coordinate discrepancies are discussed in the original study. This identity record does not assert a dissolution, synthesis or microbial mechanism, a recovery yield, industrial use or criticality inherited from titanium.
