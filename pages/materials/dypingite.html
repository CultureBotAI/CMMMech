Dypingite mineral

Dypingite is the hydrated magnesium carbonate mineral species Mg5(CO3)4(OH)2·5H2O, listed as approved with IMA number 1970-011 in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list. It is distinct from hydromagnesite, nesquehonite, elemental magnesium and the magnesite commodity. The laboratory products below were identified as dypingite by the study authors; they are not an isolated magnesium-metal product. No constituent-element criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Dypingite formation in cyanobacteria-dominated enrichment biofilms

biomineralization Microbial

In a six-week laboratory experiment, carbonate-free cyanobacteria-dominated enrichments from the Atlin wetland formed biofilm-associated precipitates identified by micro-X-ray diffraction as predominantly dypingite. This supports formation in the mixed microbial system under the tested conditions, not exclusive biological production of this mineral or a pure-culture species-specific mechanism.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Predominantly filamentous cyanobacteria were enriched from Atlin sediment through three successive cultures over three months in Allen's medium at room temperature (about 20 degrees C for enrichment) with sunlight. Portions of carbonate-free biofilm inoculated 10 mL of 0.45-micrometer-filtered natural groundwater or wetland water in push-capped test tubes. The wetland biotic system received 15 mg NaNO3 and 0.375 mg K2HPO4 per 10 mL; the described abiotic controls contained only filtered water, so nutrient matching is not established. Cultures and controls were incubated at room temperature with sunlight for six weeks. The enrichment was a community, not a demonstrated axenic isolate; the field mat's Lyngbya sp. identification is not assigned as an exact taxonomic identity or sole cause for the laboratory biofilm.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: power_2007
Section 4.3, Figure 5A-B and Figure 6 identify rosette-like biofilm precipitates as predominantly dypingite by SEM, EDS and micro-XRD, with no nesquehonite detected in either biological laboratory system. Table 2 records dypingite in both biotic waters, nesquehonite in the untreated wetland-water abiotic control and no precipitate in the groundwater abiotic control. These are distinct arms within one experiment, not independent studies. Photosynthesis-driven alkalinization is the authors' interpretation; biofilm microsite pH was not directly measured.
Partial Source: power_2007
The NaOH-only wetland-water control also formed dypingite together with nesquehonite and an unidentified magnesium carbonate at pH 10.3, so dypingite occurrence does not establish microbial necessity. Section 3.2 specifies 0.5 M NaOH whereas Section 4.3 specifies 0.1 M; that dose contradiction is unresolved. Section 4.3 describes approximately equal parts of three phases whereas Section 5.1 calls nesquehonite dominant; no quantitative phase fractions are accepted. Nutrient additions and community composition limit causal attribution, and the stated protocol does not establish a replicate-level precision for the phase comparison.
Partial Source: power_2007
Sections 4.2 and 5.1 say pre-existing carbonate prevented identification of newly formed phases in the separate field microcosms. Their dissolved magnesium decreases therefore are not dypingite-specific formation yields. Minor dypingite in the field abiotic precipitate may have formed by nesquehonite decomposition after collection. The laboratory study does not demonstrate a quantified pure-phase recovery yield, magnesium-metal production or industrial deployment, and it does not establish a universal microbial enhancement of magnesium-carbonate precipitation.
