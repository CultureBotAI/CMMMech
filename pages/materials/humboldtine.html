Humboldtine mineral

Humboldtine is the iron(II) oxalate dihydrate mineral Fe(C2O4).2H2O, listed as a grandfathered species, with original-description year 1821, in the September 2026 IMA compilation. It is distinct from elemental iron, dissolved iron-oxalate complexes, other metal oxalates and the heterogeneous siderite- and pyrrhotite-bearing experimental feeds. No criticality designation is inherited from iron.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Humboldtine formation in Aspergillus niger cultures on two iron-bearing rocks

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Aspergillus niger (NCBITaxon:5061) [TaxonMech]

Humboldtine-assigned crystals formed during laboratory culture of Aspergillus niger Ch 4/07 on siderite-bearing and altered pyrrhotite-bearing rocks. Powder diffraction supports the product identity, while the reported absence of iron-oxalate crystals in uninoculated controls supports a fungal contribution under these conditions. Organic-acid dissolution followed by iron-oxalate precipitation is the proposed explanation; neither a specific enzyme nor a measured iron-redox pathway is established. No isolated product yield, complete conversion or industrial deployment is demonstrated.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Ch 4/07 was isolated from weathered Proconesos marble at Chersonesos, Crimea; the authors identify it by ITS accession KF768341. The siderite feed from Dalnegorsk was chiefly Mg/Mn-bearing siderite with pyrite, muscovite, quartz and montmorillonite. The Pyrrhotite Gorge feed was largely replaced by poorly crystalline iron oxides/hydroxides and also contained quartz, kaolinite, albite and locally biotite. Neither feed was a pure mineral reagent. Autoclaved fragments about 1 by 0.5 by 0.5 cm were incubated in Petri dishes with 15 mL autoclaved Czapek-Dox medium: NaNO3 3.0, KH2PO4 1.0, MgSO4.7H2O 0.5, KCl 0.5, FeSO4.7H2O 0.015 and glucose 30.0 g/L. The medium therefore supplied some iron itself. Initial pH was 5.5; temperature is reported only as room temperature. Conidia and mycelial fragments from ten-day agar cultures were added, without a stated inoculum density. Triplicate syntheses and uninoculated controls ran for 3-18 days, with observations on days 3, 5, 7, 10, 14 and 18. No no-rock, killed-biomass, cell-free-metabolite or matched-pH comparison is reported, and iron origins were not mass-balanced.

Support Source: zelenskaya2021
Sections 2.1-3.2 and Figure 1a-d show PXRD-assigned humboldtine on siderite at day five and on altered pyrrhotite at day seven, at reported pH 3.5 plus or minus 0.5. Partial Source: zelenskaya2021
The claim that formation begins earlier on pyrrhotite relies on day-three prismatic Fe-rich crystals seen by SEM/EDX, before the day-seven PXRD assignment. Morphology and elemental iron alone are not an independent early phase confirmation. The claimed slower crystallization, influence of impurity ions and coupled Fe(II)/Fe(III) reactions remain interpretations without quantitative dissolution rates, oxalate concentrations, redox measurements or independently varied impurity controls. The reported pyrrhotite-medium pH falls to 2 at day 14 and returns to 3.5 plus or minus 0.5 at day 18; the latter is called an initial value in Results, whereas the Methods' initial medium pH is 5.5.
Context_only Source: zelenskaya2021
Section 4 and Figure 5 describe a separate search in lichen-dominated biofilms from Kola Peninsula and Karelia iron ores, in which iron oxalates were not found and goethite was reported instead. This field observation limits transfer of the culture result to natural biofilms; it neither refutes the laboratory phase assignment nor establishes universal absence of humboldtine in nature. Earlier disputed lichen iron-oxalate claims cited in the Introduction are not adopted here.

Organism- and feed-dependent humboldtine assignments in brown-rot fungal cultures

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Serpula himantioides (NCBITaxon:80602) [TaxonMech], Coniophora puteana (NCBITaxon:80637) [TaxonMech], Amyloporia xantha (NCBITaxon:1333629) [TaxonMech], Serpula lacrymans (NCBITaxon:85982) [TaxonMech]

A separate laboratory comparison reports humboldtine in selected brown-rot fungal cultures on siderite- and altered pyrrhotite-bearing rocks. Phase assignments depend on the organism, feed and analytical method. Serpula lacrymans is included as a tested comparator without reported iron-oxalate products, not as a demonstrated producer. The study supports culture-associated mineral formation, without establishing a specific biochemical pathway, comparative recovery efficiency or deployment. The source name Antrodia xantha is retained in context; its sequence and current NCBI Taxonomy XML use Amyloporia xantha.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Table 2 identifies LE-BIN 1368 Serpula himantioides (KY352492), LE-BIN 1370 Coniophora puteana (KY433978), LE-BIN 1029 Antrodia xantha (KY433983) and LE-BIN 1192 Serpula lacrymans (KY352493). Table 3 describes Dalnegorsk Mg/Mn-bearing siderite with pyrite, muscovite, quartz and montmorillonite, and Pyrrhotite Gorge ore largely replaced by poorly crystalline iron oxides/hydroxides with other silicate phases. These are the same feed types/localities as the 2021 study, not purified siderite or pyrrhotite and not proof of identical individual specimens. Approximately 1 by 0.5 by 0.5 cm blocks received 20 mL liquid glucose-peptone medium in plastic Petri dishes, initial pH 5.5, at room temperature for 30 days. Inocula were mycelial fragments from ten-day glucose-peptone agar cultures; syntheses were in triplicate with uninoculated controls. Inoculum density and substrate sterilization are not specified. The listed agar-medium recipe includes FeSO4.7H2O 0.005 g/L; the liquid formulation is not separately tabulated, so a background-medium iron contribution is not excluded. No iron-source balance, oxalate assay, isolated-product mass, killed-biomass or cell-free comparison is reported. The paper specifies uninoculated controls but supplies no separate iron-arm control results or phase plots.
