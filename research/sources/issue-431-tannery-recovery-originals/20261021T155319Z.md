### Feed, preparation and measurement boundaries

The proof describes real Mexican tannery wastewater as both inoculum source and chromium feed. The source plant's stated production is not deployment of this treatment. Nominal acetate:Cr ratios are A=0.5:1, B=1:1 and C=2:1, initial chromium about 2,760 mg/L or rounded 0.053 M. Thirty-five-day anoxic incubations use 120 mL bottles with 30 mL headspace, duplicate reactors, 35°C and 100 rpm. The geometrical difference of 90 mL is not a verified reconstruction of effluent/medium mixing volumes.

The selective broth includes CaCl2 0.02, NaH2PO4 1.6, NaCl 2, NH4Cl 0.56, Na2SO4 10 and **yeast extract 10 g/L**, plus acetic acid. Later statements about no added nutrients/chemicals therefore cannot be adopted without qualification. Acetate is not established as the sole carbon source. Table 1 reports total Cr 2760.023 and Cr(VI) 0.023 mg/L, yielding source-derived Cr(III) 2760 mg/L by subtraction, not an independently direct Cr(III) species measurement. The selected text describes ICP-AES and a 540 nm colorimetric method. The complete 21-cell Table 1 transcription preserves all source labels and units, including literal mercury zero without a detection limit and the printed sediment/cyanuric entries.

OD600 and protein absorbance at 595 nm are biomass/protein proxies. The proof's drying/FTIR and SEM/EDX preparation do not independently measure a complete chromium mass balance or establish intracellular uptake. SEM images are not intracellular sections; EDX co-location is not a chromium oxidation-state assay. No isolated Cr product, measured desorption yield or product-reuse balance was established in the inspected material. This is a bounded evidence finding, not a whole-unread-source absence assertion.
