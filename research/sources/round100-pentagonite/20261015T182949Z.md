## Scientific boundaries

The 2009 thermal study used natural Wagholi material, about 10 mg powder in air at 10°C/min for TG-DTA, separate diffraction aliquots, and 5 mg ESR samples. Source-assigned 16.07 wt% water loss and 1.73 wt% oxidation-associated gain are not metal recovery percentages. Figure 5 distinguishes the 550°C cooling arm from the 700°C terminal amorphous product. The abstract's statement about no intermediate amorphous phase has ambiguous scope; it is not promoted into a claim that pentagonite never becomes amorphous. Hydrogen positions could not be refined, and a V4+ ESR signal does not exclude ESR-silent V5+ by itself.

The 2015 accepted manuscript separates powder in methanol:ethanol:water 16:3:1 and silicone oil from a single crystal in the aqueous mixture. Powder transition brackets are 2.45(5)–2.96(5) GPa and 1.27(5)–1.71(5) GPa, respectively. Powder cell reversibility does not contradict irreversible single-crystal damage above about 2.5 GPa. Oil patterns were poor, probably partly nonhydrostatic. The high-pressure triclinic atomic structure was not refined. O10 occupancies of 0.94(3)/0.96(3) make ideal five-water formulas shorthand; disorder, displacement constraints and geometric restraints prevent exact water-uptake claims. Table 1B's row heading says mm while its size cells explicitly use micrometres; the record retains that mismatch.

The 2025 field study challenges earlier natural formation inferences using paragenesis. Its fluid-inclusion method succeeded only on calcite. Its abstract gives 0.01–0.03 GPa while the conclusion gives 0.1–0.3 GPa. Neither interval is adopted as directly measured pentagonite formation conditions. The separate 2025 supplement was not acquired from the failed landing route; no supplement assay cells or twinning-count claim is imported.
