Thomasclarkite-(Y) mineral

Hydrated sodium-yttrium bicarbonate-hydroxide mineral with current September 2026 IMA-CNMNC approved formula NaY(HCO3)(OH)3(H2O)2·2H2O and approval number IMA 1997-047. Grice and Gault (1998) characterized the cotype specimens (CMNMI 81553, CMNMI 81554, and CMNMI 81555, formerly provisional phase UK93) from a large late-stage hydrothermal miarolitic cavity on level 7 of the Poudrette quarry, Mont Saint-Hilaire, Rouville County, Quebec, Canada. Electron-microprobe WDS analysis (2 points on 1 grain, p. 1295) combined with thermogravimetric analysis yielded Na2O 6.72, CaO 0.33, Y2O3 14.52, La2O3 8.63, Ce2O3 17.11, Pr2O3 1.92, Nd2O3 5.63, Sm2O3 1.22, Gd2O3 1.22, Dy2O3 2.04, Er2O3 2.20, Yb2O3 1.32, ThO2 0.54, F 0.17, CO2 12.00, H2O 26.60, O=F -0.07, total 102.10 wt%, corresponding on the basis of 10 anions to the empirical formula (Na0.80Ce0.18Ca0.02)Sigma1.00(Y0.48La0.20Ce0.21Pr0.04Nd0.12Sm0.03Gd0.03Dy0.04Er0.04Yb0.03)Sigma1.22(HCO3)[O6.97F0.03]H9.93. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction refined in monoclinic space group P2 (pseudotetragonal cell a = 4.556(1), b = 13.018(6), c = 4.556(2) A, beta = 90.15(3) degrees, V = 270.2(2) A3, Z = 1, R = 0.055, wR2 = 0.157 for 1616 observed reflections with > 4sigma(F) using reflection twin law (101), twin fraction 0.496(9)) revealed a layered structure with 2-fold orientational disorder of the bicarbonate [HCO3]- groups and half-occupied W7 and OH4 sites. Hydrogen positions were not directly located by X-ray diffraction; the bicarbonate versus carbonate assignment rests on charge balance, C-O bond- valence sums, and infrared absorption bands. Laboratory thermogravimetric analysis showed a two-stage weight loss up to 510 degrees C, and the mineral dissolves readily with strong effervescence in 10% HCl. Distinct from adamsite-(Y), shomiokite-(Y), and elemental yttrium; no criticality designation, microbial mechanism, or industrial recovery yield is asserted.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Laboratory thermal dehydration and decarbonation of natural thomasclarkite-(Y)

other

Heating a 5.970 mg powdered sample of natural thomasclarkite-(Y) under flowing dry nitrogen from room temperature to 1000 degrees C produced two resolved weight-loss stages between 20 and 510 degrees C totaling 2.302 mg (38.6 wt%), attributed by the authors to loss of weakly H-bonded [H2O] groups between 20 and 110 degrees C (1.222 mg, 20.5 wt%) followed by a bimodal DTG loss between 380 and 510 degrees C (1.080 mg, 18.1 wt%) from decomposition of (OH)- and (HCO3)- groups into H2O and CO2.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Thermal analysis in Grice and Gault (1998), pp. 1295-1296 and Figure 3 used a 5.970 mg powdered natural sample from Mont Saint-Hilaire in a Mettler-Toledo TA8000 / TG50 thermal system with an M3 microbalance heated from room temperature to 1000 degrees C at 5 degrees C/min under dry nitrogen flowing at 200 mL/min. Because the 380-510 degrees C step co- evolved H2O and CO2 without online gas analysis, the authors calculated ideal CO2 (12.0 wt%) from crystal-structure stoichiometry and subtracted it from the total 38.6 wt% TG weight loss to obtain 26.6 wt% total H2O.

Pages 1295-1296 and Figure 3 report two TG/DTG weight-loss stages on the 5.970 mg sample (total loss 2.302 mg or 38.6 wt%): Stage 1 (20-110 degrees C) lost 1.222 mg (20.5 wt%, assigned to loss of weakly H-bonded [H2O] molecules), and Stage 2 (380-510 degrees C, bimodal DTG peak) lost 1.080 mg (18.1 wt%, assigned to combined loss of H2O and CO2 from (OH)- and (HCO3)- decomposition).
Partial Source: grice_gault_1998_thomasclarkite
Because the bimodal 380-510 degrees C DTG peak reflects overlapping dehydroxylation and decarbonation without evolved-gas spectroscopy or diffraction identification of the heated solid residue, the partition of the 38.6 wt% total loss into 26.6 wt% H2O and 12.0 wt% CO2 relies on the ideal structural content of one (HCO3)- group per formula unit.
