Childrenite is the Fe2+-dominant aluminium phosphate mineral with ideal formula Fe2+Al(PO4)(OH)2(H2O), distinct from the Mn2+-dominant endmember eosphorite and oxidized related phases. The September 2026 IMA list records grandfathered status and original-description year 1823. Toupal et al. (2024) characterized two natural grains in granite collected on 11 July 2020 from two outcrops near the southern boundary of the Homolka granite, Czech Republic. Twenty electron-microprobe spots across those two grains gave Fe/(Fe+Mn) from 0.51 to 0.86; these are analytical spots, not twenty independent specimens. Single-crystal diffraction and Raman measurements on Grain A support an orthorhombic Cmca childrenite structure with divalent-cation bond valence at the mixed Fe site. This is specimen-specific structural evidence, not a direct oxidation-state assay of every natural childrenite. It improves on the older Handbook's Bba2 assignment and possible monoclinic interpretation without treating all historical specimens as identical. The crystals show patchy Fe/Mn zoning and probable uraninite inclusions; the inclusions are not independently confirmed as a uranium mineral here. Calculated water raises all microprobe totals above 100 wt%, which the authors attribute to beam-induced partial dehydration; this is not a controlled dehydration experiment. The chemical and structural observations distinguish the investigated grains from a merely compositionally similar ferric phase. The official CIF and main Table 2 disagree on temperature, 273(2) versus 293(2) K, and measured-reflection count, 5429 versus 5682; neither disputed value is adopted as a resolved condition. The CIF's Fe/Ca scattering-site model and formula-sum field are not treated as the measured Fe/Mn/Ca chemistry, and its analytical Si0.04 differs from the main formula's approximately Si0.01. These source limits do not remove the agreement on the unit cell, Cmca symmetry and the 602 observed reflections underlying R1=0.0295. This record establishes natural-mineral identity only, without an experimentally demonstrated formation, extraction, microbial transformation or recovery mechanism. No criticality designation is inherited from constituent elements.
