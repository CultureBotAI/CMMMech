Luxembourgite mineral IMA: Lux

IMA-approved silver-copper-lead-bismuth selenide sulfosalt-like mineral (IMA 2018-154; symbol Lux in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 129; status A, Approved 2019; full description published August 2020) with ideal end-member formula AgCuPbBi4Se8 (reported on p. 450 as requiring Ag 5.58 wt%, Cu 3.44 wt%, Pb 11.22 wt%, Bi 45.28 wt%, and Se 34.22 wt% for a total of 100.00 wt%, where Ag 5.58 wt% is a digit-transposition misprint for the true stoichiometric value 5.84 wt% so the printed ideal values sum to 99.74 wt%; Philippo et al., 2020, European Journal of Mineralogy 32:449-455). Represents the ordered Ag-Cu analogue of watkinsonite, Cu2PbBi4Se8 (with Ag ordered at the octahedral Ag1 site and Cu ordered at the tetrahedral Cu2 site, and closely related to litochlebite, Ag2PbBi4Se8, with which it shows very limited solid solution), and is named after the city and Grand Duchy of Luxembourg as the first new mineral species discovered in Luxembourg. Discovered during winter 2012 tunnel excavation works by the Societe Electrique de l'Our at Bivels in the Oesling (Ardennes) region of northern Luxembourg (49 deg 57 min 8 sec N, 6 deg 10 min 41 sec E), where it occurs in hydrothermal veins cutting Lower Devonian reddish schists Affected by Variscan metamorphism, deposited as tiny acicular fibres on translucent rhombohedral dolomite crystals (up to 2 mm) associated with siderite; cotype specimens are deposited in the mineralogical collection of the Natural History Museum of Luxembourg (Musee National d'Histoire Naturelle, catalogue number FD040; portion used for EPMA) and the Laboratory of Mineralogy, University of Liege, Belgium (catalogue number 21302; portion used for SCXRD). Forms grey metallic millerite-like fibres up to 200 um in length and 5 um in diameter (Figure 1) with a black streak, metallic lustre, opaque diaphaneity, brittle tenacity, no observed cleavage, Mohs hardness 3 (estimated by comparison with litochlebite), and calculated density reported as 8.00 g/cm3 in the Abstract (p. 449), Section 3 (p. 450), and Table 6 (p. 454) from the chemical analysis and unit-cell parameters vs Dcalc = 7.397 g/cm3 in Table 3 (p. 452; optical properties not determined due to the 5 um fibre diameter). Wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analysis (average of 5 point analyses, JEOL JXA-8200, 15 kV, 5 nA, 1 um beam; Table 1) yielded Bi 43.73 wt% (range 42.66-44.26), Se 31.04 wt% (30.37-32.04), Pb 11.95 wt% (10.50-12.79), Ag 6.60 wt% (5.68-9.04), Cu 2.66 wt% (2.23-2.81), Fe 0.02 wt% (0.00-0.05), S 0.01 wt% (0.00-0.02), and As 0.00 wt%, total 96.01 wt%, corresponding on the basis of 15 atoms per formula unit to the empirical formula Ag1.00(Cu0.82Ag0.20Fe0.01)Sigma1.03Pb1.13Bi4.11(Se7.72S0.01)Sigma7.73. Crystallizes in the monoclinic system, space group P21/m (#11), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 13.002(1) A, b = 4.1543(3) A, c = 15.312(2) A, beta = 108.92(1) deg, V = 782.4(2) A3, and Z = 2 (refined to R1 = 0.0476 for F2 > 2sigma(F2), R1(all) = 0.0730, wR2 = 0.1069, and GoF = 1.077 for 2117 unique reflections from 5904 measured at 293(2) K on a 0.005 x 0.005 x 0.150 mm fibre using a Rigaku Xcalibur diffractometer with MoKalpha radiation, Table 3; powder unit cell refined with LCLSQ is a = 13.01(13) A, b = 4.15(2) A, c = 15.36(14) A, beta = 109.4(6) deg, V = 783(16) A3, Table 2). The crystal structure (Tables 4-5) alternates four-anion-thick PbS-like pseudotetragonal Se layers hosting octahedral Bi1 (<Bi1-Se> = 2.9885 A), Bi2 (<Bi2-Se> = 2.9370 A), Bi3 (<Bi3-Se> = 2.9554 A; BVS = 3.08-3.25 v.u.), and Ag1 (<Ag1-Se> = 2.9219 A; BVS = 1.33 v.u.) with one-anion-thick pseudohexagonal Se layers, between which lie distorted [3+1]-tetrahedral Cu2 (refined s.o. 0.965(10) Cu, <Cu2-Se> = 2.5438 A, BVS = 1.00 v.u.; note that Section 6 on p. 454 misprints "average M-O bond lengths = 2.544 A" for M-Se), 8-coordinated Pb (refined s.o. 0.989(4) Pb, <Pb-Se> = 3.2361 A, BVS = 2.08 v.u.), and 7-coordinated Bi4 (refined s.o. 0.977(4) Bi, <Bi4-Se> = 3.0923 A). The nine observed powder X-ray diffraction lines (dobs in A (Iobs; hkl), MoKalpha, Table 2 and Table 6) are 2.984 (100; 3 1 -1), 2.085 (60; 0 2 0), 1.355 (30; 6 2 -7 / 8 1 -8), 1.188 (30; 0 2 10), 4.61 (20; 2 0 -3), 3.59 (20; 0 1 2), 2.425 (20; 2 1 -5), 1.916 (20; 3 1 -7 / 6 1 -1), and 1.490 (20; 6 2 -3). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
