Amblygonite

Amblygonite is the fluorine-dominant lithium aluminium phosphate mineral with ideal formula LiAl(PO4)F, distinct from hydroxyl-dominant montebrasite and from lithium as an element. Natural crystals form a substituted F/OH series rather than necessarily pure endmembers. Groat2003 provides primary neutron-diffraction and NMR evidence for this series, including F-rich natural specimens. Triclinic P-1 and the centered C-1 convention describe settings of the same structure. A separate thesis reports heating of a natural specimen labelled amblygonite, but its mixed starting material and conflicting fluorine estimates straddle the species boundary. The thermal observation is retained only for that author-assigned specimen, not as a universal amblygonite decomposition threshold, microbial mechanism or lithium recovery yield.

Argentojarosite

Argentojarosite is the silver ferric hydroxysulfate mineral with ideal formula AgFe3(SO4)2(OH)6, with ferric iron. The September 2026 IMA list records it as redefined, 1987 s.p. It is distinct from potassium jarosite and from silver ions, metallic silver or a generic industrial jarosite precipitate. Mukherjee's 2013 Ohio State MSc thesis identified synthetic argentojarosite following silver-nitrate addition to an iron-oxidizing culture's ferric sulfate and poorly crystalline ferric precipitates. Cavansite

Cavansite is a hydrated calcium vanadyl silicate mineral, Ca(V4+O)(Si4O10)(H2O)4, approved as IMA 1967-019 and retained in the September 2026 IMA list. It is a distinct species from pentagonite despite their related composition. The primary Oregon description and later natural-crystal work support a porous silicate framework containing vanadyl groups, calcium and water. Pcmn in the original description and Pnma in the 2012 study are different settings of the same orthorhombic space-group type, with the a and c axes interchanged. The 2009 proposed H3O+/OH-bearing formula is disputed by the 2012 hydrogen-bond refinement, and is not substituted for the current IMA formula. Natural-crystal heating experiments support protocol-dependent abiotic dehydration, without a microbial mechanism, mineral-specific recovery yield or industrial deployment. No criticality designation is inherited from vanadium or other constituents.

Coffinite

Coffinite is the uranium-bearing silicate mineral listed by IMA-CNMNC in September 2026 as U(SiO4)·nH2O, with grandfathered status G and original-description year 1956. That year is not a numbered mineral approval. Coffinite is distinct from uraninite, dissolved uranium, uranium-bearing ores and synthetic mixtures that merely contain uranium and silicon. The variable water term is not a measured hydration number for every specimen. Suzuki et al. (2016) identified natural coffinite nanoparticles in the L3 calcium-carbonate layer of a fracture-filling vein from borehole 07MI07 at the Mizunami Underground Laboratory, Japan. Elemental mapping, selected-area electron diffraction and lattice imaging support their assignment; the particles occur with aluminosilicate and contain other elements including phosphorus, zirconium and yttrium. The authors' proposed microbial origin is an interpretation of a natural setting, not a controlled organism-specific formation experiment. The separate laboratory mechanism below concerns newly formed coffinite on a prepared UO2 pellet, not bioleaching of that natural vein or testing of irradiated spent fuel. No criticality designation is inherited from elemental uranium, and no isolated-metal yield or industrial operation is asserted.

Corkite

Corkite is the lead ferric sulfate-phosphate hydroxide mineral with ideal formula PbFe3(SO4)(PO4)(OH)6, distinct from arsenate-bearing beudantite, plumbojarosite, pyromorphite and elemental lead. The September 2026 IMA list records its redefinition by special procedure in 1987. Ferrenti et al. (2023) synthesized corkite-bearing powders and characterized their structure and thermal behavior. The best products remained mixed with approximately 9.5 weight percent Pb5(PO4)3Cl; refined phase fractions are not synthesis yield or absolute purity. Both diffraction models fit similarly, and substitution, disorder and sample differences remain relevant. Goldmanite

Goldmanite is the calcium-vanadium garnet mineral with ideal formula Ca3V3+2(SiO4)3, status A and approval 1963-003 in the September 2026 IMA list. It is distinct from elemental vanadium, generic vanadium-bearing garnet and mixed uranium-vanadium ore. Moench and Meyrowitz described fine natural grains from a prospect pit in the Sandy mine area, Laguna district, New Mexico. The chemically analyzed separate was a substituted garnet, calculated as 59.8 mole percent goldmanite endmember with grossular, andradite, pyrope and spessartite components; this is a composition calculation, not a phase fraction or recovery yield. Its total vanadium was reported as V2O3, but the authors explicitly could not determine vanadium and iron valences chemically and inferred trivalence from crystal chemistry. Earlier unusually high vanadium assays may have included spinel contamination. The ideal species formula therefore does not describe every natural grain as a pure endmember. Abiotic laboratory syntheses are documented below. No criticality designation is inherited from vanadium, and these studies do not establish microbial extraction or industrial recovery.
