Used platinum-bearing catalyst from petroleum reforming, represented by a source-described monometallic Pt/Al2O3 catalyst. This use-defined secondary resource is distinct from automotive converters, nickel steam-reforming catalysts, refinery hydroprocessing catalysts, fresh catalyst and elemental platinum. The selected feed composition does not define every reforming formulation. No constituent criticality or mineral identifier is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Anion exchange and selective platinum elution from real catalyst leachate

separation

A commercial quaternary-ammonium anion-exchange resin loaded platinum and iron from an acidic spent-catalyst leachate. Sequential dilute hydrochloric acid and thiourea elution separated an iron-rich fraction and a platinum-bearing liquid. This is an abiotic laboratory separation, not microbial activity or production of isolated platinum metal.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Sun et al. section 2.1 identifies actual spent Pt/Al2O3 catalyst leachate. Table 1 gives feed Pt 0.25, Fe 0.07, Al 45.78 and Si 0.08 mass%, with minor other elements; its plant and service duration are unspecified. Table 2 gives Pt 124, Fe 23.7, Al 4870 and Si 5.4 mg/dm3 in 5.9 kmol/m3 HCl. The Introduction and Figure 6 attribute pretreatment to a previous study: roasting at 800 degrees C and HCl/H2O2 leaching; Figure 6 adds 5 hours and 10 degrees C/min for roasting. Those details are contextual rather than a newly verified complete leaching protocol. Diaion SA10AP resin was used as received. Batch experiments at 25 degrees C mixed 20 mL liquor with 0.5-100 g/dm3 resin for 24 hours; liquid metals were measured by ICP and resin loading inferred by balance. Columns were 250 by 10 mm at 25 degrees C with downward flow at 1.5 mL/min. The breakthrough arm used 1 g resin; the separation arm used 5 g resin and 200 cm3 real leachate. Independent replicate counts and uncertainty estimates for these arms are not clearly reported.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: sun_2015
Sections 3.1.2 and 3.2.2, Figure 2: Pt and Fe co-loaded while Al and Si largely remained in solution. The 5 g column arm reports 99.7% of feed-liquor Pt and 99.6% of feed-liquor Fe loaded. Sections 3.2.3-3.2.4 and Figures 4-5 report iron removal with 0.0001 mol/dm3 HCl, followed by 0.1 mol/dm3 thiourea eluting 99.6% of loaded Pt within 20 bed volumes. These percentages have different denominators and are not whole-catalyst metal yields.
Partial Source: sun_2015
Section 3.2.4 reports 99.3% Pt recovery from the leaching solution and 99.6% purity. Figure 6 ends in Pt-containing effluent: no isolated metallic product or independently quantified bulk product-purity assay is established. Overall catalyst-to-product recovery, resin reuse, industrial operation and economic performance are not demonstrated.
Partial Source: sun_2015
Section 3.1.1, Table 4 and Figure 1 fit a separate synthetic PtCl4 solution at 124 mg/dm3 Pt and 5 kmol/m3 HCl. The 128.1 mg/g Langmuir/Sips capacity is a fitted parameter, not measured capacity in the real multi-element leachate. Good model fit does not uniquely prove a molecular adsorption mechanism; the paper's proposed ion-exchange/speciation equations are not independent speciation measurements.
Partial Source: sun_2015
Tables 5-6 retain less effective comparison eluents. Increasing batch HCl from 0.0001 to 0.01 mol/dm3 reduced reported Fe elution from 99.9% to 83.2%. Table 6 reports Pt elution as nil for NaCl/NaOH, 1% for Na2S2O3, 4.7% for HNO3 and 5% for Na2CO3. Prose instead describes Na2S2O3 as unable to elute Pt and both HNO3/Na2CO3 as below 5%; these small table/text discrepancies are unresolved. The greater-than-99.9% thiourea value is a batch loaded-resin elution result, distinct from the column and whole-process figures.
