Aluminosugilite mineral

Aluminosugilite (IMA 2018-142) is an IMA-accepted potassium-sodium-aluminium-lithium double-ring silicate mineral of the milarite (osumilite) group, the A-site Al3+-dominant analogue of sugilite (KNa2Fe3+2Li3Si12O30), with September 2026 IMA Master List ideal formula KNa2Al2Li3Si12O30 (status A, PDF p. 8) and holotype empirical formula K0.99Na1.99(Al1.38Mn3+0.31Fe3+0.24Ti0.05Mg0.01)Sigma1.99Li3.06Si11.99O30 on the basis of O = 30 (Nagashima et al. 2020, pp. 57, 60). Combined WDS electron-microprobe analysis (35 points at 15 kV, 6 nA) and laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS, 48 spots on the Li 670.706 nm emission line) in Table 1 (p. 60) report SiO2 72.20 (range 70.51–74.23, SD 0.73), TiO2 0.43 (0.15–1.15, SD 0.21), Al2O3 7.06 (5.03–9.44, SD 1.04), Fe2O3 1.92 (0.69–5.60, SD 1.20; total Fe as Fe2O3), Mn2O3 2.42 (0.48–4.43, SD 0.78; total Mn as Mn2O3), MgO 0.04 (0.00–0.65, SD 0.11), CaO 0.01 (0.00–0.10, SD 0.02), Na2O 6.17 (5.99–6.43, SD 0.11), K2O 4.68 (4.39–4.86, SD 0.11), Li2O 4.59 (4.33–4.70, SD 0.10), total 99.52 wt% (V, Cr, Rb, and Cs below detection limits). Described by Nagashima, Fukuda, Matsumoto, Imaoka, Odicino, and Armellino (2020) from the Cerchiara mine, near Faggiona, Borghetto Vara–Pignone, La Spezia province, Liguria, Italy (44.116° N, 9.420° E; collected in 1997 by Gianluca Armellino; holotype deposited at the National Museum of Nature and Science, Tsukuba, Japan, registration number NSM-MF-16503), where it fills millimeter-scale interstices in Jurassic ophiolitic manganiferous metacherts (metamorphosed at prehnite–pumpellyite facies, P = 0.25 +/- 0.05 GPa, T = 250–300 °C, and affected by oxidizing Na–K–Li-rich hydrothermal metasomatism) in close association with quartz and K-feldspar (within a deposit assemblage that also includes braunite, hematite, calcite, pectolite, aegirine to aegirine-augite, richterite, andradite, piemontite, ganophyllite, balestraite, and Mn-bearing carbonates; pp. 57–58). Forms aggregates of transparent pinkish-purple prismatic and/or granular crystals up to 1 mm in length (individual grains typically 50–100 um) with pale purple to white streak, vitreous lustre, brittle tenacity, Mohs hardness 6–6.5, indistinct/poor cleavage on (0001), Dmeas = 2.71–2.72 g/cm3 (by flotation in methylene iodide–toluene heavy liquids), Dcalc = 2.73 g/cm3, uniaxial (–) optics with omega = 1.577–1.586 (average 1.581), epsilon = 1.575–1.585 (average 1.580), weak pleochroism, Gladstone–Dale compatibility index 1 - (KP/KC) = +0.003 (superior, for average Dmeas = 2.71 g/cm3), magnetic attraction in a Frantz isodynamic separator at 0.9 A (15° side tilt, vs 0.6 A for Fe3+-rich sugilite from Iwagi Islet), and insolubility in HCl, HNO3, and H2SO4 (pp. 57–59). Hexagonal, space group P6/mcc (Z = 2); single-crystal X-ray diffraction on a 0.038 x 0.036 x 0.028 mm crystal (MoKalpha, lambda = 0.71073 Å, 590 unique reflections refined to R1 = 0.0217, wR2 = 0.0599, S = 1.071; Table 2, p. 62) gives a = 9.9830(4) Å, c = 13.9667(5) Å, V = 1205.45(7) Å3 in the abstract and text (printed as 1205.45(1) Å3 in Table 2, p. 62), while CuKalpha1 powder X-ray diffraction gives refined cell parameters a = 9.954(7) Å, c = 13.95(4) Å, V = 1197(4) Å3 (p. 61). The crystal structure (Tables 3–4, pp. 62–63) consists of double six-membered [Si12O30] rings of T1O4 tetrahedra (<T1–O> = 1.6102 Å) cross-linked by edge-sharing octahedral A sites (A–O3 = 1.9395(12) Å, refined scattering occupancy Al0.629(6)Fe0.371) and strongly angularly distorted T2(Li)O4 tetrahedra (T2–O3 = 1.9700(12) Å, bond-angle variance sigma2 = 545.8), with K+ at the 12-coordinated C site (C–O2 = 2.9950(13) Å) and Na+ disordered across the split 9-coordinated B' site (1/3, 2/3, 0.008(6), occupancy 0.5, <B'–O> = 2.660 Å). A second occurrence at the Funakozawa mine, Hirono Town, Iwate Prefecture, Japan (40°15'08'' N, 141°37'04'' E shaft; 40°15'10'' N, 141°36'44'' E river boulder at the Ono–Funakozawa confluence), documented in the second IMA-listed reference by Nishio-Hamane, Nagashima, Mori, Ohki, and Suzuki (2024, Journal of Mineralogical and Petrological Sciences 119:240509), forms foliated reddish-light purple crystals up to 1 mm in diameter and 50 um thick (Mohs hardness 6, indistinct cleavage, almost colorless with weak pleochroism in thin section) in <= 5 mm quartz layers along the schistosity of thermally metamorphosed biotite-zone slate, coexisting with aegirine, amphibole (ferri-leakeite, potassic-richterite), quartz, norrishite, braunite–neltnerite series minerals, albite, titanite, Mn-bearing diopside, marsturite, and cerchiaraite-(Mn), and altering in outcrop into swinefordite (Li-rich smectite) with foliated texture retained (pp. 1–3). SEM-EDS analyses (JEOL IT-100, 15 kV, 0.8 nA, 1 um beam; Table 1, p. 4) yield average compositions (on Si = 12 apfu, Li = 3 apfu fixed) of K2O 4.55 (4.19–4.88), Na2O 5.93 (5.46–6.41), Al2O3 6.59 (6.03–7.87), Fe2O3 3.47 (1.46–4.68), Mn2O3 1.32 (0.78–1.85), Li2O 4.44 (calculated), SiO2 71.36 (70.35–72.47), total 97.66 wt% (K0.98Na1.93(Al1.31Fe0.44Mn0.17)Sigma1.91Li3Si12O29.82) for the boulder sample (n = 10) and K2O 4.42 (4.08–4.83), Na2O 6.12 (5.67–6.60), Al2O3 5.72 (4.29–6.95), Fe2O3 4.02 (1.48–6.30), Mn2O3 2.36 (1.56–3.29), Li2O 4.45 (calculated), SiO2 71.54 (69.64–73.48), total 98.63 wt% (K0.95Na1.99(Al1.13Fe0.51Mn0.30)Sigma1.94Li3Si12O29.88) for the outcrop sample (n = 15). Synchrotron powder XRD at SPring-8 BL02B2 (lambda = 0.999393 Å, 49 peaks indexed in d = 8.65–1.35 Å) refines P6/mcc cell parameters a = 9.9834(4) Å, c = 13.9669(8) Å, V = 1207.00(11) Å3 (p. 4), and single-crystal XRD refinement on a 0.11 x 0.08 x 0.04 mm boulder crystal (Rigaku HyPix-6000HE, MoKalpha, 8,184 collected / 1,022 unique / 786 observed reflections, Rint = 5.81%, R1 = 2.67%, wR2 = 7.97%, S = 1.07; Tables 2–4, pp. 4–7) yields a = 9.9894(7) Å, c = 13.9527(9) Å, V = 1205.77(14) Å3 in abstract/text (pp. 1, 4) and 1205.77(19) Å3 in Table 2 (p. 5), structural formula KNa2(Al1.34M0.66)Li3Si12O30 (M = Fe + Mn; AAl occupancy Al0.669(5)Fe0.331, A–O3 = 1.9385(10) Å, T2–O3 = 1.9735(10) Å, C–O2 = 2.9922(11) Å, BNa split at z = 0.0124(9) with 0.5 occupancy), and attributes the <= 20–30% Mn3+ limit in P6/mcc sugilite-series minerals to the regular AO6 octahedron resisting Jahn–Teller distortion (pp. 5–6). Distinct from Fe3+-dominant sugilite, Mn3+-bearing sugilite varieties, and elemental lithium; no criticality designation, microbial mechanism, or industrial recovery yield is asserted.
