## Primary experimental evidence and identity

[Gao et al. 2020, DOI 10.16108/j.issn1006-7493.2019032](https://doi.org/10.16108/j.issn1006-7493.2019032) was acquired from the [official PDF endpoint](https://geology.nju.edu.cn/EN/PDF/10.16108/j.issn1006-7493.2019032). All eight original pages, the Chinese Methods/Results/Discussion, three tables and six figures were read and visually inspected. The PDF is *Geological Journal of China Universities* 26(3):286–293; an English portal label saying *Acta Metallurgica Sinica* is inconsistent with the original and is not used. The DOI resolver identifies the same Chinese title/authors through China DOI/CNKI; Crossref returned 404, which is not evidence that the DOI is invalid. No separate supplemental file is linked in the inspected original or article-specific page links; generic supplement-handling JavaScript is not treated as a scientific supplement. Redistribution terms for the paper are unknown; raw copies remain outside Git.

Sections 2.1–2.4 describe synthetic nanomaghemite prepared by reducing goethite to magnetite and oxidizing that product. Four assay arms distinguish MA/MB media and no-cell/inoculated conditions, with three parallel bottles per arm. All receive ascorbic acid. Washed cells, anoxia, lactate media, 37°C and 31 days are explicit. Starting cell density and initial pH are not provided. The reported 0.22 g mineral/30 mL and 50 mmol/L are not silently reconciled. MA and MB replace sulfate/chloride salts; this is not a perfectly identical ionic matrix. Triplicate experimental design is distinguished from the three susceptibility readings averaged by the instrument protocol.

Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–5 support reduced-iron and structural/magnetic changes in inoculated arms relative to no-cell controls. The recorded result is movement toward magnetite-like products; M3 is modeled as cation-deficient and M4 closer to ideal magnetite. Section 3.2 describes no separate XRD phase besides maghemite before interpreting lattice shifts. This overlap and the fitted XPS/Mossbauer evidence do not yield pure phase fractions or recovery percentages. Sulfur signals are not unique evidence for separate pyrite/greigite/mackinawite owners. M4 aqueous Fe(II) and total Fe are operational dissolved concentrations; below-detection dashes elsewhere are not zero. Table 1 solid Fe(II) percentages, fitted spectral areas and changing susceptibility are different quantities.

Figure 3's English caption repeats Fe labels; its Chinese caption and Section 3.3 describe sulfur. Table 3's doublet assignments are retained as source model labels, not independently verified phase fractions. Figure 6 is a cartoon, not microscopy or a direct-contact count. No contact-exclusion, killed-cell, cell-free-metabolite or sulfide-only arm isolates the proposed direct electron-transfer pathway. Sulfide chemistry, metabolites and interactions with ascorbate are not apportioned. Tables/time curves show no dispersion despite stated biological triplicates. The paper cites Zhou2019 from overlapping authors as preceding work; distinct publication does not prove an independent experiment. This record imports no uninspected Zhou2019 measurement or complete-conversion claim.
