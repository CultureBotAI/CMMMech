Ginelfite mineral IMA: Glf

IMA-approved complex silver-thallium-lead-antimony-arsenic boxwork sulfosalt mineral (IMA 2022-110; symbol Glf; status A, Approved 2023; full description published May 2025) with ideal end-member formula Ag2(Ag0.5Fe2+0.5)TlPb24.5(Sb,As)32.5S75.5 reported in the primary type description title, abstract, Section 4.1, and Table 7 (Biagioni et al., 2025, European Journal of Mineralogy 37:319-335; note that Table 8 on p. 332 prints the formula with S76 as Ag2(Ag0.5Fe0.5)TlPb24.5(Sb,As)32.5S76, and the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC Master List, PDF p. 82, lists the pre-refinement formula Ag2(Ag0.5Fe2+0.5)TlPb23.5(Sb,As)33.5S76 prior to recognizing the half-occupancy of the S(71) site and final Pb/(Sb,As) site assignments). Named in honour of Carlo Gini (born 1954; technician from 1988 to 2019) and Francesco Guelfi (born 1947; technician from 1981 to 2011), former technicians at the X-ray laboratory of the Dipartimento di Scienze della Terra of the Universita di Pisa, by combining their surnames. Discovered in specimens collected by co-authors Georges Favreau, Jean-Claude Boulliard, and Vincent Bourgoin during a sampling campaign authorized by the Ecrins National Park at the Tl-rich hydrothermal deposit of Jas Roux, La Chapelle-en-Valgaudemar, Hautes-Alpes department, France (44 deg 44 min 45 sec N, 6 deg 19 min 18 sec E), from the centre of the cliff of the tectonic block indicated as "compartiment 2" by Mantienne (1974), where the Tl-Hg-Ag-Sb-As mineralization occurs within a 3.8 m thick baryte-rich zone hosted in Triassic metadolostone and marble (matching the earlier unnamed mineral UM2000-44-S:AgAsPbSb of Smith and Nickel, 2007, first reported by Mantienne, 1974, indicated as "madocite argento-thallifere" by Moelo, 1983, and examined by Johan and Mantienne, 2000). In type material, ginelfite occurs associated with Fe-poor sphalerite (pale brownish grains with trace Cd) and lopatkaite (5 spot analyses averaging Pb 55.82(29), As 3.80(8), Sb 20.98(8), S 19.93(6), total 100.52(30) wt%, corresponding on a 20 apfu basis to Pb4.84(3)Sb3.09(1)As0.91(2)S11.16(3), Ev(%) = -2.8(6)%) within a silicified matrix devoid of vugs, with baryte and quartz; holotype material is deposited in the collections of the Museo di Storia Naturale of the Universita di Pisa, Calci (PI), Italy (catalogue number 20023) and in the collections of the Department of Mineralogy and Petrology, National Museum in Prague, Czech Republic (catalogue number P1P 31/2022). Forms dark-grey acicular crystals up to 0.5 mm in length with a black streak, metallic lustre, opaque diaphaneity, brittle tenacity, conchoidal fracture, no observed cleavage, calculated density Dcalc = 5.765 g/cm3 from the empirical formula and single-crystal unit-cell parameters, and historical Vickers microhardness VHN25 = 165 kg/mm2 (range 157-170 kg/mm2 for five indentations on five grains; Johan and Mantienne, 2000), corresponding to a Mohs hardness of 3 to 3.5. In reflected light in air, ginelfite is light grey with weak pleochroism and weak bireflectance, moderate anisotropism with weak brownish and blue tints, and no internal reflections; Commission on Ore Mineralogy (COM) reflectance values in air (Rmin, Rmax in %; MSP400 TIDAS spectrophotometer, 50x objective, WTiC Zeiss 370 standard; Table 1) are 35.4, 38.1% (470 nm), 34.6, 37.0% (546 nm), 33.7, 36.0% (589 nm), and 32.3, 34.6% (650 nm). Wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analysis (35 spot analyses, Cameca SX 100, 25 kV, 20 nA, 1 um beam diameter; Table 2) yielded Pb 45.44 wt% (range 44.81-45.83, e.s.d. 0.21), Sb 23.46 wt% (23.10-23.94, e.s.d. 0.19), S 21.07 wt% (20.62-21.32, e.s.d. 0.14), As 6.17 wt% (5.84-6.48, e.s.d. 0.17), Ag 2.34 wt% (2.20-2.42, e.s.d. 0.05), Tl 1.14 wt% (0.98-1.34, e.s.d. 0.09), Fe 0.22 wt% (0.20-0.25, e.s.d. 0.01), and Cu 0.07 wt% (0.00-0.09, e.s.d. 0.02), total 99.91 wt% (range 99.21-100.59, e.s.d. 0.32), corresponding on the basis of SigmaMe = 61 atoms per formula unit to the empirical formula Cu0.12(4)Ag2.51(5)Fe0.46(3)Tl0.65(5)Pb25.40(14)Sb22.32(18)As9.54(24)S76.12(74) (Sb + As = 31.86(12) apfu, Sb/(Sb + As) = 0.701(7), and relative error on valence equilibrium Ev(%) = -1.1(1.0)% with range -2.3 to +1.7% in Table 2 assuming Fe as Fe2+, or -0.8(1.0)% if Fe occurs as Fe3+). Crystallizes in the triclinic system, space group P-1 (#2), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 8.3635(6) A, b = 27.5481(19) A, c = 29.199(2) A, alpha = 95.335(3) deg, beta = 94.123(3) deg, gamma = 94.367(3) deg, V = 6657.7(8) A3, and Z = 2 (refined with twin ratio 62.2 : 37.8(2) around [100] to R1 = 0.0876, R(all) = 0.1131, wR2 = 0.1764, and GoF = 1.221 for 23,779 reflections with Fo > 4sigma(Fo) out of 30,492 unique reflections from 291,055 measured at 293(2) K using MoKalpha radiation, Table 4). The zinkenite-related boxwork sulfosalt structure (organized around two symmetry-independent Pb6S6 triangular rods) contains 61 independent cation sites (21 pure Pb sites Pb(1)-Pb(19), Pb(21), and Pb(22); 1 mixed ninefold tricapped trigonal prismatic Tl(20) site Tl0.65Pb0.35 with <Tl(20)-S> = 3.29 A and BVS = 1.30 v.u.; 12 mixed Pb/Sb sites M(1)-M(12) with M(9)-M(12) split into a/b sub-positions; 24 Sb/As sites comprising 8 pure Sb sites, 1 pure As site As(20) with <As(20)-S> = 2.27 A and BVS = 3.15 v.u., and 15 mixed Sb/As sites including split Sb(21a)/Sb(21b); 2 pure Ag sites Ag(1) and Ag(2) in 4+1 trigonal dipyramidal coordination with <Ag-S> = 2.78 and 2.79 A and BVS = 1.05 and 1.04 v.u.; and 1 mixed (Ag,Fe) site Ag(3) with refined occupancy Ag0.51(2)Fe0.49(2) in distorted 4+2 octahedral coordination, <Ag(3)-S> = 2.70 A, and BVS = 1.68 v.u.) and 76 independent S sites (including half-occupied S(71), occupancy 0.50; Tables 5-6), built by combining three boxwork modules: partition rod A (3 polyhedra wide x 4 atomic layers thick, Me12S16), fill-element rod B (4 atomic layers thick, Me14S18), and continuous (001) wall layer C (Me35S42, formed by two-atomic-layer-thick ribbons R1, R2, and R3), whose modular formula sum on p. 328 is (Ag0.51Fe0.49)Ag2(Tl0.65Pb0.35)Pb21(Sb7.59Pb4.41)Sigma12.00(Sb15.52As8.48)Sigma24.00S75.5 (Ev = -1.7%). The seven strongest observed Gandolfi powder X-ray diffraction lines highlighted in bold in Table 3 (dobs in A (Iobs; dcalc, Icalc, hkl), CuKalpha) are 3.413 (s; 3.417, 48, 0 8 0; 3.395, 84, 0 4 7; 3.393, 28, -2 -2 5; 3.393, 27, 2 -3 4), 2.965 (s; 2.960, 15, 2 -5 5; 2.957, 32, -2 4 6; 2.956, 34, 2 3 5), 2.805 (ms; 2.816, 13, -2 -5 6; 2.816, 17, 2 -6 5), 2.729 (ms; 2.733, 19, 0 10 0; 2.732, 17, -2 7 4; 2.732, 10, 2 -4 7; 2.731, 20, 2 6 3), 2.091 (vs; 2.091, 100, -4 1 1), 1.872 (ms; 1.873, 17, 2 -13 5; 1.873, 15, -2 -12 6), and 1.785 (ms; 1.780, 13, 4 3 6). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
