Rhodium element Critical Material

Rhodium (Rh). The element is distinct from the Rh(III) chloride hydrate used as experimental feed, monometallic biomass-supported particles and mixed PdRh nanoparticles. The US 2025 list names the rhodium commodity individually; that designation is mapped to the element without extending it to every Rh-bearing compound, mineral or secondary resource. Curated evidence concerns laboratory transformation in synthetic solutions, not extraction from ore, purified-metal recovery or industrial deployment.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Resting-cell transformation of soluble Rh(III) to Rh(0)-bearing particles

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Geobacter sulfurreducens (NCBITaxon:35554) [TaxonMech]

Washed resting Geobacter sulfurreducens cells supplied with acetate removed Rh from a synthetic single-metal solution and formed cell-associated particles identified as metallic Rh by XRD and selected area electron diffraction. Cell-free controls did not show comparable Rh removal. The observation supports cell-associated reduction under these conditions, without establishing complete conversion to Rh(0), a specific cytochrome requirement or an isolated-metal recovery yield.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory 10 mL batches in serum bottles contained 200 micromolar Rh supplied from a 50 mM RhCl3.xH2O stock in deionized water, 10 mM sodium acetate and 30 mM sodium bicarbonate. Bottles were flushed for at least 30 minutes with 80:20 nitrogen/carbon dioxide, pH 6.8-7.0, then received resting cells at OD600 0.4 and were incubated at 30 degrees C for up to 24 h. Biomass had been grown anaerobically at 30 degrees C with 20 mM acetate and 40 mM fumarate to late log phase, harvested at 4920 g and 4 degrees C for 20 minutes, and washed twice in 30 mM bicarbonate pH 7. The paper does not provide a culture-collection or strain accession; the species identifier does not resolve that omission. Rh remaining in solution was quantified by ICP-MS after 14800 g centrifugation for 20 minutes and nitric-acid dilution of the supernatant. Results 3.1.1 reports greater than 95% aqueous Rh removal within 1 h, whereas no-cell Rh controls retained the dissolved pool. This is centrifuged-supernatant depletion, not a quantitative Rh(0) fraction. Washed 24 h solids were assessed by XRD and TEM/SAED; the inferred crystallite size and image particle size have different measurement bases.

Partial Source: xie_2026

The record does not transfer the bimetallic Rh XPS percentages to this preparation.
Partial Source: xie_2026
Methods 2.2 says controls without electron donor were prepared, but Rh-specific donor-free outcomes are not shown in Figure 1 or tabulated in the inspected article or supplementary Table S1. Replicate counts and definitions of Figure 1 error bars are not specified. A matched killed-cell comparison and direct genetic or protein-level demonstration of Rh-reducing cytochromes are not reported; acetate dependence, viability and a unique enzyme pathway therefore remain unestablished.

Rh transformation during simultaneous Pd/Rh exposure of resting cells

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Geobacter sulfurreducens (NCBITaxon:35554) [TaxonMech]

A separate simultaneous exposure of resting Geobacter sulfurreducens to Pd(II) and Rh(III) generated cell-supported mixed nanoparticles. The authors' combined XRD, atomic-resolution imaging and elemental mapping support a PdRh alloy interpretation, with mixed surface oxidation states. This preparation is distinct from monometallic Rh particles and from sequential Pd-seeding protocols.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Methods 2.2 repeats the 10 mL, OD600 0.4, 10 mM acetate, 30 mM bicarbonate, pH 6.8-7.0, 30 degrees C anaerobic assay with 100 micromolar Pd plus 100 micromolar Rh added together, supplied from Na2PdCl4 and RhCl3.xH2O stocks. Results 3.1.2 reports 97% aqueous Rh removal within 1 h with cells; the cell-free mixture also lost approximately 20% Rh by the later endpoint, unlike the single-metal no-cell Rh control. The authors propose co-removal during Pd hydrolysis as an explanation. Characterized solids were collected after 24 h. No purified alloy product yield, full Rh mass balance, real waste-feed trial or deployment is established by this synthetic-feed experiment.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: xie_2026
Figure 1d and Results 3.1.2 support faster and greater Rh depletion in the cell-containing mixture than in its no-cell control. Figures 2 and 5b and Results 3.5.2 combine an intermediate lattice spacing and Pd/Rh co-localization to support the authors' alloy assignment. The XRD/Vegard-law estimate of Pd71/Rh29 and EDS estimates near Pd60/Rh40 are method-dependent estimates, not one exact uniform particle composition; Figure 5 labels field averages.
Partial Source: xie_2026
Figure 3 and Table 1 fit the bimetallic surface Rh signal as 81% Rh(0) and 19% Rh(III). These are fitted surface proportions, not whole-feed reduction yields. The partial abiotic Rh removal and unknown donor-free outcomes preclude assigning all Rh depletion to metabolism. Proposed changes in complex speciation, reduction potential and cytochrome mediation were not directly resolved in this assay; no specific microbial enzyme is established.
Context_only Source: xie_2026
Methods 2.4, Results 3.6, Figure 6 and supplementary Table S1 concern subsequent NaBH4-driven 4-nitrophenol catalysis at room temperature, normalized to 4 mol% total metal relative to the initial organic substrate. Bio-PdRh performed better than monometallic bio-Rh in this benchmark, while bio-Pd had the fastest initial rate. These catalyst results are not Rh recovery percentages or demonstrations of microbial metabolism during the catalytic test.
