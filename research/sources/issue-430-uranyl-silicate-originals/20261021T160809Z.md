## Giammar final article and thesis comparison

All eleven final pages and all twenty-six Chapter 6 pages were read and viewed, including the original plot axes/captions. Other thesis chapters and appendices were not scientifically reviewed. The original-paper method is synthetic soddyite, not Plášil's natural museum crystal: two separately synthesized powders, batch versus stirred flow conditions, and zero versus 100 µmol/L added Si remain separate. Methods (article pp3236–3237; thesis physical 147–150) specify 22 ± 2 °C, 5 mmol/L MES, 0.01 mol/L NaNO3, pH 6.00 and 3.15 m²/g BET area. Batch loading is 0.13/0.12 g/L; approximately 50 mL flow reactors use 0.41–0.42 g/L. Residence-time ranges are 1.14–1.26 h without added Si and 1.22–1.88 h with Si. These are not industrial conditions or biological controls.

Final §3.3 (p3240) and thesis physical 160–161 give flow means 0.70 ± 0.11 and 0.45 ± 0.06 µmol m⁻² h⁻¹ for zero/100 µmol/L Si. The final abstract and §4.4 instead give 0.71 and 0.44. The thesis repeats 0.70/0.45 in its discussion/conclusion; it does not explain the final mismatch or define the ± statistic. The final calculation averages after ten reactor volumes; the thesis's averaging paragraph does not supply that same exclusion instruction. No aliquot-by-aliquot rate ledger or independent per-arm replication count was acquired. The displayed concentration trajectory changes within each run, so a single rate summary is not a constant observed trajectory.

Thesis physical 160 explicitly defines the calculated release basis as uranium, using effluent concentration divided by hydraulic residence time, solids loading and specific area. There are two U atoms per soddyite formula unit: a hypothetical congruent formula-loss conversion would divide a U-release rate by two, but no such converted value replaces the reported measurement. The separate batch fitted rates use different windows: U 4–198 h and Si 90–700 h. Thesis physical 153–154 additionally prints mass-normalized rates 0.20 µmol U g⁻¹ h⁻¹ and 0.11 µmol Si g⁻¹ h⁻¹. Dividing those rounded numbers by 3.15 gives 0.0634921 and 0.0349206 µmol m⁻² h⁻¹, near the reported 0.064 and 0.035; this is a rounding diagnostic, not reconstruction of raw fits. The ratio 0.064/0.035≈1.82857 cannot establish simultaneous stoichiometry because the fitting windows differ.

The earlier draft adds genuine version conflicts. Its physical 153 describes uranium behavior through 365 or 200 h, while the final fitted endpoint is 198 h; physical 154 says silicon was available for the first 365 h although it also fits 90–700 h, and physical 166 says later samples were lost after 700 h. The final paper explicitly retains the 700 h loss boundary. These are preserved text differences, not a guessed corrected time series. The thesis Figure 6.3 includes flow and batch comparison lines; final Figure 3 does not duplicate both lines.

Both versions show late new needles and a new XRD reflection near 15° 2θ, with residual soddyite. The final article discusses clarkeite and structurally related sodium uranates rather than resolving a unique secondary phase. Figure 5's 1680 h label differs from the 1683 h SEM/text; this three-hour discrepancy remains literal. Raman is another version distinction: thesis physical 159 assigns the 820/866 cm⁻¹ features to two sites for the same symmetric stretch, whereas final p3240 discusses symmetric/antisymmetric assignments. The separately synthesized reference and model comparison do not determine a unique hydration stoichiometry or bulk phase fraction. Thesis homogeneous-nucleation language is not promoted over the final paper's unresolved mineral/wall nucleation location. Wall adhesion and lost Si samples remain mass-balance limits.

Article Table 1 has fifteen thermodynamic constants; thesis Table 6.1 (physical 151) lists only six selected reactions. Article Table 2 (p3242) and thesis Table 6.2 (physical 162) are compilations/recalculations of earlier solubility studies, not additional direct Giammar experiments. The thesis's literal U and Si column headers are **mM**, whereas the final table uses **µM** for the same displayed values (e.g. 19.3 and 5.12). Ionic-strength headers also differ, M versus mM, with correspondingly different printed values. The thesis Pérez row prints NaHCO3 ≥5 mM while nearby prose uses >2 mM; the final row gives 1–20 mM. No unit, range or solubility constant is silently repaired or transferred across versions.

Equilibrium outputs also changed. Thesis physical 164–165 uses a secondary-phase log K of 8.81 and predicts 16.1% of U in soddyite, 83.6% in secondary solid and 0.3% dissolved; an alternative log K of 9.00 predicts 35.4% in secondary solid and 64% in soddyite. Final p3242 uses log K 9.02, dissolved U/Si 2.2/24.4 µmol/L and modeled secondary/soddyite U 47/311 µmol/L. These are distinct assumption-dependent equilibrium models, not measured conversion, isolated uranium recovery or experimentally determined phase fractions. Thesis and article saturation indices and free-uranyl calculations also differ. The new version supplies context, not grounds to choose the preferred model as fact.
