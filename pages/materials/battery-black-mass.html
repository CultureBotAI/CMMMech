Spent lithium-ion battery black mass secondary resource

A heterogeneous powder stream produced by pretreatment of spent lithium-ion batteries. Composition depends on battery chemistry and processing; the curated experiment used nickel-manganese-cobalt battery material after discharging, dismantling, thermal treatment, crushing and sorting. This secondary resource is distinct from whole batteries, an individual cathode compound, cobalt or lithium elements, and recovered battery-grade products. Constituent-element criticality is not inherited by this mixed feed.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Adapted acidophile consortium leaching of NMC battery black mass

bioleaching Microbial

A living acidophilic enrichment from Lake Hromnice sediment mobilized metals from a pretreated NMC battery black mass. Culture-associated acidification and iron cycling accompanied release; the experiment does not separate acid chemistry from a direct cellular contribution or establish which community member caused the effect. Adaptation to dissolved-metal mixtures improved several measured leachate outcomes.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory flasks contained 50 mL FeS medium with 50 mM ferrous sulfate, 1% w/v elemental sulfur and initial pH 2.0, inoculated at 10% v/v. Seven days of preculture preceded addition of 1% w/v black mass, followed by seven days at 30 degrees C and 150 rpm. The tested powder had d10, d50 and d90 of 4.4, 17.2 and 55.0 micrometres; thermal pretreatment temperature and time were not specified. Original FeS enrichment was dominated by Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans, with Alicyclobacillus also detected; the post-adaptation community was not established. No pure strain or causal organism identifier is assigned. Adaptation consisted of three 14-day exposures to synthetic mixtures of Li, Co, Ni, Mn and Cu salts. Abiotic sterile FeS medium and non-adapted culture were tested alongside adapted culture. Figure captions report n=3 and standard deviations. This is laboratory solubilization, not industrial operation.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: lalropuia2024
Sections 2.5-2.6 and 3.3, Figures 4-6: adapted cultures produced higher day-two leachate concentrations of the monitored metals except Cu than non-adapted cultures and abiotic controls, according to the reported comparisons. Concentrations generally peaked by day two. ICP-OES measured filtered, acid-treated leachate and day-zero metal backgrounds were subtracted; this establishes mobilization rather than isolation of pure products. Section 2.5 describes 0.45-micrometre filtration, while Section 2.6 specifies a 0.2-micrometre analytical sample filter; the precise relationship of these preparation steps is not explicit.
Partial Source: lalropuia2024
Figure 4 shows different pH and iron speciation in sterile and inoculated arms, preventing attribution of the difference uniquely to direct cell contact. Iron-mediated reduction, sulfur oxidation and copper catalysis are proposed explanations, not separately demonstrated molecular pathways. Table 2 adaptation doses do not consistently equal Table 1 composition multiplied by the stated black-mass equivalents. Figure 5 adapted-Co concentrations near 2 g/L also exceed the 1.45 g/L inventory implied by Table 1's 145 g Co/kg and the stated 1% w/v feed loading. These unresolved source quantities prevent acceptance of a closed mass balance or the abstract's complete-recovery claim; no corrected yield is inferred.
