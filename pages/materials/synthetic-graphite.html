Synthetic graphite commodity Critical Material

Manufactured crystalline graphite commodity produced industrially by high- temperature graphitization (~3,000 degrees C) of carbonaceous precursors such as calcined petroleum coke and coal-tar pitch. USGS 2021 Minerals Yearbook (Graphite chapter, printed pp. 32.1-32.16) and USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (Graphite (Natural) chapter, printed pp. 92-93) distinguish synthetic graphite from natural flake, amorphous, and vein/lump graphite (cmmmech:natural-graphite and mineral species cmmmech:graphite) and track synthetic graphite across electric-arc-furnace electrodes, unmachined shapes, motor brushes, steelmaking carbon raisers, and lithium-ion battery active anode material (HTS 3801.10, 3801.20, 3801.90, 6903.10, and 8545.11). In the 2023 US Department of Energy Final Critical Materials List (88 FR 51792-51798), DOE evaluated 'Graphite (natural and synthetic)' and 'Graphite--synthetic' in Table 1 (p. 51796, where synthetic graphite alone was scored 'Not Critical' in both short and medium terms) while explicitly noting in Footnote 6 (p. 51796) that the 2022 DOI/USGS Final List of Critical Minerals defined 'graphite' to include both natural and synthetic graphite. This record captures the manufactured synthetic-graphite commodity and its explicit authority scope boundaries without asserting a standalone mineral species identity or microbial mechanism.
