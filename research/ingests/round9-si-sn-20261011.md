## Silicon evidence

[Shrestha and Hildebrand (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1128/EC.00209-14),
Eukaryotic Cell 14:29–40, provides full primary methods and results. Its PMC
HTML was acquired successfully after an initial browser challenge/API failure.
Figures 7 and 8 were downloaded from the article's NCBI image links and visually
inspected. Supplementary construct/alignment files were unavailable through
checked PMC, Europe PMC, publisher and institutional routes; downloaded error
HTML/XML was identified and excluded from evidence.

The living CCMP1335 cultures were maintained in NEPC medium with 100 micromolar
Si(OH)4 at 18 °C under continuous 100 microeinsteins/m²/s illumination.
Silica-incorporation Methods specify 24 h silicon deprivation, whereas figure
captions say overnight. Sodium silicate then supplied a nominal 20 micromolar
Si(OH)4. The uptake assay measured disappearance from centrifuged supernatant
using silicomolybdate colorimetry, normalized per cell. The incorporation assay
used 100 ng/mL PDMPO added five minutes before silicate and flow cytometry.
Net depletion and fluorescent silica incorporation are distinct proxies.

Figure 7 and its Results report faster depletion by wild type than joint
SIT1/SIT2 RNAi/antisense knockdowns; Figure 8A–B shows lower incorporation signal
at 20 micromolar. In Figure 8C, a subset of clones at 200 micromolar has the
reported null incorporation comparison, despite nonidentical trajectories.
The Discussion sometimes calls this uptake, but the actual panel measures
PDMPO incorporation. The record uses the assay-specific interpretation.

The perturbation does not separate SIT1 from SIT2, delete either gene, or prove
an exclusive pathway. The immunoblot antiserum recognizes all three SITs.
Replication is stated as n=3 with SD, without specifying independent cultures.
An abiotic depletion blank, measured pH and rescue experiment are not described
in the inspected main text. The sensing interpretation remains a hypothesis,
including an alternative upstream sensor. No purified silica mass, phase-pure
quartz, metallic silicon or industrial application is demonstrated by this study.

## Tin evidence

[Finnegan et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23030627),
Molecules 23:627, was read in full Europe PMC XML and a successful PMC HTML
variant. Figures 3–4 were downloaded and visually inspected. Publisher and
institutional PDF attempts included HTTP errors and challenge HTML; none was
misrepresented as an acquired PDF. The article XML states CC BY 4.0 and no
supplement, while a supplementary API request timed out. Raw files remain
outside Git regardless of their terms.

C22-inoculated Courtown Beach sediment was prepared by autoclaving at 121 °C
for 40 min, sieving to 1 mm, spiking nominal 100 ng/g tributyltin chloride in
methanol, and air drying for one week. Sealed laboratory microcosms held 200 g
sediment, a 2 cm water column and, for the inoculated arm, 10 mL overnight
culture in nutrient broth. Incubation was dark at room temperature, with exact
temperature, salinity, pH, water composition and cell density unresolved.
Sampling ran from day 0 to day 120 in 30-day intervals.

Figure 3 shows lower TBT in C22 than its nearly stable uninoculated control,
with DBT and MBT signals. Figure 4 confirms the chromatographic pattern.
Selected-ion GC-MS with reference compounds supports chemical speciation after
ethylation. The ethylated formulas in the figure are analytical derivatives,
not environmental products. Loss of butyl groups does not demonstrate reduction
of tin, complete destruction of organotin, biological detoxification or a
recoverable tin product.

Several limits are material to interpretation. Methods describe 5 g from each
replicate to a 15 g total, suggesting pooled biological samples; analytical
triplicates do not recreate biological replication. A sterile-broth-matched
control and killed-cell condition are not described. The reported C22 day-120
TBT value of 52.73 ng/g exceeds the highest stated linear calibration range
of 50 ng/g (§2.3.1). Separately, §2.3.2 reports sediment spike-recovery accuracy
tests at 5-60 ng/g wet weight (n=3), with 94-103% analytical recovery and relative
standard deviations below 10% across the tested organotins. These tests do not
establish microbial extraction yield or linear calibration above 50 ng/g;
dilution or a calibration extension is not reported, and the recovery tests do
not extend to the nominal 100 ng/g starting spike. The record retains the endpoint
as an author-reported result with this narrower quantitative uncertainty, not a
validated percentage or elemental mass balance. DBT and MBT are reported
as 28.5 and 13.8 ng/g wet sediment; MBT visibly declines after day 90 without
measurement of its subsequent fate. No certified-reference sediment validation,
viability trajectory, total-tin balance or toxicity endpoint was supplied.

## Alternatives and follow-up

The known [Willner et al. LCD study](https://doi.org/10.5277/ppmp1878) was inspected
as full publisher PDF text through web access; local downloads returned 403 and
figure pixels were not assessed. Its 9K sterile control leached tin at least
comparably to the inoculated system, with final tin concentrations of 4.59 versus
3.15 mg/dm³. Proposed reprecipitation and phase identities remain hypotheses.
It is a valuable separate comparative-leaching candidate, requiring graph and
organism/chemistry review before curation. The existing indium record was not
changed. The primary [purified-SIT study](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms11926)
is also a separate future assay; its protein results are not transferred to
living CCMP1335. Earlier diatom regulation/uptake studies and isotope-enriched
organotin tracer work remain bounded leads in the JSON handoff.

Exact-title/DOI correction and retraction queries plus targeted counterevidence
searches returned the original articles and related work without a matching
notice in the inspected first-result sets. This is not an exhaustive registry
survey. The strongest limiting evidence here comes from the primary methods,
control comparisons, high-supply null result and incomplete chemical endpoints.
