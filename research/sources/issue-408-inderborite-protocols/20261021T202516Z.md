## Pressure experiment: preserved arms and source disagreements

The complete main and SI were read and all 15 original page renders viewed: main Figures 1–3, Tables 1–3, SI Table S1 and Figures S1–S4. The same natural sample as the neutron study supplied two distinct approximately 20 × 15 × 10 micrometre crystals. Printed p. 475 describes ESRF ID15b, helium pressure medium, ruby calibration uncertainty 0.05 GPa, two membrane DACs, approximately 30 keV/0.41 Å radiation, Eiger2X detector and 0.5-degree/0.5-second scan steps. Sample lineage does not establish a separate chemical assay for either tiny crystal.

Table 1 (PDF 3/printed 475) has 16 first-arm and 13 second-arm rows. The first arm changes from the last starting-phase point at 8.11(5) GPa to the first indexed high-pressure point at 8.80(5), reaching 10.10(5). The second arm brackets the change at 7.45(5)/8.96(5). Prose on the same page instead says 9.84(5) maximum. These sampled intervals are not an equilibrium boundary. The first observed volume step, 1494.6 to 1390 Å³, is about 7.00%; the second, 1513.3 to 1375, is about 9.14%. Each includes a pressure increment, so neither is an independently determined isobaric discontinuity. Figure 3 and the methods describe severe diffraction deterioration and failure to recover the starting crystals within the experiment. The high-pressure space group and atomic structure remain unsolved; no universal thermodynamic irreversibility is inferred.

Table 2 (PDF 6/printed 478) gives O1–O6–O4 increasing 165.7(1)→172.3(3)° and O6–O1–O8 decreasing 125.6(1)→118.6(2)°. Printed p. 479 states the opposite directions. In addition, the table defines delta as initial minus final, but its O2–O3–O6 row prints −7.8(3) although 89.8−82.0 gives +7.8. These source disagreements are preserved, not repaired or used as specific causal directions. Conditional endpoint arithmetic also distinguishes the printed p. 479 lattice-contraction prose from Table 1 values: the first-arm c change is about −5.65%, whereas the beta-angle fractional change is about +0.331%. These are diagnostics on displayed endpoints, not a replacement strain analysis.

Table 3 retains the second-order Birch–Murnaghan fit with bulk K=41(1) GPa and K′ fixed at 4; the axis fits are not the principal strain eigenvalues. Ca/Mg/B1/B2 K values are 53/81/260/170 GPa. Relative to bulk 41, the B2 ratio is about 4.15, so the broad p. 479 statement that both B polyhedra exceed five times the bulk modulus is not supported by these central values. No re-fitting was attempted.

All 37 Table S1 rows × 14 pressure columns, plus the 14 shared pressure tokens, are retained. Quadratic-elongation rows near 10 and B2=19.919 at 1.80 GPa are literal original values. The stated definition on p. 475 is the mean squared ratio of bond length to the centre-to-vertex length of a same-volume regular polyhedron. Conditional calculations from the printed bonds/volumes give values near unity for these Mg/B tetrahedral or octahedral reference geometries. They establish a scaling/consistency question, not an authorized division by ten or correction of 19.919. Mean-bond diagnostics preserve displayed precision and all repeated cells. Figure S3 expressly treats inderite as an exception to the proposed water-content trend; no universal causal rule is adopted.

All four pressure CIFs contain 16 non-hydrogen sites and no H atom sites. Their `constr` hydrogen-treatment scalar does not create missing H coordinates. Their doubled non-hydrogen formula/Z convention is not proof of dehydration. Cell geometry identifies their Table 1 first-arm points at 0.43, 3.84, 6.23 and 8.11 GPa, allowing differences in displayed precision: 16 of 20 central cell values are equal; three a values and one volume differ in precision. The current official dictionary defines `_diffrn_ambient_pressure` and the legacy `_cell_measurement_pressure` alias in **kPa**. The first pressure CIF therefore says 0.33 GPa from its literal 330000, not 0.43. The other three convert to 3.84, 6.23 and 8.11. Unit verification does not resolve which first pressure is intended.

The deposited geometry is also not uniformly identical to Table S1: only 8 of 72 selected unique cation–oxygen central-value joins are exactly equal. ## Neutron deposit: what it adds and what it cannot settle

All 25 R1 pages were inspected, including all Table 1 concentrations on p. 19, Table 2 on pp. 20–21 and both figures, with repeated monochrome versions. The final published first page 1258 was also viewed and separately bound. R1 describes specimen #8029, Quarry 98/Inder, EPMA-WDS on three crystals with quartz micro-inclusions and modest beam damage; near-ideal chemistry is specimen-specific. Table 1 reports 41 analytes with six-analysis means, 22 censored concentrations and absent sigma cells for those censored entries, not zero concentrations. The B-isotope weighted mean is +35.15±0.49‰ (2σ,N=6), also present on the final first page. The R1 p. 10 range uses a literal percent sign despite the per-mille mean/table context; it is not silently normalized here.

The official July deposit metadata identify American Mineralogist 109 (2024), first page 1258 and the correct DOI. Its two structural blocks contain 27 sites each, including 11 H labels, with eight symmetry operations; the X-ray model has 16 anisotropic non-H sites, and the neutron model 24 anisotropic sites, with B represented isotropically. No `_refln_` intensity loop occurs in either block. Their formula CaMgB6O22H22 and Z=4 distinguish these complete hydrous models from the pressure refinements. Temperatures are 293(1) and 20(1) K; ambient pressures are 100(2) kPa.

The R1 methods (pp. 8–9) describe locating H from negative neutron difference-Fourier peaks and implementing the neutron H model in the X-ray refinement; the X-ray H atoms share Uiso=0.057 Å². These are not independent unconstrained determinations of every H coordinate in both datasets. Table 2 starred O–H distances explicitly include a riding-motion correction and are excluded from the simple coordinate-distance checks.

The native monoclinic cells, eight symmetry operations and periodic images independently generate 12 listed hydrogen-bond contacts per model. All 96 uncorrected D–H/H···A/D···A/angle comparisons fall within one printed Table 2 standard uncertainty. This tolerance uses the printed table uncertainty only; coordinate covariance was not propagated, no refinement was performed, and agreement does not prove final/R1 identity. In the 20-K model, H10···O9 is about 2.27036 Å, O11···O9 about 3.04415 Å and O11–H10···O9 about 136.43°. These support R1 Table 2's 2.271(4)/3.044(2)/136.4(3) assignment. They do not support calling 2.271(4) the O11···O9 separation as R1 p. 12 does. The final main-paper wording remains unchecked.

Version differences prevent an unqualified merge of manuscript and CIF metadata. R1 names ILL **D9**, while the neutron CIF names **D19**. The latter includes a literal double-underscore `__diffrn_radiation_source` tag and lacks a neutron wavelength scalar, while R1 specifies 0.8348 Å. The X-ray CIF gives lmax=25 and theta-max=29.088°, whereas the R1 methods give lmax=26 and nominal coverage to 2theta=60°. These are recorded as provenance/coverage differences, not chosen corrections. The current CIF parser preserves literal backslashes such as `MoK\a` and distinguishes metadata and structural data blocks.
