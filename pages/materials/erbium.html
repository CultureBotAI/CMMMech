Erbium element Critical Material

The element Er, distinct from its dissolved ions, experimental nitrate salt, host minerals and manufactured materials. The dated US designation concerns the named erbium mineral commodity, without transferring criticality to every Er-bearing substance. The curated laboratory experiment documents element in harvested cyanobacterial biomass, not a resolved uptake pathway, purified metal recovery or industrial deployment.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Erbium association with harvested cyanobacterial biomass

other Microbial

Associated Organisms: Limnospira platensis (NCBITaxon:118562)

Cultures reported as Arthrospira platensis CNMN-CB-02 yielded harvested dried fractions containing Er after growth in synthetic Er(III)-supplemented medium. Increasing nominal exposure was associated with increasing element concentration in this fraction. Total-element analysis does not distinguish intracellular bioaccumulation from surface association or nonbiological material retained with the biomass.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The study reports Arthrospira platensis CNMN-CB-02 from the Institute of Microbiology and Biotechnology, Technical University of Moldova. The official collection lists CB-02 as Spirulina platensis. NCBI maps both species names to Limnospira platensis; the species identifier does not verify a strain genome or a strain-specific NCBI node. Living cultures were exposed to Er(III), supplied as Er(NO3)3·6H2O, in separate single-element treatment series at 10, 20 and 30 mg/L elemental concentration. These are not mixed-REE selectivity assays. Supplementation occurred on day 1 with 0.4 g/L inoculum, followed by five more days of cultivation. Flasks held 500 mL of nutrient medium, including 8 g/L bicarbonate and 0.08 g/L EDTA, at pH 10.0 and 30 degrees C. Illumination was reported as 55 µM photons/m2/s; the paper uses this nonstandard printed unit, retained without silently converting it. Cultures were shaken at 100 rpm for 45 min twice daily, 12 h apart. Controls were cultures grown without added REEs. Experiments were reported in triplicate, with Student t-tests against controls. Biomass was retained on 5-8 µm filters and part was dried for instrumental neutron activation analysis (NAA); reference materials supported analytical quality control. The methods do not describe a post-filtration wash, a biomass-free exposure control or cell-surface versus intracellular fractionation. NAA measures total element in the harvested dried fraction, not free-ion speciation or aqueous depletion. Initial solution concentrations were checked by ICP-OES, but no final dissolved-element balance is reported for this experiment. No desorption, isolated product or industrial demonstration was tested.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: zinicovscaia2024
Sections 2.1-2.3 and Section 3/Figure 1 report Er in the dried biomass fraction increasing with the nominal Er concentration; the text gives 5470-21,400 mg Er/kg of that fraction. This is a source-reported concentration range, not an isolated-product yield. Figures 2-4 and the results describe lower biomass quantities and phycobiliproteins, with increased MDA, under these particular Er exposure conditions.
Partial Source: zinicovscaia2024
The harvested-fraction result supports biomass-associated Er accumulation only. Without a reported wash, abiotic exposure control, aqueous mass balance or localization assay, surface binding, intracellular uptake, retained medium and co-retained precipitate are not separated. Alkaline bicarbonate-containing medium further makes unmeasured chemical partitioning relevant; precipitation is a possible alternative, not a demonstrated phase. The record therefore uses process other rather than asserting biosorption, biomineralization or metabolic transport as a resolved cause.
Partial Source: zinicovscaia2024
The 2024 text reports a 21.3% biomass decline at 30 mg/L, but the corresponding Figure 2 bar heights do not clearly reproduce that percentage against their plotted control. Only the direction of inhibition is retained here; neither exact percentage nor a general toxicity threshold is inferred.
Partial Source: yushin2022
The earlier study of the same named strain reports no statistically significant biomass-quantity change at 10-30 mg/L Er (Section 3.4 and Figure 6), unlike the 2024 growth result. Its living-culture protocol used erbium sulfate, six days at 25-28 degrees C and pH 8-9 with lower reported illumination (Section 2.5), so these are different conditions, not direct replications. Its control concentration is printed in mg/L in the prose while Figure 6 uses g/L, preventing unqualified reuse of that number. Section 3.2 also explicitly avoided pH above 6 in the separate dried-biomass sorption experiments because of Er(OH)3 formation; this reinforces the need to resolve chemical partitioning in alkaline living cultures without proving the same phase formed in the 2024 experiment.
