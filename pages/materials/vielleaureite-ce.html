Vielleaureite-(Ce) mineral

Vielleaureite-(Ce) (IMA 2022-134, symbol Vlr-Ce; formerly reported as UM2006-15-SiO:AlFHMgMnREE after Cenki-Tok et al. 2006) is an IMA-accepted manganese-cerium-magnesium-aluminium fluor-hydroxy disilicate-orthosilicate mineral of the dollaseite group within the epidote supergroup with September 2026 IMA-CNMNC Master List ideal formula Mn2+Ce(MgAlMn2+)(Si2O7)(SiO4)F(OH) (PDF p. 228, status A), representing the A1 = Mn2+ analogue of khristovite-(Ce) CaCe(MgAlMn2+)(Si2O7)(SiO4)F(OH) and the (A1 = Mn2+, M3 = Mn2+) analogue of dollaseite-(Ce) CaCe(MgAlMg)(Si2O7)(SiO4)F(OH), stabilized by the coupled heterovalent substitution M1Me3+ + O4O2- = M1Mg2+ + O4F-. Described by Ragu, Bindi, Bonazzi, Remusat, and Chopin (2025) from millimeter-thick rhodochrosite–sulfide–quartz veinlets crosscutting massive microcrystalline rhodochrosite ore in Lower Carboniferous greenschist-facies siliceous metasediments (~4 km from the 302–305 Ma Néouvielle granite pluton) collected ~10 m from the adit entrance at the Coustou manganese mine above the village of Vielle-Aure, Aure valley, Hautes-Pyrénées, France (holotype sample VA2016-5 and X-rayed crystal deposited at the Musée de Minéralogie, École des Mines de Paris, catalogue no. ENSMP 83943; cotype deposited at the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle, Paris, catalogue no. MNHN_MIN_223.001, pp. 627–629). Occurs as scarce dark-brown to dark reddish-brown anhedral to subhedral prismatic grains elongated along [010] up to 200 um across, patchily zoned from F-poor, V-rich vanadoandrosite-(Ce)/vanadoakasakaite-(Ce) cores to near-endmember vielleaureite-(Ce) overgrowths, associated with rhodochrosite, quartz, chalcopyrite, vanadian spessartine (up to 1.7 wt% F in crystal cores), friedelite, Mn2+-dominant hellandite-(Y) relics (Mn/(Mn + Ca) = 0.49–0.61), monazite-(Ce), baryte, vanadian pyrophanite, minor hübnerite, galena, pyrite, ZnS, alabandite, cobaltite, chalcocite, rare skinnerite(?), helvite, welinite, manganhumite, and armoured relict inclusions of parisite-(La), fluorite, an unidentified Y-silicate, and a BaFCl phase (likely zhangpeishanite) (pp. 628–630, Fig. 1). Exhibits a reddish-brown streak, translucent diaphaneity, vitreous to adamantine lustre, brittle tenacity with uneven fracture, no cleavage or parting, no fluorescence under the electron beam, Mohs hardness ~6 (by analogy with allanite-group minerals), Dcalc = 4.33 g/cm3, and biaxial optics (589 nm) with alpha > 1.74 (Gladstone–Dale calculated n = 1.79) and strong pleochroism (very light yellow < light brown < brown // b, pp. 629–630). Mean of 4 WDS electron-microprobe analyses (Cameca SX Five, 15 kV, 40 nA, 2 um beam; Section 4, pp. 627, 630–631, Table 1) on the extracted holotype crystal fragment VA2016-5 (whereas NanoSIMS light-element analysis in Section 8.2, pp. 635–636, Table 8 was conducted on the coexisting Mn-dominant hellandite, showing trace Li, Be/Si = 0.0096(6) or BeO 0.09 wt%, stoichiometric B/Si = 0.98(8), and OH/Si = 0.56(7)) yielded SiO2 28.08 wt% (range 27.19–28.84, esd 0.85), MnOtot 21.75 (19.76–22.73, esd 1.36; partitioned for electroneutrality on 8 cations and 12 anions into MnO 21.57 and Mn2O3 0.20), Ce2O3 17.23 (17.11–17.47, esd 0.16), Al2O3 8.44 (8.00–8.97, esd 0.43), La2O3 8.36 (8.09–9.03, esd 0.45), MgO 4.71 (2.02–5.92, esd 1.83), F 2.56 (0.94–3.38, esd 1.11), V2O3 1.63 (0.39–4.00, esd 1.63), H2Ocalc 1.43 (for OH = 1.00 apfu), CaO 1.21 (0.57–2.27, esd 0.79), Fe2O3 0.70 (0.18–1.73, esd 0.71), Nd2O3 0.57 (0.15–0.81, esd 0.29), Er2O3 0.41 (0.08–0.77, esd 0.29), Gd2O3 0.05 (0.00–0.19, esd 0.09), Y2O3 0.04 (0.03–0.06, esd 0.01), Cl 0.04 (0.02–0.06, esd 0.02), P2O5 0.03 (0.00–0.05, esd 0.02), B2O3 0.03 (0.00–0.09, esd 0.04), Sm2O3 0.03 (0.00–0.07, esd 0.03), Yb2O3 0.03 (0.00–0.12, esd 0.06), Na2O 0.03 (0.00–0.06, esd 0.03), Dy2O3 0.02 (0.00–0.06, esd 0.03), SrO 0.01 (0.00–0.03, esd 0.01), -O=F,Cl 1.08 (0.41–1.43, esd 0.46), total 96.32 wt% (low total due to small crystal size and heterogeneity), giving on 8 cations pfu the empirical formula A1(Mn2+0.85Ca0.14Na0.01)Sigma1.00 A2(Ce0.66La0.32Nd0.02Er0.01)Sigma1.00 M1(Mg0.71V0.14Mn2+0.08Mn3+0.02Fe3+0.06)Sigma1.01 M2Al1.00 M3(Mn2+0.98Mg0.02)Sigma1.00 T1-3(Si2.94Al0.04B0.01)Sigma2.99 O11(F0.85Cl0.01O0.14)Sigma1.00(OH) (note that the rounded A2 cation proportions sum to 1.01 while printed as Sigma1.00, and Table 5 on p. 633 lists the proposed M1 site population with combined Mn0.09). Monoclinic, space group P21/m, Z = 2 (Section 5, pp. 630–634, Tables 2–6, Fig. 2): single-crystal MoKalpha XRD refinement on a 0.030 x 0.025 x 0.020 mm crystal (Bruker D8 Venture Photon III, 1565 reflections with Fo > 4sigma(Fo) out of 2224 unique, Rint = 0.0747, R1 = 0.0575, R(all) = 0.1002, wR(Fo2) = 0.1168, GoF = 1.028, Table 2, p. 632) gives a = 8.8238(13) Å, b = 5.7131(9) Å, c = 10.0034(16) Å, beta = 112.823(6)°, V = 464.80(13) Å3, with bond-valence sum 1.17 v.u. at O4 confirming F- dominance (Table 6, p. 634); CuKalpha powder XRD refinement (Oxford Diffraction Excalibur PX Ultra, p. 630) gives a = 8.8123(8) Å, b = 5.7108(6) Å, c = 10.0048(13) Å, beta = 112.810(9)°, V = 464.13(6) Å3. Distinct from khristovite-(Ce), dollaseite-(Ce), vanadoandrosite-(Ce), vanadoakasakaite-(Ce), piemontite, and elemental cerium, lanthanum, manganese, or vanadium; no criticality designation for vielleaureite-(Ce) itself, no microbial or biological mechanism, and no industrial recovery yield are asserted.
