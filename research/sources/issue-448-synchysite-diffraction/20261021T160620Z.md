## Specimen and method mapping

Final Table A1 (p244/physical12) and thesis Table5.1 (physical85/printed77) agree: FCBN1 and FCBN4 supplied SCXRD and are marked lost; FCBN2 supplied SCXRD and SEM–EDS; FCBN3 supplied SCXRD, SEM–EDS and Raman; FCBN5 supplied TEM–EDS and 3DED on two thin foils. Generic preparation prose does not justify treating every method as performed on all five crystals. Missing cells are not measurements of zero.

FCBN5 TF01 spans the Ce-rich core/Y-rich rim boundary; TF02 spans the Y-rich rim/Th-rich outer rim boundary. Figure A1/thesis Figure5.2 shows a third prepared location that was lost, not a third successfully analysed lamella. Ten 3DED datasets were collected across the two retained foils. Dataset7 from the Ce-rich core was selected for dynamical refinement; the Table1 caption identifies the reported 3DED result as TF01/FCBN5. It is not one of the four SCXRD specimens. The site matrix and spatial identity must accompany any future deposited file.

Final Methods pp234–235 specify full-matrix least squares on F² with SHELX2018/3, monoclinic C2/c and the Wang1994 model. Ca/Ce/proxy Yb are anisotropic; C/O/F are isotropic, with soft C–O restraints. The 3DED method uses 200 kV, a holder capable of ±65°, a maximum120° range in1° steps, 0.2 s exposure and1° precession, with an approximately30 nm beam. These are acquisition settings, not growth conditions. The SIR2019 solution lacked some C/O sites; the subsequent Jana2020 dynamical model uses C–O and O–C–O restraints and planar carbonate constraints.

For 3DED, the chosen artificial occupancy scheme fixes .90Ca+.10Th at both Ca sites and .90Ce+.10Th at both Ce sites. It cannot be read as a measured10%Th chemical fraction. Table1 calculated densities and electron counts similarly depend on scattering models, defects and constrained occupancy; they are not independent elemental analyses or bulk product densities. The paper's 3DED absorption discussion concerns diffraction reduction, distinct from the absorption correction used for TEM–EDS.
