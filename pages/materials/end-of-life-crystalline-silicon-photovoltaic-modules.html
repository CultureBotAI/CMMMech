Discarded crystalline-silicon photovoltaic modules and their separated cell fractions are a composite secondary resource generated by photovoltaic equipment use and dismantling. Their silicon wafers, metal contacts, glass and encapsulants remain distinct from elemental silicon, silicon-metal commodity, silver and mineral species. This record excludes CdTe and CIGS thin-film modules, manufacturing scrap of unspecified origin and generic electronic waste. The curated experiment used a chemical extract from end-of-life Hyundai m-Si units, not intact modules in direct contact with microorganisms. Criticality designations of constituent elements are not inherited by this waste feed.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Silver-bearing cathode deposition during sludge-inoculated microbial fuel-cell treatment of panel leachate

recovery Microbial

A living anaerobic-sludge-derived bioanode supplied current in a laboratory dual-chamber microbial fuel cell treating a prepared photovoltaic-panel leachate at a separate graphite cathode. Dissolved silver decreased and silver-rich deposits appeared on the cathode. This follows thermal processing and chemical leaching; it is not direct microbial dissolution of the modules. The experiment does not establish an isolated-product yield for the original module inventory or identify a causally responsible microbial species.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Kanellos et al. (online 2023, volume 2024), Materials and Methods, pp. 77–78: end-of-life Hyundai m-Si modules were dismantled, fragmented, heated at 550 degrees C for 15 minutes, screened and their recovered cells ground. A 5 M nitric-acid extraction at a reported solid/liquid ratio of 30%, ambient temperature and one-hour contact preceded NaOH pretreatment and tenfold dilution. The resulting R4 catholyte started at pH 1.5 with approximately 200 ppm Ag, 50 ppm Cu and 900 ppm Al. A 70 mL anode chamber and 140 mL cathode chamber were separated by Nafion; operation was at 30 degrees C across a 100-ohm external resistance. Startup inoculum was 10% v/v anaerobic sludge from the municipal wastewater treatment plant of Attica, Greece, with glucose supplied at 1.5 g COD/L in buffered anolyte. Taxonomic composition and strains were not resolved. After oxygen-based acclimation, the cathode was changed from Pt-coated cloth to 3-by-3 cm graphite paper; catholyte was nitrogen-sparged for one minute. R4 comprised two consecutive approximately seven-hour batch cycles with fresh cathode paper per cycle, rather than two independent reactor replicates. Dissolved metals were measured by atomic absorption and electrode deposits by SEM-EDS and XRD. The biological compartment and the metal-deposition compartment are separate.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: kanellos2023
The R4 results on pp. 82–84 and Figures 7, 9 and 10 support decreasing dissolved Ag and Ag-bearing deposits from an actual panel-derived extract during operation of the inoculated cell. The authors report complete aqueous Ag removal within seven hours and mean removal rates of 31.4–33.6 micromol Ag/h. AAS detection limits, harvested deposit mass and a closed whole-module balance are not reported, so the stated complete recovery is retained as the authors' solution-based interpretation, not verified 100% recovery of panel silver.
Partial Source: kanellos2023
Methods and Figures 1–8 compare real mixed leachate R4 with separate synthetic single-metal solutions R1–R3. R4 silver removal was slower than the synthetic silver case, with a reported 61% lower mean rate. The solutions differed in co-solutes and conductivity, so this comparison does not isolate a single inhibitory mechanism. No matched uninoculated, killed-biomass or open-circuit R4 time course is reported; microbial necessity and the abiotic share of removal remain unresolved.
Partial Source: kanellos2023
Figure 9 and pp. 83–84 report local EDS Ag fractions of 68–96% w/w at different electrode positions. The separate 86% estimate is derived from solution analyses and is not a bulk assay of an isolated product. Figure 5 is assigned to fcc silver by the authors, but the displayed trace labelled carbon paper contains peaks at the same labelled positions; without raw diffraction or a resolved blank comparison, unequivocal phase identification from that figure is not assumed. Elemental EDS signals do not independently establish the proposed Cu, Al or Pb oxide phases.
Partial Source: kanellos2023
R4 retains most dissolved Cu and Al after seven hours: the text reports approximately 10% and 4% removal respectively. Co-deposition, electrode passivation and oxygen reactions are proposed explanations, not independently isolated causes. The starting Table 1 is labelled chemical-extract composition yet is silicon-dominated on a percentage basis that is not reconciled with the aqueous R4 concentrations; it is not used as a whole-feed recovery denominator. No purified reusable silver product, completed continuous process or industrial deployment is demonstrated.
