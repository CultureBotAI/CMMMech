## Companion: useful additional detail without a run join

The companion reports Mosaic phosphate-processing plant material in Florida, with concentrate about80% fluorapatite below200µm and clay-rich tailings below150µm. Supplementary Table1a repeats all18 main-paper REE values, including inconsistent totals. Repeated values and the same author team support a related-program inference; they do not prove an identical specimen, assay batch or experiment. There is no independently identified raw dataset or sample/run key in the inspected assets.

Its bacterium is reported as ATCC-derived `AC9989`. Exact-token and ATCC-site searches found no matching official strain record in returned results; this does not prove the identifier nonexistent. No guessed correction or substitution with an NCBI-listed type strain is made. This companion contains no corresponding fungal arm, so it cannot resolve the main paper's fungal dose or timing.

Companion pp92–93 use250mL flasks with90mL9K plus10mL inoculum,25°C,150–200rpm, five days and1/2/5/10/15wt% pulp. The nominal final liquid inoculum fraction is10%, materially different from the main paper's1mL in250mL and optimization1%. The reported growth/leaching acid concentrations also differ. Particle-size text uses32µm while Figure3's legend uses35µm; no preferred correction is assigned.

The chemical comparison uses100mL25% sulfuric acid atpH<2 under similar temperature/agitation. That arm is not uninoculated9K matched for medium, pH history, acidity and iron chemistry. Consequently it does not isolate a microbial contribution to otherwise identical tailings leaching. Bacterial fluoride concentration was not reliably determined according to the authors; chemical-arm ion-selective-electrode measurements do not validate a bacterial fluoride time course or a molecular inhibitory target.

Assay p93 adds a Sigma-Aldrich multi-element stock, each of16 named elements at50mg/L in2% nitric acid, and states that a calibration curve was used. This is useful information beyond the main article. It still provides no curve points/equation, calibration residuals, matrix blanks/spike recoveries, detection/quantification limits, elemental sample ledger or raw replicates. A multi-element calibration mixture does not turn a single ArsenazoI signal into16 separately measured leachate concentrations. Stock composition is not a recovery measurement.

The column work uses concentrate,105µm or a105/35µm mixture; it is not a demonstrated tailings-column arm. Methods give1mL/min, while the discussion reports about4mL/min. Fresh culture replacement changes several solution conditions together; faster leaching cannot uniquely prove that selective fluoride removal caused an improvement. Supplementary Figure1's rate is derived from cumulative REE curves using Origin8, without supplied fit/smoothing or raw data. These rates, cumulative percentages and shake-flask outcomes are not pooled.

Supplementary Table1b mixes oxide and mineral labels under a wt% header, including literal `P2O5 (Fluroapatite)` and `SO2`, with an explicit `RE2O3` row of826/304ppm. The same numbers under elemental-REE and oxide labels leave the analytical basis unresolved. No elemental-to-oxide conversion, normalized composition or pure-mineral assignment is inferred. Its tailings `<10%` remains censored; three dashes remain missing, not zero; `Dolomite` spans both sample columns.

Original supplementary figures show further limits: the zeta-potential plots in Figure6 lie predominantly below zero and vary with pH, whereas discussion p94 calls the values positive and discusses time. The plots cannot establish that prose as written or uniquely prove a calcium speciation/precipitation mechanism. Attachment/growth panels are representative curves without an authenticated feed-specific replicate key; no color is assigned to a particular feed without a legend. SEM imagery attributed to gypsum is not a quantitative phase inventory. These are source inconsistencies or inference limits, not newly confirmed defects in the unchanged owner.
