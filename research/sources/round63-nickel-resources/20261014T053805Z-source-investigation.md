### Feed, organism and assay boundaries

Page 33/Figure 4 and page 36 explicitly identify Votorantim Metais nickel-extraction wastes from Serra de Fortaleza-MG: flash-furnace **escoria** versus flotation **lama**. Table 7 contains percentage assays of total HF/microwave digestion versus partial aqua-regia digestion. These are not water-soluble fractions. The slag has Ni 0.26% total and 0.07% aqua-regia-soluble; the tailings 0.30% and 0.25%. XRD indicates a slag dominated by fayalite/forsterite-type silicate and heterogeneous carbonate/silicate/sulfide tailings. Minor phase labels have ambiguities and overlaps; no pure mineral, quantitative phase fraction or assured nickel host is inferred.

The source identifies *Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans* LR from acidic uranium-ore leachate at Lagoa Real, Bahia, citing Garcia 1991. The live [NCBI species authority 920](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=920&mode=info) resolves the species name but has no LR authentication. Its type-strain entries are not assigned to LR. Culture collection/genome linkage remains unresolved.

Pages 37-39: duplicate 250 mL flasks, 150 mL T&K, 5% w/v waste, 5% v/v inoculum, 30 C, 150 rpm, 14 days; the tailings repeat was 50 days. T&K preculture pH 1.8 is not identical controlled pH throughout leaching. Periodic acid corrections and differing trajectories appear in Figures 7 and 13. Arms were no added iron, 120 mmol/L Fe(II), and grown culture with about 120 mmol/L Fe(III), with corresponding uninoculated or source-labelled sterile-culture controls. Washed cells belong to separate Warburg respirometry, not explicitly these leaching flasks; iron/broth carryover is unresolved. The strong-acid D arm compares live ferric culture plus acid with sterile acid alone, so it is not iron matched. Solution assays, not isolated products, are measured; industrial feed origin is not industrial bioleaching.

### Results, counterevidence and exclusions

Slag Table 9 reports total-Ni inoculated/control 13/13%, 12/12% and 11/10% after 14 days. Thus similar Ni release is preserved, not falsely advertised as successful biological recovery. Total-Cu pairs are 8/5%, 8/7% and 4/4%; no statistical significance is inferred. Elevated redox and minor jarosite-assigned peaks do not establish nickel-pathway causality. Separate respirometry or stronger chemical-acid leaching is not a matched microbial-benefit control. The static slag section contains mol/L versus mmol/L and total-versus-soluble outcome inconsistencies, so static numerical yields are excluded.

Tailings Table 11 reports total-Ni pairs 42/24%, 46/30% and 45/37%, with total-Cu pairs 16/14%, 17/14% and 16/13%. Original Figure 14 is consistent with a conditional inoculation association, without displayed uncertainty or a formal statistical test. Figures 15 and 18 do not uniquely separate overlapping jarosite/silicate peaks or yield a mineral mass balance. The 50-day strong-acid arm shows total-Ni 44% versus 45% control; its chemistry is not matched. Static figures/tables show no general inoculation benefit, with a ferrous concentration mismatch (Figure 19: 120 mM; Figure 20: 30 mM) and denominator discrepancies. Precise static yields and inconsistent soluble-fraction transformations are not curated. Proposed oxygen/mass-transfer limitation is an author interpretation without direct oxygen measurements. These counterexamples limit extension to heaps or industrial use.

Returned results did not identify a notice for the accepted thesis or related paper. This bounded search cannot certify notice absence; original internal discrepancies are retained regardless.
