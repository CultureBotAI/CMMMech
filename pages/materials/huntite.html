Huntite mineral

Huntite is the calcium-magnesium carbonate mineral species CaMg3(CO3)4, grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list with original-description year 1953. That year is not an approval identifier. Huntite is distinct from dolomite, magnesite, hydrated magnesium carbonates and elemental magnesium. The reported culture products are mixed mineral assemblages, and no constituent-element criticality is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Huntite assigned in low-calcium aerobic halophile culture precipitates

biomineralization Microbial

The study identifies huntite in mixed precipitates from two low-calcium magnesium-rich agar media inoculated separately with halophilic bacterial cultures. The accepted claim is medium-associated phase formation; no exact strain-specific huntite production rate or pure-product recovery is established.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Separate surface-inoculated cultures from a panel reported as 21 moderately halophilic bacterial strains, predominantly historical Halomonas labels plus Marinomonas, Marinobacter, Chromohalobacter and Salibacillus, were incubated aerobically at 30 degrees C for up to 30 days. These were separate strain cultures, not one mixed consortium. The agar medium contained 1% yeast extract, 0.5% proteose peptone, 0.1% glucose, 3.5% NaCl (all weight/volume), and 20 g/L Bacto-Agar, initially adjusted to pH 7.2 with 0.1 M KOH. Calcium and magnesium were supplied as acetate salts. End-point surface pH was measured with indicator paper. Uninoculated and dead-cell controls were described for all experiments. Solids were scraped, washed with distilled water and dried at 37 degrees C before XRD and SEM-EDX; unchanged morphology after washing does not establish unchanged phase hydration. For the huntite-bearing arms, Table 1 lists C2 as 5.7 mM Ca and 28 mM Mg (reported Mg/Ca 5), and C3 as 5.7 mM Ca and 65 mM Mg (reported Mg/Ca 11.5). Table 3 reports huntite in C2 and C3 alongside dolomite, hydromagnesite and struvite; no huntite is assigned to the other listed media.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: sanchez_roman_2011
Table 3 identifies huntite by the reported XRD analysis in C2/C3. Figure 1B shows a C3 huntite spherulite with fibrous/radial structure and a Ca/Mg-bearing EDX spectrum; its caption specifies 30-day incubation for analytical material. Table 5 includes separate huntite carbon-isotope entries for C2 and C3 after the Section 2.5 sorting procedure. Isotope separation and morphology do not independently prove complete phase purity.
Partial Source: sanchez_roman_2011
Section 2.3 describes uninoculated and dead-cell controls; Section 4.1 explicitly reports no precipitation in those controls. However, Section 3 literally states that no precipitates occurred in any culture experiments, contradicting its surrounding positive results. The record retains this source inconsistency and uses the explicit control statement only as a reported result, not an independently tabulated control measurement. Replicate counts, strain-specific phase replicates and quantitative matched-control spectra are not supplied.
Partial Source: sanchez_roman_2011
The mineral assemblages in Table 3 and Figure 1 are resolved by medium, not by an independently documented strain-to-phase matrix. Table 2 reports only struvite versus carbonate onset, not phase-specific onset times. Methods and Table 2 disagree on strain names/accessions, including H. pantelleriensis DSM 9661 versus DSM 3051 and the presence of H. halmophila versus H. meridiana; H. eurihalina and H. salina also share a printed ATCC 49509. These conflicts are not silently corrected. No exact taxon identifier or claim that every named strain individually forms the target phase is assigned.
Partial Source: sanchez_roman_2011
Section 3 describes huntite after struvite in low-calcium C1-C3 media, whereas Table 3 assigns huntite only to C2/C3. The broader prose is not used to assert huntite in C1. Table 2 has carbonate onset times rather than huntite-specific times, and its struvite entries in other media conflict with some Table 3 assemblages. The source does not support a universal phase sequence or occurrence in every culture.
Partial Source: sanchez_roman_2011
XRD phase assignments are reported in Table 3, supported by SEM-EDX morphology/composition, but the article does not display the underlying XRD patterns for this phase. Its mineral percentages are not treated as calibrated product purity or recovered-magnesium yield. Surface alkalinization and organic-matter degradation are compatible with the proposed mechanism, but microsite pH, individual metabolic pathways and a specific molecular nucleator were not directly demonstrated. PHREEQC saturation indices are modeled initial bulk conditions, not measured cell-surface conditions; the model input Na/Cl concentrations also differ from those calculated from the stated NaCl recipe. The experiment does not establish industrial deployment or net atmospheric carbon sequestration.
