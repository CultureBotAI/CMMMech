Boron-ore concentration tailings secondary resource

Residual solids generated during concentration of boron-bearing ore. The curated feed is the minus-25-mm solid waste from the Espey concentrator of the Eti Mine Boric Acid Operation in Emet, Kütahya, Turkey. It contains colemanite, muscovite, quartz and dolomite according to the study's XRD characterization. This processing residue is distinct from unprocessed borate ore, traded borate products, elemental boron and a pure colemanite mineral. The sampled composition does not define every concentrator residue; no criticality designation is inherited from its constituents.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated alkali-metal leaching from boron concentrator waste

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Aspergillus niger (NCBITaxon:5061) [TaxonMech]

Cultures reported as Aspergillus niger mobilized lithium, rubidium and cesium into filtered leachate from the characterized mixed waste. Organic-acid dissolution and complexation are proposed explanations; no matched abiotic leaching series establishes the microbial increment or the necessity of either chemical route. The laboratory assay does not demonstrate isolated metal products or industrial deployment.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
A living fungal culture was supplied by the Department of Molecular Biology and Genetics at Erzurum Technical University; no strain accession or sequence authentication is reported. Collected waste was dried at 110 degrees C for 48 h, homogenized, pulverized and sieved through 38 micrometres. Medium, waste and containers were autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 15 min. A seven-day preculture used sucrose at 100 g/L with yeast extract, nitrate and mineral salts. Leaching used 250 mL flasks with 100 mL medium, 2.5 mL spore suspension and 1 g waste, at 30 degrees C and 125 rpm for 31 days. Spore concentration is unspecified. Samples on days 4, 10, 13, 18, 24 and 31 were centrifuged, filtered through 0.45 micrometres and stabilized in 0.65% nitric acid for ICP-MS. Figure 2 reports means and standard deviations with N=5, without resolving whether those replicates were independent cultures or analyses. Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: ertan2026
Materials pp. 2-3 establishes concentrator-waste origin and preparation; Table 1 reports the feed's measured Li, Rb and Cs concentrations. Figure 2 and Bioleaching Kinetic p. 4 show increasing assayed release over time, with reported day-31 extraction of approximately 79% Li, 37% Rb and 30% Cs. These are feed-relative leachate percentages, not isolated-product yields or exact dated mg/kg observations.
Partial Source: ertan2026
Methods and Figure 2 provide no uninoculated or matched organic-acid leaching series. Figure 1 labels its complexes as possible; solution speciation, mineral-specific metal release and causal contributions of individual metabolites were not directly resolved. The pH account on p. 3 explicitly cites Ertan 2023. This later article is not treated as independent replication of the earlier fungal study; its exact experimental overlap remains unresolved.
Partial Source: ertan2026
The p. 4 text gives 43.71% Li at day 24, whereas Figure 2 plots that point near 35%; no exact intermediate yield is adopted. Table 2 and Figure 3 compare fitted shrinking-core models, which do not independently prove a controlling biochemical mechanism. Figure 3d's rubidium diffusion ordinate is inconsistent with the printed equation and Figure 2 endpoint, so kinetic coefficients and a definitive chemical-control conclusion are not adopted. The experiment did not vary temperature to establish Arrhenius behavior.

Aspergillus niger CexA-associated organic acid secretion and one-step versus two-step alkali/alkaline-earth metal bioleaching from Espey boron-ore concentration tailings

REPRESENTATIVE EXAMPLE Mechanism: fungal_alkali_metal_mobilization

Causal pathway linking reviewed Swiss-Prot Aspergillus niger plasma-membrane MFS citrate exporter CexA (UniProtKB:A0A254TZW7 in NCBITaxon:5061 and UniProtKB:G3Y4N5 in ATCC 1015 UP000006706) and co-secreted fungal oxalic/gluconic acids (1 x 10^7 spores inoculated into 100 mL sucrose-based medium [100 g/L sucrose, initial pH 5.5] in 250-mL flasks at 30 C and 120 rpm over 45 days with 0.5%, 1%, or 2% w/v of <72 um Espey boron-ore concentration tailings [12.23 wt% B2O3, 0.21 wt% Li2O (~975 mg/kg Li), 2.70 wt% K2O, 9.29 wt% CaO, 8.72 wt% MgO]) to mode-dependent acid accumulation (889.8 mg/L oxalic, 91.6 mg/L gluconic, and 6.5 mg/L citric acid at pH ~2.15 in one-step 0.5% w/v vs 1006.9 mg/L citric, 512.9 mg/L gluconic, and 252.8 mg/L oxalic acid at pH ~2.8-3.5 in two-step 0.5% w/v; Figures 5-6) and day-45 mobilization of Li (76.5% one-step vs 60.2% two-step at 0.5% w/v; 82.1% in 50 mM abiotic oxalic acid control), K (37.2% vs 28.5%), Mg (26.8% vs 50.0%), Ca (34.2% vs 30.8%), Rb (22.5% vs 21.2%), Cs (12.6% vs 11.5%), Al (9.4% vs 7.2%), and Fe (7.9% vs 6.1%) in Ertan (2026), while noting that dissolved boron (B) extraction was not reported.
