Keutschite mineral

IMA-approved copper-silver-arsenic sulfosalt mineral of the stannite group (IMA 2014-038; status A, Approved 2014; full description published January 2026; no mineral symbol is printed in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 112, or in the primary type description) with ideal end-member formula Cu2AgAsS4 (requiring Cu 29.01 wt%, Ag 24.62 wt%, As 17.10 wt%, S 29.28 wt%, sum 100.01 wt%, printed as "29.01 Cu, 24,62 Ag, 17.10 As, and 29.28 S, for a total of 100.00" with a comma decimal separator in "24,62 Ag" in Section 3, p. 3; Topa, Stoeger, Dinca, and Downs, 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:1-7). Represents the Ag-As analogue of stannite (Cu2FeSnS4) and is named in honour of Dr. Frank N. Keutsch (b. 1971), Associate Professor in the Department of Chemistry and Chemical Biology at Harvard University, mineral collector, and discoverer of the mineral (Abstract, p. 1, and Section 1, p. 2). Discovered in a museum-grade specimen collected prior to 2011 from the alabandite-rich zone in the Socorro section of the Uchucchacua polymetallic deposit, Oyon province, Lima Department, Peru (latitude 10 deg 37 min 31 sec S, longitude 76 deg 41 min 17 sec W; telescoped intermediate-sulfidation epithermal vein/replacement Ag-Mn-Pb-Zn mineralization hosted in Upper Cretaceous limestones intruded by 12.7-7.5 Ma dacitic stocks and dykes; associated with proustite, xanthoconite, smithite, menchettiite, manganoquadratite, alabandite, galena, and calcite, with the same alabandite zone also hosting structurally/chemically related agmantinite, pirquitasite, stannite, and kesterite, and subsequently reported from the Clara mine, Germany, by Kolitsch et al., 2019, and the Sacaramb Au-Ag-Te deposit, Romania, by Dinca et al., 2025; Abstract, p. 1; Section 2, p. 2; and Section 5, p. 5); the holotype material is deposited in the mineralogical collections of the Natural History Museum, Vienna, Austria (catalogue number O 2394, mineral mount in polished block), and the Mineralogical Museum, Harvard University, Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA (catalogue number 145633, crystal fragment used for crystal-structure determination) (Section 1, p. 2). Occurs on proustite and manganoan calcite as anhedral to subhedral equant crystals up to 2 mm (Figure 1 caption on p. 3 notes a twinned crystal 2.5 mm in size) and commonly forms penetration twins around [112]; crystals are dark grey to black and opaque with a metallic lustre, black streak, brittle tenacity, uneven fracture, no observed cleavage or parting, Mohs hardness ~3.5-4, and calculated density Dcalc = 4.66 g/cm3 from the single-crystal X-ray formula and unit-cell volume (or 4.68 g/cm3 from the empirical formula; Abstract, p. 1; Section 2, p. 2; and Table 3, p. 4; density and micro-indentation hardness could not be measured directly owing to limited material). In reflected light in air, keutschite is light grey without internal reflections, very weakly pleochroic in greenish-grey to grey, and weakly anisotropic in shades of greenish-grey to grey; reflectance values (Rmin, Rmax in %) measured in air relative to a Zeiss WTiC standard (Cavendish Instruments Onyx software, 400-700 nm in 0.823 nm steps; Abstract, p. 1, Section 2, pp. 2-3, and Table 1, p. 3) at the four COM wavelengths are 25.2, 26.1 (470 nm), 29.6, 29.4 (546 nm), 29.4, 29.2 (589 nm), and 28.5, 28.6 (650 nm) (reported from 400 to 700 nm in 20 nm steps in Table 1, p. 3). Wavelength- dispersive field-emission electron-microprobe analyses (JEOL Hyperprobe JXA-8530F at Central Research Laboratories, Natural History Museum Vienna; 25 kV, 20 nA, 2 um beam diameter, 10 s peak / 5 s background; standards synthetic Ag metal for AgLalpha, natural chalcopyrite for CuKalpha, synthetic hauerite for MnKalpha, synthetic InAs for AsLalpha, natural stibnite for SbLalpha, natural galena for PbMalpha, and natural sphalerite for SKalpha; n = 5 analyses on one grain; Section 3, p. 3, and Table 2, p. 4) yielded mean Ag 23.46 wt% (range 23.14-23.94, SD 0.34), Cu 29.53 wt% (range 29.32-29.74, SD 0.18), Mn 0.031 wt% (range 0.02-0.04, SD 0.01), As 16.09 wt% (range 15.29-16.71, SD 0.67), Sb 1.20 wt% (range 0.48-2.14, SD 0.84), Pb 0.11 wt% (range 0.04-0.14, SD 0.07), S 29.06 wt% (range 28.80-29.11, SD 0.15), and mean of spot totals 99.46 wt% (range 99.12-99.97, SD 0.50; sum of rounded oxide/element means = 99.481 wt%). Three empirical formula normalizations and two typographical discrepancies appear across the primary paper: the Abstract (p. 1) reports Cu2.00Ag0.97(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma0.99S4.03 on the basis of 8 apfu; Section 3 (p. 3) prints Cu2.05Ag0.96(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma0.99S4.00 "calculated on the basis of eight atoms" (printing Cu2.05 instead of Cu1.99 on the 4 S apfu basis); and Table 2 (p. 4) reports Cu2.00Ag0.97(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma1.99S4.03 (SigmaMe = 3.97) on the basis of 8 apfu (4Me + 4S), Cu1.99Ag0.96(As0.95Sb0.04)Sigma1.99S4.00 (SigmaMe = 3.95) on the basis of 4 S apfu, and Cu2.02Ag0.97(As0.96Sb0.04)Sigma2.00S4.05 (SigmaMe = 4) on the basis of 4 Me apfu (where Table 2 prints Sigma1.99 instead of Sigma0.99 for 0.95 + 0.04 = 0.99 and Sigma2.00 instead of Sigma1.00 for 0.96 + 0.04 = 1.00). Crystallizes in the tetragonal system, space group I-42m (#121), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters (Oxford Diffraction Xcalibur E diffractometer, graphite-monochromatised MoKalpha radiation, lambda = 0.71073 A in Section 4, p. 4, and Table 3, p. 4, misprinted as "lambda = 0.7173 A" in Section 4, p. 3, at T = 293(2) K on a 0.118 x 0.088 x 0.050 mm crystal fragment) a = 5.5834(15) A, c = 10.021(3) A, V = 312.40(14) A3, and Z = 2 (note that the Abstract on p. 1 prints the axial ratio as "a:b:c = 1:1:0.897", which equals c/(2a) = 0.8974 rather than c/a = 1.7948). The crystal structure was solved by charge flipping (SUPERFLIP) and refined on F2 (JANA2006) to R = 1.99% and Rw = 4.61% for 286 observed reflections with I > 3sigma(I) (R = 2.17%, Rw = 4.78% for all 308 unique reflections out of 1490 measured reflections, Rint = 2.63%, S = 1.05 for 15 parameters; Table 3, p. 4). In the stannite-derivative structure (Table 4 and Section 5, p. 5), tetrahedrally S-coordinated cations occupy Wyckoff positions Cu at 4d (0, 0.5, 0.75; Ueq = 0.024 A2), Ag at 2b (0, 0, 0.5; Ueq = 0.032 A2), and As at 2a (0, 0, 0; Ueq = 0.009 A2) with S at 8i (0.77733(13), 0.77733(13), 0.86852(10); Ueq = 0.014 A2), forming alternating (001) cation layers of Cu atoms and ordered (Ag + As) atoms. The five strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines measured on a Rigaku Rapid II 2D image-plate diffractometer with MoKalpha radiation [dmeas in A (Im, Irel, dcalc) (hkl); Abstract, p. 1, and Table 5, p. 5] are 3.09 (vs, 100, 3.1011) (112), 2.79 (ms, 11.4, 2.7917) (200), 1.966 (s, 20.4, 1.974) (220), 1.864 (s, 34.2, 1.8646) (204), and 1.659 (s, 27.2, 1.6653) (312). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
