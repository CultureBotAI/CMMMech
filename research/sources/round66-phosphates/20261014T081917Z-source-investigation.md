## Bobierrite: two bounded source assignments

[Rivadeneyra, Ramos-Cormenzana and García-Cervigón (1987)](https://doi.org/10.2465/minerj.13.443), official J-STAGE scan, pages 443–447, was inspected visually in full. Of 96 carbonate-forming soil isolates, 21 formed phosphate crystals: 20 struvite and one phenotypic Acinetobacter sp. isolate with bobierrite-assigned aggregates. No deposited stock accession or species ID is inferred. B-17 contained 4 g/L yeast extract, 2.4 g/L magnesium acetate of unspecified hydration, 0.5 g/L potassium nitrate, and 1.6% agar for the solid arm. Initial pH was adjusted to 8 with 0.1 M NaOH; sterilization was at 121°C for 20 minutes, followed by aerobic incubation at 27°C for up to 35 days. Triplicates repeated three times mean nine reported experiments per medium. Controls were uninoculated medium and autoclaved cells, but the article supplies no explicit outcomes for them. A negative control result must not be invented.

Figure 1 PXRD, Figure 2 IR and Figure 3 SEM support the historical recovered-phase assignment; unmatched reflections and intensity differences explicitly indicate impurities. The 1983 cited separation/purification method is not reproduced and was not acquired here. Thus no wet-phase assurance, purified product yield, in situ soil mineral claim or biochemical pathway is admitted. The 1989 renal/urine isolate paper, DOI 10.1159/000281508, remains an abstract-only enrichment lead.

[Rivadeneyra et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph110403689), full retained publisher PDF and PMC4025044 XML, provides a separate qualified laboratory agar observation. Only six of 12 selected isolates have spherulites in Table 3; no isolate-to-bobierrite/baricite map exists. The artificial media, temperature/time, boiling-water agar dissolution and 37°C drying are retained. Figure 4's bobierrite/baricite assignments conflict with Figure 5's companion dihydrate label. Unchanged morphology after heating does not prove unchanged phase. No particular heat conversion is invented. Controls reportedly lacked crystals; wastewater medium grew bacteria without crystals after 30 days; liquid counterparts had struvite without spherulites. Taxonomic sequence-length and identity inconsistencies prevent exact taxon IDs. Reactor COD removal is not mineral recovery. These qualifications make the accepted assertion a recovered source-assigned phase, not certainty about phase formation before handling.

## Barićite: independent hydrogen/acetate culture evidence

[Han et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemgeo.2016.04.022) was acquired from the official Montana State repository as a 12-page author manuscript including its cover. The cover warns that editorial changes may differ from the publisher version; Crossref verifies DOI, title, authors and year. Six actual publisher DOCX supplements were acquired and checked for content (SI Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3), after bounded route probes; exact URLs, hashes and failures are in JSON. Main Figure 1 and Table 2 were visually checked. SI tables were read with preserved row/cell structure. SI Figures 1 and 3 and the formate/acetate panels of Figure 2 were inspected as native extracted images. The lactate EMF panel remains unrendered; it supplies no accepted numerical or model claim.

Live NCBI 207559 resolves strain Oleidesulfovibrio alaskensis G20 with equivalent historical name Desulfovibrio alaskensis G20. The experiment used two drops of fresh culture, 100 mL synthetic medium per 120 mL bottle, an iron finishing nail, a one-hour hydrogen purge, duplicate biotic and sterile bottles, 25°C and ten months. Main Table 1's acetate 47 mM, sulfate 22.3 mM, Mg 50.7 mM, Ca 8.22 mM, ammonium 10.58 mM and phosphate 0.89 mM are recipe values, not environmental concentrations. Endpoint pH 8.3 in both biotic bottles contrasts with 7.7 and 7.9 in sterile bottles. Filtered solids were dried at 30°C in nitrogen overnight and examined within a week.

Figure 1c and Table 2 assign barićite alongside magnesium-phosphate decahydrate, calcite and S8; sterile Figure 1d and Table 2 give calcite with dominant amorphous material. Fitted barićite proportions of 30.6 and 27.8 wt% are phase-analysis results, not Mg/P recovery or purity; rounding, unavailable uncertainty and the historical mixed Mg/Fe formula remain explicit. Crystal-specific elemental characterization of acetate products was not shown or further performed. No modern site-occupancy or pure-endmember confirmation is claimed.

The experiment's strongest counterweight is similar Mg loss in biotic and sterile acetate bottles (Table 2 and SI Figure 1). There is no demonstrated net microbial recovery advantage. Sulfate, sulfide, cell growth, DIC and phosphate loss were not measured. Reactive iron nails and trace pre-existing solids matter. Models cannot establish the observed biochemical cause: SI Table 1's phosphate 0.089 mM differs tenfold from Table 1's 0.89 mM; iron and formate-medium sodium inputs also differ. Figure 1 prints the co-phase formula Mg2(PO4)3·10H2O instead of Table 2's Mg3(PO4)2·10H2O. These source conflicts are preserved, not repaired or converted to an unverified extra species. No industrial deployment or natural cold-seep attribution is admitted.
