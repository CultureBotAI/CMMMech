Amblygonite

Amblygonite is the fluorine-dominant lithium aluminium phosphate mineral with ideal formula LiAl(PO4)F, distinct from hydroxyl-dominant montebrasite and from lithium as an element. Natural crystals form a substituted F/OH series rather than necessarily pure endmembers. Groat2003 provides primary neutron-diffraction and NMR evidence for this series, including F-rich natural specimens. The thermal observation is retained only for that author-assigned specimen, not as a universal amblygonite decomposition threshold, microbial mechanism or lithium recovery yield.

Ameghinite

Ameghinite is the approved sodium-borate mineral NaB3O3(OH)4, IMA1966-034, in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list. The original Tincalayu, Salta, Argentina specimens contained ameghinite nodules separated from surrounding borax by tincalconite. The 1967 chemical aliquot retained an optically estimated two percent tincalconite; its analysis is not a pure end-member composition. Dal Negro, Martin Pozas and Ungaretti's 1975 single-crystal study established a C2/c structure with isolated triborate units, each containing one boron tetrahedron and two triangles, linked through sodium coordination and hydrogen bonds. This mineral species is distinct from elemental boron and the borates commodity; criticality and microbial activity are not inferred.

Burbankite

A natural anhydrous carbonate mineral with the IMA-CNMNC September 2026 formula (Na,Ca)3(Sr,Ba,Ce)3(CO3)5. It is grandfathered; the listed 1953 value is an original-description year, not an IMA approval accession. Pecora and Kerr (1953) characterized natural hexagonal burbankite from calcite-rich veins at the Vermiculite Prospects, Big Sandy Creek, Bearpaw Mountains, Montana. Powder and single-crystal diffraction support the mineral characterization. Observed weathering assemblages contained several minerals and do not resolve a unique reaction pathway. A later synthetic Na-Ca-La carbonate with burbankite structure is treated below as an experimental analogue, not as the measured composition of these natural specimens. No dated criticality designation or microbial mechanism is asserted.
