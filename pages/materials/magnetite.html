Magnetite mineral

Magnetite is the Fe(II)Fe(III)2O4 mineral species, grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA list with original-description year 1845. It is distinct from elemental iron, maghemite, ferrihydrite and a mixed magnetic precipitate. The experiment below forms a magnetite-rich, probably cation-deficient mixed product from synthetic ferrihydrite; it does not demonstrate pure ideal magnetite or recovery from natural magnetite ore. The Ax17 assay is also represented under iron and the paired ferrihydrite/magnetite record; these are substrate/product perspectives on one experiment, not independent replication. No iron criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Magnetite-rich mixed product during Ax17 ferrihydrite reduction

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Desulfovulcanus ferrireducens (NCBITaxon:2831190) [TaxonMech]

Growing Desulfovulcanus ferrireducens Ax17 (DSM 111878) reduced synthetic two-line nanophase ferrihydrite and produced a black magnetic solid interpreted from complementary analyses as primarily cation-deficient magnetite. This is microbial Fe redox transformation with secondary mineral formation, not elemental iron production or a purified recovery product. The taxon identifier is species-level; the paper and DSMZ identify the experimental strain.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory triplicates used 50 mL modified DSM 981 medium in 160 mL serum bottles, reported 100 mmol/L ferrihydrite, initial pH 6.80 +/- 0.05, 0.5 mM cysteine-HCl, and 1.3 mM FeCl2 supplementation. The headspace was H2:CO2 (80:20) at 200 kPa; cultures received a 1% logarithmic-phase inoculum previously transferred at least three times on the acceptor, and grew at 55 degrees C for about five days. Identically composed uninoculated controls were incubated at 55 degrees C or room temperature. Ferrozine after oxalate dissolution gave reported Fe(II) of 24 mM in the biotic medium versus 2-3 mM in controls; this extraction-based measurement is not a dissolved-iron concentration or a recovery percentage. No pilot, industrial, or natural-ore extraction operation was tested. The source reports 84% retention of biogenic iron product in a separate pre-oxalate filtration comparison; this is an operational particulate fraction, not a magnetite phase percentage. No ideal starting ferrihydrite stoichiometry is inferred from the reported 100 mmol/L mineral addition.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: sklute2023
Methods and Results, Cell growth on ferrihydrite, link Ax17 growth, greater oxalate-extractable Fe(II), and the magnetic product to the inoculated treatment. Figures 1A, 2-4 and Supplementary Figures S2-S5 compare controls and mineral standards using reflectance, FTIR-ATR, Mossbauer spectroscopy, XRD, and TEM with electron diffraction.
Partial Source: sklute2023
This supports a magnetite-rich mixed product, not pure stoichiometric magnetite or uniquely biological magnetite formation. Low-temperature Mossbauer spectra exclude a pure magnetite product; XRD alone cannot distinguish magnetite, maghemite, and cation-deficient magnetite. The heated abiotic control formed lepidocrocite and may contain minor magnetite. Cysteine and added Fe(II) were present in all arms. Akaganeite and lepidocrocite treatments gave different, nonmagnetic poorly resolved products, limiting extrapolation across iron minerals.
