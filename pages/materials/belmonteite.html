Belmonteite mineral IMA: Bmo

IMA-approved hydrated calcium manganese arsenate mineral (IMA 2024-040; symbol Bmo; status A, Approved 2024; full description published August 2026) with structural ideal formula CaMn2(AsO4)2(H2O)5*2H2O in the title, Abstract (p. 531), and Section 6.5 (p. 539: Ca(2)(Ca[])Mn(1)Mn2As(1)+As(2)(AsO4)2(H2O)5*2H2O, reflecting 5 cation-bonded H2O molecules at Ow(2), Ow(5), Ow(10), Ow(11), and Ow(12b) plus 2 free H2O molecules at Ow(9) and Ow(12a) when Ca(2) is half-occupied), printed as CaMn2(AsO4)2(H2O)6*H2O in the September 2026 IMA Master List (PDF p. 23) and as the stoichiometric end-member formula CaMn2(AsO4)2*7H2O in Section 4 (p. 533) and Table 8 (p. 541; requiring As2O5 41.49 wt%, CaO 10.12 wt%, MnO 25.61 wt%, and H2O 22.77 wt% for a printed sum of 100.00 wt%, where the four rounded constituent values sum to 99.99 wt%; Biagioni, Sejkora, and Dolnicek, 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:531-543). Named in honour of Donato Belmonte (b. 1978), Associate Professor of Petrology and Petrography at the University of Genoa, Italy, for his contributions to the mineralogy of Liguria (co-authoring the descriptions of seven new mineral species from this region — cerchiaraite-(Fe), alpeite, ramazzoite, arsenmedaite, isselite, cortesognoite, and marioantofilliite — as well as participating in the study of the 1M polytype of lavinskyite and providing insights into the mineral associations in rodingitic and meta-rodingitic rocks from the Ligurian Alps and Apennines; Section 1, p. 532). Discovered in a sample collected more than 30 years earlier on the dumps of the Gambatesa mine (44 deg 21 min 34 sec N, 9 deg 26 min 59 sec E), Graveglia Valley, Ne, Genoa Province, Liguria, Italy (which exploited braunite ore bodies near the base of the Upper Jurassic "Diaspri di Monte Alpe" Formation metachert sequence overlying Middle Jurassic northern Apennine ophiolites and is also the type locality for cavoite, gravegliaite, poppiite, reppiaite, saneroite, and vanadomalayaite). Occurs in vugs of a carbonate vein associated with tennantite-(Cu), calcite, luzonite, and bluish spherulae of an unidentified mahnertite-like (Ca,Cu)-arsenate-chloride secondary phase, formed during circulation of As-rich oxidizing fluids in the late-stage evolution of the Mn ore deposit; the holotype specimen is deposited in the mineralogical collection of the Dipartimento di Scienze della Terra dell'Ambiente e della Vita (DISTAV), University of Genoa, Italy, under catalogue number MO720, and the grain used for single-crystal X-ray diffraction is kept in the Museo di Storia Naturale, University of Pisa, Calci (Pisa, Italy), under catalogue number 20076. Forms aggregates up to 0.5 mm in length of thin tabular crystals, white in colour, with a white streak, silky lustre, transparency, brittle tenacity, perfect {010} cleavage, no fluorescence under short- or long-wave UV radiation, calculated density Dcalc = 2.677 g/cm3 (from the empirical formula and single-crystal unit-cell parameters), and a Gladstone-Dale calculated mean refractive index n = 1.578 (optical properties and direct density were not measured owing to the scarcity of material). Micro-Raman spectroscopy (532 nm green diode-pumped solid-state laser at 2.5 mW, Horiba Jobin-Yvon XploRA Plus spectrometer with 50x objective and 1200 gr/mm grating; Figure 2, Section 3, p. 533) shows a strong AsO4 stretching band at 839 cm-1 with shoulders at 815 and 891 cm-1 (nu1 and nu3 modes), weaker AsO4 bending bands at 336, 406, and 467 cm-1 (nu2 and nu4 modes), and O-H stretching bands at 2900-3700 cm-1 confirming H2O groups. Wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analysis (n = 8 spot analyses, Cameca SX100 at the National Museum in Prague, 15 kV, 10 nA, 10 um beam diameter on a polished crystal surface; Na, P, Mg, Si, Pb, S, Cl, Sr, Fe, Zn, F, N, V, and Cr were below detection limits; Table 1) yielded (mean wt%, range, e.s.d., and value normalized to 100.00 wt% with stoichiometric H2O) As2O5 48.57 wt% (45.93-50.35, SD 1.71; normalized 41.60 wt%), MnO 29.94 wt% (28.85-30.35, SD 0.49; normalized 25.65 wt%), CaO 10.12 wt% (9.75-10.40, SD 0.22; normalized 8.67 wt%), CuO 1.28 wt% (1.00-1.64, SD 0.22; normalized 1.10 wt%), Al2O3 0.22 wt% (0.08-0.69, SD 0.20; normalized 0.19 wt%), K2O 0.10 wt% (0.04-0.18, SD 0.05; normalized 0.08 wt%), and H2Ocalc 26.51 wt% (normalized 22.71 wt%), for an unnormalized total of 116.74 wt% (reflecting partial dehydration under vacuum and electron-beam instability) and normalized total of 100.00 wt%. On the basis of 15 O atoms per formula unit and the crystal-structure analysis, the empirical formula is (Ca0.86Mn0.11K0.01)Sigma0.98(Mn1.90Cu0.08Al0.02)Sigma2.00As2.01O8*7H2O. Crystallizes in the orthorhombic system, centrosymmetric space group Cmce (#64), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 8.8418(9) A, b = 23.031(2) A, c = 13.5270(14) A, V = 2754.6(5) A3, and Z = 8 (refined from 29,235 measured and 1507 unique reflections collected at 300(2) K on a 0.100 x 0.050 x 0.030 mm crystal using microfocus MoKalpha radiation on a Bruker D8 Venture diffractometer to R1 = 0.0385 for 1195 reflections with F > 4sigma(F), R1 = 0.0525 for all data, wR2 = 0.1047, Goof = 1.069, 129 parameters, Table 3; Gandolfi-like microfocus CuKalpha powder X-ray diffraction on the same diffractometer, refined with Burnham's least-squares program, yielded a = 8.854(7) A, b = 23.02(2) A, c = 13.533(13) A, V = 2759(3) A3, Section 5.1, p. 533; note that Table 2 on p. 537 prints two column headers as "d*calc" instead of "d*calc" and "dcalc" and misprints d*calc = 2.224 A for reflection (0 4 5) alongside dobs = 2.446 A and dcalc = 2.449 A, where the true powder-cell spacing is 2.450 A). The crystal structure (Tables 4-7 and Figures 3-7) consists of {010} Mn4O10(H2O)4 layers (topologically analogous to those in switzerite, metaswitzerite, and castellaroite) formed by Mn(1)O4(H2O)2 octahedra (Mn(1) at 16i, BVS = 2.04 v.u.) decorated on both sides by As(1)O4 (8f, BVS = 5.18 v.u.) and rotationally disordered As(2)O4 (8f, split O(7a)/O(7b) and O(8), BVS = 5.08 v.u.) tetrahedra and connected to [100] rods of half-occupied Ca(2) polyhedra (16i, s.o. Ca0.50; surrounded by 10 O positions within 3.0 A in the average disordered model, where Ow(12a) at 1.139(12) A is mutually exclusive with Ca(2) occupancy and Ow(12b) occurs when Ca(2) is occupied, yielding six-fold distorted octahedral coordination Ca(2)O3(H2O)3 with ligands O(6), O(7b), O(8), Ow(10), Ow(11), and Ow(12b) across all 8 ordered configurations in Table 7, p. 540, with weighted average <Ca(2)-O> = 2.433 A and BVS = 1.87 v.u. in Table 6, and <Ca(2)-O> = 2.33 A and BVS = 2.18 v.u. in the most probable ordered configuration no. 1) and H-bonded free H2O molecules (Ow(9) and Ow(12a)). The eight strongest observed powder X-ray diffraction lines (dobs in A (Iobs; hkl), Table 2) are 11.6 (s; 0 2 0), 8.9 (mw; 0 2 1), 7.1 (mw; 1 1 1), 5.37 (mw; 1 3 1), 3.51 (mw; 2 4 0, 1 5 2), 3.049 (mw; 2 2 3), 2.905 (mw; 0 4 4, 2 6 0), and 2.684 (mw; 2 0 4, 3 1 2). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
