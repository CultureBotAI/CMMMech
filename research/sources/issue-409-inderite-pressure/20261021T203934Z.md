## Pressure experiment and quantitative limits

The AAM methods use a natural Kramer/Borax mine crystal from Boron, California, approximately 20 × 15 × 10 micrometres, a membrane diamond-anvil cell, helium pressure medium and ruby calibration with 0.05 GPa uncertainty. ESRF ID15b used 30 keV radiation (0.4107 Å) and an approximately 3 × 4 micrometre beam. The transition is a structural/coordination interpretation from diffraction, not oxidation of boron. The neutron crystal and 280/10 K experiments are a separate arm.

Table 1 (AAM PDF pages 7–8) has 22 rows, including three decompression points. Its high-pressure setting doubles the cell; comparison requires the authors' a/2 and V/2 convention. The conditional displayed-value difference `100 × [1 − (1657.5/2)/891.41]` is about 7.029%, consistent with the qualified approximately 7% discussion. This is a cell-volume discontinuity, not a metal yield. Recovery of an ambient cell does not locate a precise reverse threshold or demonstrate an industrial cycle.
