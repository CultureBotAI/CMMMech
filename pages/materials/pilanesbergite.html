Pilanesbergite mineral IMA: Pbg

IMA-approved sodium-calcium-iron-titanium oxy-fluor-disilicate mineral of the wohlerite group (IMA 2023-007; symbol Pbg in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 173, and Pbt in Figure 2 of the type description; status A, Approved 2023; full description published January 2024) with ideal end-member formula Na2Ca2Fe2Ti2(Si2O7)2O2F2 (written Na2Ca2Fe2+2Ti2(Si2O7)2O2F2 in the IMA Master List; requiring Na2O 8.38 wt%, CaO 15.16 wt%, FeO 19.42 wt%, TiO2 21.60 wt%, SiO2 32.48 wt%, F 5.14 wt%, and -F=O 2.16 wt% for a printed total of 100 wt%, arithmetic sum 100.02 wt%; Dal Bo et al., 2024, European Journal of Mineralogy 36:73-85). Represents the Fe2+- and Ti4+-dominant member of the wohlerite group (isomorphic with lavenite Na2Ca2Mn2Zr2(Si2O7)2O2F2, normandite Na2Ca2Mn2Ti2(Si2O7)2O2F2, and madeiraite Na2Ca2Fe2Zr2(Si2O7)2O2F2 via the homovalent substitutions Mn2+ <-> Fe2+ on X3 and Zr4+ <-> Ti4+ on X1), and is named after its type locality in the 1395 +/- 10 Ma Pilanesberg Alkaline Complex (LA-ICP-MS U-Pb titanite age; Elburg and Cawthorn, 2017), North West Province, South Africa. Discovered in a river gulley near the southern margin of the Pilanesberg Complex (25.3463 deg S, 27.0658 deg E; sample MEPB62, (Na + K)/Al = 1.24, femic CIPW-normative minerals summing to 16.95 wt%), where it occurs as a late magmatic rock-forming mineral in medium-grained nepheline syenite (locally known as green foyaite) showing flow foliation defined by alkali feldspar crystals completely exsolved to end-member albite and microcline. Occurs interstitially to feldspar (post-dating alkali feldspar, nepheline, and sodalite) associated and partly intergrown with aegirine (sharing inclusions of euhedral nepheline, titanite, and minor sodalite) and in stable grain contacts with arfvedsonite, apatite, and lamprophyllite, accompanied by eudialyte, minor pyrrhotite and Fe-Mn-Ti oxide minerals, aenigmatite, scarce lorenzenite, late-crystallized natrolite, and an undescribed fibrous Ti-Zr silicate mineral; cotype specimens are deposited at the Natural History Museum, University of Oslo, Norway (catalogue number KNR 44406; sample used for EPMA, SCXRD structural refinement, and petrographic description) and the Laboratoire de Mineralogie, University of Liege, Belgium (catalogue number 21980; sample used for PXRD, optical properties, and density measurement). Forms translucent brown-orange grains and blocky crystals with a brownish streak, vitreous lustre, brittle tenacity, no observed cleavage, estimated Mohs hardness 5-6 (by comparison with lavenite), and measured density Dmeas = 3.47 g/cm3 (Berman balance in toluene) vs calculated density Dcalc = 3.40 g/cm3 in the Abstract and Section 3 (p. 74) and 3.49 g/cm3 in Table 3 (p. 79). Optically biaxial positive (+) with alpha = 1.743(3), beta = 1.768(3), gamma = 1.795(5) (determined by the immersion method in Cargille refractive index liquids under 589 nm sodium light) and a 2V angle close to 90 deg (2Vcalc = 88 deg; Table 8), with strong pleochroism in thin section between straw yellow and orange red and in immersion between light yellow (alpha) and yellowish orange (gamma); optical orientation was not determined because the crystals could not be oriented under the polarizing microscope due to the absence of cleavage planes, and the Gladstone-Dale compatibility index 1 - (Kp/Kc) = 0.037 corresponds to the excellent category (Mandarino, 1981). Wavelength-dispersive electron microprobe analysis (n = 15 analyses, CAMECA SX100 with four spectrometers at the University of Johannesburg, 15 kV, 15 nA, 5 um beam diameter; Table 1) yielded SiO2 32.28 wt% (range 31.88-32.62, 2sigma SD 0.37), TiO2 18.33 wt% (17.63-19.12, SD 0.94), CaO 16.87 wt% (16.27-17.64, SD 0.79), FeO 9.70 wt% (8.54-10.26, SD 0.98), Na2O 9.43 wt% (9.23-9.60, SD 0.20), F 5.54 wt% (5.42-5.63, SD 0.11), MnO 4.96 wt% (4.62-5.52, SD 0.52), ZrO2 2.95 wt% (2.19-4.16, SD 1.10), Nb2O5 1.36 wt% (1.06-1.76, SD 0.39), MgO 0.49 wt% (0.39-0.63, SD 0.14), SrO 0.18 wt% (0.11-0.22, SD 0.06), Al2O3 0.03 wt% (0.01-0.04, SD 0.02), Ce2O3 0.02 wt% (0.00-0.08, SD 0.05), and K2O 0.01 wt% (0.00-0.02, SD 0.01), with Table 1 printing Total = 102.13 wt%, O=F = 2.33 wt%, and net Total = 99.80 wt% whereas the exact arithmetic sum of the 14 printed mean constituents is 102.15 wt% and 99.82 wt% after -O=F (a 0.02 wt% rounding difference). Normalized to 18 (O + F) anions per formula unit, the empirical formula is Na2.00(Ca1.74Na0.26)Sigma2.00(Fe1.00Mn0.52Ca0.49Zr0.05)Sigma2.06(Ti1.69Zr0.14Mg0.09Nb0.08)Sigma2.00(Si2O7)2.00O1.84F2.16. Crystallizes in the monoclinic system, non-standard wohlerite-group space group P21/a (#14), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters a = 10.7811(2) A, b = 9.7836(1) A, c = 7.0348(1) A, beta = 108.072(2) deg, V = 705.41(2) A3, and Z = 2 (refined to R1 = 0.0206, wR = 0.0250 for 3214 observed reflections with I > 3sigma(I) and R2 = 0.0767, wR2 = 0.0798, GoF = 1.04/1.08 for all 3692 unique reflections from 34,780 measured at 293 K on a 0.065 x 0.043 x 0.025 mm orange blocky crystal using MoKalpha radiation on a Rigaku Synergy-S diffractometer, Table 3; powder unit-cell parameters refined in P21/a using LCLSQ8N from step-scan data collected on a Bruker D8 Advance ECO diffractometer with CuKalpha radiation, 2theta 2-70 deg, step size 0.02 deg, Table 2, are a = 10.761(7) A, b = 9.756(7) A, c = 7.020(4) A, beta = 108.23(5) deg, V = 700.0(5) A3). The refined structural formula (Tables 4-7) is X4(Na)2X2(Ca0.85Na0.15)2X3(Fe0.48Mn0.25Ca0.25Zr0.02)2X1(Ti0.85Zr0.07Nb0.04Mg0.04)2(Si2O7)2(F2.16O1.84) (note that Section 6.1 on p. 77 mislabels site W1 as "W1 (O4)" whereas Tables 4 and 7 correctly identify it as O8 (W1), and Table 4 on p. 80 misprints Ueq for X3 (Fe) as "0.349(4)" A2 instead of ~0.0135 A2). The six strongest observed powder X-ray diffraction lines (dmeas in A (Imeas; hkl), Table 2) are 2.851 (100; 1 2 -2), 3.219 (60; 3 1 0), 2.802 (51; 3 2 0), 2.743 (27; 2 2 -2 / 2 3 0), 2.423 (19; 4 0 -2) and 1.723 (19; 4 4 -2), and 2.366 (18; 3 2 1). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
