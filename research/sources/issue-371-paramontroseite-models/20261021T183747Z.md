## Specimen and experimental arms

The studied specimen comes from Prachovice, Czech Republic, not the historical Colorado crystals. Original figures 1–3 and sections 2.1–2.2 show paramontroseite lamellae in a carbonate matrix, an associated uraninite-like phase, Pb-rich areas and core-to-rim Fe/Al zoning. The reported oxide ranges are not a single complete analysis row. The two-oxygen average formula is `V0.84Fe0.19Al0.03O2`; its cation sum is 1.06. The source reports roughly 0.01 apfu analytical uncertainty and an average from six crystals. This measured average is distinct from the ideal species formula `VO2`, the later Al-free reaction approximation, and the refined site model.

- **Untreated single crystals:** section 2.3 uses two crystals, Mo Kα diffraction, absorption/Lorentz-polarization corrections and SHELX-97 refinement. Only one similar result is tabulated. Table 1 gives octahedral M occupancy 0.762(3) V and tetrahedral T occupancy 0.196(2) Fe; Al is omitted from the simplified discussion. The electron-count comparison in Table 2 is a model/chemistry comparison, not a direct site-specific oxidation-state assay. The two deposit numbers cannot yet be assigned to independently inspected model contents or to the one displayed table.
- **In-situ powder heating:** sections 2.4 and 3.2 select crystals from mapped U-free areas, retaining about 8 wt% calcite. Powder sits in a flat Pt holder; heating is 20 °C/min, nominal 50 °C steps up to 550 °C, with a two-hour hold before an approximately 150-minute scan. Full-profile fits use literature product structures; structural parameters were not refined in that powder series. Atmospheric composition, flow and oxygen fugacity are not quantified. These are not pure-VO2 or sterile/biotic experiments.
- **Separate heated crystals:** sections 2.2/2.5/3.3 describe six hours at 400 °C for picked crystals, subsequent EDS and Raman observations, including black metallic and brown-red areas. This is not the powder hold/scan protocol or a matched replicate of it.
- **Laser response:** original natural-crystal Raman initially lacks a peak in the reported range; continued irradiation produces changing spectra. Figure 9 compares a literature V2O5 trace, two heated-crystal regions and an older RRUFF natural-specimen trace. Those are four source/condition roles, not four independent thermal-product assays. No Fe-tetrapolyvanadate reference spectrum or unique assignment of every overlapping peak is supplied.

Figure 5 is a 450 °C fitted powder pattern with retained parent and product phase markers. Table 3 includes parent cell values through 450 °C; its starred 400/450 °C rows indicate metastability. Figure 7 is plotted through 500 °C and shows no parent segment at its final temperature. The methods' 550 °C endpoint does not create an unshown 550 °C quantitative assay. Figure 8 draws product frameworks; it is not microscopy of recovered product. No recovery, dissolved V, isolated-product purity or deployment claim follows.

## Quantities and unresolved source disagreements

The four original XLSX archives pass CRC checks and contain 52 file members, 237 stored cells across four populated worksheets and four empty worksheets. `numeric-audit.json` saves 51 conditional calculations with exact formulas, stated units and limitations. It includes cation/electron sums, printed-bond means, cell products, endpoint changes and an explicitly unweighted fit to the ten displayed Table 3 rows. Such a recalculation does not establish the authors' unreported weighting/subset or a new physical coefficient. No plot endpoints were digitized.

**Thermal phase fractions.** Section 3.2, printed page 378, assigns about 65 wt% to Fe2V4O13 and 35 wt% to V2O5. Original Figure 7, page 379, labels the larger final segment V2O5, the smaller non-calcite segment Fe2V4O13, and retains calcite at the top. The chart's exact component values and normalization table are absent. We do not swap labels or choose a corrected number. In particular, a 100%-normalized plotted assemblage containing calcite cannot automatically be identified with a two-product 65+35 narrative denominator.

The source's approximate equation is atom-balanced:

`10 V0.8Fe0.2O2 + 1.5 O2 → Fe2V4O13 + 2 V2O5`.

Using stated computational atomic masses V=50.9415, Fe=55.845 and O=15.999 gives approximately **59.00% Fe2V4O13 / 41.00% V2O5 by mass of those two modeled products**, and 5.719% oxygen mass gain relative to the approximate parent. These are stoichiometric consequences of the chosen approximation, not measured fractions. If one further assumes 92 g of that parent plus 8 g inert calcite initially, complete conversion and no other mass transfer, the final calcite fraction would be about **7.600%**. That conditional denominator example shows why a stable calcite amount is not automatically a constant normalized weight fraction. It does not prove amorphous material, correct Figure 7 or contradict the authors' fitting within an unreported precision.

The same pure-product assumption converts a 65/35 Fe-product/V2O5 mass mixture into Fe/V≈0.281715; the hypothetical reversed 35/65 assignment gives ≈0.136150. Neither is the approximate parent's Fe/V=0.25. The measured chemistry gives ≈0.226190 and refined occupancies ≈0.257218; those refer to different representations and must not be pooled. No assumption supplies the missing phase-fit table, specimen-specific feed balance or measured isolated product.

**Published structure-table diagnostic.** Table 1 lists T–O distances 1.825, twice 1.916, and 1.754 Å. Their multiplicity-weighted arithmetic mean is **1.85275 Å**, not the printed mean **1.812(2) Å**, which the prose repeats. All current primary PDF/XML/XLSX representations preserve those same tokens. The M–O weighted mean does reproduce about 1.995433 Å; direct distance between the displayed M and T representatives is about 1.745967 Å, consistent with the separately printed 1.746(2) Å. These calculations do not identify which T-distance or average is erroneous, and do not replace an authentic deposited-model audit. The accepted owner does not adopt the disputed mean, so no owner correction is proposed.

**Cell and Raman boundaries.** The Table 3 endpoint a contraction is about −0.7721% from 25 to 450 °C, whereas section 3.2 prints −0.2% in its qualitative summary; the baseline/interval behind that prose is not fully specified. Table 3 gives its own 25 °C a uncertainty, distinct from Table 1's single-crystal uncertainty. The plot and table retain nonmonotonic cell trends and metastable rows. Table 4's missing peaks and overlapping positions do not uniquely identify separate products; exact peak tokens and source uncertainties are preserved rather than reassigning them. These source-level diagnostics broaden the handoff's unresolved questions without inventing a repository defect.
