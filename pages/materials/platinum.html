Platinum element Critical Material

Platinum (Pt). The element is distinct from its experimental dissolved chloro-complexes, the potassium salts used to prepare them, and platinum-bearing minerals or wastes. The US 2025 designation names the platinum commodity individually; it is mapped here to the element without designating every platinum compound or secondary resource as critical. Curated evidence concerns laboratory transformation in the presence of bacterial biomass, with substantial abiotic reduction and no demonstrated isolated-metal yield or industrial use.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Hydrogen-driven Pt(II) transformation in the presence of CH34 biomass

redox transformation Microbial

Associated Organisms: Cupriavidus metallidurans CH34 (NCBITaxon:266264) [TaxonMech]

Washed Cupriavidus metallidurans CH34 biomass exposed to a synthetic Pt(II) chloro-complex under hydrogen acquired platinum-bearing particles. The authors interpreted biomass-associated XANES and EXAFS spectra as consistent with metallic Pt(0). Hydrogen alone also removed Pt(II), so the experiment supports a transformation with microbial biomass present, without establishing that living cells or a particular enzyme are necessary.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory anaerobic batches: CH34 from SCK-CEN was first grown aerobically overnight in LB-Lennox at 28 degrees C, centrifuged at 7000 g for 7 minutes, washed twice with 25 mL phosphate buffer (8.5 g/L Na2HPO4.7H2O and 3 g/L KH2PO4), and resuspended to OD610 of 1. Fifty mL suspensions in 120 mL serum bottles received Pt(II) supplied as K2PtCl4, at 100 mg/L Pt from a 2.5 g/L Pt stock in 1 M HCl. Bottles underwent 20 nitrogen/vacuum cycles and the headspace was replaced with 100% hydrogen; batches were mixed at 100 rpm and 28 degrees C, with initial assay pH adjusted to 1.8-2.2. Aerobic denotes biomass cultivation, not the anaerobic transformation assay. Table 1 reports 99% Pt removal with hydrogen at 48 h versus 24% in the no-electron-donor CH34 arm and 99% in the chemical hydrogen control (screening experiments n=1). These ICP-OES-based removal percentages are not measured Pt(0) fractions or purified-product yields; the 2017 methods refer detailed ICP sample preparation to an earlier paper, whose full procedure was not verified here. Membrane-intact cell counts decreased during exposure, so sustained metabolic activity is not assumed.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: maes_2017
Methods, Figure 1, supplementary Figure S1, and the Platinum speciation analysis section support the authors' Pt(0) interpretation for aerobically cultivated CH34 exposed to Pt(II) under hydrogen. Figure 3 shows particulate deposits in the CH34 Pt(II) panel. The phase claim depends on X-ray absorption evidence, not the color change or microscopy alone, and is not a quantitative determination of complete conversion.
Partial Source: maes_2017
Table 1's chemical hydrogen control also reaches 99% Pt(II) removal; thus the CH34 endpoint does not establish a microbial enhancement over that abiotic arm. The paper additionally reports 99% removal with heat-killed Shewanella cells, a different organism and not a matched CH34 viability control. No specific hydrogenase or other enzyme was demonstrated to cause Pt reduction in CH34.
Partial Source: maes_2017
Figure 5 and the membrane-integrity results show CH34 intact-cell counts declining to the assay detection level by 27 h in the acidic Pt(II)/hydrogen experiment. Membrane staining is not proof of growth or metabolic activity. Table 1 also reports only 3% carboplatin removal and zero for the tetraamine preparation with CH34 and hydrogen; the Pt(II) chloro-complex result cannot be extended to all Pt complexes.
Partial Source: maes_2017
Spectroscopy reporting has unresolved internal inconsistencies: Methods specifies the Pt L3 edge and a 11919 eV calibration value, while Figure 1 is plotted near 11560-11630 eV and Results calls the edge K-edge. This record retains the authors' qualitative XANES/EXAFS interpretation without adopting those conflicting absolute energy assignments or claiming independent spectral refitting. The source also distinguishes solution removal from reduced Pt in biomass.

(2017).
