Fengruiite mineral IMA: Fru

Fengruiite (IMA 2024-045) is an IMA-accepted silver–copper–antimony–tellurium sulfosalt mineral of the pearceite–polybasite group with September 2026 IMA-CNMNC Master List ideal formula [Ag6Sb2S7][Ag9CuS2Te2] (PDF p. 68, status A; IMA symbol Fru, printed as "Frui" in the Fig. 3 caption on p. 523; ideal composition Ag 67.20 wt%, S 12.84 wt%, Sb 10.11 wt%, Te 7.21 wt%, Cu 2.64 wt%, total 100 wt%), representing the first tellurium-bearing member of the polybasite subgroup with a Te-dominant crystallographic site in the [Ag9CuS2(Te,S)2]2+ ([Ag9CuS2Te2]2+) B-layer module and the polybasite-Tac (111 polytype) unit-cell topology (Abstract, p. 519, Section 5.1 & Table 5, p. 526). Described by Tian, Li, Sun, Liu, Mao, Dong, Liu, Jian, Yao, Wang, and Ye (2026) from a galena–quartz vein sample at 380 m elevation of the H5 polymetallic sulfide orebody in the Haopinggou Ag–Pb–Zn–Au deposit (34°10'54" N, 111°16'55" E), Xiayu orefield, ~60 km southwest of Luoning County, Henan Province, eastern Qinling, China (named in honor of Rui Feng, born in 1963, a renowned geologist specializing in mineral exploration; holotype polished section and the X-ray-studied single crystal deposited in the Geological Museum of China, Beijing, under catalogue no. GMCTM2024004, pp. 519–520). Occurs as irregular strip-like grains less than 20 x 80 um intergrown with galena, cervelleite, and chalcopyrite (Fig. 3), with other vein minerals quartz, ankerite, electrum, and tetrahedrite-group minerals (plus pyrite and sphalerite in Fig. 2 and acanthite in Section 5.3, p. 527), deposited at intermediate temperatures (163–213 °C) from 7.2–14.0 wt% NaCl eq. aqueous fluids (Section 2, p. 520, Figs. 1–3). Macroscopically black-gray ("gray" in Abstract, p. 519) with a steel-gray to iron-black streak and metallic luster, brittle with uneven fracture, no cleavage or parting, Mohs hardness 3–4, non-fluorescent and nonmagnetic, and Dcalc = 6.62 g/cm3 from the empirical formula and single-crystal unit-cell volume (Section 4.1, p. 522); in reflected light it is opaque, gray with a bluish tint, with no observable pleochroism, anisotropy, bireflectance, or internal reflections, and reflectance values in air (400–700 nm, <= 2 nm spectral resolution, Section 3.4, p. 521, and Section 4.1, p. 522, Supplement Table S1) spanning Rmax = 32.358%–44.680% and Rmin = 30.657%–36.184%. Micro-Raman spectroscopy (523 nm laser, 25 mW output power, 100–2000 cm-1, Section 3.3, p. 520, and Section 4.4, p. 525, Supplement Fig. S1) displays bands at 144, 764, 937, 1474, and 1564 cm-1, with no definitive vibrational mode assignments reported in the study. Mean of 11 WDS electron-microprobe point analyses on 2 crystals (JEOL JXA-8230, 20 kV, 10 nA, 3–5 um beam, corroborated by 4 additional analyses with an 8 um beam; Section 3.1, p. 520, and Section 4.2, p. 522, Table 1) yielded Ag 66.92 wt% (range 65.89–68.63, SD 0.84), S 12.88 (12.56–13.11, SD 0.17), Sb 9.61 (9.14–10.13, SD 0.33), Te 6.80 (6.34–7.56, SD 0.34), Cu 3.43 (3.26–3.76, SD 0.17), As 0.10 (0.01–0.29, SD 0.09), total 99.74 wt%, corresponding on 29 apfu to the modular empirical formula [(Ag5.87Cu0.29)Sigma6.16(Sb1.89As0.03)Sigma1.92S7][Ag9CuS2(Te1.28S0.63)Sigma1.91] (printed in Abstract, p. 519, and Section 4.2, p. 522, with a typographical "S1.91" in place of "Sigma1.91" after "(Te1.28S0.63)") and simplified formula [(Ag,Cu)6Sb2S7][Ag9CuS2(Te,S)2]. Trigonal, space group P-3m1 (no. 164), Z = 1 (Section 4.3, pp. 522–526, and Sections 5.1–5.2, pp. 526–527, Tables 2–5, Figs. 4–5): single-crystal MoKalpha XRD refinement on a 0.02 x 0.02 x 0.01 mm crystal (Rigaku XtaLAB AFC12 (RINC) Kappa single diffractometer, 548 observed reflections with I > 2sigma(I) out of 626 independent, 3288 measured, Rint = 0.050, R1 = 0.0667 in text / 0.067 in Table 2, wR2 = 0.109, S = 1.29, refined formula Ag15CuSb2S9.64Te1.36, Mr = 2407.68) gives a = 7.6087(9) Å, c = 11.970(2) Å, V = 600.13(17) Å3, with alternating negatively charged [Ag6Sb2S7]2- A-layer modules (isolated trigonal-pyramidal SbS3 groups with three S3 atoms, plus split Ag1/Ag2 sites at 6i with occupancies 0.78(4)/0.21(4)) and positively charged [Ag9CuS2(Te,S)2]2+ ([Ag9CuS2Te2]2+) B-layer modules (Cu linearly coordinated by two S4 atoms as [S4–Cu–S4], disordered Ag3/Ag4/Ag5 sites at 12j, and a Te-dominant mixed 2d site Te1/S1 with occupancy Te0.680(12)S0.320(12); Table 4 on p. 525 lists anisotropic displacement parameters and supplementary Table S4 gives interatomic distances); X-ray powder diffraction on the same crystal (Rigaku Oxford Diffraction XtaLAB PRO-007HF microfocus rotating-anode diffractometer, MoKalpha radiation, lambda = 0.71073 Å, 50 kV, 24 mA, Section 3.2, p. 520, and Section 4.3, p. 523) refines to a = 7.6087(11) Å, c = 11.9700(7) Å, V = 600.13(21) Å3, Z = 1, with individual powder diffraction lines (including the seven strongest reflections) tabulated in supplementary Table S3. Distinct from polybasite, cupropolybasite, selenopolybasite, benleonardite, and cervelleite; no criticality designation for fengruiite itself, no microbial or biological mechanism, and no industrial recovery yield are asserted.
