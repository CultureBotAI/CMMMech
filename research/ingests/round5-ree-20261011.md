## Lanthanum: Kang, Csetenyi and Gadd

[DOI 10.1007/s00253-018-9489-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00253-018-9489-0),
*Biotransformation of lanthanum by Aspergillus niger*, appeared online in 2018
and in *Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology* 103:981-993 (2019).
Full methods/results/discussion, Table 1, and relevant figure captions were
read; Figures 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 were visually inspected. The XML describes no
separate supplementary-material item; its EuropePMC package contains figure
and equation assets.

The accepted mechanism concerns cultures producing a precipitation medium,
followed by LaCl3 addition to the filtered culture fluid. ATCC 1015 grew in
100 mL modified nitrate-AP1 medium, initially pH 5.5, with 20 g/L glucose,
for seven days at 25 C and 125 rpm in darkness. The 63 micrometre mesh removes
bulk biomass but does not establish sterility. Harvest pH was 3.41 +/- 0.10.
The 10 mL assays used 9 mL supernatant plus 1 mL LaCl3 solution, final La
1-50 mM, rolled for 24 hours at room temperature, with at least three
independent replicates. Gravimetric precipitate yield and Arsenazo III
remaining-supernatant La are different quantities.

Figure 7a,c and its results text report 100% aqueous removal at 1, 3 and
5 mM initial La, 49.8% at 15 mM, and a maximum dry precipitate mass of
23.3 mg per 10 mL at 15 mM. That precipitate mass is not elemental La.
The rounded 100% result has no independently established zero-residual or
purified-product interpretation here. The comparison reaction used 9 mL
of 25 mM chemical oxalic acid and also precipitated La, with reported 100%
removal through 11 mM. It was not pH-matched, and oxalate in the actual fungal
supernatant was not directly quantified: the 25 mM model input came from
earlier literature. A normalized precipitant-efficiency or biological-
necessity claim is therefore unwarranted.

Figure 4 shows phosphorus in a high-La supernatant product. Figure 9 shows
mixed-phase XRD at 20 mM. Its phosphorus-phase formula is printed as
La7P3O18 in the figure/caption but La7P3O8 in the results text; the record
intentionally assigns no exact formula to that phase. The chemical-control
removed-La amount is also mislabeled in prose as mg dry weight, whereas
Figure 7 uses micromoles; that control amount is not copied into the record.
The source's written removal equation lacks the denominator implicit in
its plotted percentage; no new percentage was calculated from that equation.

Figure 5's oxalate and heated-oxide XRD applies to crystals from the separate
1 mM La agar experiment, incubated for 14 days, then thermally treated at
1000 C. It is kept distinct from the supernatant assay and does not imply a
microbial redox transformation to La2O3. No ore dissolution, selective REE
separation, real-waste performance or industrial deployment was tested.

## Dysprosium: Zinicovscaia and colleagues

[DOI 10.3390/microorganisms11082009](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms11082009),
*The Remediation of Dysprosium-Containing Effluents Using Cyanobacteria
Spirulina platensis and Yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, *Microorganisms*
11:2009, 4 August 2023. Full-text Sections 2-4, all four numerical tables,
Figures 1, 2 and 4, and supplementary Figure S1 were inspected.
Only the yeast arm is curated; this avoids implying that the unresolved
commercial cyanobacterial material has an independently verified strain.

Yeast identified by the authors as *S. cerevisiae* came from brewery residues
in Chisinau. Drying at 105 C for 24 hours establishes an inactive biosorbent
context. Triplicate assays used 0.5 g biomass with 50 mL synthetic solution,
200 rpm, varying pH 2-6, time 5-120 minutes, Dy 10-100 mg/L and temperature
20-50 C. The pH experiment peaks at 68% reported removal at pH 3; the
conclusion assigns this to one hour. Figure 1b independently shows about
85% at 60 minutes in the kinetics series. These are separate arms, not two
estimates that can be averaged into a generic maximum.

Material limitations retained in the record:

- Methods fix kinetic pH at 3, while the later results text says subsequent
  experiments used pH 4. Figure 1 captions do not resolve the conflict.
- The reagent formula is printed Dy(NO3)2·6H2O despite a Dy(III) discussion.
  The synthetic nitrate feed is recorded without assigning a precise salt ID.
- NAA is described for dried biomass; a removal equation refers to initial
  and final aqueous concentrations without specifying the final aqueous assay.
- No matched biomass-free solution-control result is described. Biomass FTIR
  and elemental changes support a binding interpretation but cannot assign
  all apparent solution loss to one surface process or close an ion-exchange
  mass balance.
- Table 1's 5.84 +/- 0.68 mg/g is a **fitted** Langmuir capacity. Figure 1c
  and text report 3.85 mg/g as the highest tested yeast uptake at 100 mg/L.
- There is no desorption/reuse result, isolated Dy product, selectivity test
  against competing REEs, real-effluent assay or scale-up evidence.
