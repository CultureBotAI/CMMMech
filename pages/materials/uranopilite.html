Uranopilite mineral

Uranopilite is the uranyl sulfate oxide hydroxide hydrate mineral with the September 2026 IMA formula (UO2)6(SO4)O2(OH)6(H2O)6·8H2O. Its grandfathered status G and original-description year 1882 are not a numbered approval accession. It is distinct from elemental uranium, a uranium commodity, zippeite and phases historically called metauranopilite. Burns determined the triclinic P-1 structure from a natural Joachimsthal, Bohemia specimen in Harvard Museum collection 89612. The structure contains uranyl sulfate chains with six coordinated and eight interstitial water groups per formula unit. Hydrogen positions were not directly located; the hydrogen-bond model and hydroxyl/water assignments used crystal-chemical and bond-valence arguments. The historical (UO2)6(SO4)(OH)10·12H2O formulation has the same total elemental composition but a different partition of hydroxyl and molecular water. The later natural Cervena-vein study confirmed the structure with substantial twinning and refinement limitations. Its Table 15 water content was calculated from the assumed fourteen-water structure, not independently measured. Natural crystals in ore fractures support occurrence in an alteration assemblage; they do not establish a controlled formation rate, exact formation pH or microbial causation. This record accepts natural identity and structural evidence, with thermal transformations deferred. No criticality designation, extraction yield or industrial recovery is inferred.
