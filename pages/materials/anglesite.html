Anglesite mineral

Anglesite is the Pb(SO4) mineral species listed with grandfathered status G and original-description year 1832 in the September 2026 IMA compilation. It is distinct from elemental lead, galena, dissolved lead and a mixed lead-bearing concentrate or battery paste. The observation below concerns a laboratory reaction product identified in leached galena-concentrate residues. It does not establish the identity of every lead-sulfate reagent or inherit a criticality designation from lead.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated anglesite formation in a galena-concentrate residue

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans (NCBITaxon:920) [TaxonMech]

Leaching a natural galena-rich concentrate with a living F3-02 culture produced a residue containing X-ray-diffraction-identified anglesite. The source reports a higher relative anglesite proportion in the inoculated residue than in its sterile-labelled control, which also contains anglesite. This supports culture-associated formation in a mixed reaction system, not a requirement for cells to precipitate lead sulfate or direct enzymatic crystallization. The proposed iron/sulfur oxidation and passivating-film explanations remain interpretations. The product is a component of a laboratory residue, not an isolated anglesite or lead recovery yield. This is the same experiment represented from the substrate perspective in the galena record, not an independent replication.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The ZGH Boleslaw flotation feed was reported as approximately 80% galena with hydrocerussite, marcasite, sphalerite, pyrite, gypsum and quartz; the approximate listed phase percentages total about 101%. Table 1 gives 69.3% Pb. The source-labelled F3-02 isolate came from ferruginous mineral water at Piwniczna-Lomnica and was grown in modified sulfate-containing 2K medium without prior lead adaptation. Assays used 300 mL flasks, a reported 5% weight/volume concentrate loading, 100 mL solution and 10 mL culture, 25 degrees C and 130 rpm. The final-volume basis of that loading is not fully specified. Nominal initial pH 2 and Fe(II) 2.0 g/L differ from Table 2's initial pH 2.2 and Fe(II) 2.159 g/L. Sampling extended through 384 h; the reported residue phase comparison is at 288 h. Cu-Kalpha XRD with a Philips PW3710 supports bulk phase identification. SEM specimens were dried on a filter in ambient air and gold-coated. Sterilization details, biological replicate counts and phase-fraction uncertainties are not supplied. The cultures and controls developed different pH and redox trajectories, so they do not isolate a biological effect at matched solution chemistry.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: pacholewska2004
Phase Analysis of Residues on p. 286 and Figure 1 on p. 287 identify anglesite peaks in both leached residues. The source estimates about 40% anglesite and 43% galena in the inoculated 288 h residue, compared with about 25% anglesite and 62% galena in the control. These are approximate proportions of different mixed residues, not fractions of initial lead converted to anglesite, absolute product masses or an isolated-product yield. No phase-quantification procedure or uncertainty supports a more precise comparison. Other phases, including pyrite and jarosite, remain present.
Partial Source: pacholewska2004
Table 2 records faster Fe(II) depletion and different pH/Eh in the inoculated concentrate, consistent with an indirect role for microbial oxidation but not an isolated molecular pathway. Dissolved Pb at 288 h is 0.0021 g/L in the inoculated arm versus 0.0022 g/L in the control; these solution measurements do not demonstrate enhanced soluble-lead recovery. The medium itself contains sulfate, and the feed contains other sulfur minerals, so product sulfate cannot be assigned exclusively to oxidation of galena sulfur. The separate synthetic-PbS arm developed pH near 5, retained Fe(II) and lacked observed bacterial colonies while a chemically formed PbSO4 coating was reported. It is not a matched low-pH test of the natural concentrate.
Partial Source: pacholewska2004
The Table 3 quantity called sulfur conversion is the residue sulfate-sulfur divided by total sulfur minus sulfate-sulfur, expressed as a percentage. This operational difference term does not identify each residual sulfur phase, and the ratio is not an initial-feed sulfur or lead mass balance. Its printed control entries at 96 and 192 h do not reproduce the displayed constituent ratios; no corrected values or conversion yield are adopted. The statement of no further change at 384 h also overstates the displayed ratio's plateau. Figures 2-3 show surface coatings and cell-like objects, but morphology alone does not authenticate F3-02 or chemically identify every coating. A passivating role of anglesite with possible elemental sulfur is proposed rather than independently measured or established for all media.
Context_only Source: ncbi_aferrooxidans
NCBI resolves taxon 920 as Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans at species rank. The primary paper supplies the F3-02 isolate designation; no culture-collection or sequence accession is established here and no separate strain identifier is invented.
