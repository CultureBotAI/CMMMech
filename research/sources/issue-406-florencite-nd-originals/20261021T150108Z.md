## Original 1971 evidence and quantitative boundaries

Pages 166–167 describe a weathered fracture occurrence near Sausalito, California. Figure 1 is a locality photograph; it is not microscopic phase evidence. Associated churchite, lithiophorite, todorokite, hematite and clays do not constitute replicate Nd-florencite specimens. The source-labelled florencite is moderate-brown, earthy and pulverulent; individual crystals were not resolved microscopically. Churchite often overlies it. The churchite pattern matches existing standards, while the florencite pattern remains indeterminate within the plumbogummite group (page 167, lower left).

The three tables have different sample and unit scopes. Table 1, page 167, concerns **lithiophorite**: the left column is percent and the right column ppm. Mn is qualitative, Cu is a lower bound, and the separately reported Li2O result uses atomic absorption. Table 3 concerns **chert, shale and basalt**, with `n.d.` explicitly meaning not detected but without numeric limits. Neither table is a florencite assay or a feed/product balance.

Table 2, page 168, contains separate semiquantitative spectrographic churchite and florencite analyses by Harry Bastron. The florencite column reports Nd 3%, La 2%, Ce 0.5% and Ba 7%, alongside Fe 10% and Mn 7%. It identifies the source's barian, Nd-dominant preparation, without assigning every measured element to a resolved crystallographic site or establishing a pure endmember. Si, Al and K were not sought. Parenthesized limits, two churchite greater-than values and a churchite Ti double dash are preserved rather than treated as zeros. Missing Al alone prevents a measured complete NdAl3(PO4)2(OH)6 formula from being reconstructed from this table. There is no unique registered specimen accession, purified phase mass or complete elemental closure in the inspected original.

The manually keyed ledger covers **82 primary numerical components**, including eight censored components, plus one qualitative `major`, one double dash and eight `n.d.` cells. It separately captures **21 selected Handbook numerical components**, for 103 selected components in all. The 28 Decimal calculations retain source units and sample identities. They are conditional checks, not new assays or estimates of measurement uncertainty.

The florencite ratio Ce/(Nd+La) is 0.5/(3+2) = **0.1**, agreeing with the paper's 1:10 statement. Nd/(Ce+La) = **1.2** supports the reported predominance under these semiquantitative weight components. Host-shale Ce/(Nd+La) = **0.8** belongs to a separate sample. No result is transferred to solution recovery, ore grade or a pure mineral formula.

Figure 2 uses **percent of total lanthanoids**, with separate even- and odd-atomic-number curves; it is not the Table 2 whole-preparation percentage scale, and Y is outside that denominator. A source-internal discrepancy remains: Table 2 visibly prints churchite Gd 1% and Dy 7%, while the solid even-element curve is approximately equal at Gd and Dy, near 22–23%. Summing only the printed detected churchite lanthanoids gives 25.2%; allowing its three upper limits gives a strict upper total of 25.40%. Conditional normalization therefore yields Gd about 3.94–3.97% and Dy about 27.56–27.78%, not equal values. No common denominator can turn the printed 1:7 ratio into equality. This is an approximate visual comparison, not digitization. No corrected Gd value is selected. The analogous florencite Nd check gives about 28.37–28.68% and is compatible with its approximate plotted maximum.

The paper's geological account proposes concentration on manganese oxides followed by phosphate crystallization. It discusses trees at other localities, but explicitly leaves a biogeochemical concentration stage at Sausalito undetermined (page 167, right column). It contains no controlled microbial experiment, strain, biomass, medium, sterile comparator, recovery yield or industrial demonstration. Such fields are inapplicable to this characterization source; associations are not admitted as mechanisms.
