Heimaeyite mineral

IMA-approved anhydrous sodium-aluminium sulfate mineral (IMA 2023-127a; status A, Approved 2024 in CNMNC Newsletter 81, Bosi et al., 2024; full description published 13 February 2025; no mineral symbol is reported in the primary paper or in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 91) with ideal end-member formula Na3Al(SO4)3 (also written Na3AlS3O12 in Section 3, p. 81, requiring Na2O 24.20 wt%, Al2O3 13.27 wt%, SO3 62.53 wt%, total 100.00 wt%, with 10% to 25% replacement of Al by Fe in the natural sample and single-crystal structure refinement formula Na3Al0.93Fe0.07S3O12 in Table 3, p. 84; Balic-Zunic, Garavelli, Mitolo, Nestola, Pamato, Rasmussen, and Jolnaes, 2025, European Journal of Mineralogy 37:79-89). Named after the Icelandic island of Heimaey (hosting the largest Icelandic fishing harbour of the same name), on which Eldfell volcano (63 deg 25 min 50 sec N, 20 deg 14 min 48 sec W) is situated (Abstract and Section 1, p. 79; Section 7, p. 88). Collected in 2009 on Eldfell volcano by a team of geologists from the University of Copenhagen led by the late Sveinn P. Jakobsson from the Icelandic Institute of Natural History, corresponding to the fumarolic phase with powder X-ray diffraction characteristics similar to the unnamed mineral previously given the two-letter label "EN" by Jakobsson et al. (2008) (Section 1, p. 79; Section 5.2, pp. 87-88). The holotype specimen is deposited in the mineral collection of the Icelandic Institute of Natural History in Gardabaer, Iceland, under sample number NI-30218 (Section 1, p. 80). Formed in a tube (where the measured fumarolic temperature at collection was between 200 and 300 deg C) inside red scoria consisting mostly of a mixture of anhydrite, hematite, and cristobalite through which fumarolic gases percolated, producing a ceramic-like white-yellow-reddish mass that consists of an intimate micrometre-scale porous mixture of heimaeyite, acicular koryakite ((Na,K)MgAl(SO4)3 in Section 2, p. 80; written NaKMg2Al2(SO4)6 in Section 3, p. 81), acicular crystals of the still-undescribed new mineral labelled "EA" (NaMgAl(SO4)3), and products of their hydration: tamarugite, hexahydrite, and loweite plus some langbeinite (Abstract, p. 79; Section 2 and Figure 1, p. 80; Figure 2, p. 81). Occurs as microscopic rounded or globular aggregates (<50 um) of platy crystals and individual rounded isometric grains (<10 um; the largest grain found had a longest diameter of ~70 um and was used for single-crystal XRD structure analysis; Abstract, p. 79, and Section 2, p. 80). Natural Fe-bearing heimaeyite is orange in colour (observed on the 0.07 x 0.05 x 0.015 mm orange plate used for single-crystal XRD; Section 2, p. 80, and Table 3, p. 84), translucent, with a vitreous lustre and a yellow streak (described as "yellowish streak" in the Abstract, p. 79, and "yellow" in Section 2, p. 80; pure synthetic Na3Al(SO4)3 is colourless with a white streak). Cleavage was not observed and is not expected as no preferred orientation could be registered on PXRD diagrams; all observed fragments show uneven fractures; heimaeyite is soluble in water (Section 3, p. 81); magnetism was not observed; fluorescence, hardness, tenacity, and experimental optical properties could not be measured due to small grain size and admixture with other minerals (Section 2, p. 80). Density could not be measured; the calculated density is 2.783 g cm-3 on the basis of the empirical chemical analysis and single-crystal XRD unit-cell dimensions (Abstract, p. 79, and Section 2, p. 80), 2.747 g cm-3 calculated from the results of the crystal structure analysis in Section 2 (p. 80), and 2.7411 in Table 3 (p. 84, where the row header prints a typographical unit error "Calculated density (g cm-1)" instead of "g cm-3"). The calculated mean refractive index from the Gladstone-Dale relationship (Mandarino, 1976) is 1.5222 (Section 2, p. 80). After preliminary qualitative SEM-EDS analysis on a Cambridge S 360 SEM coupled with an Oxford Link Ge ISIS EDS equipped with a Super Atmosphere Thin Window, quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron-microprobe analyses (JEOL JXA-8200; 15 kV, 5 nA, 10 um spot size, 10 s peak and 5 s background counting times on a diamond-polished, 30 nm carbon-sputtered epoxy mount; average of n = 11 analyses selected for the smallest contribution of impurities; Section 3, pp. 80-81, and Table 1, p. 80) yielded mean Fe2O3 4.18 wt% (range 3.38-5.35, SD 0.58, standard Hematite), MnO 0.43 wt% (0.14-0.71, SD 0.16, standard MnTiO3), K2O 0.98 wt% (0.69-1.17, SD 0.14, standard Orthoclase), SO3 56.72 wt% (53.28-58.53, SD 1.40, standard Barite), SiO2 0.10 wt% (0.01-0.22, SD 0.07, standard Orthoclase), Al2O3 10.10 wt% (9.05-11.12, SD 0.57, standard Corundum), MgO 1.00 wt% (0.74-1.21, SD 0.12, standard Olivine), Na2O 19.39 wt% (17.53-20.31, SD 0.79, standard Albite), with printed Total 92.89 wt% (note that the exact sum of the eight rounded mean wt% values is 4.18 + 0.43 + 0.98 + 56.72 + 0.10 + 10.10 + 1.00 + 19.39 = 92.90 wt%, a 0.01 wt% rounding difference; the low total is explained by high micrometre-scale sample porosity incorporating epoxy and embedded polishing diamond grains). The total unsubtracted composition corresponds to K0.10Na2.95Mn0.03Mg0.12Fe0.25Al0.94Si0.01S3.35O13.5; after removing the Mn and Si impurities and the contributions of admixed acicular koryakite (NaKMg2Al2(SO4)6) and "EA" (NaMgAl(SO4)3) by subtracting Al' = Mg, Na' = Mg - K, and S' = 3 x Mg, the resulting empirical formula of heimaeyite is Na2.93Al0.82Fe0.25S2.99O12.05 (Abstract, p. 79, and Section 3, p. 81). Crystallizes in the trigonal system, space group R-3 (#148), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters (Rigaku Oxford Diffraction Supernova diffractometer, microfocus Mo X-ray source, lambda = 0.71073 A, PILATUS DECTRIS 200K area detector, T = 293 K, 2321 reflections for unit-cell refinement, multi-scan absorption correction; Section 4.2, pp. 82-83, and Table 3, p. 84) a = 13.4326(9) A, c = 8.9818(7) A, V = 1403.5(2) A3, Z = 6, and c:a ratio = 0.6687 (Section 7, p. 88). Powder X-ray diffraction (Bruker AXS D8 ADVANCE diffractometer, Bragg-Brentano geometry, primary Ge111 monochromator, CuKalpha1 radiation lambda = 1.54059 A, LYNXEYE silicon-strip detector; Section 4.1, p. 81, and Figure 3, p. 83) on a multiphase sample reported as containing 41 wt% heimaeyite, 18 wt% tamarugite, 15 wt% koryakite, 11 wt% langbeinite, 9 wt% EA, and 7 wt% hexahydrite (note that the rounded integer percentages sum to 41 + 18 + 15 + 11 + 9 + 7 = 101 wt%) yielded refined trigonal unit-cell parameters a = 13.3872(4) A, c = 8.9411(4) A, V = 1387.7(1) A3. Structure solution by charge flipping (SUPERFLIP) and anisotropic refinement in Jana2006 (with Al2/Fe2 occupancy sum constrained to 1 and shared ADP) converged to R1 = 4.42% for 550 observed reflections with I > 3sigma(I) and R1 = 10.87% (wR = 5.64%, GoF = 1.3031) for all 998 reflections (Rint = 0.104) and 60 refined parameters (Section 4.2, p. 83, and Table 3, p. 84). Heimaeyite is isostructural with Na3V(SO4)3 (PDF 39-0243; Boghosian et al., 1994) and high-temperature Na3Fe(SO4)3 (Scordari et al., 2011) in the K7Nb(SO4)6-K7Ta(SO4)6 structure type (Borup et al., 1990): chains parallel to [001] are formed by two symmetry-independent [AlO6] octahedra sitting on 3-bar inversion centres and rotated by ~180 deg relative to each other in a "pin-wheel" configuration bridged by three [S1O4] sulfate tetrahedra (S1-O4 = 1.446(4) A, S1-O1 = 1.454(2) A, S1-O3 = 1.473(3) A, S1-O2 = 1.480(3) A, mean <d> = 1.463 A, BVS = 6.2 vu, Vp = 1.602(8) A3), namely a smaller pure-Al site Al1 at (0, 0, 0) (occupancy 1, Al1-O2 x 6 = 1.877(2) A, BVS = 3.3 vu, Vp = 8.79(2) A3, volume distortion vd = 0.0029) and a significantly larger mixed site Al2/Fe2 at (1/3, 2/3, 1/6) (occupancy 0.853(10) Al + 0.147(10) Fe, Al2/Fe2-O3 x 6 = 2.055(4) A, BVS = 2.1 vu, Vp = 11.47(4) A3, vd = 0.0093), while triplets of chains are interconnected into a 3D framework by 6-coordinated [Na1O6] distorted trigonal prisms (Na1-O = 2.342(3)-2.799(3) A, mean <d> = 2.518 A, BVS = 0.9 vu, Vp = 16.42(4) A3, vd = 0.2128; Section 5.1-5.2, pp. 83-86, and Tables 4-6, pp. 85-86). Pure synthetic Na3Al(SO4)3 (95 wt% with 5 wt% synthetic metathenardite Na2SO4), prepared by heating stoichiometric powders of Na2SO4 and aluminium sulfate hydrate (printed as "Al3(SO4)2(H2O)17" in Section 6, p. 87, a typographical transposition of subscripts for Al2(SO4)3*17H2O) in a platinum crucible for 1 month at 550 deg C, yielded Rietveld-refined PXRD parameters a = 13.33936(8) A, c = 8.90165(6) A (Rwp = 9.65%, Rp = 6.70%, RBragg = 2.00%; Al1-O2 x 6 = 1.873(4) A, Al2-O3 x 6 = 1.893(4) A, and 7-coordinated Na1-O = 2.299(5)-2.968(4) A; Section 6 and Tables 7-8, p. 87). The eight strongest powder X-ray diffraction maxima in Table 2 [dmeas in A (Imeas, dcalc in A) hkl; p. 82, where the footnote states "* The intensities of the eight strongest maxima are in bold", although only the first seven Imeas values — 40, 17, 32, 100, 55, 10 at 2.72 A, and 11 — are typeset in bold while the tied eighth at 2.026 A (Imeas = 10) is typeset in regular font] are 6.68 (40, 6.69) (2 -1 0), 4.16 (17, 4.17) (1 0 -2), 3.93 (32, 3.93) (3 -1 1, 3 -2 -1), 3.13 (100, 3.13) (3 -2 2 + L, 3 -1 -2), 3.02 (55, 3.03) (4 -1 -1, 4 -3 1), 2.72 (10, 2.72) (2 -1 -3, 2 -1 3), 2.55 (11, 2.55) (5 -3 -1, 5 -2 1), and 2.026 (10, 2.028) (6 -1 1, 6 -5 -1). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
