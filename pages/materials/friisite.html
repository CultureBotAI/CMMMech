Friisite mineral

IMA-approved hexagonal anhydrous lead-aluminium-chlorine phyllosilicate mineral with a polysomatic relation to jagoite (IMA 2024-047; IMA symbol Fii; status A, Approved; full description published 2 April 2026; September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 78; note that the 11th sentence of the Abstract on p. 169 contains the typographical spelling "Friiste") with ideal end-member formula Pb8Al3Si8O27Cl3 (requiring PbO 71.38 wt%, Al2O3 6.11 wt%, SiO2 19.22 wt%, Cl 4.25 wt%, Cl = O -0.96 wt%, sum 100.00 wt%; Holtstam, Camara, and Karlsson, 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:169-177). Named in honor of Danish mineralogist Prof. Henrik Friis (born 1977), curator of the Mineral Collection at the Natural History Museum, University of Oslo, Norway, and former Chief Editor of the European Journal of Mineralogy (Section 1, p. 170). Discovered in a museum specimen of jagoite with heterogeneous skarn associated with hematite ore collected from the Canberra stope at a depth of 220 m in the Langban Fe-Mn-(Ba-As-Pb-Sb-W-Be-B) mine (59 deg 51 min N, 14 deg 15 min E), Filipstad district, Varmland, Sweden, and provided in 1961 by John Gustaf Herman Weslien (Section 1-2, p. 170). The holotype specimen is housed in the type mineral collection of the Department of Geosciences, Swedish Museum of Natural History, Stockholm, Sweden, under collection number GEO-NRM #19610234 (Section 1, p. 170). Occurs within a medium-grained skarn matrix and contiguous to jagoite (ideally Pb11Fe5Si12O41Cl3), associated with melanotekite, aegirine-augite, jagoite, albite, baryte, fluorapophyllite-K, margarosanite, alamosite, native lead, a serpentine-group mineral, and a wickenburgite-like mineral (designated W, Pb3CaAl2Si10O27 * 4H2O; Abstract, p. 169, Section 2, p. 170, and Section 7.1-7.2, p. 175). Forms subhedral flaky grains up to 150 um in size (in aggregates of 10-150 um across), white to colorless in appearance with a white streak and a sub-adamantine luster; transparent, non-fluorescent under ultraviolet light, brittle tenacity with an uneven fracture, perfect {001} cleavage, Mohs hardness 4-5 (inferred by analogy with jagoite), and calculated density Dcalc = 5.54(1) g cm-3 in the Abstract (p. 169) and Section 3 (p. 170) vs rho_calc = 5.54 g cm-3 in Table 3 (p. 173) from the empirical formula and single-crystal XRD unit-cell volume. Optically non-pleochroic and uniaxial negative (-), with a Gladstone-Dale calculated average refractive index of n = 1.88 (Mandarino, 2007; Abstract, p. 169, and Section 3, pp. 170-171). Polarized micro-Raman spectroscopy (Horiba LabRAM HR Evolution spectrometer, 532 nm solid-state YAG laser at 2.06 and 5.02 mW, 100x VIS objective, 1 um spot, 600 grooves mm-1 grating, 15 s x 10 accumulations; Section 5 and Figures 3-4, pp. 171-172) shows no Raman bands above 1100 cm-1 (confirming the absence of H2O and CO2), weak SiO4 stretching bands at 1095, 1060, 1053, 986, 976, 969, 954, 907, 905, 883, and 865 cm-1, ring Si-O-Si bridging vibrations at 658, 611, 563, 561, 543, and 541 cm-1, SiO4 bending and (Al,Fe)-O vibrations at 482, 436, 409, 408, 340, 337, 306, and 303 cm-1, and intense low-wavenumber lattice/Pb-O/Pb-Cl modes reported on p. 171 at 29, 42, 52, 63, 72, "96 123" (printed without a separating comma), 179, and ~213 cm-1 (whereas p. 172 prints "64 and 73 cm-1" for the two low-frequency bands). Quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron-microprobe analyses (FEI Quanta 650 FEG-SEM equipped with an AZtecWave WDS system; 20 kV, 14 nA, 10 mm working distance, 20 nm carbon coat, average of n = 6 spot analyses; Section 4 and Table 1, p. 171) yielded mean Na2O 0.14 wt% (range 0.10-0.17, 2sigma 0.05, standard jadeite), Al2O3 4.42 wt% (3.27-4.87, 2sigma 1.10, standard almandine), SiO2 19.80 wt% (19.10-20.38, 2sigma 0.93, standard almandine), Cl 4.19 wt% (3.99-4.42, 2sigma 0.30, standard tugtupite), CaO 0.19 wt% (0.16-0.26, 2sigma 0.07, standard bustamite), MnO 0.31 wt% (0.17-0.57, 2sigma 0.30, standard rhodonite), Fe2O3 0.63 wt% (0.45-0.91, 2sigma 0.33, standard hematite), ZnO 0.42 wt% (0.34-0.61, 2sigma 0.18, standard willemite), PbO 69.83 wt% (69.10-71.16, 2sigma 1.50, standard crocoite), O = Cl = -0.94 wt%, and printed Total = 98.98 wt% (range 98.26-99.99; note that the exact arithmetic sum of the rounded mean wt% values is 0.14 + 4.42 + 19.80 + 4.19 + 0.19 + 0.31 + 0.63 + 0.42 + 69.83 - 0.94 = 98.99 wt%, a 0.01 wt% rounding difference). Normalized on a basis of 30 anions (O + Cl), the empirical formula is (Pb7.89Na0.11Ca0.08)sum=8.08(Al2.19Si0.31Fe3+0.20Zn0.13Mn2+0.11)sum=2.94Si8O27.02Cl2.98, with Al2O3 negatively correlated with Fe2O3 + MnO + ZnO (linear R2 = 0.95; Section 4, p. 171). Crystallizes in the hexagonal system (ditrigonal dipyramidal class, point group -6m2), space group P-62c (#190), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters (Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy diffractometer, PhotonJet microfocus MoKalpha radiation lambda = 0.71073 A, HyPix-6000HE detector, T = 293 K, crystal size 0.102 x 0.063 x 0.033 mm3; Section 6.2, p. 172, and Table 3, p. 173) a = 8.5955(1) A (Abstract, p. 169) / 8.59550(10) A (Table 3, p. 173), c = 23.4092(2) A, V = 1497.82(4) A3, Z = 2, and c:a = 2.7234, while Gandolfi powder X-ray diffraction (lambda = 0.71073 A; Section 6.1 and Table 2, p. 172) gave a = 8.599(6) A, c = 23.379(8) A, V = 1497.11(4) A3, Z = 2. Full-matrix least-squares refinement on F2 in SHELXL (87 parameters, 0 restraints, merohedral twin ratio 0.735(7)/0.265(7), Flack parameter 0.000(3)) converged to R1 = 0.0144 for reflections with I > 2sigma(I) and R1 = 0.0163 (GooF = 1.062) for all 2461 independent reflections out of 60680 collected (2theta = 3.48 to 72.776 deg, Rint = 0.0500, Rsigma = 0.0160), with largest diff. peak and hole 1.16 and -1.03 e A-3 (Table 3, p. 173). The layered phyllosilicate structure (Section 6.2 and Tables 4-6, pp. 172-175, and Section 7.2, p. 175; Nickel-Strunz group 09.EG, ideal structural formula Pb8Al[Al2Si8O27]Cl3) has a layer sequence of SiO4 tetrahedra (T) and metal octahedra (O) between double tetrahedral layers (*) corresponding to *TOT* (whereas jagoite, ideally Pb11Fe5Si12O41Cl3, has layer sequence *TOTOT* with an additional single tetrahedral sheet): double tetrahedral sheets [Al2Si8O27]16- formed by corner-sharing Si1 (<Si1-O> = 1.614 A), Si2 (<Si2-O> = 1.627 A), and Al-dominant T tetrahedra (<T-O> = 1.750 A; refined occupancy 0.600(6) Fe in Table 4, observed site scattering 15.60(8) e and assigned cation occupancy Al0.63Fe3+0.07Mn2+0.06Zn0.07Si0.16 in Table 6) alternate along [001] with octahedral layers containing isolated M octahedra (<M-O> = 1.900 A; refined occupancy 1.054(16) Al in Table 4, observed site scattering 13.7(4) e and assigned occupancy Al0.94Fe3+0.06 in Table 6), three inequivalent Pb sites (Pb1 with <Pb1-phi> = 2.910 A, Pb2 with <Pb2-O> = 2.767 A, and Pb3 with <Pb3-phi> = 2.830 A and assigned occupancy Pb0.93Na0.04Ca0.03), and one Cl site (Wyckoff 6h, 0.995(9) Cl, BVS = 0.97 vu; note also that reaction (R2) in Section 7.1, p. 175, prints "NaAlSi3O8 (Ab)" without the stoichiometric coefficient 3 required to balance 3NaOH(aq) and 3Al). The eight strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines (Abstract, p. 169, and Table 2, p. 172) [d, A (Irel) (hkl)] are 5.848 (31) (004), 5.375 (20) (103), 4.040 (96) (-1 -1 2, 112), 3.680 (40) (201), 3.463 (100) (114, -1 -1 4), 2.886 (21) (116, -1 -1 6), 2.795 (20) (-2 -1 1, 211), and 2.4828 (35) (300). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
