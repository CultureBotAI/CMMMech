Kaliborite mineral

Kaliborite is the grandfathered potassium-magnesium borate species KHMg2B12O16(OH)10(H2O)4 in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list. Its 1889 entry is an original-description year, not an approval identifier. Burns and Hawthorne studied a natural Kazakhstan crystal from Canadian Museum of Nature specimen 53372 and refined a monoclinic C2/c structure with borate chains parallel to b. Their 1994 study located hydrogen by difference-Fourier analysis with O-H distance restraints. The apparently symmetric hydrogen bond was interpreted as dynamically disordered from structural and bond-valence arguments; this was not a direct measurement of hydrogen motion. The paper did not provide a new bulk chemical analysis of that crystal. A separate museum material supplied the thermal experiments below. This mineral is distinct from elemental boron and the borates commodity; neither criticality, microbial activity nor industrial recovery is inferred.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Laboratory thermal amorphization and recrystallization

other

Heating natural kaliborite produced an X-ray-amorphous solid during water and hydroxyl loss, followed by crystallization of magnesium borates and subsequently a potassium borate. These are reported laboratory phase transformations, not a measured boron extraction efficiency or recovery of an isolated product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Stoch and Waclawska used coarse-crystalline natural borate minerals described as pure from the Mineralogical Museum of Wroclaw University; they did not give a kaliborite accession, locality, quantitative purity or composition analysis. Thermal analysis used Q-1500D and C-derivatographs. For the structural examination arm, samples were heated at 2.5 degrees C per minute to characteristic temperatures, cooled and examined by powder X-ray diffraction and infrared spectroscopy. That stated ramp is not assigned here to every DTA/TG run. Sample mass, atmosphere, hold times and replicate counts are not specified.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: stoch_waclawska1994
The kaliborite results on pp. 183-185 report thermal events at 258 and 272 degrees C and progressive water/OH release through 400 degrees C. Table 1 assigns an amorphous state at 400 degrees C and recrystallization at 679 degrees C; Figure 2a(C) distinguishes the original, amorphous and two recrystallized powder patterns. Magnesium-borate reflections are assigned to MgB4O7 and Mg2B2O5, with KB5O8 in the later recrystallized pattern. Table 1 places that later step at 729 degrees C and melting at 954 degrees C. The evidence identifies phases, without quantitative product fractions.
Partial Source: stoch_waclawska1994
The 640-degree endotherm is interpreted as a glass-transition effect, rather than directly establishing a useful glass product. Tables 1 and 4 print different amorphous bulk formulas, so neither is adopted as a balanced reaction or measured composition. The reported temperatures are features of the authors' heating experiments, not demonstrated equilibrium boundaries. This article cites the same authors' 1993 kaliborite study as reference 5; the two publications are not counted as independent replication. The complete 1993 paper was not acquired, and its abstract's inconsistent dehydration/dehydroxylation ordering is not imported.
