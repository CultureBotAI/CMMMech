Zeunerite mineral

Zeunerite is the copper uranyl arsenate mineral Cu(UO2)2(AsO4)2(H2O)4·8H2O in the September 2026 IMA list, with grandfathered status G. The year 1872 denotes its original description, not a numbered IMA approval. Its ideal twelve-water composition is distinct from eight-water metazeunerite, phosphate torbernite, calcium meta-autunite and elemental uranium, copper or arsenic. Synthetic-crystal X-ray diffraction and a later partially deuterated synthetic-powder neutron study support P4/nnc models under their respective conditions. Hydrogen positions and disorder are model-dependent; these experiments do not establish natural-specimen homogeneity, long-term containment, a universal dehydration threshold or an extraction yield. No criticality or microbial activity is inferred from composition.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Abiotic laboratory crystallization of a synthetic zeunerite analogue

other

Copper, uranyl and arsenate reagents produced crystals or powder assigned to zeunerite by diffraction. Slow gel growth and solution precipitation are separate laboratory preparations; neither is a microbial process or a demonstrated industrial recovery operation.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Locock and Burns grew zeunerite at room temperature over three weeks by slow diffusion of reported 0.167 M H5As3O10 and 0.5 M uranyl-nitrate solutions into copper-bearing silica gel. A crystal was epoxy-coated to limit dehydration before room-temperature MoK-alpha single-crystal diffraction. Separately, MacIver-Jones precipitated material from uranyl nitrate in 0.2 M nitric acid, copper nitrate and arsenic acid, matured it for 48 h, filtered and washed it, and dried it at room temperature. Subsequent repeated reflux in D2O at 75 degrees C prepared the neutron sample; residual H was detected by FTIR. The zeunerite refinement used data collected at 50 degrees C, not an untreated natural crystal. Neither preparation supplies a mass-balanced metal recovery yield.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: locock2003
Pages 490-492 and Tables 1, 3 and 7 support synthetic twelve-water zeunerite in P4/nnc. The refined cell is a = 7.1797(3) and c = 20.857(1) angstrom, with R1 = 1.6% for 468 observed unique reflections. Page 498 identifies pseudo-I-centering and argues that observed reflections require P-centering, disagreeing with the earlier Hennig et al. I4/mmm model. This is the authors' interpretation of their dataset, not an independent re-refinement here.
Partial Source: locock2003
Hydrogen positions were refined with O-H distance constraints and fixed isotropic displacement parameters. Page 497 presumes equivalence to natural zeunerite from compatible cell dimensions; natural material was not the measured crystal. The hydrothermal 220-degree-C synthesis and twinning refinements belong to metazeunerite and are not zeunerite results. The inaccessible Hennig full text and CISTI reflection deposit limit independent adjudication of the competing model.
Support Source: maciver_jones_thesis
Printed pages 61-65 and 77-82, Figures 3.1-3.3 and Tables 3.1-3.4 support preparation and a P4/nnc neutron model of partially deuterated zeunerite at 50 degrees C, with twelve water molecules per formula unit. Figures 3.8-3.9 and pages 93-97 propose a split H/D site and hydrogen-bond network. The model used soft bond restraints and constrained displacement parameters; AIRSS verification was performed only for metatorbernite.
Partial Source: maciver_jones_thesis
The source calls the sample container stainless steel in Chapter 2 but vanadium in Chapter 3. Copper occupancy uncertainty is 1.00(2) in prose versus 1.00(3) in Table 3.1; water-angle uncertainties also differ between prose and Table 3.3. Appendix 3.2 repeats a zeunerite caption despite phosphorus-labelled spectra, so it is not used as a zeunerite assay. The proposed hydrogen-bond explanation for greater stability than torbernite remains a hypothesis, with neutron analysis of torbernite requested as future work. Raw neutron runs were not reanalysed. No quantitative solubility, microbial mechanism or long-term field performance is established by this scoped structural evidence.
