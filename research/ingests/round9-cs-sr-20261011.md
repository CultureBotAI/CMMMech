## Selection and identity

- [Cesium](../../data/records/cesium.yaml): Endo, Karasawa and Aoyagi (2024),
  [DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-57678-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-57678-8),
  Scientific Reports 14, 7630; PMID 38561437, PMC10984957. Direct analysis of
  washed biomass and the same-strain sequential Sr comparison provide a useful,
  bounded first Cs mechanism without adopting the proposed molecular cause.
- [Strontium](../../data/records/strontium.yaml): Horiike and colleagues (2017),
  [DOI 10.1128/AEM.00855-17](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.00855-17), AEM 83(20),
  e00855-17; PMID 28802269, PMC5627000. Uninoculated controls, salinity comparisons,
  microscopy and XANES provide complementary evidence of biomineral-associated Sr.

## Cesium: observations and adversarial limits retained during curation

Endo's Methods and Figures 1–4 were read; the four downloaded main figure images
were inspected. The complete Europe PMC assets ZIP passed all member CRC checks
and contains the four main figures as JPEG/GIF. The article data statement offers
data on request; no additional experiment dataset was present in that archive.

JCM 1275 grew for 24 h at 37 degrees Celsius, statically, in the specified rich
bifidobacterial medium with 50 mM non-radioactive CsCl. Cells were pelleted,
water-washed twice, nitric-acid/peroxide digested and measured by ICP-MS.
The text reports 70.5 mg Cs/g dry cells. Mapping used a **different 100 mM** Cs
exposure. Figure 1's local Cs/P signals do not identify polyphosphate, uniquely
prove intracellular localization, or exclude surface association. There is no
Cs metabolic-inhibitor, killed-cell or genetic comparison. The record uses
`process: other` rather than selecting an established transport/sorption pathway.

Figure 3 confirms a **sequential** design: Cs-grown biomass was moved into
1 mM SrCl2 in 1 mM Bis-Tris, pH 6.8, for 3 h at 37 degrees Celsius. The final
reported contents were 55.7 mg Cs/g dry cells and 8.1 mg Sr/g dry cells. The
reported Cs decrease is 21%. There is no described matched transfer into Sr-free
buffer for the same time, so Sr-specific displacement is not isolated from
handling/incubation-associated release. Contents are not initial-feed removal
fractions, and no pure product is obtained. Figure 4 error bars are standard
errors with n=3; replicate type and exact numerical errors are not resolved.
The dry-mass conversion is stated but its drying protocol is not.

Figure 2f includes a zero-Cs growth arm and shows marked inhibition at the
100–200 mM conditions. The authors explicitly disclaim practical gastrointestinal
conditions and propose future in vivo study. No clinical recommendation, proof
of radionuclide removal in vivo, or industrial deployment is inferred.

## Strontium: observations and adversarial limits retained during curation

The full publisher text and acquired PMC HTML, main Table 1 and all main
Figures 1–6 were inspected. Europe PMC fullTextXML returned HTTP 500; the
publisher's direct HTML request returned 403 but its text was accessible through
web browsing, and PMC HTML acquired successfully. The supplement and main PDF
routes returned challenge HTML, not PDFs; publisher supplement returned 403, and
Europe PMC supplementaryFiles explicitly reported non-open-access status.
Supplementary S1–S8 images and chemical-control curves were **not** independently
inspected. The record states that access limit and uses main-article evidence.

The source assay uses 50 mL in 100 mL flasks, 3.74 g/L marine broth, 20 g/L urea,
1 mM reagent SrCl2, source-labelled 3% NaCl, 30 degrees Celsius, 120 rpm and
triplicate cultures. After centrifugation and 0.2 micrometre filtration,
ICP-AES measured soluble total Sr. A separate analytical triplicate is reported.
The plate pH is 7.6; it is not treated as a measured or maintained broth pH.
The sampling and analytical sections give 4 and 20 degrees Celsius for
centrifugation; the record preserves that unresolved procedural difference.

Text and Figure 1 support a decline to 0.13 mM on day 2 and the source's
reported bound below 1e-7 mol/L after day 4. This bound is not zero or a
100% recovered-product yield. The described negative-control medium contained
no isolate; the screening results describe little control decline, but the
main time-course panels do not overlay complete control curves. Increased
ammonium supports ureolysis as a contributor without a urease perturbation test.
Figure 2 shows much weaker removal at 5–10% NaCl than at 0.3–3%; urease-positive
isolates did not all remove Sr effectively.

Figures 3–5 show Sr/Ca-associated precipitates and reported aragonite-consistent
electron diffraction. Table 1 fits Sr XANES using SrCl2 and SrCO3 standards:
for 1 mM initial Sr, SrCO3-like fractions are 52% at day 5 and 87% at day 10.
These are **model-dependent local-environment fractions**, not phase purity or
feed recovery. Cellular P-rich dense regions are distinct from later
extracellular carbonate material; no purified strontianite product is asserted.
The chemical carbonate comparison also immobilizes Sr but uses a different
recipe; it is not a chemistry/pH-matched microbial-necessity test. Mg channels
are proposed, not demonstrated. Figure 1b shows some Mg change even though the
prose says none; the record claims stronger Sr/Ca depletion, not exact Mg
exclusion. Selected-solid EDS nondetection has an approximately 0.1% weight/weight
limit. Reagent Sr is not a demonstrated 90Sr treatment; the paper explicitly
leaves actual contaminated seawater tests for future work.

## Alternative sources and follow-up

1. [Arthrobacter KMSZP6, DOI 10.1186/s13568-016-0247-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13568-016-0247-3),
   PMC5020004: full main XML and supplement descriptions inspected. Figures and
   the five supplementary PPTX files were not fully audited. The reported
   maxima 9612 mg Cs/g dry cells and 9989 mg Sr/g dry cells are unusually high,
   but **not invalid merely because they exceed 1000 mg/g**. At the stated
   75 mM feeds and 0.28/0.25 g dry biomass/L, the nominal supplied-metal ceilings
   are about 35,600 mg Cs/g and 26,286 mg Sr/g, respectively. These are curator
   arithmetic, not observed capacities. Further review should reconstruct the
   soluble-fraction assays, independent pellet calibration, backgrounds,
   growth/dry-mass basis and abiotic controls before adding this mechanism.
2. [Halo-/metal-tolerant community study, DOI 10.3390/microorganisms10010079](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms10010079),
   PMC8780871: main XML inspected. Autoclaved biomass and filtered-supernatant
   controls are useful, but exact isolate-to-crystal mapping needs supplementary
   Table S4. Cs-rich crystal Raman/EDS and Sr-rich amorphous material need a
   separate spectral audit; Sr diffraction identifies SrCl2, while the second
   Sr/P/N/O phase is **inferred** struvite-like. No abstract-level generic
   Cs/Sr-struvite mechanism was imported.
3. [Microalgal radiocesium and magnetic separation, DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-46586-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-46586-x),
   PMC6626050: a reference lead from Endo. Full primary evidence not acquired
   in this pair; it could expand isotope/biomass/separation coverage later.
