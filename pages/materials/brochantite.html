Brochantite mineral

Brochantite is the monoclinic copper hydroxysulfate mineral Cu4(SO4)(OH)6. It is distinct from elemental copper, other copper minerals and mixed corrosion patinas. The laboratory mineral experiment below used a synthetic preparation. No criticality designation is inherited from copper.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Fungal-associated copper oxalate formation on synthetic brochantite

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Beauveria bassiana (NCBITaxon:176275) [TaxonMech]

Laboratory cultures of Beauveria bassiana grew on synthetic brochantite-containing malt agar and the authors assigned copper oxalate products. This supports a qualified culture-associated transformation and precipitation observation. Acid-mediated dissolution followed by precipitation is a proposed explanation; neither causal necessity of oxalate nor isolated-metal recovery was established.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The live, unnamed Beauveria bassiana isolate came from copper-contaminated vineyard soil at Bevaix Abbey, Neuchatel, Switzerland. The article mentions a local collection deposit but supplies no accession; the species TaxID does not identify the isolate. Synthetic substrate was prepared using cited chemical methods, exposed to UV for 30 minutes and added after medium autoclaving. Malt agar contained 12 g/L malt and 15 g/L agar; incubation was at room temperature in the dark, with numerical temperature and pH unspecified. Five-mm square fungal plugs were used. Radial growth was followed in two perpendicular directions every 3-4 days over 18 days. The methods specify two or three replicates, whereas Figure 1 specifies three with standard errors. Figure 1 labels loadings 10, 20 and 50 g/L Cu(II) compounds, while the caption and prose vary between copper, Cu(II) and compound concentrations; no elemental-copper conversion is accepted. Copper-free malt agar was the growth comparison. No matched mineral-only sterile, killed-cell, pH-matched or acid-specific control is reported for this mineral assay. The silver-arm sterile control cannot be transferred. Separate product estimates used cultures after three months, dried at 60 degrees C, ground below 40 micrometres and pressed as 800-mg samples for Cu-K-alpha XRD, with a copper oxalate dihydrate/quartz calibration.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: joseph2012
Figure 1A shows fungal growth on brochantite-containing agar across the labelled 10-50 g/L series, decreasing with loading. Figure 1B supports the authors' copper-oxalate product estimate. The ring patterns described after 18 days are a visual observation consistent with redistribution, not a measured metal recovery. Table 1 separately defines MABRO at 5 g/L brochantite; this is not the entire concentration series. The methods formula Cu4(OH)6(SO4)4 is a source error; the identity authorities establish Cu4(SO4)(OH)6.
Partial Source: joseph2012
Results page 5 assigns moolooite, CuC2O4.nH2O with n below 1, to products on all three copper media using ESEM, XRD and FTIR, partly referring to Joseph et al. 2011 for characterization. The complete 2012 article was inspected, but does not display the original powder patterns for these mineral arms. The accepted outcome is the authors' copper-oxalate product assignment, not independent confirmation of hydration, phase purity or complete substrate conversion. The related 2011 article's original graphics were not successfully acquired in this investigation and are not accepted as directly inspected evidence.
Partial Source: joseph2012
Figure 1B provides semiquantitative XRD estimates after three months. Its caption uses percentage of dry treated mycelium, while methods describe dried culture and Results use an ambiguous amount-of-copper denominator. The prose percentages are not unambiguously mapped to each mineral arm. No numerical extraction efficiency or recovered-product yield is imported; neither a copper mass balance nor purified product recovery was demonstrated.
Partial Source: joseph2012
Figure 3 and organic-acid methods concern separate mineral-free malt-agar cultures after 21 days at 8 or 12 g/L malt. Oxalic acid was detected among eleven acid standards. This supports an oxalate-producing phenotype, not proof that oxalate is the sole or necessary cause in the mineral arm. Reported production-rate units and extrapolated quantities are unresolved and are not imported or silently corrected. No industrial deployment or long-term conservation performance follows from these laboratory observations.
Context_only Source: ncbi_bassiana
The live authority resolves NCBITaxon:176275 to Beauveria bassiana at species rank. It does not verify the unnamed study isolate, strain accession or causal pathway.

Fungal-associated oxalate formation on brochantite-bearing roof coupons

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Beauveria bassiana (NCBITaxon:176275) [TaxonMech]

In a separate laboratory surface treatment, fungal growth on copper roof coupons bearing source-assigned brochantite patina was accompanied by copper-oxalate bands and crystals. This is a surface-associated transformation observation; it does not establish complete bulk mineral conversion, a metal recovery yield or durable protective performance.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The same unnamed live fungal isolate was inoculated as conidia suspended in water, at least 200 per 85-mm Petri dish. Unsterilized R1 and R2 copper roof coupons measured 0.1 by 1.5 by 2 cm. They received 2-mm layers of either malt agar (12 g/L malt, 15 g/L agar) or MABRO additionally containing 5 g/L brochantite. Incubation was at room temperature in the dark; numerical temperature and pH were not supplied. Optical microscopy and reflectance FTIR were followed weekly for three weeks. The two coupons are distinct treatment samples, not demonstrated biological replication; no matched sterile roof-coupon control is shown. Unsterilized surfaces limit attribution exclusively to the inoculated fungus.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: joseph2012
Figures 4C-D show crystals after seven days; Figure 5 shows increasing copper-oxalate FTIR bands over one, two and three weeks. The authors interpret this as conversion of the original brochantite patina. These surface spectra and images do not measure complete bulk conversion or establish an isolated product mass.
Partial Source: joseph2012
The authors report apparently greater oxalate formation with MA than MABRO, so additional brochantite did not improve this qualitative outcome. Separate liquid treatments on other copper objects produced uneven or poorly adherent deposits and were considered unsuitable for a uniform coating. Those results constrain general application claims but are not a controlled comparison on the same roof coupons. Long-term corrosion protection and industrial deployment were not tested.
