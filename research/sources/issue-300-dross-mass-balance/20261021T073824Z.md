## Conditional reconstruction and independent arithmetic

The actual Equations1–2/p5 contain an opening square bracket without its closing bracket in both PDF and XML. Let `m1,m2` be clinker and recovered washed-residue masses, and `w1E,w2E` their mass fractions of elementE. **If Mg is conserved**, then:

```
m2/m1 = w1Mg/w2Mg
eta_hat_E = 1 - (w2E/w1E)*(w1Mg/w2Mg)
percent = 100*eta_hat_E
```

This is an analyst reconstruction, not a corrected equation issued by the authors. All33 composition cells from Table1/p4 and Table4/p13 were independently visually transcribed and matched to XML. Printed sums are99.83%,99.742%,99.78% for feed, clinker, residue; they were not renormalized. The reproducible Decimal audit and raw tokens are outside Git at `S/sources-300/mass-balance-audit.json` and `audit-mass-balance.py`, referenced by checksum.

Table4 Mg gives a **conditional** `m2/m1=4.98/21.27=0.2341325811`. On a hypothetical100g clinker basis this corresponds to23.41325811g residue; it is not a measured recovered mass.

| Element | Clinker wt.% | Residue wt.% | Inferred nonresidual fraction, % | Reported, % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Al |27.06|10.64|90.79389999|90.79|
| Na |29.67|10.10|92.02986495|92.03|

Both reproduce the reported value at two decimal places. Agreement shows the denominator interpretation is arithmetically coherent; it is not an independent validation of extraction, because the same compositions define the inference. The residue still contains10.64wt.% Al and10.10wt.% Na; concentration alone is not mass recovery. Na already enters with the roasting reagent and again with liquor, so this result cannot be assigned to the2.16wt.% Na initially in the waste.

If `fMg=(m2*w2Mg)/(m1*w1Mg)`, the apparent fraction absent from the recovered residue is `1-fMg*(1-eta_hat_E)`. Mg loss, addition or nonrepresentative sampling changes the inferred result. Furthermore, apparent absence from the recovered solid does not partition dissolved material from fines, deposits, transfer losses or any volatile material; added elemental inputs also prevent unique attribution to the initial clinker. Bulk XRF/phase assignments and selected SEM-EDS spots do not supply that missing inventory.

For a leach-stage elemental balance, the required relation is `clinker_E + liquor_input_E = recovered_residue_E + final_filtrate_E + separate_wash_E + other_collected_E + unaccounted_E`, with pooled washes counted once. A whole-process balance additionally requires original feed and roast reagent inputs, atmosphere exchange where relevant, clinker transfer, dust, gas and condensate inventories. The study does not provide all these measured masses/volumes/compositions or a Mg-conservation test. Its thermal curve is not a substitute: Fig10's1.43%/8.38% labels differ from prose1.24%/8.78%, already noted in the prior review, and the TG specimen is not a closed process inventory.

N/Cl provide a further denominator diagnostic. The paper's98.93% N and97.14% Cl equal the **concentration decreases** `100*(1-0.072/6.7)` and `100*(1-0.12/4.2)` after rounding. They omit a clinker/feed mass ratio. Illustratively imposing conserved Mg from raw feed to clinker would give `m_clinker/m_feed=6.18/4.98=1.2409638554`, and conditional nonresidual N/Cl values98.66642690%/96.45438898%. These are alternative conditional calculations, not corrected measured removals or proof that Mg is conserved. Neither calculation identifies nitrogen species, quantifies collected chloride, establishes condensate purity or demonstrates environmental detoxification.
