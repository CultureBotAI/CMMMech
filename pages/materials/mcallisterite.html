Mcallisterite mineral

Mcallisterite is the Mg2[B6O7(OH)6]2·9H2O mineral species in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list, approved as 1963-012. The original 1965 description used the spelling Macallisterite and the equivalent oxide formula 2MgO·6B2O3·15H2O. Natural material from the Mott prospect, Furnace Creek Wash, California was intimately mixed with ginorite, sassolite and other impurities. Its powder pattern matched separately synthesized mcallisterite, with residual impurity peaks; the authors did not obtain an impurity-free natural powder pattern. Larger synthetic crystals supplied the single-crystal and accurate physical-property measurements. Natural chemical compositions were calculated after subtracting assumed impurity contributions, rather than measured on pure mineral. This boron-bearing species is distinct from elemental boron and the borates commodity. Criticality, microbial activity and industrial recovery are not inferred.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Laboratory water loss from synthetic mcallisterite on heating

other

A synthetic analogue progressively lost mass interpreted as water during a stepped heating experiment. This is evidence for thermal dehydration under the reported laboratory protocol, not measured boron recovery from the mixed natural material or identification of a particular dehydrated crystalline product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Schaller et al. used a half-gram synthetic sample because enough uncontaminated natural mineral was unavailable. Table 3 reports successive temperatures from 105 to 450 degrees C, holding the sample for 16 hours at each stated temperature, with an additional 16-hour hold at 280, 400 and 450 degrees C. The increments and cumulative values belong to this heating sequence; they are not independent equilibrium phase boundaries. Atmosphere, replicate count and evolved-gas analysis are not specified.

Support Source: schaller1965
Page 633 identifies the chemical-property material as synthetic. Table 3, p. 636, reports zero loss at both 105 and 115 degrees C, cumulative loss of 5.10 wt% at 130 degrees C and 22.62 wt% at 140 degrees C, reaching 35.24 wt% after the second 450-degree hold. The table's calculated total water content is 35.15 wt%. The low-temperature nulls apply to this protocol. No heated product structure or isolated-product yield was determined.
Partial Source: schaller1965
The prose below Table 3 calls the loss at 140 degrees C half the total water, whereas the table gives 22.62 wt% of the initial sample out of a final cumulative 35.24 wt%. The record retains the table values and does not adopt that fractional interpretation or infer discrete molecular intermediates.
