## Identity and source roles

The reused dated IMA list identifies Ikaite as `Ca(CO3)(H2O)6`, statusA, approval1962-005, Denmark/Greenland. Approval number and publication year are distinct. The Handbook identifies monoclinic calcium carbonate hexahydrate, space groupC2/c. These support mineral identity, not biological origin or a taxonomic assignment. Its Antarctic chemistry example partitions CO2 and H2O from weight loss using stoichiometry; it is not an assay of the tree material.

Garvie2022 is the primary field/phase study. Tollefsen2020 is a separate controlled chemical-precipitation context, not a matched sterile Garvie control. The later *Pseudogemmobacter sonorensis* lead appears in citing/indexed material but was not acquired as a complete original here; it does not identify the mineral-producing organism in Garvie's samples. A conference-program abstract and later ACC–ikaite transformation article appeared in search results but were not promoted into evidence or a same-sample join. Criticality-list classification is outside this phase/causality task; calcium membership does not confer criticality on ikaite.

## Separate abiotic context: Tollefsen2020

[Ikaite nucleation at35°C challenges the use of glendonite as a paleotemperature indicator](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-64751-5), Scientific Reports10:8141, provides a complete original chemical-precipitation experiment. It tests natural seawater mixed50:50 with carbonate/bicarbonate stocks; it is not an inoculated live/dead/sterile comparison. Grade3 filtration removes particles larger than10µm, and must not be called sterilization.

Methods on pages2–3 describe0.1M Na2CO3 and0.1M NaHCO3 stocks mixed at1:5,1:3,1:2,1:1 and3:1; stock pH at5°C is9.49,9.70,9.86,10.14 and10.59. These are distinct from the modeled mixed-solution pH values9.09,9.32,9.50,9.82 and10.31 used for the series labels. Runs span nominal5–35°C;32 experiments plus seven duplicates give39 samples. Mixing rate is0.67±0.21mL/min until approximately300mL. The mixture then spent a night at5°C, was filtered next day and dried overnight at5°C, followed by−18°C storage. A−18°C precooled holder and an11min XRD scan were used. These ex situ handling steps qualify the authors' nucleation interpretation; this assessment neither removes the published35°C result nor pretends it is an in situ time-resolved measurement excluding all cold-storage change.
