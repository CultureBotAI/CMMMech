## Source assessments

### United States: final 2025

[USGS/Department of the Interior final notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-07/html/2025-19813.htm),
Federal Register 90: 50494–50497, 7 November 2025, document 2025-19813, explicitly
contains 60 mineral commodities. It supports jurisdiction-specific, dated
criticality. Its current USGS entry point continues to identify 2025 as the list;
this does not make the designation timeless. Arsenic and tellurium remain in
the **final** list even though the draft proposed their removal. The table
includes natural/synthetic graphite context and identifies byproducts, which
do not establish microbial recovery. Barite, fluorspar, graphite, metallurgical
coal, phosphate and potash require commodity-aware identity mapping.
[GovInfo policy](https://www.govinfo.gov/about/policies) distinguishes federal
public-domain work from any third-party content. Only factual list entries,
source metadata and curator-written assessments are committed here.

### European Union: original 2024 legal instrument

[Regulation (EU)2024/1252](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ:L_202401252)
was adopted 11 April 2024 and published 3 May 2024. Original Annex II has 34 entries
on PDF pages 57–58; Annex I has 17 on page 55. This pass rechecks the previously
acquired authentic bytes, not a fresh consolidated edition. Source hashes
match `research/ingests/eu-2024-acquisition-20261009.json`.

Annex II(w) specifies **nickel—battery grade**; Annex I(l) does likewise.
Annex II(v) says **graphite**, so the older 2023 natural-only wording must not
be silently substituted. Strategic Li/Mn/graphite are battery-qualified;
boron is metallurgy-qualified. Combined bauxite/alumina/aluminium is a
value-chain designation, not proof of three equivalent identities. Article 3/4
value-chain context must be reviewed when assigning a qualified designation.
REE/PGM groups remain explicit; the earlier Commission 2017 grouping source
was rehashed and checked but not automatically projected into every new group
mapping. The earlier dossier documents reuse terms; fresh legal-notice access
was not repeated in this pass. No raw legal text is committed.

### Canada: 2024 update

[Current official list portal](https://www.canada.ca/en/campaign/critical-minerals-in-canada/critical-minerals-an-opportunity-for-canada.html)
identifies 34 materials and its 2024 update; the
[10 June 2024 announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/natural-resources-canada/news/2024/06/government-of-canada-releases-updated-critical-minerals-list.html)
provides the release date. Additions were high-purity iron ore, phosphorus,
and silicon metal. The announcement abbreviates high-purity iron in one
sentence and misspells phosphorus as phosphorous; the current complete list
and explanatory text resolve these names. Group membership for Canadian
REE/PGM designations remains to be checked at Canadian identity sources.

High-purity iron ore is a qualified ore commodity, not elemental iron;
silicon metal and phosphorus must not collapse into phosphate minerals.
[Canadian terms](https://www.canada.ca/en/transparency/terms.html) distinguish
noncommercial reproduction from commercial redistribution. No blanket open
license is assigned to the downloaded portal; the committed output is a
factual attributed assessment, not a mirror of its narrative content.

### Australia: 20 February 2024 revision

[Department authority](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/australias-critical-minerals-list-and-strategic-materials-list)
explicitly dates the revision 20 February 2024 following the
[16 February nickel decision](https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/king/media/nickel-placed-critical-minerals-list).
Thirty-one critical entries and five strategic entries were inspected.
Copper/aluminium/phosphorus/tin/zinc are **strategic** in this edition;
nickel is **critical**. The 2026 strategic reserve is not a new complete list.

Footnote 16 gives six PGEs including osmium; footnote 17 gives 16 REEs including
promethium but not scandium, which is separately listed. Those memberships
are explicitly mapped in the inventory. Fluorine is not interchangeable with
fluorspar. High-purity alumina is not elemental aluminium or a kaolin species.
The page's foreign-list comparison is dated to older sources; use each
jurisdiction's own authority instead. A guessed `/copyright` endpoint was
unavailable; reuse terms remain unchecked. No source HTML or table layout
is committed.

### United Kingdom: 2024 assessment, 2026 policy context

[BGS OR/24/047](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/538471/1/OR24047_report.pdf)
by Mudd and colleagues (2024) has 34 critical entries in Tables 1 and 11.
The report says it is not government policy; the separate
[government technical annex](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-critical-minerals-strategy/critical-minerals-technical-annex),
updated 23 January 2026, reproduces the list for policy use. The report's sodium
**compounds** qualifier is retained despite the policy page's shorter label.
Iron, borates, magnesite and natural graphite provide additional discovery
leads. Palladium falls below the assessment threshold; a generic PGM
membership must not override that finding. The glossary defines REEs as
15 lanthanoids plus Sc/Y.

The policy separately lists 23 **growth** minerals, explicitly including
copper and uranium; growth is not equivalent to critical or strategic and
has no direct current-schema CriticalityClass. Its PGM subset is Ir/Pt/Rh/Ru.
The government page declares OGL v3; the BGS PDF reserves copyright and permits
reasonable attributed extracts. Neither full text nor figures are committed.

### India: June 2023 committee list

[Ministry of Mines report](https://mines.gov.in/admin/storage/app/uploads/649d4212cceb01688027666.pdf)
provides 30 final materials on PDF page 40, with a summary on PDF page 11. The
[28 June 2023 launch notice](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1936083&lang=2&reg=48)
supports the edition. Its explicit PGE 6 and REE 17 expansions include Os and
Pm/Sc respectively; those component mappings are preserved. Cadmium,
selenium, strontium and molybdenum are useful additions to the frontier.
The source's phosphorous spelling is mapped to phosphorus with the original
label retained. The 30-member committee list is distinct from the 24 statutory
critical/strategic minerals in MMDR Schedule I Part D. Reuse license was not
resolved, and no full PDF content is committed. This is a historical list
assessment, not a current Indian-law certification.

## Limits and unresolved questions

This is an expandable frontier. China, Japan, Korea, South Africa, New Zealand
and other jurisdictions; older editions; USDOE critical **materials** lists;
all mineral-species/host associations; and primary microbial literature are
not exhausted. The six selected jurisdictions do not define all allowable
CMMMech records. Future passes should add authorities or experiments and
record new candidates, duplicates, deferred identity mappings and access gaps.
Fresh EU authority access failed; preserved bytes establish the original 2024
edition only. Canadian group definitions and some grouped/qualified commodity
mappings require further work. No global absence or completion claim follows
from this inventory. Chalcopyrite remains a separate deferred mineral-species candidate pending primary identity verification; copper authority membership does not establish it. No bulk importer or schema change is proposed.
