Spent tantalum capacitors secondary resource

Discarded tantalum capacitor components separated from electronic equipment, distinct from tantalum element, whole printed circuit boards, other capacitor technologies and a recovered tantalum product. The accepted laboratory feed comprised visually identified surface-mounted capacitors manually removed from spent mobile phones and milled. One primary study supports qualified base-metal mobilization by acidophile cultures and a fungal-culture filtrate, while tantalum was not detected in the reported leachates. It does not establish industrial deployment, pure feed phases or isolated-metal recovery.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Acidophile-associated base-metal mobilization from capacitor scrap

bioleaching Microbial

An actively growing mixed iron- and sulfur-oxidizing culture was associated with mobilization of several base metals from milled capacitor scrap into sampled liquor. Biogenic ferric iron and acid generation are proposed contributors, alongside externally maintained acidity. The experiment does not isolate each consortium member, establish a unique molecular pathway or demonstrate recovery of purified metals. Tantalum nondetection is a condition-specific limitation, not proof that every microbial route fails or that a purified tantalum residue was produced.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The source names Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, Leptospirillum ferrooxidans and Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans without strain accessions, relative abundance or member-specific tests; no taxon identifiers are assigned to this unresolved experimental mixture. Visually identified capacitors were removed manually and ground to 0.5 and 0.75 mm fractions. Table 1 reports bulk elemental analysis after an HF-containing microwave digestion, not mineralogical phase identity or proof of device purity. Laboratory assays used 250 mL flasks, 100 mL active modified 9K culture, 2% w/v scrap, 30 degrees C and 150 rpm for ten days. Activation medium included sulfur and ferrous sulfate; the final iron concentration for the capacitor arm is not unambiguously specified by the separate PCB iron-series design. Added iron prevents attributing all liquor iron to the scrap. Sulfuric acid maintained pH 1.8 as required. Trials were described as triplicate with uninoculated medium controls. Every second day, 1 mL of 0.45 micrometre filtered liquor was taken for analysis; sterile water replaced sampling and evaporation losses. The sentence describing sample sterilization is ambiguous. Bulk digestion and leachate analysis used ICP-OES; no numeric tantalum detection limit or complete metal mass balance is supplied.

Fungal-culture filtrate-associated mobilization compared with spores

bioleaching Microbial

A separately grown fungal-culture filtrate produced greater reported base-metal mobilization from capacitor scrap than the source's spore treatment under the tested conditions. The low spore response and tantalum nondetection are retained. The filtrate was not demonstrated sterile or chemically characterized sufficiently to assign the effect to a particular acid, enzyme or metabolite. Lower metal release with spores does not by itself establish absence of growth or universal fungal intolerance to electronic waste.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The paper identifies the fungus as Aspergillus niger DSM-2182, while the current DSMZ catalogue assigns DSM 2182 to Aspergillus tubingensis. The experimental culture was not sequence-verified in the paper, so the historical label and present authority are kept distinct and no unqualified species identifier is assigned. Cultivation was described in a sucrose-containing mineral medium at initial pH 5.6 plus or minus 0.1, 30 degrees C and 150 rpm for 48 hours, followed by filtration. The paper does not give the culture-filtration pore size or prove removal of viable cells. Separate filtrate and spore treatments used 0.75 mm capacitor scrap at 2% w/v in 250 mL flasks, 30 degrees C and 150 rpm over twenty days; a separate fungal working volume and inoculum quantity are not explicitly specified. A comparator was adjusted to the same initial pH with HCl; its composition and arm-specific quantitative metal results are incompletely documented. pH was measured every five days, and endpoint liquor was analyzed. No isolated-product yield, residual Ta purity, matched final-pH comparison or industrial operation is shown.

This documents present nomenclatural context without proving experimental strain identity or transferring the assay to every member of either species. The fungal and acidophile arms share one publication and feed preparation, not two independent replications of one mechanism.
