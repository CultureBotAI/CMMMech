Cinnabar mineral

Cinnabar is the trigonal mercury sulfide mineral alpha-HgS, distinct from cubic metacinnabar, hypercinnabar, elemental mercury and unspecified Hg-S particles. The September 2026 IMA list gives grandfathered status but an unknown original-description year; no approval accession is inferred. The curated studies concern laboratory mercury mobilization and volatilization from source-assigned cinnabar, not microbial formation of the species or isolation of a recovered product. No criticality designation is inherited from mercury or sulfur.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Enhanced aqueous mercury mobilization in oxic community microcosms

bioleaching Microbial

Living biofilm material from the New Idria acid mine drainage system enhanced aqueous mercury release from source-assigned cinnabar relative to gamma-inactivated biomass and uninoculated controls under oxic laboratory conditions. The mixed community contained iron- and sulfur-oxidizing taxa, but neither a particular organism nor direct cell-surface sulfur oxidation was established as the cause. Indirect iron cycling and nonmetabolic organic ligands remain alternatives.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The August 2008 experiment used commercial Alfa Aesar cinnabar, reported as greater than 99.99 percent pure, with uncontrolled particle size. Mineral solids and killed-biomass controls received 18 hours of cesium-137 gamma irradiation. Each 250 mL flask received 2 g mineral and filtered New Idria drainage water at pH 3.25; oxic reactors contained 150 mL, whereas anoxic reactors and abiotic controls contained 100 mL. Inoculated reactors received 100 microlitres of living or irradiated biofilm, corresponding to 7.88 or 8.12 micrograms protein. Incubation was unshaken at 21 degrees C for 30 days oxically or 18 days in an anoxic glove box. Anoxic medium was initially unpurged and vessels were removed for sampling. Mercury samples were filtered at 0.02 micrometres, BrCl-preserved and measured by cold-vapor atomic fluorescence; this is operationally filtered total Hg, not independently speciated Hg(II) or an isolated recovery yield. No causal taxon or strain identifier is assigned to the unresolved consortium.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: jew2014
Results, Hg concentrations, and Figure 2 (pages 25-26) show higher filtered Hg in the live oxic cinnabar series than the corresponding killed series. The reported abiotic series declined below detection within nine days but is not plotted in Figure 2. Figure 4 separately shows Fe(II) depletion in the live cinnabar arm. These observations support community-associated mobilization, without establishing which coupled chemical or microbial reaction caused it. The large concentrations reported for calcine and metacinnabar are not transferred to cinnabar.
Partial Source: jew2014
Materials and Methods, Sample preparation (pages 22-23), describes bulk XRD and peak matching, then reports mineralogy for the mixed tailings and calcine. No cinnabar-feed diffractogram or quantitative polymorph refinement is displayed; the commercial name and stated purity are not independent proof of phase purity after irradiation. This qualified feed assignment does not identify every HgS particle in the separate mixed-waste experiments as cinnabar.
Partial Source: jew2014
Figure 2 reports error of plus or minus eight percent with n=3, while Methods describes 25 total microcosms across five substrates and five conditions without specifying independent reactor replication. Analytical repeat measurements cannot be assumed to represent three biological reactors. Different solution volumes, slightly different added protein amounts, undefined particle size and irradiation-altered biomass limit quantitative attribution. Filtration reduces colloids but does not establish a dissolved-ion mass balance or exclude particles smaller than the pore size.
Partial Source: jew2014
Figure 3 (page 26) shows a small initial Hg pulse followed by decline in the anoxic cinnabar series. Residual oxygen and sampling outside the glove box qualify a strict oxygen-necessity interpretation. Discussion, HgS dissolution (page 29), reports no Hg(II) increase for an isolated Thiomonas strain in separately prepared laboratory medium; this null does not prove inactivity in the complete drainage community. The Fe/S-cycling cartoon in Figure 6 is explicitly hypothetical, and no causal mutant or enzyme test is presented.
Partial Source: jew2014
The text on page 29 derives an activity quotient using the metacinnabar day-three sample and compares it with idealized equilibrium constants. Its orders-of-magnitude solubility claim is not a measured cinnabar recovery factor. Page 25 describes abiotic Hg declining below detection, whereas pages 28-30 discuss release in abiotic and killed oxic controls and say they closely mirrored each other; the absent plotted abiotic Hg series leaves this wording conflict unresolved. No environmental flux, industrial deployment or successful detoxification is established.

Culture-associated mercury mobilization and volatilization from cinnabar

redox transformation Microbial

The historically identified Thiobacillus ferrooxidans strain SW9K1 produced more elemental mercury in headspace and more mercury in solution than the mercury-sensitive strain 13661 in laboratory suspensions containing source-assigned natural cinnabar without added iron. The headspace also exceeded an uninoculated comparison. These observations support culture-associated mobilization and reduction to volatile Hg(0), without establishing cinnabar as an energy source or identifying the dissolution reaction, causal enzyme or recovered product.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The page 774 cinnabar-only assay used 1.5 g mineral in 50 mL of 9K salts minus iron, a final cell concentration of 10^8 per mL, and incubation at 29 degrees C and 250 rpm. After two days, compressed air removed accumulated Hg(0); the vessel was then sealed for 15.5 hours before headspace analysis. The 150 mL headspace measurement concerns this closed interval, not cumulative release over the preceding incubation. The feed was natural material from Abbadia San Salvatore, Tuscany, ground below 74 micrometres and autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 15 minutes. SW9K1 is retained as the source's strain label; no sequence, deposit accession or causal molecular identifier is assigned.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: baldi1987
Results, page 774, reports 39 ng Hg(0) in the SW9K1 headspace, less than 1.4 ng for strain 13661, and a trace of about 1.3 ng above uninoculated solution. Solution mercury was 6.8 ng/mL for SW9K1 and 2.1 ng/mL for 13661; no corresponding numerical uninoculated liquid value is stated. Gas mass and liquid concentration are different assay fractions. The trace and detection-limit wording are retained without treating either as zero.
Partial Source: baldi1987
Materials and Methods, page 772, reports greater than 95 percent HgS and no detectable pyrite, marcasite or elemental sulfur by XRD. No diffractogram, quantitative polymorph refinement or analysis after autoclaving is displayed; the residual fraction is unspecified. The cinnabar-only recipe omits added iron, but residual iron and inoculum carryover were not quantified. Page 773 explicitly describes washing the column-leaching inoculum; the page 774 cinnabar-only assay does not separately restate that preparation. There is no reported killed-cell comparison for this assay.
Partial Source: baldi1987
Chemical determinations on page 773 describe cold-vapor and purge-and-trap/GC-AA methods, but the cinnabar-only liquid result does not define a filtration fraction or independently resolve dissolved mercury species. The prose gives no replicate count or dispersion for these gas and liquid values. Prior gas was purged, and residual solids, biomass-associated mercury and all output fractions were not balanced; no extraction percentage, isolated recovery yield or environmental flux follows.
Partial Source: baldi1987
Results on pages 774-775 states that none of the strains could be maintained with cinnabar as the sole energy source. Table 1 reports only slight SW9K1 oxygen uptake with cinnabar, 14 plus or minus 1 microlitres per hour per 10^9 cells, versus zero for BA-4 and 13661. The table defines standard deviations but does not state replicate counts. Growth, energy conservation and direct sulfide oxidation are not established by Hg(0) detection. The proposed constitutive reducing system derives from a separate soluble-mercury challenge; no SW9K1 enzyme or genetic test establishes the cinnabar pathway.
Context_only Source: baldi1987
Figures 1, 3 and 4 and Results on pages 773-774 concern separate pyrite-cinnabar mixtures with added ferrous iron. Column and shaken flask inhibition patterns differ. Column gas traps sampled different total air volumes for inoculated and uninoculated systems, and column liquid mercury remained below its stated detection limit. These mixed-feed observations are not substituted for the cinnabar-only gas and liquid comparisons.
