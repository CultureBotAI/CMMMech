## Boron

[Record](../../data/records/boron.yaml) ·
[CREATE history](../../history/records/boron/2026-10-11T072436Z-codex-b9ef43.yaml)

The fresh [original EU Regulation 2024/1252](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1252/oj)
HTML was inspected, including Annex II section 1(g), unqualified **boron**.
Annex I section 1(c) separately qualifies strategic boron as metallurgy grade.
The record adopts only the original 3 May 2024 critical designation, not a
current consolidated-law claim or a designation of every borate.

The selected primary is [Moraga et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jece.2017.06.044),
*Journal of Environmental Chemical Engineering* 5:3373–3383, PMC5828252.
The publicly accessible accepted manuscript was acquired from CONICET and
cross-checked with PMC HTML and DOI metadata. Its manuscript disclaimer and
unknown blanket redistribution permission are retained; full text is not
committed. EPMC fullTextXML returned HTTP 500, while the CONICET PDF and PMC
HTML succeeded. No separately linked supplement was located in the inspected
PDF and PMC page; that is a bounded source-page observation.

Methods sections 2.1–2.8 and Results 3.1–3.3 were read. Figures 2–9 and Tables
1–2 were visually inspected; Figure 1c–d was inspected visually and all of its
caption/text was read. The selected microscopy stage is 72 h at 30°C and
250 rpm in minimal medium with nominal 20 or 40 mM boric acid. The post-addition
pH values were 5.58/5.44. Cells were fixed, ethanol dehydrated, critical-point
dried and Au/Pd coated. Figures 3–4 and Table 2 show B/O-containing accretions
associated with prepared *Streptomyces* 053/128 cultures. No-boric-acid culture
controls lack the illustrated accretions. These observations justify the narrow
culture-association claim with `process: other`.

Several source limitations materially constrain it:

- A boric-acid-containing uninoculated medium processed identically is not
  described. Raw solid boric acid in Figures 6–7 is a different comparator.
  Biological necessity and preparation-independent precipitation remain open.
- Table 2's title says atomic percentage, its column says Weight (%), and its
  oxide-normalized composition includes B2O3. That calculation cannot identify
  a pure boron-oxide phase. No diffraction-based identity is supplied.
- The Figure 1 caption equates 20/40 mM with 1.22/2.78 mg/L, while the plotted
  axis carries a factor of 10³. The stock arithmetic also differs: 0.84 or
  1.67 mL of 440 mM in 20 mL implies 18.48/36.74 mM before inoculum, rather
  than exactly 20/40 mM. The study's nominal concentrations are retained.
- Methods 2.4 calibrate 0.25–2 mg/L but do not specify the sample dilution
  that would reconcile that scale with the nominal feeds. Table 1 removal
  percentages and Figure 2 capacities/rates are therefore not adopted.
  There is no solid-phase mass balance linking solution depletion to the
  illustrated accretions.
- Figures 8–9 show unidentified protein bands; the authors' proposed transport
  response is not an identified causal pathway. No soil-treatment or
  industrial deployment result is asserted.

The 2009 Miwa/Fujiwara study
[10.1111/j.1747-0765.2009.00402.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1747-0765.2009.00402.x)
remains a lead: indexed text is available, but direct primary PDF acquisition
returned 403 and the complete protocol/figures were not inspected here.
OSTI 1051297 acquisition failed (connection reset); no finding is inferred
from that failure.

## Fluorine

[Record](../../data/records/fluorine.yaml) ·
[CREATE history](../../history/records/fluorine/2026-10-11T072439Z-codex-95b027.yaml)

The official [Australian list](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/australias-critical-minerals-list-and-strategic-materials-list)
was read through the web provider after three direct acquisitions failed
(two timeouts and a closed connection). Its stated edition is the **20 February
2024 update**. Table 1 explicitly names **Fluorine**; footnote 13 explains that
the US and EU list fluorspar. Only the Australian designation is adopted; no
cross-jurisdiction element/commodity substitution is made. There is no invented
raw-page checksum for this provider-only successful inspection.

The primary is [Dodge et al.](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.02785-23), mBio
15(1):e02785-23, online 8 December 2023 and January 2024 issue, PMC10790756.
Main XML/HTML, figures and a valid EPMC supplemental ZIP were acquired.
The ZIP's DOCX was parsed, including all figure captions and sequence/table
text. Main Figures 2–4 and supplemental Figures S1–S5 were visually inspected.
The main article is CC-BY-4.0; ASM's supplemental-material notice retains
copyright with the authors and directs reuse requests to them. All raw
artifacts are kept outside Git. A nominal DOCX download from PMC was actually
HTTP 200 challenge HTML; the publisher route returned 403. Neither failure
was mistaken for inspected supplementary content.

Table 2 and Methods directly support the chosen two 20 mM racemate assays.
They report 9.9 ± 0.1 mM released fluoride for alpha-fluorophenylacetic acid
and 9.9 ± 0.2 mM for 2-fluoropropionic acid, with times to maximal OD600 of
29 and 78 h. These are three biological replicates plus a stated independent
repeat with similar results. They are approximately **one half of the total
racemic fluorine**, not complete feed conversion. The record keeps enzyme
and culture assays separate, including enzyme-free controls, species versus
recombinant strain, and general 28/30°C flask methods versus a specifically
30°C plate assay.

Figure 3's culture-residue polarimetry and purified-enzyme lactate product
analysis support selective hydrolysis of S substrates. Computational docking
is not crystallographic or mutational proof. Table 1's below-0.01 activity
results for selected difluorinated substrates and a fluoroolefin constrain
substrate breadth. Table 2's lower final OD600 and Figure 2F's transient
colony-count decline constrain tolerance claims. No purified fluoride product,
elemental fluorine, ore processing, universal PFAS degradation or industrial
performance is asserted.

The following discrepancies and limits were preserved or excluded from claims:

- The introduction once prints 12663, while the Methods, Results, figure
  captions and culture authority identify ATCC 12633.
- Methods point to supplemental Figure S9 for codon-optimized genes, but the
  acquired DOCX ends at S7, which contains those sequences. GenPept
  BAC81979.1 independently identifies a 294-aa haloacetate dehalogenase from
  *Delftia acidovorans* strain B, originally *Moraxella* sp. B. The record
  does not claim that the native accession identifies the recombinant vector.
- Supplemental S4 shows lower fluoride release with 5 mM NaF, although the
  main text characterizes inhibition as minimal. The record makes no claim
  of absent product inhibition.
- S3's substrate concentration wording differs from the main morphology
  description; no numerical morphology result is imported.
- CrcB conservation and another strain's prior knockout are not a causal
  transporter perturbation in this recombinant culture. No shared-expression
  database analysis is needed to establish the two measured transformation
  assays; no iModulon or transcriptomic claim is adopted.
- Wild-type nongrowth does not by itself quantify abiotic fluoride release
  in culture flasks. Neither intracellular fluoride concentration nor an
  isolated product mass was measured in the curated assays.

[Zhou et al. 2017 fluorapatite dissolution](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70195843)
is a useful next primary lead, but only its USGS abstract/metadata was
inspected. The strain/feed methods and figures require acquisition before
adding that mechanism. Fluoride-precipitation papers discovered in the search
are also leads, not extra accepted mechanisms.
