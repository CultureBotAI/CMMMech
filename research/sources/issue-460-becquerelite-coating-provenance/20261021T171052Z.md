## 2006 original: synthetic surface experiments and the proof note

Page 1212 specifies 0.31 g uranyl acetate, 0.05 g calcium carbonate and 4 mL ultrapure water in a 23 mL Teflon-lined vessel at 160 °C for 50 h, followed by rapid washing and air drying. Four or five crystals, with basal dimensions spanning 0.04 × 0.06 to 0.2 × 0.3 mm, were placed in 1 mL solution. These dimensions bound individual planar faces, not measured total reactive surface area or weighed mineral loading.

The pH 2 arms lasted 30 min at unspecified numerical room temperature. They used HCl alone, 1.0 mol/L NaCl or KCl, or 0.5 mol/L BaCl2, SrCl2, MgCl2 or Pb(NO3)2 in acidic solution. The paper calls the electrolyte arms equal in cation normality, not equal in ionic strength. Table 1 distinguishes measured pH from calculated ionic strengths and saturation indices. High-ionic-strength pH uncertainty is approximately ±0.2. Separate exposures used water for 24 h and HCl pH 3.5 or carbonate pH 10.5 for one hour. Crystals were washed and dried before ex-situ AFM, SEM and optical examination; the paper expressly says it did not perform in-situ dissolution experiments.

Figures 4 and 6–9 show condition-dependent pit morphology and orientation. Figure 5 shows hillocks after the water/weak-acid/carbonate exposures. The page 1219 proof-stage note attributes hillocks to precipitation of a uranium-enriched phase on the basis of later XPS and in-situ AFM. It limits any earlier interpretation based only on residual dissolution relief. Neither a pit shape nor the presence of a hillock is a dissolved-uranium inventory, isolated-product yield or inhibition percentage.

Complete Table 1 rekey preserves eleven rows, including two carbonate concentrations, and leaves three unspecified concentration cells blank. The two carbonate rows print the same ionic strength 0.2 despite 0.1 versus 0.5 mol/L concentrations; HCl pH 2 prints 0.001. These are retained as source-model outputs, not repaired by a presumed electrolyte calculation. Table 2's seven ion rows expand to ten coordination-specific radius entries; all ten printed absolute differences from the 1.12 Å Ca radius recompute exactly. This agreement does not independently verify the cited Shannon radii or the proposed adsorbed cation arrangement.

Figure 3d labels a 0.758 nm vertical difference, while page 1212 gives half of the 14.930 Å cell repeat as about 7.46 Å. Exact half-cell arithmetic gives 0.7465 nm, approximately 1.54% below the image label. Figure 5d labels 5.180 nm and the prose rounds it to 5.2 nm; dividing by the half-cell repeat gives about 6.94 equivalent repeats. These are conditional geometric comparisons, not assignments of a pure secondary phase or a measured coating mass.
