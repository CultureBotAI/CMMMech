## Source assessments

Gilkes and Palmer (1983) uses hydrothermal gels at 200 degrees C for two weeks; major series phases contain minor boehmite and substituted apatites. The Sr axis is starting-gel composition, not measured mineral occupancy. Flowing-air thermal analyses used different sample masses for TGA/DTGA and DTA, with systematically different peaks. The six-hour furnace series applies only to crandallite. Approximately 80% dehydroxylated at 450 degrees C and 6% remaining up to 500 degrees C are source solid-transformation statements, not extraction yields; no closed mass balance is reported. Table I is prior literature, not independent new experiments. No fertilizer or industrial efficacy is inferred from the introduction.

Kato (1987) characterizes a natural North Groton crystal from Smithsonian specimen NMNH 141049. Its 417-reflection refinement and hydrogen-related structural graphics support goyazite identity. It is distinct from synthetic endmember preparations and provides no microbial, extraction or recovery experiment.

Bermanec et al. (2012) identifies crandallite in mixed pink/mixed phosphorite by diffraction, microscopy and local composition. Native saprophytes did not significantly increase in the pink fraction during the four-day assay; the suggested aluminium effect was not isolated. Crucially, the A. junii ICP-MS and polyphosphate images are explicitly Ervenik_white, predominantly fluorapatite. Later hydroxylapatite attribution and the blank-description ambiguity remain visible. None of these results establishes crandallite-specific dissolution or formation. The source-reported DSM 1532 stock is not independently authenticated.
