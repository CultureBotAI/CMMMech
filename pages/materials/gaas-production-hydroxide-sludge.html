GaAs wafer-production hydroxide sludge secondary resource

Heterogeneous metal-hydroxide sludge generated by neutralizing waste streams from industrial gallium arsenide wafer production. The curated evidence concerns the sludge supplied for Fritze et al. (2026), not crystalline GaAs, a pure iron mineral or all semiconductor wastes. Table 1 reports 32.81% Si, 6.97% Fe, 1.21% Ga, 1.34% As, 0.27% P and 1.83% Ca by handheld XRF; Section 2.1 separately reports 0.538% fluoride by ion-selective electrodes. These are the study's reported composition percentages, not a complete phase analysis or a specification for every sludge batch. No elemental identifier or constituent criticality designation is assigned to this waste. The gallium and arsenic records describe constituent outcomes from the same experiment; they are not independent replications.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Sulfur-oxidizing culture leaching of GaAs-production hydroxide sludge

bioleaching Microbial

Associated Organisms: Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans (NCBITaxon:930) [TaxonMech]

Living Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans cultures produced acidity during laboratory leaching of the sludge, with gallium, arsenic and iron mobilized into solution and some dissolved ferric iron reduced to ferrous iron. Acid dissolution accounts for much of the gallium release; additional microbial facilitation remains provisional. The proposed involvement of sulfur intermediates in iron reduction is not a demonstrated direct gallium or arsenic redox reaction. Dissolution does not establish selective separation or purified-product recovery.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
The study used type strain DSM 14887, adapted to 5% w/v sludge with aluminum; NCBITaxon:930 anchors the species, not a strain-specific taxon. Sludge was dried at 60 degrees C for 48 hours and mortar-homogenized. Section 2.1 describes the iron phase as amorphous ferrihydrite, while arsenate association and gallium association with iron are assumed or expected rather than measured speciation. The selected assay used 3% w/v sludge in 50 mL shake-flask setups, HBS minimal medium, 1% elemental sulfur, initial pH 1.8, 30 degrees C and eight days. Table 2 specifies 12.9 mM aluminum, molar Al:F 1.5:1, and 1 mL sulfuric acid for initial acidification. Aluminum was added to counter fluoride inhibition. Section 2.3 reports 10% inoculum as w/v; this unusual basis is retained. Biotic setups were triplicate. Uninoculated controls included an initial-pH negative control and a comparator acid-adjusted in response to the biotic pH. Dissolved Fe, Ga and As were measured by ICP-MS. Main-assay shaking speed and sampled-liquid separation are unresolved in Section 2.3. Industrial sludge provenance does not establish industrial deployment of the microbial process.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: fritze2026
Sections 2.1-2.3, Tables 1-2, Section 3.2 and Figures 2-3 support the feed-specific laboratory process. The source reports final leached fractions of 94% Ga, 68% As and 73% Fe at 3% sludge, versus 77% Ga, 66% As and 62% Fe in the acid-adjusted abiotic comparator. These percentages refer to each element's leaching yield, not a total sludge recovery fraction or isolated-product yield. Figure 3 shows biotic means and SD; numeric SD values are not inferred from pixels. Figure 2 supports incomplete ferric iron reduction in the leachate.
Partial Source: fritze2026
Section 3.2 reports 86% gallium leached within the first day and attributes rapid release chiefly to low pH. It calls the abiotic controls simplified and requests replicated confirmation of microbial facilitation; Figure 2 displays nonidentical biotic and abiotic pH trajectories. The small arsenic endpoint difference does not establish a meaningful microbial advantage. Supplementary Figure S2, a separate 5% sludge assay, shows final biotic arsenic leaching below its acid-adjusted comparator; no numeric value is digitized here. These results do not support a universal microbial enhancement across loadings, elements or waste batches.
Partial Source: fritze2026
The abstract's Al:Fe wording conflicts with the methods' Al:F; the selected flask dose follows Table 2 rather than the separate 1.4:1 optimization/reactor setting. Section 3.4 reports faster chemical processing at higher solids loading and discusses further iron reduction, silica removal and downstream precipitation as process-development issues. No selective microbial product isolation, product purity or commercial microbial operation is established for this feed.
