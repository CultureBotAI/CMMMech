Powellite is the calcium molybdate mineral species Ca(MoO4), listed as grandfathered in the September 2026 IMA-CNMNC list. It is distinct from elemental molybdenum, wulfenite and an uncharacterized calcium molybdate precipitate. Sidorenko and colleagues (2008) examined black Ca-Mo-rich grains from the Bota-Burum Mo-U deposit, South Kazakhstan. Powder diffraction and infrared spectroscopy supported crystalline powellite in the uranophane-beta-associated material. Chemically similar grains from the uramarsite-associated material were optically isotropic and X-ray amorphous, with no characteristic powellite infrared bands; the authors interpreted these as metamict powellite. That interpretation is kept distinct from the directly characterized crystalline material. Radiation-induced alteration and the origin of the black colour were inferred, without a controlled irradiation test. The article's page 29 discussion is internally inconsistent about retention versus loss of anisotropy and diffraction in the silicate assemblage; the explicit characterization on pages 23-27 anchors this limited record. These observations establish neither a microbial transformation nor a mineral recovery yield. Criticality is unrecorded.
