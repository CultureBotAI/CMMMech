## Decision and material boundary

**Defer Jiang et al. (2010) as a record source until complete primary methods and original phase/control evidence are available.** The title is *Biological nano-mineralization of Ce phosphate by Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, Chemical Geology 277(1–2):61–69, [DOI 10.1016/j.chemgeo.2010.07.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemgeo.2010.07.010), PII `S0009254110002603`. Crossref, publisher API metadata and both authors' institutional listings agree on the publication identity. These are multiple access routes to one experiment, not independent replication.

The abstract and JAEA outreach identify a monazite-like structure; this is not the rhabdophane assignment anticipated by the previous frontier. The September 2026 IMA master, original PDF pages 1 and 146 inspected, lists monazite-(Ce), `Ce(PO4)`, status `Rn` (renamed), with `1966 s.p.` describing a special nomenclatural procedure rather than an approval-number CURIE. The dated authority establishes the species' existence; it does not establish the identity of these laboratory precipitates. No mineral identifier is minted from that year, no hydration is inferred from nominal CePO4 or EDX, and the cerium element's criticality is not transferred.

## Phase qualification and causal limits

[Ochiai and Utsunomiya 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/min7050084), PDF page 2, discusses earlier P-free microbial nanocrystal studies and says diffuse diffraction plus amorphous material prevented complete particle identification. References 20 and 21 on page 18 explicitly identify Jiang 2010Ce and Jiang 2012Yb. Page14 reports no initial monazite in the authors' separate room-temperature synthetic experiments and discusses rhabdophane and amorphous phases. This is relevant counterevidence to confident species assignment, **not a formal correction/retraction or proof that the earlier yeast particles were rhabdophane**: the experimental systems differ. The earlier abstract's assignment and later qualification must be reconciled against original 2010 diffraction indexing, reference patterns and preparation/beam effects before adopting a mineral owner.

The institutional abstract gives pH 3/4/5 and a cell-surface precipitation interpretation. Those are discovery clues, not a substitute for verified biomass state, strain, loading, medium, temperature, replication, phosphorus balance or time-series values. JAEA outreach describes an abiotic phosphate-precipitation comparator and future actinide applications. Without the complete methods, neither comparator matching nor active metabolism, viability, phosphate transport, enzymatic cause, purified product yield, REE selectivity or industrial deployment is established here. No abstract-derived measurement is placed in a record.

The fresh [NCBI 4932 page](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=4932&mode=Info) identifies *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* at species level. It cannot identify the experimental strain; that remains unavailable. The IMA PDF is reused, not freshly downloaded: its source is the issuing [September 2026 master](https://cnmnc.units.it/files/editor/IMA_Master_List_(2026-09).pdf), original retained copy `/private/tmp/cmm-round38-katsarosite-research/raw/ima2026.pdf`, SHA256 `2257c653491b346669ee5d38c98226a2bddbec0562a3675043730a01ca146973`.
