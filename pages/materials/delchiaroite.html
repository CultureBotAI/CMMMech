Delchiaroite mineral IMA: Dch

IMA-approved copper iodide-methanethiolate mineral (IMA 2025-076; symbol Dch in Section 1, p. 154; status A, Approved 2025; full description published 1 April 2026; no separate symbol column is printed in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 55) with ideal end-member formula Cu3I(CH3S)2 (requiring Cu 46.30 wt%, S 15.57 wt%, H 1.47 wt%, C 5.83 wt%, and I 30.82 wt%, whose rounded two-decimal values sum to 99.99 wt%; Section 4.2, p. 158; Biagioni, Sejkora, Moelo, Criscuolo, and Dolnicek, 2026, European Journal of Mineralogy 38:153-167). Constitutes the first natural iodide-methanethiolate and first natural copper iodide-organochalcogenide (the 13th known organic Cu-bearing mineral and the first containing S as an essential constituent; Table 8, p. 164), belonging chemically to the pseudo-binary system CuI-Cu(CH3S) at a 1:2 ratio (Abstract, p. 153, and Section 4.5, p. 164). Named in honour of the mineral collector Lorenzo Del Chiaro (born 30 April 1951) for his contribution to the knowledge of the mineralogy of the cavities of the Carrara marbles and the Alpi Apuane metamorphic complex, including providing type material for carraraite and moeloite, the specimen of robinsonite used for its structure solution, and co-authoring the 2019 monograph on Carrara marble vug mineralogy (Abstract, p. 153, and Section 1, pp. 154-155). Discovered in a specimen collected in May 2025 during quarry exploitation by the quarryman Paolo Cagnoni at La Piana quarry (latitude 44 deg 04 min 41 sec N, longitude 10 deg 08 min 23 sec E; Figure 1, p. 155), Colonnata marble basin, Carrara, Apuan Alps, Massa-Carrara Province, Tuscany, Italy (Sections 1-2, pp. 154-155, and Section 4.4, pp. 162-163). Type material is represented by two cotype specimens: one specimen (a marble fragment with supergene minerals and the single crystal used for the X-ray diffraction study; referred to as "Holotype specimen" in the Figure 2 caption, p. 155) is kept in the mineralogical collection of the Museo di Storia Naturale of the University of Pisa, Via Roma 79, Calci (Pisa, Italy), under catalogue no. 20083, and the second cotype specimen (polished grain used for chemical analysis) is stored in the National Museum in Prague, Czech Republic, under catalogue no. P1P 29/2025 (Section 1, p. 154). Hosted in vugs of the Lower Liassic Marble Formation of the Apuane Unit (greenschist facies metamorphism, peak T = 350-450 deg C during the ~27 Ma Oligocene-Miocene D1 and D2 tectonic events, with late-stage extension veins formed at T = 250 deg C and P = 0.2 GPa), where delchiaroite formed as a very late-stage low-temperature (T < 50-70 deg C) supergene alteration product of Cu+-bearing enargite through interaction with halogen- and methanethiolate-bearing meteoric fluids percolating along karstified fractures, associated in type material with light-blue lavendulan, native copper, theisite, green zincolivenite, and altered enargite on calcite (Section 2 and Figure 2, pp. 154-155; Section 4.4, pp. 159, 162-163). Occurs as light-yellow acicular crystals elongated on [010], up to 0.1 mm in length, transparent, with a light-yellow streak and a greasy lustre, non-fluorescent under short- and long-wavelength UV radiation, soft (hardness not determined owing to the paucity of material), and brittle with a perfect {100} cleavage parallel to the crystal elongation observed by SEM and explained by interlayer van der Waals stacking along a (Abstract, p. 153; Section 2, pp. 154-155; Section 4.3, p. 158). Calculated density, based on the ideal formula and single-crystal XRD unit-cell parameters, is 3.565 g cm-3 (Section 2, p. 155). In reflected light on polished grains embedded in epoxy, delchiaroite is light gray with abundant yellow internal reflections (which likely mask pleochroism and bireflectance) and moderate anisotropism with gray tints; reflectance values (Rmin / Rmax in %, measured in air with a Tidas MSP400 spectrophotometer on a Leica microscope, 100x objective, WTiC Zeiss no. 370 standard; Abstract, p. 153, and Section 2 and Table 1, p. 155) at the four COM wavelengths are 20.6 / 22.2 (470 nm), 20.9 / 23.4 (546 nm), 20.7 / 23.3 (589 nm), and 20.1 / 22.4 (650 nm) (full 400-700 nm dataset in 20 nm steps in Table 1, p. 155, where at 400 nm the column labelled Rmax is 18.5% and Rmin is 18.8%, crossing over at 420 nm where both are 19.7%; Gladstone-Dale mean refractive index could not be calculated due to the lack of a parameter for (CH3S)-). Micro-Raman spectroscopy (Horiba Xplora on confocal Olympus BX41 microscope, 532 nm laser, 100-4000 cm-1; Section 3.1, p. 156; Section 4.1, p. 157; Figure 4, p. 160) shows C-H stretching bands of CH3 groups at 2902 cm-1 (strong) with weaker bands at 2809 and 2974 cm-1 (and no O-H bands), symmetrical CH3 bending at 1308 cm-1, weak CH3 rocking modes at 945 and 1082 cm-1, C-S single-bond stretching at 685 cm-1, weak Cu-S bands at 332 and 464 cm-1, weak peaks at 196 and 239 cm-1 labelled in Figure 4 (p. 160), and a strong Cu-I/lattice band at 129 cm-1 (comparable to 122 cm-1 in marshite CuI and 128 cm-1 in siidraite). Quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron-microprobe analyses (Cameca SX 100 at the National Museum, Prague; 15 kV, 2 nA, 2 um beam diameter, using a 20 s time-dependent protocol in five 4 s intervals extrapolated to time 0 to correct for S volatilization under the electron beam, with PAP matrix correction; average of n = 12 spot analyses; Section 3.2, p. 156; Table 2, p. 157; Section 4.2, pp. 157-158) yielded Cu 47.27 wt% (range 44.68-48.92, SD 1.57, standard chalcopyrite), I 31.09 wt% (27.47-33.64, SD 1.90, standard Tl(Br,I)), S 14.90 wt% (13.96-16.16, SD 0.69, standard chalcopyrite), Ccalc 5.58 wt% and Hcalc 1.40 wt% (calculated from stoichiometry), Total 100.24 wt% (exact sum 47.27 + 31.09 + 14.90 + 5.58 + 1.40 = 100.24 wt%), giving on the basis of Cu + I + S = 6 apfu the empirical formula Cu3.07(8)I1.01(7)(CH3S)1.92(8) (printed without uncertainties as Cu3.07I1.01(CH3S)1.92 in the Abstract, p. 153, and corresponding to an atomic Cu:I:S ratio of 3.04:1:1.90 vs ideal 3:1:2 in Section 4.3, p. 159). Crystallizes in the orthorhombic system, centric space group Pmmn (#59; after initial solution in acentric Pmn21 gave a 0.5(5) racemic twin ratio and PLATON confirmed a centre of symmetry), with single-crystal X-ray diffraction unit-cell parameters (Bruker D8 Venture diffractometer, Photon III CCD detector, microfocus MoKalpha radiation, lambda = 0.71073 A, T = 293(2) K, 1326 reflections above 20sigma(I) with 4.813 deg < 2theta < 44.56 deg; crystal size 0.075 x 0.020 x 0.015 mm; Section 3.3, pp. 156-157, and Table 3, p. 158) a = 16.924(10) A, b = 4.099(2) A, c = 5.572(3) A, V = 386.5(4) A3, Z = 2 (Gandolfi-like microfocus CuKalpha powder XRD refined from 26 indexed reflections with UnitCell gave a = 16.8721(19) A, b = 4.0992(4) A, c = 5.5841(9) A, V = 386.21(6) A3; Section 3.3, p. 157, and Table 7, p. 160). Anisotropic crystal structure refinement in Shelxl-2018 (with CH3 modelled via AFIX137 and H positions fixed, and six most disagreeable reflections with Fo2 > Fc2 from misaligned grains omitted) converged to R1 = 0.0962 for 360 unique reflections with Fo > 4sigma(F) and R1 = 0.1120 (wR2 = 0.2557, Gof = 1.156, Rint = 0.1227) for all 456 unique reflections out of 6453 measured reflections and 27 parameters (removing H atoms gives R1 = 0.0968; Section 3.3, pp. 156-157, and Table 3, p. 158). In the novel layered crystal structure (Section 4.3, pp. 158-159, Tables 4-6, pp. 158-159, and Figures 5-6, pp. 161, 163), chains of trigonal-planar Cu(1) (4f, Cu(1)-S x 2 = 2.234(3) A, Cu(1)-I = 2.617(4) A, BVS = 1.01 vu) and distorted tetrahedral S(Cu3C) (4f, BVS = 2.11 vu) running along b and decorated by CH3 groups ([Cu(1)CH3S]0) are linked along a through distorted tetrahedral Cu(2) (2a, Cu(2)-S x 2 = 2.267(7) A, Cu(2)-I x 2 = 2.861(4) A, BVS = 0.96 vu; short Cu(1)-Cu(2) = 2.872(3) A and Cu(1)-Cu(1) = 2.898 A contacts) into [Cu(1)2Cu(2)(CH3S)2]1+ ribbons, which are connected along c by rows of tetrahedrally coordinated I atoms (2a, average <Cu-I> = 2.739 A, BVS = 0.88 vu) to form electroneutral [Cu3I(CH3S)2]0 {100} layers stacked along a via van der Waals interactions (interlayer H...S = 3.11 A; C-S = 1.80(3) A; C BVS = 4.24 vu using an ideal C-H distance of 1.079 A vs 5.44 vu from the artificially short fixed C-H distances of 0.95(3)-0.97(2) A). The seven strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines highlighted in bold in Table 7 [dobs in A (Iobs, dcalc in A, Icalc) hkl; p. 160, noting a column-transposition typo in Table 7 for the four consecutive rows dobs = 2.362, 2.310, 2.289, and 2.222 A where the dcalc and Icalc columns are swapped, printing 13 under dcalc and 2.363 under Icalc for the bold dobs = 2.362 A reflection] are 8.46 (100, 8.46, 100) (2 0 0), 3.989 (9, 3.984, 8) (1 1 0), 3.366 (12, 3.370, 19) (4 0 1), 3.315 (19, 3.316/3.302, 6/12) (3 1 0, 0 1 1), 2.362 (10, 2.363, 13) (5 1 1), 2.049 (7, 2.049, 7) (0 2 0), and 1.947 (9, 1.951, 12) (7 1 1). Although the authors note that the (CH3S)- methanethiolate groups probably have a biological/organic origin (from overlying Tuscan Nappe sediments, organic-rich soils, or marine aerosols) and may represent a kind of biosignature (Section 4.4, pp. 162-163, and Section 5, pp. 164-165), no organism-specific microbial extraction, transformation, or recovery experiments are reported in the type description.
