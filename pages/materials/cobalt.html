Cobalt (Co), atomic number 27. This element-level record uses the ChEBI cobalt-atom identifier for chemical identity. Cobalt-bearing cathodes and mixed battery black mass are material forms and secondary resources, respectively; neither is a mineral species or synonymous with the element. The dated US designation maps the listed cobalt commodity to this element record; it does not designate every cobalt-bearing mineral or waste stream.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Acidophilic consortium bioleaching of battery black mass

bioleaching Microbial

An adapted Lake Hromnice acidophilic consortium mobilized cobalt from pretreated spent nickel-manganese-cobalt lithium-ion battery black mass. Sulfur oxidation supplied acidity; iron cycling may also contribute. This is community-level laboratory evidence, not a pure-strain mechanism or industrial deployment.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Two-step flask assay: 10% (v/v) inoculum, 50 mL FeS medium (50 mM ferrous sulfate; 1% w/v elemental sulfur; initial pH 2.0), seven-day preculture, then 1% w/v black mass for seven days at 30 degrees C and 150 rpm. Feed pretreatment included discharging, dismantling, thermal treatment, crushing and sorting. The original enrichment was dominated by Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans (95%); Alicyclobacillus comprised 3%. Post-adaptation composition was not established here. Adaptation used dissolved-metal mixtures in three 14-day stages; Table 2 doses do not consistently match Table 1 black-mass equivalents, limiting reproducibility.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: lalropuia_2024
Sections 2.1-2.5 and 3.1-3.3; Figures 4-6: dissolved Co peaked by day 2 and was higher with adapted culture than non-adapted and sterile-medium controls. ICP-OES measures mobilization, not isolated product recovery. The abiotic control differed in pH and iron speciation, so it does not isolate acidity from other microbial contributions.
