Chromite beneficiation tailings

Mixed residual mineral solids from physical beneficiation of chromite-bearing ore. The curated 2018 case is fresh superficial tailings collected in May 2017 at a beneficiation plant in Sukinda valley, Odisha, India, with a separate stockpiled-tailings core used for chemical extraction comparisons. Neither constituent criticality nor industrial microbial processing is inferred from these feeds' compositions or industrial origins.

Chromite ore processing residue

Chromium-bearing waste from chemical processing of chromite ore, distinct from the chromite mineral species and physical-beneficiation tailings. The curated case is a cementitious, high-lime-process residue from a Glasgow waste-fill site that received material from the Rutherglen Chromite Processing Works. High-lime processing oxidized ore Cr(III) to soluble Cr(VI), followed by aqueous extraction. The 2015 study sampled field-moist material from an exploratory borehole during the M74 motorway project and initially identified it tentatively as COPR from its pale granular appearance and high-pH yellow leachate. Subsequent XRF and XRD characterized a mixed residue rather than proving a pure feed. XRD identified cementitious and hydrated phases, including calcite, portlandite, brownmillerite, pyroaurite, hydrogarnet and brucite; remnant chromite was not detected by that assay. Table 4 reports 3.8 wt% Cr in the XRF composition, without loss-on-ignition correction; this is neither a mineral fraction nor a field-wet feed concentration. This record does not confer a constituent element's criticality designation on the residue or assert commercial microbial processing.

A processed, mixed mineral residue remaining after ammonium-sulfate extraction of ion-exchangeable rare earths from weathered ion-adsorption ore. Theisen sludge

Fine-grained flue-dust residue from top-gas scrubbing during copper-shale smelting in a blast furnace, also called Theisenschlamm. The resource scope here is the historical processing waste represented by the Helbra mono-landfill in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany. It is distinct from unprocessed copper shale, a mineral species, an elemental commodity and a prepared leaching solution. Meschke et al. (2016) sampled material at a depth of 4 m in May 2015. They measured Cu, Mo, Pb, Sn, Sb and Zn by energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence and Ag, Co, Ge and Re in an aqua-regia extract by ICP-MS. Their Figure 5A identifies zinc and lead as major measured components and reports other measured constituents; the two analytical fractions are not interchangeable measures of complete extractability. This sampled-feed characterization does not establish uniform composition throughout the landfill or a recoverable reserve. The same paper's membrane experiments used prepared Ge/Mo/Re solutions, rather than the sampled sludge or its actual leachate, and its Figure 2 reproduces bioleaching-solution information attributed to Klink et al. (2016). Those distinct evidence streams do not establish a newly verified microbial mechanism, isolated-metal yield or industrial recovery process for this record. No criticality designation is inherited from constituent elements.

Tungsten skarn-processing tailings

Residual mineral solids generated by processing tungsten-bearing skarn ore. The curated case is historical tailings in the Smaltjärnen repository at the closed Yxsjöberg tungsten, copper and fluorspar mine in Sweden. The processing plant used gravity separation, magnetic separation and flotation. The characterized layered material contains scheelite among mixed silicate, carbonate, sulfide and other minerals; composition and particle liberation vary by sampled layer. This resource is distinct from tungsten as an element, pure scheelite, unprocessed ore, waste rock and a saleable concentrate.
