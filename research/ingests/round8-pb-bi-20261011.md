## Lead evidence and adverse direction

[Topolska et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11356-013-1968-3), published online in 2013 and in volume 21 (2014), is the principal mechanism source. Full primary XML and 11-page publisher PDF were acquired. Methods, Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–6 were read; PDF pages 6–7 were rendered and visually inspected for the pH, Pb and P plots. Exact values were transcribed from Table 3, not digitized curves. Triplicate 305 h assays compared P-rich/P-deficient medium with and without Pseudomonas putida IBPRS KKP 1136. NCBI taxon 303 resolves the reported species; it does not reidentify the numerically classified strain.

Synthetic pyromorphite was characterized by XRD, SEM-EDS and wet chemistry. At 0.05 g/100 mL, bacterial cultures increased filtered-solution Pb. The record preserves all four Table 3 Pb concentrations and their standard deviations. The separate 100 h cell-digestion fraction is kept distinct: 94% refers to released Pb assigned to cells in that comparison, not 94% of the mineral recovered. Binding can be reversible. Both the initial mineral identity and the operational 0.2 micrometre filtrate matter.

Biotic pH rose while control pH remained stable. The authors argue pH alone is insufficient but do not eliminate it experimentally. Metabolites, nutrient scavenging and sorption remain proposed contributors, not a demonstrated single enzyme or organic-acid pathway. The source reports FeEDTA as 30 mM; that unusual medium specification is retained and flagged rather than silently normalized. Laboratory release from a synthetic mineral is neither industrial recovery nor measured long-term soil failure.

[Kato et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms23052483) is retained only as contrasting context: a different isolate, KKY-29, in lead-acetate experiments produced material assigned to pyromorphite and hydrocerussite. Full XML/HTML and direct PMC Figures 6–7 were inspected; these show the authors' EDS/diffraction and mixed-phase XRD assignments. The main text conflicts on phosphate-dependent phase mapping (Results 2.6 versus Discussion); biomass units alternate mg/L and mg/mL, and phosphate concentrations/figure labels are inconsistent. The nanoparticle-removal claim and phosphate-metabolism causality are not accepted here. Supplements were linked but not acquired, so their quantitative panels were not assessed. This deposition result under different conditions cannot establish that microbial effects always immobilize lead or negate the controlled remobilization experiment. No fabricated refutation label is used.

## Bismuth evidence and denominator

[Dodge and Wackett 2005](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.71.2.876-882.2005), PMC546758, provides complete primary methods/results via downloaded PMC HTML. Direct deposited Figures 2–4 were downloaded and visually inspected. The PMC PDF routes returned HTML rather than a PDF; Europe PMC XML returned HTTP 500; publisher HTML returned 403. These failures do not limit access to the inspected full HTML but are recorded to avoid claiming a PDF was read.

Fusarium sp. BI was selected from sludge by growth on bismuth subsalicylate. [ENA AY725264.1](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/view/AY725264.1) explicitly links the isolation context, same DOI and taxon 292829; [NCBI](https://api.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/v2/taxonomy/taxon/292829/dataset_report) retains the informal Fusarium sp. BI name. Its database species-rank field does not resolve a named biological species. Sequence similarities to other Fusarium taxa do not justify substituting them.

The 1.5 mM suspension was incubated in phosphate-buffered minimal medium at 30 degrees Celsius. The soluble-Bi time course used a 0.2 micrometre filtrate and uninoculated control. The approximately 75% figure applies only to initial soluble Bi, not to all Bi in the nominal 1.5 mM feed. Figure 2 lacks error bars and the assay method does not state replication. There is no total mass balance, isolated yield or quantitative claim of metabolic necessity.

At 96 h, TEM-EDS located Bi/P-rich granules inside cells, alongside extracellular Bi-rich material interpreted as residual feed. Salicylate-grown controls also had granules but no detected Bi. The beam was larger than individual granules. At 72 h, Bi co-fractionated with long-chain and granular polyphosphate; Figure 4 gives standard deviations of three replicates, without proving biological replication. Association is supported; molecular Bi-polyphosphate coordination, mineral identity and a causal tolerance mechanism are not established. Bi oxidation state was not measured. Salicylate-catabolism enzyme assays address the organic ligand and are not evidence of a bismuth reductase.
