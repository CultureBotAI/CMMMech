Studtite mineral

Studtite is the uranyl peroxide hydrate mineral (UO2)(O2)(H2O)2·2H2O, distinct from the lower hydrate metastudtite, elemental uranium, uranyl oxide hydrates and uranium commodities. The September 2026 IMA list gives grandfathered status G; 1947 denotes the original-description year, not an approval accession. Burns and Hughes determined the monoclinic C2/c structure using natural Krunkelbach material from museum specimen RC4372. The peroxide-linked uranyl chains contain coordinated water and are connected through hydrogen bonding involving interstitial water. Natural identity, synthetic thermal treatment and surface alteration of synthetic U3O8 are separate evidence populations. No criticality, microbial involvement or industrial deployment is inferred.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Abiotic thermal dehydration of synthetic studtite

other

Heating synthetic studtite produced metastudtite and subsequently X-ray-amorphous uranium-bearing products. Observed transitions depended on measurement times and heating history. Loss of interstitial water and an inward-moving reaction front are structural and kinetic interpretations, not direct imaging of a unique molecular pathway or universal transition temperatures.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Spano et al. combined equal volumes of 0.5 M uranyl nitrate and 30 percent H2O2, added dropwise, then vacuum-filtered the settled precipitate, rinsed it with three 50 mL portions of deionized water and dried it in ambient conditions. PXRD and Raman samples were heated in air from approximately 25 to 250 degrees C, with observations every 5 degrees C. Nominal rates were 1, 2, 5 and 10 degrees C/min. The one-hour PXRD scans reduced effective rates to 0.075-0.080 degrees C/min; Raman effective rates were 0.830, 1.419, 2.470 and 3.283 degrees C/min. Separate approximately 25 mg TGA samples used continuous argon, a 30-minute equilibration at 25 degrees C, and uninterrupted nominal heating rates.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: spano2020
Proof pages D-F and H-L, Figures 3, 5-7, 10-11 and 14 support the studtite-to-metastudtite sequence. PXRD-based partial least squares regression placed the product-dominant crossover near 45-50 degrees C under long measurement dwell times; Raman crossovers were 62.1-67.6 degrees C across the nominal rate series. These are model-derived crossovers, defined as more product than reactant, not first appearance or phase-pure yields. The Raman series is not strictly monotonic: its nominal 1 degrees C/min crossover exceeded the 2 degrees C/min value.
Partial Source: spano2020
Page I and Figure 11 report a first-stage TGA mass loss of 9.6 percent only at 1 degrees C/min; faster runs retained more mass. The authors interpret this as incomplete dehydration, not equal production of pure metastudtite at every rate. Mixed products persisted at 250 degrees C and their exact composition was unresolved. Figure 3 gives approximately 0.078 degrees C/min for a nominal 1 degrees C/min PXRD run, while Table 1 gives 0.075; Figure 4's caption says 44.07 degrees C although its plotted vertical line is near 47 degrees C. These discrepancies preclude an exact universal crossover from those captions.

Abiotic studtite formation during peroxide exposure of U3O8

other

Studtite became the principal identified alteration product on synthetic U3O8 surfaces after an early metastudtite-rich stage. Dissolution and reprecipitation were proposed to explain this succession; direct solid-state rehydration of isolated metastudtite was not demonstrated.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Chamberlain et al. exposed separate approximately 50 mg alpha- and beta-U3O8 powders to 50 mL solutions with measured initial H2O2 concentrations 0.16 M, 0.0030 M or 0.000054 M. Beakers prepared in ambient atmosphere were sealed with parafilm between sampling. Water-only U3O8 and peroxide-only controls were included. Test strips indicated approximately pH 6.5; a numerical incubation temperature was not specified. Approximately 1 mg solid aliquots were air-dried for at least four hours before Raman and SEM analysis at ten time points from day 1 through day 83. Fifteen randomized Raman spots per sample are subsamples, not fifteen independent reaction vessels.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: chamberlain2025
Figure 4 and Tables S-2-S-4 and S-6-S-8 show metastudtite as the largest alteration-phase contribution on day 1 and studtite as the largest from day 8 onward in the peroxide-added arms. U3O8 itself often remained the largest total spectral component, especially at low peroxide; this is not complete bulk conversion. Table S-2 illustrates the fitted normalized surface-spectral contributions for alpha-U3O8 in 0.16 M H2O2: metastudtite 0.49734 on day 1 and 0.0225 on day 8, with studtite 0.04102 and 0.85713, respectively. These are PLSR contributions, not mass fractions, recovery percentages or independent yields.
Partial Source: chamberlain2025
Pages 10-11 limit the analysis to surfaces because bulk PXRD of each reacted aliquot was not feasible. Pages 15 and 21-22 propose dissolution/reprecipitation but do not isolate its contribution from continuing U3O8 alteration. Air drying and model-reference selection also limit an in-solution phase assignment. Tables S-5 and S-9 contain small nonzero fitted alteration components in water controls; SEM showed no corresponding obvious morphological alteration. Residual spectra include an unidentified feature near 620 cm-1 in fewer than ten reported spectra. Accordingly, the observations narrow any universal irreversibility claim without proving direct reversible hydration or an industrial process.
