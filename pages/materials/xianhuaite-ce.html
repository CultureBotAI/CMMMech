Xianhuaite-(Ce) mineral

Xianhuaite-(Ce) (IMA 2024-091, symbol Xhu-Ce) is an IMA-accepted potassium-cerium-niobium oxide mineral with September 2026 IMA-CNMNC Master List ideal formula K2CeNb5O15 (PDF p. 236, status A), representing the first naturally occurring mineral with a tetragonal tungsten bronze (TTB)-type framework structure and the natural analogue of room-temperature centrosymmetric synthetic K2CeNb5O15. Described by Yang, Xue, Yang, Sun, Li, Yu, Yan, Liu, Zhao, Meng, Chen, Chen, Liu, Yan, Hou, Ao, and Wang (2026) from two specimens in the giant Bayan Obo REE–Nb–Fe deposit, Baotou, Inner Mongolia, China (named in honor of Academician Xianhua Li; holotype specimen GMCTM2024012 deposited in the Geological Museum of China, Beijing, and cotype specimen BYEB-3 deposited at the Crystal Structure Laboratory, China University of Geosciences, Beijing, pp. 449–450): (1) sample BY-E2024-103 from the East Orebody (41°48'01" N, 109°59'39" E), occurring as black-brown megacrystalline aggregates at the contact between aegirine-type and dolomite-type Nb–REE–Fe ores in association with fersmite and fergusonite-(Ce); and (2) sample BY-EC2024-14 from the East Contact Zone (41°48'47" N, 110°01'27" E), occurring in dolomite-type Nb–REE–Fe ore quantified by TIMA as dolomite (71.89 vol%), magnetite (12.19 vol%), fersmite (8.00 vol%), calcite (2.69 vol%), columbite-(Fe) (1.72 vol%), xianhuaite-(Ce) (0.40 vol%), aeschynite (0.37 vol%), fergusonite-(Ce) (0.28 vol%), kinoshitalite (0.21 vol%), pyrochlore (0.19 vol%, preserved as relict inclusions within fersmite and xianhuaite-(Ce)), phlogopite (0.18 vol%), barite (0.15 vol%), apatite (0.10 vol%), chlorite (0.09 vol%), biotite (0.05 vol%), forsterite (0.02 vol%), and other minerals (1.47 vol%), recording fluid-mediated Nb remobilization during Permian granitic skarnization (pp. 450–451, 453–454, Fig. 1). Occurs as irregular granules or tetragonal prisms from 0.03 x 0.03 x 0.1 mm to 0.4 x 0.4 x 0.6 mm; dark brown to black-brown in hand specimen and pale yellow-brown to brownish-red in transmitted-light thin fragments, with white streak, adamantine lustre, non-fluorescence under 365 nm UV, no magnetism, brittle tenacity with conchoidal fracture, perfect {001} cleavage (no parting), Mohs hardness 5–6 (VHN200 = 469.5 kg/mm2 mean, range 448.7–490.2 kg/mm2), Dcalc = 5.23 g/cm3, Gladstone–Dale calculated refractive index Ncalc = 2.22, and grey reflected-light color with air reflectance Rmin–Rmax (CRAIC 20/30PV Pro, 6 spots across 6 grains, p. 450, Fig. 2) of 17.6–18.5% (470 nm), 17.4–18.5% (546 nm), 17.3–18.3% (589 nm), and 17.2–18.4% (650 nm). Micro-Raman spectroscopy (532 nm laser, WITec 300R, 50–4000 cm-1, Section 5, pp. 452–453, Fig. 3) shows no peaks above 1000 cm-1 and exhibits diagnostic TTB modes at ~853 cm-1 (NbO6 octahedral vibrations), 645 and 537 cm-1 (Nb–O stretching), 175 cm-1 (internal NbO6 mode), and 135 and 81 cm-1 (external cation modes). Mean of 10 WDS spot analyses on a single grain (JEOL JXA-iHP200F, 15 kV, 20 nA; Section 4, pp. 450, 452, Table 1) yielded Nb2O5 65.45 wt% (range 64.38–66.47, SD 0.65, KNbO3 standard), BaO 11.19 (8.96–12.28, SD 1.02, barite), Ce2O3 6.65 (6.33–7.07, SD 0.28, H2CeO5P), K2O 6.28 (5.97–7.03, SD 0.32, KNbO3), La2O3 5.26 (4.78–5.82, SD 0.32, H2LaO5P), Fe2O3 1.18 (0.67–1.74, SD 0.33, magnetite), CaO 0.91 (0.67–1.13, SD 0.14, apatite), Nd2O3 0.75 (0.63–0.83, SD 0.07, H2NdO5P), TiO2 0.63 (0.44–1.21, SD 0.25, rutile), Na2O 0.31 (0.26–0.35, SD 0.02, jadeite), Pr2O3 0.28 (printed range 0.00–0.23, where the printed mean 0.28 wt% exceeds the printed upper range bound 0.23 wt%, SD 0.14, H2PrO5P), MgO 0.17 (0.09–0.27, SD 0.06, forsterite), SrO 0.16 (0.09–0.23, SD 0.04, celestite), Ta2O5 0.08 (0.00–0.20, SD 0.09, LiTaO3; rounds to 0.003 apfu on 15 O and is omitted from the printed empirical formula), total 99.30 wt% (ideal K2CeNb5O15 requires Nb2O5 72.01, Ce2O3 17.78, K2O 10.21, total 100 wt%), giving on 15 O apfu the empirical formula A1(K1.30Ba0.69Sr0.02)Sigma2.01 A2(Ce0.39La0.31Nd0.04Pr0.02Ca0.15Na0.09)Sigma1.00 B(Nb4.75Fe3+0.13Ti0.09Mg0.04)Sigma5.01 O15 and simplified formula (K,Ba)2(Ce,La)(Nb,Fe)5O15. Tetragonal, space group P4/mbm (#127, mean |E2 - 1| = 0.925; non-centrosymmetric P4bm refinement gave R1 = 0.0197 without improving the model), Z = 2 (Sections 6 and 7.2, pp. 449, 452–458, Tables 2–7, Fig. 4): single-crystal MoKalpha XRD refinement on a 0.02 x 0.02 x 0.01 mm crystal fragment (Rigaku XtaLAB PRO-007HF, 293 K, 427 reflections with I > 2sigma(I) out of 480 independent and 4897 measured, Rint = 0.036, R1 = 0.0189, wR(F2) = 0.042, S = 1.11, SREF formula K1.285Ba0.715Ce0.812Ca0.188Nb4.947Fe0.053O15, Mr = 972.07, Table 3, p. 455) gives a = 12.5355(4) Å, c = 3.9213(2) Å, V = 616.19(5) Å3, with corner-sharing NbO6 octahedra at B1 (2c, Nb0.947(15)Fe0.053(15), <B1–O> = 1.964 Å, BVS = 5.17 v.u.) and B2 (8j, Nb1.00, <B2–O> = 1.975 Å, BVS = 5.15 v.u.) forming c-axis tunnels with five-membered rings hosting 9-coordinated A1 sites (4g, K0.642(3)Ba0.358(3), <A1–O> = 2.949 Å, BVS = 1.21 v.u.) and four-membered rings hosting 12-coordinated A2 sites (2a, Ce0.812(4)Ca0.188(4), <A2–O> = 2.678 Å, BVS = 2.63 v.u.); MoKalpha rotation-mode powder XRD refinement (Rigaku XtaLAB PRO-007HF, Chekcell, Section 6.1, pp. 452–453, Table 2, p. 454) gives a = 12.5458(8) Å, c = 3.9279(1) Å, V = 618.24(9) Å3, with seven strongest observed lines at 2.785 (100)(420, 311), 3.208 (61)(211), 3.034 (53)(410), 1.960 (44)(620, 002, 441), 2.931 (40)(330, 221), 3.469 (28)(320), and 3.915 (25)(001) Å. Distinct from fersmite, columbite-(Fe), fergusonite-(Ce), aeschynite, pyrochlore, and elemental niobium, cerium, or lanthanum; no criticality designation for xianhuaite-(Ce) itself, no microbial or biological mechanism, and no industrial recovery yield are asserted.
