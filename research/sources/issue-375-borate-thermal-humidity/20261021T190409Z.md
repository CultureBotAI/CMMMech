## Nishiyasu 2024: three separate experimental arms

The institutional manuscript pp.7–10 and typeset candidate p.534 agree on commercially supplied Wako guaranteed-reagent borax and recrystallization from saturated aqueous solution at 293 K. Their three protocols are not interchangeable:

| Arm | Source-defined protocol | Defensible result and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| DSC | Approximately 10 mg each of borax and α-alumina reference in flat-bottomed aluminum pans; cool to 143 K at 10 K/min, then stated heating range 143–323 K at 10 K/min under Ar 50 mL/min | Manuscript p.12/candidate p.535 and Figure 1 report onset/maximum 331/349 K and 383/408 K. Both maxima exceed the printed upper range. No alternate ceiling or instrument program is inferred |
| Synchrotron powder XRD | 0.6841(1) Å radiation, 0.5 mm beam, 0.7 mm capillary with one end fully open; ambient atmosphere; 303–473 K by 10 K; five-minute hold and three-minute exposure at each stage | Figure 2 shows a temperature sequence, not independent replicate samples. Borax peaks remain almost unchanged through 353 K, weaken above 363 K and disappear at 403 K; weak tincalconite peaks appear above 353 K and persist to 473 K. No phase mass fraction, isolated product or equilibrium boundary follows |
| Single-crystal XRD | Approximately 0.1 × 0.1 × 0.05 mm recrystallized crystal, 0.7 mm capillary and nitrocellulose adhesive; N₂ cryostat with stated ±1 K accuracy; 113–323 K by 30 K; 720 frames at five seconds each; crystallinity lost at 333 K | Eight temperature models of the protected-crystal experiment; no refined product above 323 K and no direct tracking of a specific FBB into a product crystal |

The authors attribute the powder/DSC difference to open versus semi-open conditions. That interpretation does not repair the separate DSC methods/results contradiction. Figure 1 visibly extends beyond 400 K and labels both maxima, but the axis does not identify the intended instrument upper setting. The manuscript and typeset candidate retain the same conflict; neither is a formal erratum.

Table 1's rounded endpoints reproduce the reported expansion coefficients: approximately 8.0417×10⁻⁶, 5.3337×10⁻⁵, 8.6117×10⁻⁶ and 7.8354×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹ for a, b, c and volume, respectively. The b direction dominates. Between 293 and 323 K, printed a, c and volume decrease slightly while b increases; those differences are not independently significant measurements without the original uncertainty/covariance treatment. The source's attempted occupancy refinement is described with the unusual literal “±1.0” wording; the deposited model rows list occupancy `1` without per-site occupancy uncertainties. Neither is a quantitative water-loss measurement.

Figure 5d labels the lower gray hydrogen-bond series O4···H6B, whereas the corresponding manuscript p.15/candidate prose names O4···H6A. The existing owner only uses the separate O4···H7B interpretation. No choice between the discrepant H6 labels is adopted. The electronic-structure calculations and shared FBB geometry support an author interpretation of rigidity; they are not atom tracking, a measured transformation yield, or proof of energy-free conversion.

## Liu and Bish 2020: the humidity sequence remains qualified

The complete accepted manuscript identifies natural qilianshanite from LCIT, decomposed in laboratory air for about 2.5 months to tincalconite plus nahcolite. The documented laboratory humidity varied over that period. The residue was tested by RH-XRD at 23±0.5 °C, increasing RH from 10% to 90% and then decreasing to 10%, in ten-percentage-point increments, with a 2.5-hour delay at each setting. The scan covered 2–70° 2θ in 0.02° steps at one second per step (pp.8, 11–13).

The nominal sequence contains 17 stages, consistent with the source's continuous-run description. It is one rise/fall sequence, not 17 cycles or 17 independent samples. A conditional schedule calculation gives 3,400 angular intervals, about 0.944 h nominal scan exposure, and about 58.56 h for 17 repetitions of delay plus exposure. Endpoint convention and instrument overhead are not included; this is not an observed elapsed time or evidence that each stage equilibrated. The separate 17 gravimetric sulfate experiments and 13 sulfate RH-XRD experiments in Tables 2–3 are not replicate counts for this borate residue.

Figure 6 and the new publisher JPEG show the same plotted phase-label arrangement. Six named 2θ labels were rekeyed: tincalconite 10.10, 20.26 and 30.59°, and borax 18.24, 31.56 and 34.80°. These labels and qualitative intensities do not provide phase fractions. The source says borax first appears near 80% RH on the upward leg, and tincalconite disappears by 90%. On drying, the Figure 6 caption says borax does not completely disappear until 40%, but the adjacent p.32 prose explicitly lists borax as present through 40%. Figure 7 schematizes the downward change at 50%, and the conclusion brackets 40–50%. The isolated publisher JPEG supplies no caption or stage-by-stage raw log to resolve those statements. No exclusive 40% endpoint is chosen.

Nahcolite remains present throughout and is omitted from the plotted labels for clarity. A borate phase's disappearance therefore does not establish a chemically pure bulk product. Table 1 lists the decomposition-product tincalconite for RH-XRD only. The authors explicitly defer detailed borate thermal analysis because the residue is mixed with bicarbonate/carbonate (p.34). The qilianshanite heating experiment, its two heating rates and mixed-sample TG losses cannot be transferred to a pure tincalconite yield, thermal water inventory or energy-storage performance claim.

The qilianshanite null-result checks in Table 4 remain separate arms. Their listed measurement times sum to 36.22 h; the stated 50.52 h includes time outside the diffractometer. The two dry repeated-scan rows total 18.98 h, with no reported phase change. Those observations concern qilianshanite under that schedule, not equilibrium of the subsequent mixed residue. No raw balance, humidity log, quantitative diffraction refinement, independent repeated cycle or final-version caption clarification was acquired.

## Ruhl 2008 as a separate comparator

Thesis pp.22–27 describe >99% synthetic borax and tincalconite prepared by one month over saturated LiCl at 11.30% RH and 298.15 K. Phase purity was assessed by XRPD. Samples of roughly 0.2–0.4 g were exposed to salt-buffer humidities; the 298.15 K experiments ran for four weeks, while the higher-temperature experiments ran about four days. These are different specimens and residence times from Liu's 2.5-hour RH steps.

Table 3-1 (p.30) brackets the 298.15 K transition at 52.98–64.92% RH. Its midpoint/half-width are 58.95±5.97 percentage points, consistent with the thesis's approximately 59±6% interpretation. The 313.15 and 328.15 K brackets are 71.00–74.68% and 80.70–89.19%; the 338.15 K borax bound is `NA`, not zero. Figures 3-2 and 3-3 show gradual mass changes and mixed borax/tincalconite in partially transformed sample X, supporting the thesis's kinetic qualification. This comparator does not settle the 2020 40% caption, establish that 2.5 hours is sufficient, or supply a universal equilibrium boundary.

The thesis's printed formula weights conditionally imply about 25.19% loss on the initial borax denominator and 33.68% gain on the initial tincalconite denominator. Those percentages use different starting masses. The associated water-formula interpretation is not adopted as a nomenclature correction, and the separate #376 scope is preserved. The thesis's other calorimetry and model conclusions are outside this assessment.
