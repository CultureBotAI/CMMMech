Liguowuite mineral

Liguowuite (IMA 2020-097) is an IMA-accepted monoclinic tungsten trioxide mineral of the perovskite supergroup, non-stoichiometric perovskite group, and the first natural member of the A-site vacant single-oxide ([]BO3) perovskite subgroup (Nickel–Strunz 04.EA.10, Dana 04.05.01.02), with September 2026 IMA Master List ideal formula WO3 (status A, PDF p. 125) and empirical formula W1.00O3 on the basis of O = 3 (Xue et al. 2022, pp. 95, 98, 102–103). WDS electron-microprobe analysis (15 spots on 4 grains at 15 kV, 10 nA, 5 um beam; Table 1, p. 98) gave WO3 99.23 (range 98.19–99.93, SD 0.56), CaO 0.06 (n.d.–0.11, SD 0.03), Na2O 0.04 (n.d.–0.10, SD 0.03), TeO2 0.03 (n.d.–0.14, SD 0.05), K2O 0.01 (n.d.–0.05, SD 0.02), Al2O3 n.d. (n.d.–0.01, SD 0.00), total 99.37 wt% (ideal WO3 = 100 wt%). Described by Xue, Sun, He, Chen, and Yang (2022) from the vicinity of Nanyang village, Huaping County, Lijiang Prefecture, Yunnan Province, southern Panzhihua–Xichang (Pan–Xi) region, Southwest China (26°46'8.21" N, 101°27'13.86" E in the abstract on p. 95; printed as 26°46'18.21" N, 101°27'13.86" E in Section 2.1 on p. 96; type specimens deposited at the Museum of China University of Geosciences, Beijing, catalogue number 21K005, and the Geological Museum of China, Beijing, catalogue number M16121), where it occurs in a Neoproterozoic (Sinian) semi-weathered biotite-quartz monzonite (biotite adamellite) stock near the contact zone with Permian basites and 300 m north of a fault-hosted pyrite-quartz vein gold deposit in Permian gabbro (pp. 95–97). Closely associated with tewite (IMA:2014-053) and wumuite (IMA:2017-067a), as well as scheelite, tellurite, monazite-(Ce), allanite-(Ce), bastnasite-(Ce), xenotime-(Y), hornblende, pargasite, ferro-hornblende, annite, hydrobiotite, phlogopite, orthoclase, microcline, albite, quartz, kaolinite, ilmenite, goethite, hematite, magnetite, pyrite, zircon, zoisite, titanite, epidote, diopside, tourmaline, almandine, fluorapatite, and moissanite (p. 96). Named in honor of Prof. Li Guowu (b. 1964) of China University of Geosciences (Beijing). Forms greenish-yellow, transparent to translucent prismatic to short-columnar grains (0.05–0.1 mm in diameter) displaying triclinic pseudomorphism ({100}, {010}, {001}, and {120} forms) composed of 100–200 nm monoclinic nanoparticles with cracks and nanovoids resulting from a high-to-low-temperature phase transition (pp. 96–97, 104–106, Figs. 1–2); greasy lustre, white streak, brittle tenacity, uneven fracture, no cleavage, Mohs hardness ~3–4, no UV fluorescence, Dcalc = 7.22 g/cm3, and calculated Gladstone–Dale refractive index n = 2.24 (p. 97). FTIR micro-reflection (Fig. 3, p. 99: [WO6] stretching bands at 870 and 792 cm-1; W–O bending bands at 548, 511, 490, 464, 439, and 418 cm-1) and 532 nm micro-Raman spectra (Fig. 4, p. 99: nu(O–W–O) stretching at 806 and 716 cm-1; delta(O–W–O) bending at 327 and 272 cm-1; lattice mode at 134 cm-1) confirm the absence of O–H or H2O groups. Monoclinic, space group P21/n (no. 14, Z = 8); Rietveld powder X-ray diffraction refinement (CuKalpha, lambda = 1.5418 Å, 106 reflections in 2theta = 15–80° refined from the ICSD 16080 starting model to Rwp = 0.0604, Rp = 0.0454, chi2 = 1.707; pp. 99–101, Tables 2–5, Figs. 5–6) gives a = 7.32582(18) Å, b = 7.54767(18) Å, c = 7.71128(18) Å, beta = 90.678(3)°, V = 426.348(19) Å3, with five strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines [d in Å (I%) (hkl)] at 3.6590 (100)(-200), 3.8552 (88)(002), 3.7685 (88)(020), 2.6258 (60)(202), and 2.6928 (43)(022) (Table 2, p. 100), and corner-sharing distorted and tilted [W1O6] (W1–O = 1.7404(16)–2.1948(16) Å, mean 1.938 Å, BVS 6.033 v.u.) and [W2O6] (W2–O = 1.7322(17)–2.1763(16) Å, mean 1.931 Å, BVS 6.195 v.u.) octahedra (Glazer tilt system a-b+c-) with vacant A sites. Distinct from hydrous tungsten oxides (meymacite WO3·2H2O, hydrotungstite WO2(OH)2·H2O), anthropogenic burned-dump orthorhombic WO3 ("krasnogorite", invalid mineral name; p. 96), and elemental tungsten; no criticality designation, microbial mechanism, or industrial recovery yield is asserted.
