## Accepted realgar experiment

Chen, Yan, Wang, Li and Li (2012), *Surface alteration of realgar (As4S4) by Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans*, International Microbiology 15:9–15, [DOI 10.2436/20.1501.01.154](https://doi.org/10.2436/20.1501.01.154). All seven original pages, Figures 1–3 and Table 1 were read and viewed.

- Feed: Shimen natural powder, traditionally purified, 200 mesh (approximately 75±10 µm). Methods say 99.01% purity; results/XRD say 97 wt% realgar and 3 wt% arsenolite. These differ in stated basis and are retained, not silently reconciled. The impurity prevents assigning all dissolved arsenic to realgar.
- Organism/medium: source-assigned live BY-3 from Baiyin abandoned copper mine, reported CCTCC-M203071; inoculum density, volume and washing not supplied here. The assay used 0.5 g feed in 100 mL 9K in 250 mL flasks, 44.69 g/L FeSO4·7H2O, initial pH 1.7, 30°C, 150 rpm and 30 days. The full basal recipe is in the record. Sterile uninoculated control under the same stated conditions; at least duplicates and averages, with no quoted uncertainty for solution values.
- Outcome: page 13 reports day-15 culture peak 121.95 mg/L As versus 16.44 mg/L control (7.4-fold), then declining culture As and below-control concentration by day 25. No solution-time graph is present; these are text-reported ICP-AES concentrations. They are not cumulative extraction, isolated recovery or a closed As balance.
- Phase: Figure 2 starting XRD identifies realgar/arsenolite; treated residue is assigned realgar, dimorphite, magnetite and jarosite. Relative 43/23/6/28 wt% are not yields. Figure 3 Raman supports realgar and does not show the authors' tested pararealgar signature. No independent raw-pattern refit is claimed.
- Causality: Figure 1 surface changes do not isolate direct enzymatic attack. Iron-mediated chemistry and later adsorption/coprecipitation remain proposed; there is no contact-separated experiment, matched evolving pH, arsenolite-only control, As solid speciation or closed balance. Loose source descriptions of Fe(III) compounds are not adopted as oxidation-state determinations. Table 1's ratios are quotients of atom fractions, but its normalized formulas use those quotients as As fractions; the record does not adopt those formulas. The cited SEM preparation reference 17 concerns another system; unreported preparation is not reconstructed from it.
- Scale: laboratory only. Medical background/application claims are outside this material-mechanism curation.

## Accepted orpiment observation and excluded transfer

Liu et al. (2019), *Formation Process of the Passivating Products from Arsenopyrite Bioleaching by Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans in 9K Culture Medium*, [DOI 10.3390/met9121320](https://doi.org/10.3390/met9121320). Previously downloaded complete original PDF/XML were freshly re-read; all 11 pages, Figures 1–6, Tables 1–2 and Appendix A were viewed. This is the same study in `data/records/arsenopyrite.yaml`, not a new replicate.

Sections 3–4: natural Yaogangxian arsenopyrite Fe1.00As0.99S0.97, with trace Si, Co and Ni, was wet-milled to particles over 90% below 74 µm and loaded at 10 g/L. Cultures used 100 mL 9K plus 15 mL inoculum in 250 mL flasks at 30°C, 160 rpm and initial pH 1.8; the medium included 44.8 g/L FeSO4·7H2O. Three-month acclimation is stated; strain accession and initial cells are unavailable. Filtered residues were washed at least ten times and vacuum-dried at 35°C overnight. Figure 2 XRD/Rietveld assigns crystalline orpiment among early mixed products, with declining relative phase content. No raw diffraction refit, absolute orpiment yield, closed As balance or industrial claim follows.

Separate cubes were polished, acid/methanol/water-cleaned, fixed, rinsed and ethanol-dehydrated. Figures 4–5/Table 1 show an early dense As/S-rich film and later porous jarosite-associated deposits; drying causes cracks. Combining particle XRD and cube EDS/morphology does not uniquely locate orpiment at a causal interface. Figure 6/Table 2 present proposed reactions and a passivation interpretation. The 25°C equilibrium calculation differs from the 30°C culture; authors acknowledge early phase-prediction disagreement. Missing sterile/matched-pH controls, explicit replicate count/error-bar definition and selective film removal preclude a demonstrated microbial increment or unique passivating role.

Newman, Beveridge and Morel (1997), [DOI 10.1128/aem.63.5.2022-2028.1997](https://doi.org/10.1128/aem.63.5.2022-2028.1997), was acquired from the author's Caltech repository. All seven pages and Figures 1–5 were read/viewed. The page-2023 “Identification of precipitate” paragraph explicitly reports electron diffraction showing amorphous As2S3. Figure 2 EDS/maps establish composition, not the orpiment crystal structure. This biologically induced precipitation study is retained as context only; its removal measurements and organism are not transferred to the orpiment mechanism. The reported diffraction pattern itself is not plotted; no independent pattern assessment is claimed.
