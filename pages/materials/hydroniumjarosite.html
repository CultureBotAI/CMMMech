Hydroniumjarosite mineral

Hydroniumjarosite is the hydronium-dominant ferric sulfate hydroxide mineral, ideally (H3O)Fe3(SO4)2(OH)6. It is distinct from potassium jarosite, natrojarosite, ammoniojarosite and an industrial jarosite-bearing residue. The September 2026 IMA list records a 1987 redefinition by special procedure; this is not an individual approval accession. Basciano and Peterson (2007) characterized synthetic material and a hydronium-dominant natural sample from Rio Tinto, Spain, using chemical analyses and powder-diffraction refinement. Their natural sample 7-13-2-1 also contained quartz and minor potassium and sodium. It does not represent a pure endmember or a demonstrated microbial product. The study supports specimen-specific identity and laboratory abiotic formation, not isolated iron recovery, long-term contaminant retention, or industrial deployment of the preparation. Constituent-element criticality is not inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Abiotic hydrothermal formation of hydronium-rich jarosite

other

Basciano and Peterson precipitated a hydroniumjarosite preparation from ferric sulfate without added potassium sulfate. The broader potassium- hydronium series initially developed compositional zoning; grinding and further heating in the reaction solution homogenized selected mixed compositions. Powder diffraction and chemical analyses supported the assigned phases. This is reagent-based mineral synthesis, not a microbial experiment or an assay of recovered iron from waste.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Laboratory sample A used 40 g ferric sulfate pentahydrate in 100 mL aqueous starting solution, with no added K2SO4, at 140 degrees C for an initial 48 hours in polypropylene containers inside steel vessels. The potassium-containing series required additional equilibration; the reported 20-day sample-B and 3-day sample-H treatments are not assigned to sample A. Products were washed, filtered and dried at 120 degrees C. Alumina was deliberately added for synthetic-powder diffraction preparation; its presence in the refinement is not evidence of a synthesis impurity. The natural quartz-bearing sample was analyzed separately. No matched biological control or microbial strain was used.
