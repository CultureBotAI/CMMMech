## Experimental boundaries and analytical denominator

Sections 3.1–3.2 (18-page PDF pp. 14–15) describe sodium carbonate at 20% of concentrate mass, pellets/drying, 1050 °C reduction roasting for 60 min, cooling and grinding. Roast gas identity/flow are not specified there. The DY-8 laboratory autoclave is named, but section 3.2.2 explicitly delegates the detailed leach procedure and percentage calculation to the 2017 paper. No precise 2024 sample identifier, run ledger, analytical recovery denominator, aliquot/wash/filtration accounting, replicate count or extraction uncertainty was reconstructed.

Table 2 reports the **reduced feed**, with 5.12 wt% B2O3 and 58.23 wt% total iron. The complete ten-cell table is keyed in the companion audit. TFe and FeO are overlapping analytical categories; summing the table as disjoint components would not close a composition balance. Figure 9 identifies Fe, sodium magnesium silicate, sodium silicate and magnesium-ferrous oxide. The absence of a crystalline sodium-metaborate peak is interpreted by the authors as possible amorphous material; it does not quantify a phase fraction or uniquely identify the original boron mineral supplying solution boron.

Section 2.1.3/Figure 3 and conclusion 1 report 93.71% at 180 °C, 6 h, 8 mL/g and representative feed size 52.31 µm. That percentage remains author-reported transfer from a mixed transformed feed. If, and only if, it shares the Table 2 dry-feed denominator, multiplying 5.12 by 0.9371 would give 4.797952 g B2O3-equivalent per 100 g reduced feed, with a 0.322048 g conditional complement. These are arithmetic illustrations, not measured liquor/residue inventories, elemental boron mass or product recovery.

The 2017 preview describes Liaoning ore with TFe/B2O3/MgO/SiO2/Al2O3 values of 52.11/5.25/13.05/5.36/0.39 wt%, and an optimum involving 25 wt% sodium carbonate and 100% CO, with a reported 92.7% leach result. Those numbers describe the earlier study. Its unroasted composition differs from the 2024 transformed-feed Table 2 and does not prove a shared specimen. Its reagent dose, atmosphere, analytical protocol and liquid/solid basis must not be imported into the 2024 record merely because the latter cites it.

- **Low temperature:** section 2.1.1/p. 3 gives 72.01% and 72.39% after 6 h at 40 and 60 °C but also calls boron unextractable at 40–80 °C. Figure 1/p. 4 visibly shows nonzero extraction. Both statements persist in the duplicate representations. No corrected prose or point-level dataset was acquired.
- **Size definition:** the four grinding/sample-preparation times 5/10/15/20 s correspond in the text to 92.94/72.37/60.16/52.31 µm. Section 3.2.4 defines the representative measure as D50, while other text calls it an average. Sections 2.3.1 and 3.2.1 describe 0–74 µm or complete 0.074 mm undersize, incompatible with a 92.94 µm D50 if they refer to the same feed distribution. They may represent differing preparations, but no run-linked explanation was acquired.
- **Distribution check:** section 2.2 gives 97.17% below 80.96 µm and a 60.16 µm representative size for the 15 s sample; Figure 6a's plotted cumulative midpoint visually occurs well below 60 µm on its logarithmic axis. This is a further plot/text consistency flag, not a digitized replacement D50. The stated postleach 55.83 µm size and the plotted distributions need raw bins and matched sample IDs. The instrument's ≤1% reference-sample error is not the uncertainty of the reported extraction.
- **Product arm:** Figure 4/p. 6 uses 180 °C, 6 h, 8 mL/g and 60.16 µm, distinct from the 52.31 µm best-result arm. It identifies sodium metaborate dihydrate together with sodium carbonate monohydrate in evaporated liquor, and Fe/MgO/ferroan forsterite in residue. Peak identities do not establish a weighed pure product, phase percentages or a complete boron balance.
- **SEM arm:** Figure 5/p. 7 compares before/after images at 2000×/5000× with 5/2 µm scale bars; the residue caption specifies 60 min at 180 °C and 8:1. It is not a matched-magnification six-hour quantitative porosity comparison. Figure 6 does not supply a unique full run join. These arms cannot be pooled to construct the missing product yield.

## Kinetic audit

The numerical audit independently keys all 16 Table 1 rows (64 numerical condition/fit components), all ten Table 2 values, and 67 selected method/result/preview components. All 74 table components match both XML representations. The 17-page table was visually checked. The 16 fit rows include repeated conditions across parameter series; they are not 16 independent replicates. Forty-two recorded calculations use the printed values as diagnostics, with formulas and limitations.

Equation 4/p. 9 is `lambda = 1 - exp(-k*t^n)`; Equation 11/p. 14 and conclusion 3/p. 16 instead put a positive 8.891 coefficient inside the outer exponential. All acquired representations preserve that positive sign. With positive temperature, liquid/solid ratio, size and time, its exponential exceeds one and lambda becomes negative. A formal positive-time evaluation in the audit demonstrates the sign problem without assigning a physical time unit or predicting an experiment. No sign repair is accepted as a validated model.

Table 1/p. 11 labels `ln k` and gives n near 0.5. Equation 6/p. 11 and Figure 8 label k in s^-1, while the extraction plots use minutes. For `k*t^n` to be dimensionless, k must carry the inverse n-th power of the chosen time unit. The source does not resolve that dimensional mismatch or the precise fitting domain. Figure 7 shows a restricted log-time range compared with the full Figures 1–3 trajectories. Original fit inputs, time units, selection rules, residuals and uncertainty are needed; a high R² or n < 1 does not identify a unique diffusion mechanism.

Figure 8a's abscissa is **1000/T**, although adjacent prose describes 1/T and identifies slope -1.060 with -Ea/R. Applying the plotted factor gives `1.060*8.314 = 8.81284 kJ/mol`, explaining the reported 8.812 value to approximate rounding. It is a reported fitted apparent energy, not an independent mechanism measurement. Ordinary least-squares regression of the seven printed Table 1 temperature ln(k) values against `1000/(C+273.15)` instead gives slope -1.180349, intercept 1.267399 and R² 0.993448, differing from the printed -1.060, 1.014 and 0.9907. Its conditional energy would be about 9.81 kJ/mol; it is **not adopted** as a corrected source value. Rounding is not assumed to explain the discrepancy.

The liquid/solid series reproduces the printed slope/intercept approximately (1.520395 and -4.672933), but ordinary R² is 0.972501 versus printed 0.9633. An adjusted-R² calculation gives about 0.963334; this is a possible diagnostic explanation, not a verified reporting convention. The size series gives slope -1.767132, intercept 5.843970 and R² 0.943267, close to the printed labels. These checks use rounded fit constants, not raw measurements, and cannot validate the complete model.

The article obtains 2.185 by adding intercepts 1.014, -4.673 and 5.844, then exponentiates to 8.891. The arithmetic is reproducible; the global-intercept derivation is not justified because each regression contains contributions from other fixed variables. Subtracting those terms under the stated fixed conditions and printed coefficients would yield differing conditional ln(A0) values near 4.7535, 4.9054 and 5.0222. These are algebra checks, **not proposed replacement coefficients**. Correcting the sign alone would therefore leave the global coefficient, time convention and fit provenance unresolved.
