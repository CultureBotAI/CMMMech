Spent lead-acid battery electrolyte secondary resource

Waste acidic electrolyte drained from used lead-acid batteries and its chemically pretreated liquid effluent. The accepted biological experiment concerns the residual sulfate-, Pb- and Cd-bearing liquid after lime precipitation and carbonation, not untreated concentrated acid, solid lead paste, battery grids or an intact battery. This use-derived liquid resource is distinct from elemental lead, galena and other spent-battery chemistries. No constituent criticality designation is inherited.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (2)

Mixed sulfate-reducing culture-associated precipitation from pretreated electrolyte

biomineralization Microbial

In a laboratory batch inoculated with an enriched sulfate-reducing community, sulfate decreased and Pb/Cd fell below the reported analytical detection limits while a mixed solid formed. The authors interpret sulfide and bicarbonate generation as promoting sulfide/carbonate precipitation. These observations support culture-associated removal in this pretreated liquid, without a matched uninoculated comparison establishing microbial necessity or advantage. No isolated Pb or Cd product yield is demonstrated.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Gu2020 Methods 2.1–2.2, pp.2–3: the liquid followed the precipitation/carbonation process cited to Vu2019. Initial Table 1 concentrations were sulfate 3013.5 mg/L, Ca 400.1 mg/L, Pb 0.924 mg/L and Cd 2.10 mg/L at pH 7.01. A mixed culture from an unnamed local wastewater plant was enriched for one month in Postgate C medium before inoculation; the paper reports Gram-negative bacteria but no species, strain or sequence identification. Methanol was supplied at 4 g/L, temperature at 30 degrees C and initial redox potential at a reported 0.1 V without a stated electrode reference. Immobilized SRB were used in a 1-L glass vessel; actual working volume, inoculum dose and immobilization carrier are not specified. Results calls the medium anaerobic and sterilized without detailing sterilization. Parameters were monitored every two days; the text assigns the principal endpoint to 12 days, while Figure 1 extends through day 14. No matched sterile, killed-biomass, no-methanol or pH-matched abiotic removal series is reported. Replicate numbers and the meaning of Figure 1 error bars are not stated. Metal concentrations were determined by ICP-OES, which does not independently resolve the oxidation states printed in Table 1.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: gu2020
Results 3.1, Figure 1 and Table 1 report sulfate decreasing to 27.1 mg/L after the SRB stage, approximately 99.1% relative to its initial 3013.5 mg/L concentration. Pb and Cd are labelled ODL, interpreted explicitly on p.6 as below detection, with limits 0.05 mg/L Pb and 0.005 mg/L Cd. The source therefore reports removal bounds greater than 94.6% and 99.8%, respectively, rather than measured zero or exact complete removal. These are liquid-concentration comparisons for the pretreated effluent, not whole-battery inventory or recovered-product yields. The 99.2% sulfate value in Table 1 includes the later chemical stage.
Partial Source: gu2020
Figure 3 and p.6 assign calcite, galena and hawleyite to the filtered solid after drying at 80 degrees C for 48 h. The prose estimates 96% calcite and approximately 2% each PbS and CdS, but does not supply a quantitative phase-refinement method, harvested mass or closed metal balance; those numbers are not adopted as verified bulk purity or recovery. Figures 2 and 4 and SI Table S1 are thermodynamic simulations, not measurements of each dissolved species or a unique cellular precipitation pathway. Post-SRB pH is 8.81 in Results 3.1.1 but 8.86 in Table 1; the conflict is retained.
Partial Source: gu2020
Methods 2.2 names a 4500-S2− sulfide method, while SI p.2 describes diluted HUMAS sulfate and sulfide kits; the main assigns bicarbonate measurement to Raman, whereas the SI describes carbonate-band analysis at 1066 cm−1 without a calibration or speciation conversion. Thus the printed sulfide/bicarbonate values and ionic labels are retained as source-reported analytical quantities, without independently resolving all species or measurement uncertainty. The supplement does not resolve the missing culture authentication, immobilization or comparative-control details.
Context_only Source: vu2019
Materials 2.1, p.2, identifies wastewater generated when sulfuric acid from used lead-acid batteries was poured into a storage tank at a battery company. Methods 2.2, pp.2–3, describes lime treatment followed by CO2 carbonation. This establishes the secondary-resource origin and upstream processing context; it is not an independent replication of the later SRB experiment. The earlier experiment mixes 200 mL wastewater with 200 mL reagent suspension and dilutes filtrate to 500 mL for analysis. Exact batch continuity and concentration/dilution normalization across the two articles are not established, so their efficiencies are not multiplied into a whole-process yield.

Separate chemical sulfide polishing after the culture stage

other

Addition of ferrous hydroxide to the filtered liquid from the SRB stage lowered the reported residual sulfide concentration and produced a further mixed precipitate. This reagent-driven downstream step is separate from the culture-associated stage and does not establish selective metal recovery or a discharge-ready effluent.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Gu2020 Methods 2.2: fresh Fe(OH)2 at 6.29 g/L was added after filtering the first precipitate, followed by 24 h stirring at 200 rpm, filtration and drying of the second solid at 80 degrees C for 48 h. No fresh microbial inoculation is assigned to this chemical step. The earlier culture may have conditioned its feed; microbial:false describes the stated reagent treatment, not proof that every cell was removed by filtration.
Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: gu2020
Results 3.2, Figure 5 and Table 1 report the sulfide-labelled liquid concentration decreasing from 1001 to 1.27 mg/L after Fe(OH)2 treatment. Figure 5 shows the initially black sediment becoming red on drying and an XRD pattern assigned to magnetite, troilite and siderite. The proposed oxidation during drying is an interpretation; the dried assemblage is not assumed to be identical to the phases in the wet reactor. No harvested product mass or metal-specific isolated recovery is reported.
Partial Source: gu2020
The abstract and conclusion claim that no new impurities were introduced and that the effluent was safe for direct discharge. Table 1 instead reports 89.5 mg/L Fe after precipitation, and final pH 9.08 and sulfide 1.27 mg/L exceed the same table's printed comparison values of pH 6–9 and sulfide 1.0 mg/L. These internal limitations prevent adopting either claim; this is not a verification of regulatory compliance. The initial and post-SRB Fe cells are NA, not measured zero. Laboratory vessel operation and industrial waste origin do not establish pilot or industrial deployment.
