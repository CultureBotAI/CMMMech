Zinkgruvanite mineral

IMA-approved triclinic ericssonite-group barium-manganese-iron sorosilicate-sulfate mineral (IMA 2020-031, printed as IMA2020-31 in Section 1, p. 660; status A, Approved; full description published 4 November 2021; no mineral symbol is printed in the primary paper or in the September 2026 IMA Master List, PDF p. 242) with ideal end-member formula Ba4Mn2+4Fe3+2(Si2O7)2(SO4)2O2(OH)2 (requiring BaO 41.58 wt%, MnO 19.23 wt%, Fe2O3 10.83 wt%, SiO2 16.29 wt%, SO3 10.85 wt%, H2O 1.22 wt%, total 100 wt%; Camara, Holtstam, Jansson, Jonsson, Karlsson, Langhof, Majka, and Zetterqvist, 2021, European Journal of Mineralogy 33:659-673). Named after the discovery locality, the Palaeoproterozoic Zinkgruvan Zn-Pb-Ag-Cu deposit (58 deg 49 min N, 15 deg 6 min E; 170 m a.s.l.), near Ammeberg, municipality of Askersund, Orebro County, Sweden, where it was discovered in drill core (at ca. 530 m borehole depth, intersecting the stratiform Burkland Zn-Pb-Ag ore body at about the 1500 m mine level in a proximal setting relative to an interpreted fossil hydrothermal feeder zone; Section 1-2, pp. 660-661, and Figure 1, p. 661). The holotype specimen (an incomplete drill core slice ~10 mm thick) is deposited in the type mineral collection of the Department of Geosciences, Swedish Museum of Natural History, Box 50007, 104 05 Stockholm, Sweden, under catalogue number GEO-NRM 20170502 (Section 1, p. 660, and Figure 2, p. 661). Occurs in a Ba-rich zone within a sphalerite-, galena-, and diopside-rich metatuffite succession (deposited at ca. 1.89 Ga and metamorphosed at 750 +/- 50 deg C and 0.4-0.6 GPa during the Svecokarelian orogeny), associated with massive baryte, radial platy barytocalcite, ferroan diopside, minor witherite, cerchiaraite-Al (first Swedish occurrence of a cerchiaraite-group mineral; Table 3, p. 665), sphalerite, galena, and minute grains of zhangpeishanite (BaFCl; Section 2, pp. 660-661, and Section 7.2, p. 671). Forms subhedral-to-euhedral platy crystals up to 4 mm, flattened on {001} and elongated along [100], occurring as single crystals and rare radiating aggregates; almost black and semi-opaque in hand specimen (reddish-black and translucent in thin fragments), with a dark-brown streak, vitreous to sub-adamantine lustre on crystal faces and resinous lustre on fractures, brittle tenacity, uneven fracture, perfect {001} and good {110} cleavages, micro-indentation hardness VHN100 = 488-616 (n = 10, mean 539) corresponding to Mohs hardness 4-5 (ca. 4.5), slow solubility in 30% HCl at room temperature (leaving a yellow solution and white residue), no attraction to a neodymium hand magnet, and calculated density Dcalc = 4.42(1) g cm-3 from the empirical formula and single-crystal XRD unit-cell volume (Section 3, p. 661). Optically biaxial positive (+), 2Vz > 70 deg, strong r > v dispersion, strong pleochroism in plane-polarized light from deep brown-red (E perp {001} cleavage) to olive-pale-brown, and Gladstone-Dale calculated overall refractive index n = 1.79 (Mandarino, 1981; Section 3, p. 661). Polarized reflectance values measured in air from 400 to 700 nm (AVASPEC-ULS2048x16 spectrometer, SiC standard; Section 4.1 and Table 1, p. 662) at COM wavelengths (R2, R1) are 8.21%, 8.07% (470 nm), 7.98%, 7.79% (546 nm), 7.87%, 7.68% (589 nm), and 7.80%, 7.65% (650 nm). Micro-Raman spectroscopy (LabRAM HR 800, 514 nm Ar-ion laser at 1 mW; Section 4.2, pp. 662-663, and Figure 4, p. 663) shows O-H stretching bands at 3655 and 3540 cm-1, a strong SO4 stretching band at 988 cm-1, Si-O stretching at 887 cm-1, O-Si-O bending at 663 (strong, overlapping with weak SO4 bending) and 569 cm-1, and (Mn,Fe)-O stretching, Si-O-Si bending, and lattice modes at 450-300 cm-1 and lower. 57Fe transmission Mossbauer spectroscopy (57Co(Rh) source, 54.7 deg magic-angle geometry, MossA fit with chi2 = 0.8; Section 4.3, pp. 662-663, and Figure 5, p. 663) resolved doublet A (CS = 0.34(2) mm/s, QS = 0.32(2) mm/s) assigned to [5]-coordinated Fe3+ and doublets B and C (CS = 1.14(2) mm/s, QSB = 2.30(10) mm/s, QSC = 1.95(10) mm/s) assigned to [6]-coordinated high-spin Fe2+, yielding Fe2+/Fetotal = 42(10)%. Quantitative wavelength-dispersive electron-microprobe analyses (JEOL JXA-8530F Superprobe; 15 kV, 10 nA, 3 um beam diameter, PAP correction, 30 point analyses across two crystals; Section 5, p. 663, and Table 2, p. 664) yielded for Crystal no. 1 (n = 15, mean [2sigma]): SiO2 17.38 [0.34] wt% (wollastonite), TiO2 1.05 [0.12] wt% (pyrophanite), Al2O3 0.20 [0.06] wt% (Al2O3), Cr2O3 0.01 [0.04] wt% (Cr2O3), unpartitioned [FeO] 17.94 [0.44] wt% (fayalite; partitioned by Mossbauer into FeO 7.53 wt% and Fe2O3 11.56 wt%), MnO 8.49 [0.32] wt% (pyrophanite), ZnO 0.03 [0.08] wt% (ZnO), NiO 0.03 [0.06] wt% (NiO), V2O5 0.03 [0.06] wt% (vanadinite), P2O5 0.31 [0.28] wt% (apatite), MgO 0.37 [0.06] wt% (MgO), CaO 0.39 [0.06] wt% (wollastonite), Na2O 0.07 [0.08] wt% (albite), K2O 0.01 [0.02] wt% (orthoclase), BaO 41.26 [0.36] wt% (baryte), F 0.10 [0.16] wt% (apatite), Cl 0.70 [0.06] wt% (apatite), SO3 8.63 [0.32] wt% (baryte), calculated H2O = 0.99 wt% (from OH + F + Cl = 2), O = Cl, F = -0.20 wt%, and printed Total = 98.93 wt% (note that the exact arithmetic sum of the rounded constituents using FeO 7.53 + Fe2O3 11.56 is 98.91 wt%, while for Crystal no. 2 [n = 15] the printed Total is 98.79 wt% vs an exact sum of rounded constituents of 98.75 wt%). The empirical formula for Crystal no. 1 on the basis of 26 (O + OH + F + Cl) anions is (Ba4.02Na0.03)sum=4.05(Mn1.79Fe2+1.56Fe3+0.42Mg0.14Ca0.10Ni0.01Zn0.01)sum=4.03(Fe3+1.74Ti0.20Al0.06)sum=2.00Si4(S1.61Si0.32P0.07)sum=1.99O24(OH1.63Cl0.29F0.08)sum=2.00 (Abstract, p. 659, and Section 5, p. 663; note that Table 8, p. 669, lists Mg0.11 [sum = 4.00] for the O-sheet population and S1.62 + Si0.32 + P0.07 for the [4]S site, and Section 7.1.1, p. 665, cites "Na (0.03 apfu; Table 1)" instead of Table 2). Crystallizes in the triclinic system, space group P-1 (#2), Z = 1 (Strunz group 9.BE.25). Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy, MoKalpha radiation lambda = 0.71073 A, T = 293(2) K, crystal size 0.050 x 0.125 x 0.190 mm; Section 6, pp. 663-664, and Table 5, p. 666) gave unit-cell parameters a = 5.3982(1) A (Abstract, p. 659) / 5.39820(10) A (Table 5, p. 666), b = 7.0237(1) A / 7.02370(10) A, c = 14.8108(4) A, alpha = 98.256(2) deg, beta = 93.379(2) deg, gamma = 89.985(2) deg, V = 554.75(2) A3, Z = 1, while CuKalpha powder X-ray diffraction (Panalytical X'Pert3, lambda = 1.5406 A, NBS 640b Si standard; Section 6, pp. 663-664, and Table 4, p. 665) gave a = 5.3989(6) A, b = 7.0319(8) A, c = 14.821(2) A, alpha = 98.274(11) deg, beta = 93.436(12) deg, gamma = 90.087(10) deg, V = 555.77(9) A3, Z = 1. Structure refinement in SHELXTL (209 parameters, 0 restraints) converged to R1 = 0.0323, wR2 = 0.0758 for I > 2sigma(I) ("R1 = 0.032" in Section 6, p. 664) and R1 = 0.0379, wR2 = 0.0787 (GooF = 1.030) for all 3204 independent reflections out of 14768 collected (theta = 3.78 to 30.03 deg, Rint = 0.0499), with largest diff. peak and hole 1.751 and -1.361 e A-3. The structure (Section 6-7.1, pp. 664-669, and Tables 6-8; note that Table 6, p. 667, prints identical inversion-center coordinates x = 0, y = 1/2, z = 1/2 for both M(1) Fe and M(3) Fe even though bond distances in Table 7 place M(1) at (0, 0, 1/2) and M(3) at (0, 1/2, 1/2), and Table 7, p. 668, omits the "x 2" multiplicity on M(3)-O(8) = 2.250(3) A and prints "<Si(2)-O(> 1.620") consists of linkage-1 HOH layers (central O sheet of Mn2+/Fe2+-dominant M(1), M(2), M(3) octahedra flanked by H sheets of [5]-coordinated Fe3+-dominant MH square pyramids [<MH-O> = 1.964 A] and Si2O7 sorosilicate groups) alternating along c with baryte-like intermediate (I) blocks of Ba atoms (AP(1)/AP(2) and BP) and (SO4)2- tetrahedra (<S-O> = 1.471 A), related to yoshimuraite via the coupled substitution 2 Ti4+ + 2 (PO4)3- <-> 2 Fe3+ + 2 (SO4)2-. The eight strongest powder X-ray diffraction lines in Table 4 (p. 665) vs the Abstract (p. 659) [dobs in A (I in Table 4, hkl)] are 3.508 (70, 1 0 -3), 2.9799 (70, 1 1 -4), 2.8136 (68, 1 -2 2), 2.7771 (70, 1 2 1), 2.6990 (71 in Table 4, misprinted as "714" in the Abstract, p. 659; 2 0 0), 2.6804 (85 in Table 4 vs "68" in the Abstract, p. 659; -2 0 1), 2.1250 (100, 1 2 4; with 2 2 1 at dcalc = 2.1271 A and 2 0 4 at dcalc = 2.1095 A), and 2.1071 (96, -2 2 1). No biological or microbial interaction data are reported in the type mineralogical description.
