## Preparation, controls and conflicting interpretations

The full methods (printed1751) recover crystals by homogenizing agar in80 degrees C water, followed by at least overnight drying in a vacuum desiccator before microscopy and diffraction. No wet in-culture diffraction or matched preparation/drying series is provided. The two harvested patterns therefore do not isolate live-culture trihydrate-to-dihydrate conversion from possible preparation effects. No unit-cell refinement, water-content assay, quantitative phase fraction or isolated lindbergite mass is supplied. The unnamed2012 trihydrate is not retrospectively assigned to falottaite solely from hydration formula.

Table3 gives initial birnessite-medium pH7.26 and25-day surface pH2.93 beneath S.himantioides, from six measurements. These are not measurements at the5/23-day diffraction harvests. Tables1–2 and morphology use23-day S.himantioides observations; pH and clearing statements use25days. These descriptions remain separate. Growth-rate/control ratios, halo/colony ratios and biomass tolerance indices do not supply a metal recovery denominator. Visual clearing and colour loss do not establish a dissolved manganese mass balance.

Figure2I supplies abiotic birnessite morphology and Tables1–2 supply no-oxide fungal growth controls. They do not supply a time-matched sterile diffraction series. Figure5/methods add50 microlitres of0.5M oxalic acid, citric acid or HCl to5mm wells in oxide agar; both organic acids clear birnessite, while HCl gives no significant reported halo. These are separate chemical tests, not pH-matched fungal controls, and their products were not reported as diffraction-confirmed lindbergite. No killed-biomass, cell-free-metabolite or directly quantified fungal oxalate series establishes microbial necessity or an exclusive causal pathway. Figure3 EDX concerns the biogenic MnOx arm, not the selected birnessite product. Figure6 is a proposed scheme, including speculative metal sorption, not a measured complete redox/sequestration pathway.

A separate primary crystallographic comparison, [PMC9738142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9738142/), was acquired as XML (already present in round37). Its Table1 maps reference00-025-0544 to alpha manganese oxalate dihydrate, C2/c, rather than the orthorhombic gamma polymorph. Only the relevant comparison table was assessed here, not its complete figures/SI for curation. It contextualizes the reference-card boundary and does not independently re-identify Wei's specimens. Search-level downstream Ferrier2022 wording about a manganese(III) oxalate intermediate is not imported: the original Wei Figure6 labels manganese(II) oxalate hydrates and supplies no such intermediate measurement.
