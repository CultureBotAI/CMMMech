- **Methods 2.1–2.4, pages 2–3:** washed Citrobacter sp. JH012-1 cells, anaerobic minimal salt medium, nitrogen purge, pH 7.5/30 mM Bis-Tris, 32°C and 200 rpm; 200 mL medium in 250-mL bottles. The printed KH2PO4 amount is 0.027 mg/L. Precursors use 10 mM ferrihydrite and 2 mM arsenate, with or without 5 mM pre-adsorbed calcium; the transformation assay states a 3 mM precursor dosage. Triplicate time points and pooling of the three recovered mineral powders are explicit. Exact collection time for the illustrated post-reaction mineral specimens is not separately stated.
- **Figures 1–2 and Table 2, pages 5–6:** initial two-line ferrihydrite, substrate morphology and elemental composition. These initial measurements do not demonstrate a pre-existing parasymplesite phase. Table 1/page 4 and Figure 3/page 6 concern surface-complexation modelling and adsorption, not mineral-specific recovery.
- **Figure 4/page 7:** morphology, reduction and growth assays provide organism context; they are not separate confirmations of the mineral phase or causal gene evidence.
- **Figure 5d/page 8:** the explicitly labelled As-Fh trace has multiple asterisk-labelled reflections and the parasymplesite PDF 35-0461 reference pattern. The other peaks remain unassigned. This is the main accepted phase evidence. No refinement, phase fraction, isolated product, direct hydration determination or matched sterile post-reaction XRD is provided.
- **Figure 6/page 8:** As-Fh strip-like products in panel a are distinct from the As-Ca-Fh comparator in b. Element maps c–f belong to the calcium comparator, not an individual parasymplesite crystal. Shape alone does not prove phase identity.
- **Figure 7/page 9 and Discussion 4/pages 9–11:** aqueous arsenic is released and As(III) increases despite precipitation. Early 5.0/8.8 mg/L concentrations are not terminal removal yields. XPS is surface-sensitive and cannot be treated as a whole-product mass balance. Modelled calcium site competition is proposed causality, not an exclusive measured mechanism.

## Preserved contradictions and limits

The Abstract/Results 3.3 assign the higher solid-associated Fe(II) proportion to As-Fh, whereas Figure 5c's symbol mapping in its caption assigns the higher square-symbol curve to As-Ca-Fh. The record retains this conflict and relies on the separately labelled diffraction trace for its narrower phase statement. The Fe(II) denominator is solid-phase total iron, not mineral abundance, initial feed conversion or arsenic recovery.

Methods 2.1 describes both 5% inoculum and 2 mL concentrated cells added to 200 mL; Methods 2.6 describes cobalt radiation on a copper target. Neither contradiction was silently repaired. The source reports arrA/arsC but does not supply gene accessions or tests that establish those genes as the causal mineralization pathway. Live GenBank MZ227386.1 explicitly identifies the source strain JH012-1 and a partial 16S sequence, linked to taxon 1896336, Citrobacter sp. Current taxonomy calls that unresolved label a species-level node; it is not a named species or strain-specific taxon. The accession is not an arrA/arsC sequence.

This laboratory system shows biological reduction associated with a secondary arsenate assignment. Abiotic precipitation from microbially generated Fe(II) is not excluded. Vacuum-dried pooled solids do not establish wet-culture hydration. No pure-product recovery, durable immobilization, environmental superiority, field performance or industrial deployment is claimed.
