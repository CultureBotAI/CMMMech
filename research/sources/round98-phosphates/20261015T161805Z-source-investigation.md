## Ludlamite: source-reported abiotic thermal oxidation

The current [IMA September 2026 list](https://cnmnc.units.it/files/editor/IMA_Master_List_(2026-09).pdf), PDF page 129, gives Fe2+3(PO4)2(H2O)4 and grandfathered status. Page 1 defines status codes. The row prints 1885, whereas secondary discovery pages commonly print 1877; no original-description-year claim is curated here. This identity is distinct from vivianite and phosphoferrite.

The complete 17-page [Moore (1971) original](https://www.rruff.net/doclib/am/vol56/AM56_1.pdf) was freshly acquired and matches the round 97 retained SHA256. Page 14 reports Palermo number 1 ludlamite crystals heated in open capillaries in air at 120, 180 or 240 degrees C for five days, with a thermocouple touching the crystals. Quenched products were dissolved in cold HCl and titrated against dichromate. At 120 and 180 degrees C, only superficial oxidation was reported and the powder patterns matched the starting material. At 240 degrees C the iron was reported ferric and diffraction became diffuse and unrelated to the initial mineral. Exact phase identity, yield and universal transformation threshold are not established.

Table 1 on page 2 reuses earlier ludlamite lattice data rather than providing a separate composition for the heated aliquots. Figure 7 on page 15 is a different-mineral series and supplies no ludlamite quantitative result. Pages 15–16 provide a structural explanation and a hypothetical oxidized formula, not an identified product. The proposed hydrogen-collection test concerns phosphoferrite and is not an observed ludlamite pathway. No sample mass, replicate count, conversion uncertainty, mass balance or oxygen-free ludlamite arm is supplied. The other mineral arms are comparisons, not matched atmospheric controls. The NAPS structure-factor deposit was not acquired; no claim depends on its unrelated natural kryzhanovskite refinement. Prior round 97 research reports later corrections to that other mineral's structural data; those are not a repeat of the ludlamite heating observation.

## Regerite: natural-crystal identity

IMA PDF page 183 lists KFe6(PO4)4(OH)7(H2O)6·4H2O, approved 2023-028. The complete official [Rewitzer et al. (2023) original](https://doi.org/10.5194/ejm-35-805-2023), eight pages, was checked with all seven figures and six tables. It describes historical Kreuzberg material from Pleystein, including holotype MSM 38039 and a cotype used for other characterization. Fifteen crystals were chemically analysed; this is not fifteen independent geological occurrences. Table 1 on page 807 reports Ti-bearing material, with water calculated using the ideal structural model and an assumed compensation scheme. Powder-derived cells and the 100 K single-crystal cell are distinct measurements, not interchangeable metadata.

Table 3 on page 809 and Table 4 on page 810 support the P21/c model and 25 located H positions out of 27 ideal positions. H22 and H30b were not unambiguously located. Fe-site bond-valence sums support ferric assignment but are not a direct iron-valence assay. Ti displacement was not unambiguously confirmed (page 811). The printed ideal formula and all-Fe scattering model are not the complete measured natural chemistry. The text's O18–O33 water-site range overlaps its hydroxyl range; Table 4 and the deposit support O17–O23 hydroxyl versus O24–O33 water sites. Other editorial limitations retained in this dossier are the duplicated first/last Table 6 row, incomplete Fe2 labels in Table 5 and a Figure 8 reference although only Figures 1–7 are supplied. No disputed exact bond value is curated.

The actual official [183-page supplement](https://ejm.copernicus.org/articles/35/805/2023/ejm-35-805-2023-supplement.pdf) is a cover plus printed CIF. Relevant header/refinement/site pages 5–12 were inspected against the article; the site list has 25 H positions and no Ti site. Its reflection table runs from PDF page 48 to 183. A text-row count gives 7303 rows, agreeing with the stated independent-reflection count; representative first and last rows and their headers were inspected. This is a consistency count, not a re-refinement or a claim that every intensity is correct. Native raw diffraction images were not examined. Model and publication metadata contain placeholder fields; the EJM original establishes the bibliographic identity.
