Cerussite mineral

Cerussite is the grandfathered lead carbonate mineral, Pb(CO3), recognized by the IMA-CNMNC September 2026 list. It is distinct from elemental lead, hydrocerussite, anglesite, lead oxalate, lead-bearing ore and battery paste. The dated list gives 1845 as the original-description year, not an IMA approval number. No criticality designation is inherited from lead.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Culture-associated lead oxalate formation on cerussite

biomineralization Microbial

Associated Organisms: Serpula himantioides (NCBITaxon:80602) [TaxonMech], Coniophora puteana (NCBITaxon:80637) [TaxonMech], Serpula lacrymans (NCBITaxon:85982) [TaxonMech], Amyloporia xantha (NCBITaxon:1333629) [TaxonMech]

Brown-rot fungal cultures incubated with a cerussite specimen produced surface crystals assigned to anhydrous lead oxalate. The strongest phase assignments combine powder diffraction and microscopy for Serpula himantioides LE-BIN 1368, Coniophora puteana LE-BIN 1370 and source-named Antrodia xantha LE-BIN 1029. The Serpula lacrymans LE-BIN 1192 comparison has a weaker microscopy-only lead-oxalate assignment. This is a laboratory transformation observation, not a measured lead extraction or isolated recovery yield.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Table 3 identifies the starting specimen as cerussite, Pb(CO3), supplied by the Central Scientific and Research Geological Museum of Academician F. N. Chernyshev in Saint Petersburg. The paper states that starting substrates were examined by PXRD and EDX, but supplies no quantitative starting purity or specimen accession for this feed. No battery-paste identity is inferred. Methods 2.2 describes approximately 1 x 0.5 x 0.5 cm blocks in plastic Petri dishes with 20 mL liquid glucose-peptone medium, initial pH 5.5, room temperature and 30 days, in triplicate. Mycelial fragments came from ten-day cultures on glucose-peptone agar. Inoculum amount, substrate sterilization and a separate complete liquid-medium formulation are not specified. The paper names Antrodia xantha; current NCBI taxonomy and its cited ribosomal accession use Amyloporia xantha for LE-BIN 1029. The organisms list includes the tested comparator S. lacrymans; it does not assert identical mineral formation by every listed culture.

Curated Evidence Points:
Support Source: vlasov2023
Table 4 assigns anhydrous lead oxalate by SEM/EDX and PXRD to S. himantioides at pH 2, C. puteana at pH 2.5 and source-named A. xantha at pH 3.5. Figure 3d is the selected C. puteana product diffraction pattern, retaining substrate reflections; Figure 4d shows source-assigned lead-oxalate morphology for S. himantioides. These observations support formation in mixed surface products without establishing complete replacement of the cerussite.
Partial Source: vlasov2023
For S. lacrymans at pH 3, Table 4 and Section 3.2.2 report isolated lead-oxalate-like crystals by SEM/EDX, while PXRD lists whewellite only; Figure 5d is microscopy, not independent diffraction confirmation of lead oxalate. Section 3.2.2 describes both whewellite and weddellite for C. puteana, whereas its Table 4 microscopy cell lists whewellite with lead oxalate and its PXRD cell lists lead oxalate only. This co-product discrepancy remains unresolved. No cerussite-specific uninoculated phase pattern or quantitative control outcome is supplied.
Partial Source: vlasov2023
Methods states that fungus-free controls were performed, but the selected mineral-arm results do not provide a matched pH-control series or a quantitative inoculated-versus-uninoculated phase balance. No killed-biomass or cell-free-metabolite comparison isolates a necessary causal step. Oxalate secretion is the proposed explanation, without an arm-specific oxalate time course or molecular intervention in this study. PXRD methods are referred to earlier papers; the displayed patterns include substrate peaks. Culture association and source-assigned phases do not establish quantitative microbial enhancement, complete feed consumption or industrial use.
Context_only Source: vlasov2023
This is one arm of Vlasov et al. (2023), also represented by the humboldtine record and separately curated mineral-feed owners. The shared paper, its XML/PDF and the different material views are not independent replications. Its comparison with earlier Aspergillus niger studies uses different protocols and does not establish superior extraction rates for the present cultures. The natural adit observations concern calcium oxalates on wood/carbonate rock; they do not establish field deployment of this particular feed transformation.

Causal Graphs & Molecular Pathways (1)

Brown-rot fungal surface transformation of cerussite to anhydrous lead oxalate

REPRESENTATIVE EXAMPLE Mechanism: brown_rot_surface_transformation

Causal pathway linking organic-acid excretion by brown-rot basidiomycetes (Serpula himantioides LE-BIN 1368, Coniophora puteana LE-BIN 1370, Amyloporia xantha LE-BIN 1029, and comparator Serpula lacrymans LE-BIN 1192) to surface dissolution of cerussite (PbCO3) and crystallization of anhydrous lead oxalate.

Causal Edges (3):
  • brown_rot_oxalate_secretion_pathway → organism-dependent organic-acid excretion and medium acidification (produces) → brown_rot_secreted_oxalic_acid : Brown-rot fungi acidify the liquid glucose-peptone medium and excrete oxalate metabolites.[vlasov2023]
  • brown_rot_secreted_oxalic_acid → acidolysis and oxalate attack on mineral substrate surface (dissolves) → cerussite_carbonate_substrate : Secreted fungal organic acids dissolve cations from the mineral block surface.[vlasov2023]
  • cerussite_carbonate_substrate → crystallization of secondary surface oxalate biominerals (transforms_into) → anhydrous_lead_oxalate_crust : Released metal cations precipitate with fungal oxalate as surface biomineral crystals.[vlasov2023]
