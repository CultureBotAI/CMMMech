## Primary experiment and fraction audit

The natural feed came from Abbadia San Salvatore, Tuscany. Page 772 reports greater than 95% HgS, ground below 74 micrometres, with no detectable pyrite, marcasite or elemental sulfur by XRD. No actual diffraction pattern, detection threshold, quantitative polymorph refinement or post-autoclave phase result is displayed. The residual fraction is unspecified. Minerals were autoclaved at 121°C for 15 minutes. Pyrite in the separate mixed-feed experiments was commercial material reported above 85% FeS2, below 177 micrometres, with less than 1 wt% Cu plus Al, Si and K by SEM/EDX. The two feeds and their impurity qualifications are not interchangeable.

The source historically identifies SW9K1 as *Thiobacillus ferrooxidans*, isolated from Campiano pyrite-mine sediment and selected through single colonies. BA-4 came from a Wyoming coal-mine settling pond; mercury-sensitive strain 13661 was supplied by R. M. Kelly. Current NCBI Taxonomy node 920 names *Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans* and lists *Thiobacillus ferrooxidans* as a homotypic synonym. That species mapping does not identify a preserved SW9K1 stock, sequence or deposit accession. No such strain identifier is inserted. A later indexed study of ATCC13661 stock heterogeneity is not transferred to the particular 1987 supplied culture without stock lineage.

The cinnabar-only experiment is a distinct prose result on p774, not Figure 3’s pyrite experiment. It used 1.5 g HgS in 50 mL 9K salts minus iron, final 10^8 cells/mL, 29°C and 250 rpm. After two days, vigorous compressed-air bubbling removed accumulated Hg(0). The vessel was then sealed for 15.5 hours before headspace analysis. The reported comparisons are:
