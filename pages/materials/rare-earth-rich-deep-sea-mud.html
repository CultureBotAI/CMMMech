Rare-earth-rich deep-sea mud primary resource

Naturally occurring deep-ocean sediment enriched in rare-earth elements and yttrium, commonly termed REY-rich mud. This record denotes the heterogeneous geological sediment resource, including pelagic clay with REY-bearing biogenic calcium phosphate grains documented in western North Pacific samples. It is distinct from individual rare-earth elements, a purified rare-earth commodity, an isolated apatite mineral, phosphorite, ferromanganese nodules and rock-surface ferromanganese crusts. Host phases and composition vary among deposits; no single mineral formula or universal grade defines this resource. Collection or physical concentration alone does not make the natural feed a secondary resource. No constituent criticality designation is inherited. A field-proxy study provides qualified evidence for an inferred microbial contribution to natural diagenetic iron transformations and REY redistribution; it does not demonstrate a controlled microbial extraction process, a recovered-metal yield, economic reserves or industrial deployment.

Microbial & Biochemical Mechanisms (1)

Inferred microbial iron cycling associated with natural REY redistribution

redox transformation Microbial

Wang et al. interpret magnetic, microscopic and geochemical signatures in natural REY-rich sediment as consistent with past microbial iron reduction and magnetite formation. They propose that transformation of poorly crystalline iron-bearing particles contributes to REY release into porewater and subsequent phosphate-associated retention. Microbial involvement describes this qualified interpretation; it is not experimentally isolated causation, demonstrated present activity, or a claim that REY themselves undergo iron-like redox cycling.

Experimental Context & Analytical Limits:
Field-derived gravity core GC112, Pigafetta Basin, northwestern Pacific, approximately 5700 m water depth; heterogeneous carbonate-free pelagic clay. Analyses used freeze-dried, homogenized sediment, chemical digestion/sequential extraction and magnetically separated particles, not inoculated mud. The authors compare enriched and background horizons and infer suboxic depositional/early-diagenetic conditions, without an organism-resolved culture, matched sterile treatment, measured in-situ microbial rate, or controlled pH/temperature time course. Magnetotactic and dissimilatory iron-reducing bacteria are proposed functional groups, not identified causal taxa. Pelagic, sediment-interface and later burial processes are not uniquely separated. No recovered product, microbial REY recovery percentage or industrial operation is established.
Curated Evidence Points:
Partial Source: wang2026
Results and Figures 2–4 associate REY-rich horizons with phosphate hosts, magnetic signatures and magnetite-like particles; Discussion and Figure 6 interpret them through microbial iron cycling. These observations support a formation hypothesis rather than a directly measured organism-to-REY transfer. Supplementary Figure 1 also fits substantial biomagnetic IRM components in background samples (78.2% at 45 cm and 76.4% at 525 cm), compared with 75.7% at the labelled enriched 225 cm sample. These are fitted magnetic-signal fractions, not measured microbial biomass or REY fractions, and do not by themselves show selective microbial enrichment.
Partial Source: wang2026
Figure 5 and Discussion use endmember-dependent bulk-iron isotope mixing; a separate calculation combines operational iron fractions, IRM unmixing and modeled sediment retention to estimate a microbial contribution. The reported 17.6% is not adopted as a measured or independently established minimum fraction of REY enrichment. Initial iron inventories, phase-specific isotope values, conversion from IRM signal to mineral mass, and a direct microbial REY mass balance remain unconstrained. Chemical extraction recovery in Supplementary Table 3 is analytical iron accounting, not biological metal recovery. The main text and Supplementary Table 6 disagree on the younger bound of the inferred depositional age; climate causation and an exact age are not assigned here.
Context_only Source: wang2026data
Data.xlsx was parsed across all seven sheets, with selected chemistry/depth, isotope, iron-fraction and magnetic-profile comparisons against the publisher supplement. These are the same study observations, not replication. Blanks, BDL labels, isotope-header ambiguity, differing table numbers and the workbook κ-T temperature-unit conflict with Figure 3a prevent unqualified cell imports. No native quantitative observations or microbial study arms are inferred from these geological samples.
Context_only Source: wang2026peerreview
Final reviewer comments on PDF pages 80–82 distinguish past signatures from ongoing activity, question whether magnetotactic signatures locate sediment porewater processes, and caution that metagenomic predictions do not establish activity. These limits qualify the final paper without treating criticism of an earlier manuscript as an unresolved rejection of its revised qualitative observations.
